## Bratton on Heidegger and Heideggerian critique

- **"The philosophy of technology lost a century wasting time with Heidegger, frankly."** — Palladium interview, "Benjamin H. Bratton on Terraforming the World Order" (January 2021). The bluntest statement of the venue's allergy.
- On phenomenology as suppression: "There are conspicuous reasons why, for example, Heidegger refused to address Darwin. … The phenomenological tradition attempted a kind of suppression: we're not going to think about that, we're going to try to recreate a history of Being that pretends none of that ever happened." — same interview. ⚑ (one clause condensed in extraction)
- On Agamben: Agamben's worldview "dripping with lurid Heideggerian theology"; "If one were to imagine Alex Jones not as a Texas good ol' boy, but rather as a Heideggerian seminary student, this would give a sense of how Agamben himself approached the requests for public comment on the COVID-19 pandemic." — "Agamben WTF, or How Philosophy Failed the Pandemic," Verso blog (2021), excerpting *The Revenge of the Real*.
- Direct engagement with the world-picture essay: *The Terraforming* (Strelka, 2019) quotes Heidegger's "I was certainly scared when I recently saw the photographs of the Earth taken from the moon" and answers that "even as they might have alarmed some phenomenologists, images like Blue Marble are at best the work of a transitional humanism," and "only as humans are uprooted from geocentric intuition can they ever have a planet at all." In "Planetary Sapience" (Noema, 2021): **"The planet did not appear suddenly as a 'world picture,' as Martin Heidegger would have it, but rather as the habitat of a particular species…"**

## Bratton's positive program (the register the essay should meet)

- **"The knowledge of 'climate change' is an epistemological accomplishment of planetary-scale computation."** — "Planetary Sapience," Noema (2021). Computation as *revelatory* — disclosure-language, repurposed.
- Technologies "actually have an epistemological function—they disclose the world in particular ways." — Palladium interview.
- **"Instead of reviving ideas of nature, we must reclaim the artificial — not fake, but designed."** — "Planetary Sapience." (The Bimba map's register: the designed world-picture.)
- AI as "a Copernican Trauma in the making. It reveals that intelligence, cognition, even mind … are not what they seem to be, not what they feel like, and not unique to the human condition." — "The Five Stages of AI Grief," Noema (2024). ⚑ (ellipsis; verify splice)
- The Copernican-turn schema: "a conceptual model develops a technical system to extend how it understands the world, but what is revealed about the world by that technical system undermines the conceptual model." — *The Terraforming* excerpt, Strelka Mag.

## What the venue treats as stale vs. wanted

- Stale: critique-only humanities ("bequeathing tremendous capacities to deconstruct and critique authority but feeble capacities to construct and compose" — Verso); AI doomerism ("a certain satisfaction in the competitive articulation of extreme and depressing outcomes" — Five Stages); fetishising "the uncomputable" as the only site of depth (Noema interview).
- Wanted: "The ideal project for us is one which leaves us unsure, in advance, whether its speculations coming true would be the best thing in the world or the worst." — Noema interview ⚑; "non-grief ways of thinking … neither optimistic nor pessimistic, utopian nor dystopian" (Five Stages); "we need a positive biopolitics … and we need a philosophy and a humanities to help articulate it" (Verso).

## Program self-description (for the emblem-return at §5→0 · #2)

- **"The name Antikythera refers more generally to computational technology that discloses and accelerates the planetary condition of intelligence."** — research.antikythera.org. "Antikythera locates the origin of computation in navigation, orientation and, indeed, in cosmology." — Bratton, Noema interview.
- Agentworld: "a preemptive anthropology of open-world centaur societies"; agents as "human-masked ghosts from the future"; coherence as "parallax effects performed to suit what humans expect to see"; constraints "psychological, philosophical, linguistic, symbolic, existential." — Agentworld site copy.
- Call criterion (drives R13): "the job of philosophy is not to project ancient axioms onto new circumstances but to actively invent the conceptual systems necessary to grasp the present and orient the future."

## Calibration takeaway

The hostility is to Heideggerian *mood* (critique-as-terminus, rootedness, tech-as-fall), not to the conceptual machinery — Bratton trades on disclosure and rewrites the world-picture essay himself. A recuperative Gestell/calculative-meditative argument lands if it (a) treats computation as revelatory, with the two logics locating the fork between disclosure-as-enclosure and disclosure-as-attunement; (b) is constructive — the saving power *built*, not awaited (§5→0 · #2's stated departure from Heidegger); (c) severs the concepts from the baggage Bratton attacks by name. The essay's emblem-return move — deriving the conditions under which the venue's own namesake is philosophically earned — addresses the journal in its own register.
