## Source scholarship

The work in which Foucault formulates the analytics of power in its most explicit form. Its load-bearing moves:

- **The repressive hypothesis and its refutation.** The modern West did not repress sex; it multiplied the discourses on sex — confession, medicine, psychiatry, pedagogy, demography, the science of sexuality. Power does not stand against desire as prohibition; it produces, solicits, and incites the discourse of desire. The "repressive hypothesis" is itself the seductive fiction that power says no; power says more, names more, and installs the obligation to confess.
- **Power-knowledge.** Knowledge and power imply each other; there is no power relation without the correlative constitution of a field of knowledge, and no knowledge that does not presuppose and constitute power relations. This is the thesis the corpus already endorses via the Jorjani notes (P1, lines 11 and 22).
- **Bio-power.** Power over life: the disciplines of the body and the regulatory controls of the population — the administration of life at the species level.
- **The deployment of sexuality versus the alliance system.** The older alliance system (marriage, kinship, the law of the bond) gives way to the deployment of sexuality (the apparatus of sexuality, the family as relay, the medicalisation and normalisation of the sexual body).

Scholarly status: the canonical formulation of power as productive rather than prohibitive, and the immediate interlocutor of Baudrillard's *Forget Foucault* (1977) — the work published the year after, which accepts the analytics of power and claims Foucault stopped one step short of the conclusion that power, like sex, is simulated. Related works requiring their own houses if selected at harmonisation: *The Order of Things* (1966, the archaeology of the episteme — the corpus already places Foucault's episteme-work at "#4 without #5 or #0" in the immutable-subject document); *Discipline and Punish* (1975, the disciplinary society); "The Subject and Power" (1982); and the care-of-the-self volumes (1984), which the MEF-plugin resources already cite (philosophy as a way of life, the Hadot lineage).

## Argument relations (candidate — for the harmonisation pass to weigh)

- **Power-knowledge inextricability (already endorsed in the corpus).** P1 Jorjani notes, lines 11 and 22: "Feyerabend also {with Foucault} sees the power of discursive practice to dialectically crystallise and then dissolve what appear to be even the most fundamental structures of knowledge" and "The point that Foucault makes with respect to the inextricability of knowledge and power holds even more radically in the case of a scientific revolution..." — both marked "we agree"/"align thoroughly" in Frank's annotations. The house records this as the corpus's existing placement: Foucault's power-knowledge is an inherited position, not a new claim of this session.
- **The episteme-archaeology placement (already in the corpus).** The immutable-subject document places postmodernism — "Derrida's différance, Lyotard's incredulity toward metanarratives, Foucault's archaeology of episteme" — at "#4 without #5 or #0": the recognition that systematisations void themselves, reaching the contextual field without completing the return. This placement is recorded as existing; whether Baudrillard's simulation-theory extends or transforms it is a harmonisation question.
- **Discipline → self-discipline via discourse (the step Baudrillard takes).** The repressive hypothesis's refutation is the pivot: if power works by multiplying discourse rather than prohibiting desire, then the discipline of the consumer society is internal — installed through the imperative to speak, to enjoy, to confess. This is exactly the step *Forget Foucault* claims Foucault stopped short of: "you don't need to discipline people anymore because you have installed an internal self-discipline mechanism that is based on desire" (satsang gloss, mRf0KYs1sT4, ~11:25–11:50). Candidate relation: Foucault's productive power is the external face of the same internalisation the essay's §2 fallen accounting describes at the level of the slash.
- **The care-of-the-self thread (already in the corpus).** The MEF-plugin resources cite Foucault's care of the self (with Hadot) as the epistemology-as-lived-practice exemplar: philosophy as a way of life. This is a separate Foucault from the power analyst; whether the essay keeps the two registers apart (power-knowledge in §2, care of the self in §4) is a placement question for harmonisation.
