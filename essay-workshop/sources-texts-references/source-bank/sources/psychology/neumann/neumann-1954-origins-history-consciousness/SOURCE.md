## Source scholarship

Neumann's work is the post-Jungian historical morphology of ego formation: a phylogenetic account in which the individual ego's development recapitulates the history of consciousness as it is carried in myth. The mythic sequence — uroboros → Great Mother → separation of the world parents → birth of the hero → slaying of the mother → slaying of the father → transformation → liberation of the captive — is read as the progressive differentiation of ego-consciousness from the maternal-unconscious ground. Part II maps the same sequence onto the historical stages of ego-consciousness proper.

Relevant operations for this essay:

- **The uroboros** — the self-containing round, the pre-egoic psychic whole that holds both parents in one figure; the psyche's own image of the undifferentiated condition before the cut.
- **The Great Mother** — the maternal uroboros as the first object-face of the unconscious; the archetype's split into good/bad mother.
- **The separation of the world parents** — the first differentiation of the primordial couple (Father Heaven / Mother Earth); the principle of opposites born from the one round. The split of the archetype into good and evil figures (the two parents, the twins) belongs to this same movement.
- **The hero** — the evolving ego-consciousness; the hero's slayings (of the mother, of the father) are the ego's achieved differentiations, and his descent/return is the relation back through the ground it left.
- **The transformation stage** — consciousness opening beyond the ego-hero toward the transpersonal; the inner counterpart of the ego's outward victories.

Neighbouring series: Neumann's stadial morphology and Gebser's structures of consciousness (archaic → magical → mythical → mental → integral) are both phylogenetic, structural accounts of consciousness; their methods, periodisations, and objects differ, and any relation drawn between them must be an operational homology, not an identification. Neumann's status here is the mythic-historical psychology that the essay's topological-mathematical claim does not derive from.
