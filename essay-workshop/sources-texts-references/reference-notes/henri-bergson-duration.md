# Henri Bergson — Duration and Creative Evolution

> [!summary]
> Bergson prevents process from being redescribed as a succession of completed states: *durée* is qualitative continuity in which the past is carried into the present, while creative evolution names novelty that cannot be reconstructed as the mechanical rearrangement of already-given parts.

**Reference kind:** philosophy. **Evidence status:** bibliography seed; edition-specific quotations and page references remain to be verified against primary texts.

## Permitted argumentative load

- **Extracted at concept level:** Bergson distinguishes lived duration from spatialised, homogeneous clock-time. Duration is not a container through which fixed states pass; states interpenetrate and inherit one another.
- **Extracted at concept level:** memory is not merely an external archive added to the present. The past continues as a condition of the present's qualitative character.
- **Extracted at concept level:** genuine novelty cannot be exhaustively predicted by treating evolution as the rearrangement of already-complete possibilities.
- **Argued for this essay:** the agentic “runtime” must be described as a state-changing history whose earlier cuts alter the field of later cuts, not as a timeless input/output table. Bergson supplies the temporal safeguard against flattening process ontology into static computationalism.

## Function in the essay

Bergson joins Whitehead and Gebser on the Western process line while performing a distinct task. Whitehead supplies concrescence, satisfaction, perishing, and renewed data. Gebser supplies mutation and diaphanous co-presence of structures. Bergson supplies lived continuity and the critique of spatialising time. Together they let QL's return carry transformed history rather than merely reset to an earlier coordinate.

## Limits

Bergson is not evidence for the QL count, the Spanda equations, or machine phenomenality. The comparison is structural: a computational process architecture that erases duration into snapshots loses the very inheritance by which one differentiation can condition another.
