# John R. Van Eenwyk — Archetypes and Strange Attractors

> [!summary]
> Van Eenwyk reads Jungian complexes, archetypes, and individuation through nonlinear dynamics: bifurcation, fractal boundaries, unstable manifolds, feedback, and strange attractors.

**Master context:** [[The Return of Zero — Central Argument Plan#§2 · #4 — Complex dynamism and the safeguard against fusion]] and [[The Return of Zero — Central Argument Plan#§4 · #1 — Jung's `X=x`]].

## Extracted working claims

1. Adaptation depends on choice and variation.
2. Chaos affords possibilities rigid order excludes.
3. Chaos can therefore be a condition of growth rather than mere failure of regulation.
4. Complexes and archetypal patterns may be modelled as attractor-like dynamics rather than static psychic objects.
5. Individuation is especially active at boundaries between basins and between order and chaos.

## QL bridge

- **Argued:** dia-ballein corresponds to symmetry breaking and bifurcation—the production of genuinely differentiated trajectories.
- **Argued:** sym-ballein corresponds to symmetry making—the recursive attraction and feedback through which trajectories compose a higher-order coherent pattern.
- **Argued:** `X/x` names the relation between an ordering basin and its concrete, non-repeating trajectories.
- **Argued:** the QL/MEF arche-topos functions as an epistemic phase-space whose lenses are attractor basins; [[Arche-Topos as Differential Field]] carries the derivation, while Van Eenwyk supplies the chaos-dynamical psychological warrant.

## Non-conflation rule

A strange attractor is a technical object in a dynamical system. An archetype is not thereby mathematically proven to be one. “Remainder” here means degrees of freedom, non-repetition, sensitivity, basin boundary, fluctuation, or unclosed trajectory—not Gödelian undecidability.
