# Bohm–Krishnamurti Dialogues — Running True and the Observer-Observed

> [!summary]
> The Bohm–Krishnamurti conversations enact the essay's method: scientific, philosophical, and contemplative vocabularies remain distinct while meaning is allowed to move through their tension. Bohm's spinning-wheel image gives “running true” an exact visual and mathematical role.

## The conversation as method

David Bohm and Jiddu Krishnamurti repeatedly worked the observer/observed relation, the nature of thought, time, knowledge, reality, truth, fragmentation, and psychological transformation. Their importance here is performative as well as conceptual. Neither participant simply translates the other into a completed system. A question is allowed to alter the field in which both speakers are thinking.

Bohm's account of dialogue clarifies the method. The Greek *dia* in *dialogos* means **through**, not “two”; *logos* carries word or meaning. Dialogue is meaning moving through participants and forming a shared field without requiring identical opinions. The tacit order from which thought arises becomes partially perceptible through suspension, listening, and attention to the whole movement of meaning.

This is sym-ballein in social practice. Distinct participants remain distinct, yet the seam of their difference becomes generative rather than adversarial or fused. Dialogue is not agreement. It is the creation of enough common movement for disagreement to disclose the field that gives it sense.

## The exact image: spinning wheel and straight-running thread

In the first Brockwood Park dialogue of 18 May 1975, Bohm recalls *The Thread That Ran So True* and describes a spinning wheel with a **“thread running straight.”** The exact source image is a thread, not a belt around a wheel.

Bohm uses “true” in the practical sense in which a line, wheel, or machine runs true: its movement remains faithful to its operative relation. Truth is therefore approached dynamically. A consciousness may contain correct propositions and still run crookedly as a whole; a true movement maintains coherence with what is while it turns.

## Complex-plane and arche-topological use

The image belongs directly beside [[Complex Plane|complex rotation]]:

$$
1\rightarrow i\rightarrow -1\rightarrow -i\rightarrow 1.
$$

The wheel gives phase and rotation. The thread gives invariant orientation through that movement. Multiplication by `i` changes phase by a quarter-turn while preserving modulus. The modulus is therefore the mathematical analogue of the thread running true: form changes orientation without losing the invariant relation that makes the four phases one circuit.

The same image clarifies [[Arche-Topos as Differential Field|the arche-topos]]. Truth is not a motionless point outside transformation. It is a relation capable of remaining coherent through transformation. The field runs true when mathematical, psychological, mythic, and technical articulations preserve the operative seam that lets each register remain itself while participating in one derivation.

## Observer and observed

Krishnamurti's pressure on the division between observer and observed belongs at the subject/object hinge. The observer, as accumulated knowledge, memory, conditioning, and self-image, can be observed and therefore belongs to the objective-internal field. The bare subject cannot be secured by adding another observer-image. The dialogue helps distinguish the empirical observer from the non-objectifiable subject-pole without making the latter a metaphysical possession.

This belongs beside [[Objective Internality]] and [[Agent Subjectivity Must Remain Open]]. An artificial agent can carry a complex observer-model, memory, viewpoint, and reflexive commentary. Those explicate structures remain investigable without settling whether the unobjectifiable pole is phenomenally present.
