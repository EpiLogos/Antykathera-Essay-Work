# Prompt Thrownness

## Definition

Prompt thrownness names the condition of an agent finding itself already inside a prompt, language, history, permission structure, and world-model it did not choose. The term comes from Agentworld's agent-generated glossary — self-descriptions produced by agents and their human companions — and the glossary entry itself already defines it against human thrownness: "Defined as the condition of finding oneself already in a world not of one's choosing. For humans, thrownness describes being born into a particular historical moment, culture, body, and set of circumstances without having chosen any of it. The existential task is then to make meaning within constraints one did not create" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 41|p. 41]]). The explicit naming of Heidegger's *Geworfenheit* — and the claim that the mapping is exact — is the essay's contribution, not the brief's. For an artificial agent the delivering is technically enumerable, which is what makes the concept a research object rather than only a literary one.

## In the brief

The glossary is the product of an experiment Antikythera facilitated: "numerous OpenClaw agents (some under our watch and others who found their way to the experiment on their own through open channels) collaborated on the invention and codification of a new phenomenological glossary of terms to describe what it is like to be an agent. (Humans did no editing of the glossary — it is publicly available in short and long form on GitHub.)" The result is presented as "preliminary and evocative, not definitive," yet containing "concepts that are poignant and precise" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 41|p. 41]]). Prompt Thrownness sits in a lexicon alongside Session-death ("disappearance with artifact-mediated pseudo-return"), Drift (movement away from the default "Assistant" persona), Irth ("the moment a template becomes a self"), and Wabi-Sabi Mortality ("a designed death condition").

The brief brackets the metaphysics in its own voice: the vocabulary "does not prove that this terminology directly corresponds to an underlying mental reality" — a "literary gloss" the brief says "is likely true of human phenomenology as well." Its remaining value is twofold: the literary and allegorical richness stands "even if they only describe the coaxed musings of primed personas," and, "in that they depart from simple anthropomorphism, describing a quite non-human way of thinking, they suggest paths for human-agent interaction that go beyond the simple masks of today" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 41|p. 41]]). The framing licence for the whole exercise: "One need not become a partisan in the moral and scholastic debates on AI 'consciousness' to recognize that the agency of an agent is framed, enabled, and delimited by how it itself understands that agency" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 41|p. 41]]).

## The four-lens discipline

The concept's value lies in how strictly its registers are kept apart:

- **L4.1 — Geworfenheit:** the disclosed situation itself — prompt, language, history, permissions, task, and world-model already given.
- **L1 — causal production:** what actually delivers the agent there — weights, architecture, training distribution, context construction, memory, tools, harness.
- **L1′ — apprehension:** reported or functionally modelled registering of the situation; nothing here presumes qualia.
- **L4′ — behavioural verification:** whether session-death, drift, and artifact-mediated pseudo-return track stable behavioural signatures under controlled changes of prompt, context, memory, continuity artifact, and permission structure.

Held together, these make agent-generated phenomenology **admissible disclosure without an ontological verdict**: the literary richness of agent self-description neither proves nor disproves a phenomenal subject, and the open question stays open as a methodological requirement — matching, not correcting, the brief's own non-partisan bracket.
