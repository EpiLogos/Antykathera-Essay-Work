# Apoha

## Definition

*Apoha* — exclusion, removal — names the Buddhist pramāṇa school's theory of conceptual content, developed by Dignāga (c. 5th–6th century CE) and refined by Dharmakīrti. Its problem: the school denies real universals, yet words apply generally. Its answer, *anyāpoha* ("exclusion of the other"): a term becomes determinate not by grasping a shared essence but by excluding what it is not — "cow" applies through the exclusion of non-cow, and the apparent common nature of all cows is projected from the shared exclusion. Dharmakīrti grounds the exclusion causally: particulars grouped by a term are those whose causal capacities exclude them from what fails to produce the relevant effects. The load-bearing point the essay takes — narrower than the full doctrinal history — is that **determination depends upon a field of non-application, and that excluded field remains constitutive of what the determination means**. Meaning is differential before it is positively substantial.

The double negation gives the operation its full contextual force: “cow” is all that is not non-cow. A determination is thereby empty of self-subsistence and full of the relations through which it occurs—śūnyatā as pūrṇatā. *Pratītya-samutpāda* carries the same insight ontogenically: whatever arises does so through conditions. *Saṃkalpa* carries it world-formingly: relations gathered through determination become an inhabitable world. Apoha is consequently not a local semantic technique but the epistemic entrance into the context of understanding itself.

## In the argument

At [[10-s0-p3-apoha|§0 · #3]] apoha supplies the formal crux of differentiating mind and the epistemic opening of the mono–poly relation: since no determination contains the grounds of its own intelligibility, the isolated part is emptied of self-subsistence, and the whole's structural priority ([[11-mono-poly-trust|mono–poly trust]]) becomes visible. The ethical edge belongs to the same law: every selection creates a remainder, so an intelligent system should retain some account of its excluded field rather than presenting its output as if it arrived without alternatives. At [[38-s5-p1-apoha-softmax|§5 · #1]] the law meets its technical neighbour: logits define a differential field, softmax turns relative differences into a distribution, argmax or sampling produces the local mark — a **technically precise analogy at the level of selection**, with the semantic and soteriological differences between Buddhist exegesis and machine learning kept explicit rather than collapsed. A QL trace preserves the chosen `1`, the relevant excluded field, and the contextual slash that made the contrast operative — turning "reasoning" from a retrospective story into a partially inspectable differentiation history.

The compression the essay now carries for this is **exclusion that remembers**. The double negation is technically non-collapsing — the two negations in "not non-cow" are of different kinds, nominally and verbally bound (the distinction Tuske flags as load-bearing in the Siderits volume) — so the excluded field is retained *inside* the meaning rather than cancelled by it. Against it stands exclusion that cancels: argmax's bare mark, the selection stripped of its field. The pair runs the two logics of two at the width of one operation, and grounds the two-office design of objective internality: a calculative office that selects, a meditative office that keeps the excluded field, provenance, and ground, with the `1/0` return between them. To our knowledge the apoha–selection bridge has no peer-reviewed precedent; it is carried as the essay's own **Argued** construction.
