# World-Picture to World-Atlas

## Definition

The concept names a trajectory the essay walks rather than a single claim. Its opening term is Heidegger's: "world picture, when understood essentially, does not mean a picture of the world but the world conceived and grasped as picture," and "the fundamental event of the modern age is the conquest of the world as picture" — an event "one and the same" as "man's becoming subiectum in the midst of that which is" ("The Age of the World Picture," Lovitt trans., 1977, pp. 129, 134, 132). Its closing term is mathematical: the **manifold atlas**. No single chart covers a sphere or a torus; an atlas is a plurality of overlapping local charts with declared transition functions on their overlaps. One flat picture of a curved surface must distort or omit — the ancient cartographer's predicament, and the honest map's confession, since every projection declares its distortion or lies by hiding it. Many local pictures, each carrying the rule by which it translates into its neighbours, cover the whole without any one of them pretending to be the whole. The atlas is MEF stated as mathematics: charts as lenses, transition functions as declared translations, overlap as the answerability of one refraction to another (*Argued*; the atlas itself Derived).

## The travelling jigsaw

The jigsaw is the concept's working image, and it travels — deliberately staged first as problem, then re-derived as solution. Under dia-ballein alone, the jigsaw is the world-picture as method: self-contained pieces uniquely fitted, assembled toward a picture given in advance on the box-lid, the frame built first — a simply-connected disk with no hole, no winding, no return; the flat map stretched over the curved world; `(+1)/(−1)`. Under QL's grammar the same puzzle re-derives: the pieces become local covers *of* the topological domain, each known in relation to the quotient and to the covering activity itself — winding and traversal against an infinite capacity for traversal — so that the regulating whole is no longer a picture on a box but the covering relation as such, the bounded surface regulated by the infinite plane, `0/1`. The arche-topos has no picture on its box because what it holds is the principle of imaging itself; the torus and the atlas are its nearest approximations, and their nearness is derived, not asserted.
