# Mytheme

Mytheme carries the forms in which a relation can be seen, heard, suffered, remembered and transformed. Myth, narrative, poetry, media, art and music belong here when their scene or form performs a movement of the argument.

The register asks what the image does. Hephaestus's net can enclose relation; Ares and Aphrodite can figure polarity and attraction; Apollo and Dionysus can stage opposed modes of disclosure; a poem can let zero and one sound through a voice; a film or television scene can expose the political count. The record identifies that operation and preserves the differences among image, doctrine, history and formal claim.

## Domains

- [[myth/README|Myth]] — inherited figures and stories read through their exact scenes and transformations.
- [[narrative/README|Narrative]] — authored and cultural sequences whose unfolding carries an argument.
- [[poetry/README|Poetry]] — Frank's poems and other admitted poetic works, with exact text and provenance.
- [[media/README|Media]] — film, television, digital culture and narrative media examined at the level of form and scene.
- [[art/README|Art]] — visual and plastic works whose composition, material or iconography performs a relation.
- [[music/README|Music]] — rhythm, interval, tension, resolution and return as audible experience.
- [[plates/README|Plates]] — composed visual arguments that gather image, caption, source and essay placement.

## Record form

A Mytheme record identifies the work or scene, describes it closely enough for its operation to be followed, and states whether it figures, embodies, presages, inverts, occludes, gathers or returns a relation. It names the precise essay blocks it serves and the limit of the inference carried from image to argument.

Frank's poems and first-person writings retain their wording, order and voice. Excerpts point to an authoritative complete text and carry a rights decision. External works carry creator, edition or release, scene locator and rights status through Episteme.
