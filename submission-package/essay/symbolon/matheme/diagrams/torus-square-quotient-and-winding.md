# The square quotient and the returning path

<!-- figure:torus-square-quotient-and-winding -->

![Left: a square with both horizontal edge arrows pointing right, both vertical edge arrows pointing up, and all four corners marked as one quotient point. Right: a lattice with a path from zero-zero to one-zero; those endpoints are distinct in the plane and identical on the torus, with winding one-zero retained.](torus-square-quotient-and-winding.svg)

Four boundary occurrences become two generating loops when opposite square edges are identified. A path from $(0,0)$ to $(1,0)$ returns to its quotient basepoint and retains its winding. The `4+2` comparison counts edges and generators as distinct offices of the same construction.

## Proposition, inputs and construction

Opposite directed edges of a square are identified with matching orientations. Its four boundary occurrences become two generating loops on the torus. In the covering plane, the path from $(0,0)$ to $(1,0)$ has distinct endpoints; in the quotient those endpoints represent one point, while the horizontal winding remains.

The SVG draws the quotient polygon and a lattice path as two views of this operation. Blue pairs the horizontal edges, red the vertical edges. The four marked corners share the quotient basepoint. A blue path spans one horizontal lattice interval. Geometry and lettering are original; no textbook figure is reproduced.

## Invariant and exact source

Paired arrows retain their directions; a closed quotient path retains its two integer winding counts. [Hatcher's source house](../../episteme/sources/mathematics-logic/hatcher/hatcher-2002-algebraic-topology/hatcher-2002-algebraic-topology.md) supplies the work identity. Located in the [author-hosted text](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf): p. 5 (polygon construction), p. 34, Example 1.13 (winding), and p. 77, Example 1.46 (lattice and translations), checked 2026-10-04.

The manuscript counts four edge occurrences and two generating loops together as `4+2`. These count different aspects of one construction; their sum is M02's comparison with its earlier operations.
