## Proposition

A pair of dice has thirty-six states while the account tracks which die shows which face. The same throws fall into twenty-one outcomes when the labels are dropped and into eleven totals when only the sum is kept. The grid shows what each account retains, and that (A=1, B=2) and (A=2, B=1) are two states with one total.

## Inputs

Two dice, A and B, each showing 1 to 6; the thirty-six ordered states (a, b); Kripke's count of thirty-six states and of twenty-one possibilities given purely qualitatively.

## Transformations

1. Drop which die is which, (a, b) ~ (b, a). The six doubles stay single, the thirty off-diagonal cells fold into fifteen pairs, and 6 + 15 = 21.
2. Drop the values and keep a + b. The totals run from 2 to 12, eleven entries, and the total 3 holds both (1, 2) and (2, 1).

## Invariant

The thirty-six throws. Every cell remains a possible throw under every account; what changes is which differences the account distinguishes.

## Proof boundary

- **Derived:** the three counts follow from the definitions of the three accounts.
- **Kripke's, not ours:** the thirty-six states and the twenty-one qualitative possibilities (*Naming and Necessity*, preface, 16–18 and n. 17). The eleven totals are elementary arithmetic added here.
- **Argued, in the movement:** that counting depends on the circumstance in which something counts. The grid supplies no claim about possible worlds beyond Kripke's own use of the dice.
