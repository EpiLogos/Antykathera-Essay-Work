# Dia — The Cut

## #0 — Begin inside the field

A discrimination needs something to distinguish, a criterion for distinguishing it, and a field within which the distinction matters. Dia names the operations that use these: contrast, exclusion, orientation and selection. [Dia/Syn](../../../section-rooms/arguments/concepts/C50-Dia-Syn.md) **defines** the productive cut in full before it distinguishes severance, and the [core spine, §IV](../../episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy.md) **derives** the passage from held polarity to cancellation and appropriation.

The input is the polar line `(−1)/(+1)`, which keeps zero as the axis of direction. Both poles have determinate signs, and neither sign produces on its own the axis that makes it positive or negative.

## #1 — Make a selection precise

Let a finite set contain two labelled alternatives `a,b`, and let a stated criterion select `a`. The output is exact relative to the input and the criterion. The unselected `b` is excluded in this selection, and the selection says nothing about whether `b` is available under another criterion.

This elementary construction is productive Dia. A distinction gains its local force by specifying its domain, and proof, measurement, criticism and decision all need that force. To treat the excluded `b` as nonexistent in every possible account would add a claim that the selection never made.

## #2 — Change the operator

Replace the held stroke with arithmetic between the two poles, and three exact results follow:

`(−1)+(+1)=0`,

`(+1)−(−1)=+2`,

`(−1)−(+1)=−2`.

Addition cancels the poles. Subtraction measures the whole span from one end and gives it to the pole it was measured from, which is appropriation, and it comes in both chiralities because either pole can do the measuring: from the positive pole the span is `+2`, and from the negative pole it is `−2`. The two subtraction results are each an exact calculation, and `±2` names the pair as the image of imbalance, the duality hardened into opposition where the held polarity would oscillate. The same span is charged to opposite accounts, so one party's `+2` is the other party's `−2`. In every case the operator has turned a retained polar relation into a scalar result.

## #3 — Identify what the result omits

A zero sum does not keep the identities of its summands, since many pairs sum to zero. A difference of 2 likewise does not keep its endpoints: `(1,−1)` and `(4,2)` have the same difference. Each map loses information that can be shown, and the loss is what cancellation and appropriation consist in.

The native reading names them accordingly. Cancellation lets a scalar terminus stand for the relation, so a balance of zero is taken to settle what passed between the parties. Appropriation lets a pole present the whole span as its own magnitude, so a harm reckoned from one end is entered wholly on that end's books. The arithmetic establishes this formal distinction, and applying it to a psyche or a polity needs the actual case's source, agency and consequences, which the equations do not carry.

## #4 — Preserve the cut's conditions

A returned selection keeps the input alternatives, the criterion, the chosen result and the effects of the exclusion. That makes it a working example of [Syn's](syn.md) gathering discipline in the middle of a cut. Recombining the records does not mean equalising unequal claims, and it makes the chosen result answerable to the conditions under which it was chosen.

The [two temporal readings](chronic.md) can each contain productive or severing cuts: a current map can expose its exclusions, and a historical sequence can conceal the field it inherited. Whether a cut is synchronic or diachronic therefore tells us nothing about its ethical or logical quality.

## #5→0 — Give the result a return route

The output is a distinction that can be used and reconsidered with its exactness intact. Its return asks what stays excluded, what changed in the field, and whether another criterion is now warranted, and the cut's local edge persists inside the gathering that makes the next operation possible.

The [two logics of two](../../../section-rooms/arguments/A13-Two-Logics-of-Two-Dia-Syn.md) **derive** how the pole, its contrast and their generating relation survive the cut. [Dia/Syn](../../../section-rooms/arguments/concepts/C50-Dia-Syn.md) **defines** how this local exactness joins the gathering through which another selection becomes possible. The [Sheffer stroke](../formal-neighbours/sheffer-stroke.md) **compares** as a neighbour that has a Boolean truth table, whose compositions are functionally complete, while selection and holding in the native AND/OR relation follow operations declared in the matheme.
