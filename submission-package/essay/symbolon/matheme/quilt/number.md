## #1 — The evolution of the equation

| Position | Calculation | Operation |
|---|---|---|
| #0 | `1+1=2` | Repeat the unit |
| #1 | `1+2=3` | Add unit and pair |
| #2 | `1+2+3=6` | Sum the proper positive divisors of 6 |
| #3 | `1+2+3=1×2×3=6` | Compare two operations on the same triple |

The proper positive divisors of 6 are exactly 1,2,3. Their sum is 6, so 6 is perfect. The product equality is a further property of this triple, not the definition of perfection.

## #2 — Work the bifurcation and the return

Adding 0 leaves 6 unchanged; multiplying by 0 yields 0. The distinction belongs to the operators, even though the inserted numeral is identical. This gives the native zero-gate a precise arithmetic carrier: retaining context and dissolving a product are different operations.

The modular map sends an integer `n` to its residue class modulo 6. It identifies 6 with 0 *in that quotient*, while 6 remains distinct from 0 as an integer. A six-step cyclic count returns to its starting residue. The chosen modulus is part of the construction; the calculation does not prove that every counting system must have period 6.

## #3 — Recover the seven named convergences

Seven routes gather the numerical convergence: the equation’s evolution through divisor perfection and modular return; the additive/product hinge; zero’s bifurcation; the embedded 3–4–5 triangle; the trigonometric grouping; the geometric sequence; and the harmonic ratios. The interval product belongs to that seventh route through the same base frame. These routes share inputs and sometimes depend on earlier results; “seven” is an inventory of articulated witnesses, not seven probabilistically independent proofs.

For the triangle, `3²+4²=5²`, area `3×4/2=6`, perimeter `3+4+5=12`. The base ratio is `16/9=(4/3)²`, and `(16/9)(9/8)=2`. The sums, area, perimeter and frequency-ratio product are separate operations. [Spanda](../spanda/spanda-equations.md) preserves their exact order of derivation.

## #4 — State the trigonometric construction's domain

Starting from sine and cosine, define `tan=sin/cos`, `cot=cos/sin`, `sec=1/cos`, `csc=1/sin` where the relevant denominators are nonzero. This chosen standard family contains two initial functions and four derived functions. `sin²θ+cos²θ=1` generates `1+tan²θ=sec²θ` and `1+cot²θ=csc²θ` on their domains.

The grouping is a concrete 2+4 construction; mathematics also admits other trigonometric functions and other generating choices. A Fourier representation of periodic phenomena requires the appropriate regularity and convergence hypotheses. Neither that broader theorem nor a universal sixfold follows from counting this selected family. What survives intact is the explicit recurrence of the authored structure through these named operations.

The point→line→angle→triangle→square→squared-circle route belongs to [T1 geometry](../process/t1-geometry.md), where its placements and gluing operation are given. A squared circle here is that symbolic construction, not a Euclidean compass-and-straightedge solution of circle squaring.
