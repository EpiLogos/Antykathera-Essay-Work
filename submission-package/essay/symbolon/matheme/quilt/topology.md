# §3 — The Topological Form

## #0 — The quotient relation

Let integer pairs act on the real plane by translation: `(m,n)·(x,y)=(x+m,y+n)`. Declare two points equivalent when their difference lies in `ℤ²`. The quotient is the torus, `T²=ℝ²/ℤ²`, equivalently `S¹×S¹` through `(x,y)↦(e^{2πix},e^{2πiy})`.

This is the exact input and operation beneath File 3's topological quilt in the [Binary Explication](../../episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-binary-explication/SOURCE.md). The [native topological reading, §VIII](../../episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy/SOURCE.md) identifies points under a stated relation while retaining the cover through which their difference remains available. ## #1 — Count the presentation and the quotient separately

A fundamental square has four corners and four oriented boundary edges. Opposite edges are paired to form the torus; all four corners become one vertex. The resulting cell structure has `V=1,E=2,F=1`, hence `χ=1−2+1=0`.

The curated `4g+2g` reading at genus 1 counts four boundary sides in the presentation and two independent generators. File 3's corner-based positional display is a source-local construction view. Neither is a claim that the quotient has four distinct vertices or that its Euler characteristic is 6. The sixfold is the native coordination of explicit presentation and generating relations, whose offices remain distinct.

## #2 — Follow a loop through its cover

A torus loop based at the image of `(0,0)` lifts to a path beginning at `(0,0)` in the plane and ending at an integer pair `(m,n)`. That displacement records its winding class. For example, `t↦(2t,t)` projects to a closed loop with class `(2,1)`. Its cover endpoint differs from its start even though its surface address returns.

The two generator classes `(1,0)` and `(0,1)` are independent and commute; `π₁(T²)≅ℤ×ℤ`. A mixed loop `(1,1)` uses both. The cover is infinite-sheeted: every surface point has infinitely many lattice-related preimages. The native two-face reading is not a claim that this cover has two sheets.

## #4 — Give pitch its quotient

For positive frequency `f` relative to `f₀`, let `u=log₂(f/f₀)`. Octave equivalence identifies `u` with `u+n`, producing `ℝ/ℤ≅S¹`. Thus `f₀`, `2f₀` and `4f₀` share a pitch class while remaining different register heights. Keeping height restores the covering coordinate; a helix can display the relation.

In the twelve pitch classes, stepping by 2 partitions the field into `{0,2,4,6,8,10}` and `{1,3,5,7,9,11}`. These are two six-orbits of one cyclic action. They are not automatically the two independent generators of a product torus. [Music's substrates](../music/chromatic-substrates.md) makes the selected tonal construction explicit; its bimba/pratibimba reading remains native.

The proposed toric-code witness concerns global logical cycles under specified coding assumptions. That engineering witness requires its distinct evidence rather than being inferred from the quotient alone.
