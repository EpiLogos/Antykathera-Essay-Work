# Valentinian Sophia, Horos, Achamoth and Soter

<a id="valentinian-pleroma"></a>
## #0 — The fullness in conjunction

Sophia belongs to a world already articulated through conjunction. Her solitary desire disturbs that world, and the response restores her place while leaving a passion outside the boundary. The telling then follows that excluded life through formation, the making of a cosmos, the growth of spiritual seed and an eventual return, so that upper Sophia's restoration is an early event in a story whose lower world has still to come into being. From this action the page reads how a limit can sustain what it distinguishes. That reading is the essay's, and the story reaches us as Irenaeus's hostile report, whose voices are kept apart below.

In the [selected English translation of Irenaeus](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#complete-selected-text) of *Against Heresies*, Irenaeus says he has read disciples' commentaries and spoken with some of them, especially members of Ptolemy's school. He also states his intention to refute them. This telling paraphrases the account transmitted in I.1–8 through the Roberts–Rambaut translation, within the reported teachings and opposing arguments of the Preface and I.1–21. Its events are events in that reported cosmology. Irenaeus's accusations, his own theological rebuttals and the later variants remain identifiable voices within the transmission.

At the beginning stands Bythus, the Depth, also called Proarche and Propator. Ennoea, also called Charis and Sige, exists with him. After an immeasurable stillness, he deposits the intended emanation in Sige. She conceives Nous, the Mind capable of comprehending the Father, together with Aletheia, Truth. These four constitute the first Tetrad. Nous, also named Monogenes, joins with Aletheia to produce Logos and Zoe; Logos and Zoe produce Anthropos and Ecclesia. The resulting eight form the primary Ogdoad. Further generation proceeds through two branches, a Decad from Logos and Zoe and a Duodecad from Anthropos and Ecclesia, and in Irenaeus’s report of this [paired generation](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-01) the first principles are kept distinct from their further productions.

These are the opening thirty Aeons in fifteen conjunctions, or syzygies, and their names stay attached to their positions: Bythius in the Decad differs from the first Bythus, and Monogenes appears both as a name of Nous and as a member of a later pair. Generation has given the fullness an order and a plurality without distributing the Father's knowledge equally among its members.

Nous alone comprehends the Father. He wishes to communicate that greatness to the other Aeons, but Sige restrains him according to the Father's will. The [desire to seek the first source](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q005) is thus shared across the fullness and precedes Sophia's crisis: each Aeon quietly longs to behold the producer of its seed and to contemplate the root without beginning, and the youngest Aeon of the Duodecad presses that desire beyond the measure her conjunction sustains.

<a id="valentinian-upper-sophia-horos"></a>
## #1 — Sophia reaches; Horos restores

Sophia seeks to comprehend the Father apart from Theletos, her consort. Her passion is a reaching toward what exceeds her capacity: she strains further into the Father's depth and comes close to dissolution in his sweetness. Horos supports and restrains her, and through [her reaching and his support](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q006) she returns to herself, recognises that the Father is incomprehensible and relinquishes the intention that arose from her passion. Her survival depends on a limit that can sustain what it bounds.

Irenaeus immediately introduces another version. In this telling Sophia attempts the impossible without her consort and brings forth an amorphous substance, a birth unlike herself. She first grieves over its imperfection, then fears that she too will end, and falls into perplexity over its origin and concealment. Turning back, she appeals to the Father; other Aeons, especially Nous, join the supplication. The account locates the beginnings of matter in ignorance, grief, fear and bewilderment. Irenaeus’s “others” marks a variant within the opening report, so that the [alternative amorphous birth](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q007), with its fear and supplication, and the preceding version of her threatened dissolution are two articulations of the passion that should stay distinguishable.

The report then describes the Father producing Horos through Monogenes, without a consort and in the Father's own image. Horos receives several names: Stauros, Lytrotes, Carpistes, Horothetes and Metagoges. His intervention separates Sophia's *enthymesis*—the intention or conception associated with her passion—from Sophia herself. Purified and established, she remains within the Pleroma and returns to her conjunction. Her enthymesis, carrying its passion, is excluded. Upper Sophia's restoration and the expulsion of this formless offspring are two consequences of the same separating act. Achamoth is the name the outcast receives later in the telling, and it must not send the restored upper Sophia into exile a second time, because the [separation](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q008) keeps the restored Sophia distinct from the intention and passion that persist beyond the boundary.

In I.3.5 Stauros names the sustaining function and Horos the separating function, and the [two functions](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q012) belong to one power, which establishes and divides together. The boundary protects a differentiated fullness, yet the life placed outside it persists and will require a further response, since the account has restored Sophia without completing the fate of her passion. The two separate Horoi reported later under Valentinus belong to the variant account considered below.

<a id="valentinian-christ-spirit-soter"></a>
## #2 — Teaching, thanksgiving and the common fruit

The Pleroma responds to Sophia's crisis with a new conjunction. By the Father's providence, Monogenes produces Christ and the Holy Spirit so that the Aeons will not suffer a similar passion. Christ teaches the nature of their conjunctions and the Father's incomprehensibility. What Sophia tried to overcome through solitary extension becomes something the fullness learns together: the Father cannot be measured by the powers that proceed from him, while his disclosure through Monogenes makes knowledge possible within their order.

The Holy Spirit brings the Aeons to thanksgiving and rest. Their forms and dispositions become equal in the account's language, they cease their private striving and share joy in the Father, and from that common state each contributes what is most beautiful and excellent in itself. The Father approves and Christ and the Spirit concur, and the contributions compose Jesus, the Saviour or Soter, also called Christ, Logos and Everything because he comes from all, with angels produced alongside him. [Teaching, shared rest and contribution](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-02) are thus the sequence through which the Aeons' learned relation produces the common fruit.

This sequence distinguishes the earlier Christ from the collective Soter even though the latter also bears the name Christ. The earlier Christ teaches and will first give form to Achamoth; the Soter is the common fruit who will later complete another formation. Their functions become difficult to follow if every occurrence of “Christ” is assigned to one undifferentiated actor. Irenaeus, in [his summary of the two Christs](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q011), distinguishes the prior Christ from the Saviour subsequently produced, keeping their successive formation of Achamoth intelligible.

The Soter's common origin gives the Aeons' differentiated contributions a consequence beyond their restored rest: what they produce together can act toward the excluded passion, and the fullness's recovery opens onto the being outside its limit.

<a id="valentinian-achamoth-formations"></a>
## #3 — Achamoth receives form twice

Outside the Pleroma, the expelled enthymesis exists in darkness and vacuity, without form, like an untimely birth. This is Achamoth, the lower Sophia of the subsequent story. The earlier Christ extends beyond Stauros and gives her form according to substance, initially distinguished from formation according to intelligence. He then withdraws his power, leaving an odour of immortality. Achamoth seeks the light that has departed. Her attempt to cross back is stopped by Horos. The report associates the exclamation Iao with that barred passage, supplying its explanation of the name.

Desire persists after the light withdraws. Grief follows her failure to comprehend it; fear follows the prospect that life itself will leave her; perplexity accompanies both. She also turns towards the one who gave her life. The selected translation contains a tension here: after distinguishing substantial formation from intelligence, I.4.1 later speaks of her having form “along with intelligence.” The second formation remains explicit at I.4.5. In the [first account of formation](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q013), substantial formation and the later attribution of intelligence coexist in the received wording. The second intellectual formation remains a separate subsequent act.

Her affects begin to acquire cosmic consequence. In the first account, tears give rise to liquid substance; the smile that sometimes accompanies the remembered light gives rise to what is lucid; grief and perplexity yield corporeal existence. Her conversion generates the psychic order, including the Demiurge. The alternation matters: sorrow does not exhaust her condition, because the memory of light can still make her smile. Irenaeus interrupts this report with mockery, eventually proposing sweat as an explanation for waters that are not salty, and that proposal is his own satirical contribution and no further event in Achamoth's cosmogenesis. [Remembered light and affective cosmogenesis](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-04) keep the reported alternation of loss and joy on the side of the telling, before the refuter's sarcasm takes the speaking part.

Achamoth petitions the Christ who has withdrawn. He sends the Paraclete, the collective Soter, accompanied by his angels. She first veils herself before their splendour, then receives strength from him and meets his coming. He forms her according to intelligence and heals her passions. The healing does not simply erase everything that has happened: he separates the passions from her, combines and consolidates them into substrates capable of further formation. Passion supplies material substance; conversion supplies psychic substance. What had been an unformed affective condition can now enter a differentiated order.

Freed from her passions, Achamoth looks upon the Soter's angels and conceives spiritual offspring in their likeness. Spiritual seed thus has a different origin from the material and psychic substrates. The Soter's formation enables this new generation, while the angels supply the image through which it occurs. The [Soter’s coming](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q016) thus relates intellectual formation, enduring substrates and spiritual seed through different acts. These stages carry the story from exclusion to the conditions of a world, and the Demiurge has yet to organise them.

<a id="valentinian-cosmogenesis"></a>
## #4 — A maker who does not know his mother

Achamoth now stands in relation to three kinds: the material derived from passion, the psychic derived from conversion, and the spiritual offspring she has conceived. Under the Soter's instruction, she forms the psychic Demiurge. Through him she begins to make the images of the higher realities. She bears an image of the invisible Father, he bears an image of Nous, and the angelic order below images the Aeons, and in this [mediated creation](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q017) her agency remains hidden from the maker she produces.

The Demiurge separates and orders the substances, making corporeal things from what was incorporeal and fashioning seven heavens. He becomes their ruler, the Hebdomas or sevenfold power. Achamoth's dwelling lies above him, below the Pleroma, and is called an Ogdoad. This intermediate “eighth” is a dwelling and relation in the created order; the primary Ogdoad names the eight first Aeons. Paradise also receives a place in the report as an angelic power above the third heaven. The narrated universe contains distinct regions whose names can repeat without making them the same region.

The Demiurge imagines that he has made everything through his own agency. Achamoth has moved him without making herself known, and his declaration that there is no other God expresses his ignorance of what exceeds him. His ordering power is effective within its world, but he cannot yet give an account of its source, since the [maternal relation](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-05) between his heavens and her intermediate dwelling is hidden from him.

### The second account of affects and elements

I.5.4 gives a further differentiation of the passions. Fear issues into psychic beings; grief into the wicked spirits and the Cosmocrator, the ruler of this world. The report calls this latter power a creature of the Demiurge, yet allows it greater knowledge of what lies above because of their different substances. Hierarchical command and knowledge do not everywhere coincide.

The corporeal elements receive the following relations:

| Element in I.5.4 | Reported affective relation |
| --- | --- |
| Earth | Stupor or bewilderment, the more fixed condition |
| Water | The movement of fear |
| Air | Grief |
| Fire | Inherent in the three, as ignorance is inherent in the passions |

The elements and their affects stand in this second reported relation, the [second elemental differentiation](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q020), beside the earlier account of tears and liquid, smile and light, and grief, perplexity and the corporeal, so that the story offers more than one articulation of affect becoming cosmos. Fire's inherence in the other three also differs from any scheme that assigns four independent passions to four elements, and these differences belong to the selected telling and go on constraining its later comparison with the native four-plus-two.

### The human being and the seed's formation

The Demiurge forms an earthly human from a substance described as invisible and fluid, breathes into it the psychic principle and subsequently clothes it with the sensible flesh figured as skins. The human is made in image and likeness through these different relations. Achamoth meanwhile places spiritual seed within the Demiurge without his knowledge, so that it can pass through him into the human soul. Embodied psychic life becomes the place in which that seed grows towards rational maturity. The human's body, psychic animation and spiritual seed thus carry distinct histories ([earthly formation, psychic animation and maternal implantation](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-05) have distinct producing powers), and earth, Demiurge and mother all remain active in the account.

The three kinds acquire different destinies. Material existence cannot receive incorruptibility; psychic existence can incline towards the better or the worse; the spiritual is formed and brought to perfection through knowledge. I.6 describes a saving embodiment composed to receive and bring these conditions to completion. It also reports claims that spiritual people possess salvation by nature, while psychic people require faith and conduct. Irenaeus uses those claims to accuse the teachers and their followers of licentiousness. [The reported destinies and conduct claims](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-06) appear beside the refuter's accusations, and his allegations stay polemical testimony with the warrant of hostile reporting, so that they do not independently establish how every member of a Valentinian community lived.

I.7.2 introduces a Christological telling held by “some.” A psychic Christ produced by the Demiurge passes through Mary; the Soter descends upon him as a dove. Spiritual seed from Achamoth, psychic Christ, specially fashioned body and descending Soter compose its fourfold account. The suffering belongs to the psychic Christ and bodily formation, while the Soter and spiritual seed remain distinguished from that passion. This marked variant supplies a particular relation between the higher saving powers and embodied events and does not replace the earlier distinction between upper Christ and collective Soter, and its [composite Christology](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q025) keeps these distinct actors within the version held by “some.”

The ignorance of the Demiurge also changes. At the Saviour's advent he learns of what is above him and joins him willingly. He continues to administer the world and care for the Church in expectation of his appointed reward. Disclosure preserves a place for his agency while changing his understanding of its scope. The account of prophetic speech similarly distinguishes utterances arising through Achamoth, the spiritual seed and the Demiurge, and assigns inspired speech to no single level. The [distributed speech and later disclosure](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-07) arise through different powers: he at first dismisses those utterances, and their later disclosed origins change his allegiance while he continues to administer the world.

<a id="valentinian-differentiated-restoration"></a>
## #5→0 — Bridal return and what remains outside

Consummation waits upon the formation of the spiritual seed. When it has reached perfection, Achamoth leaves her intermediate dwelling and enters the Pleroma. She joins the Soter as bride to bridegroom. The spiritual people relinquish their psychic souls, enter the fullness and become brides of the angels who accompany the Soter. The generation occasioned by Achamoth's sight of those angels now reaches its corresponding union.

Other beings receive other destinations. The Demiurge passes into the intermediate dwelling Achamoth has left, and righteous psychic souls rest there. Those souls do not thereby enter the Pleroma. Fire, hidden within the world until its appointed moment, consumes matter and finally itself. I.7.5 further distinguishes good and evil psychic lives while maintaining the spiritual/material/psychic account of humanity. The ending gathers some beings into higher conjunction, resituates others and destroys material existence, and [the promised consummation](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-07) keeps the conjunctions attained, the intermediate lives retained and the matter consumed distinct.

The ending changes how the earlier boundary is understood. Horos's exclusion did not make the lower passion inert: beyond the limit, Achamoth received form, generated seed and mediated a cosmos. Her return likewise does not carry every product of that cosmos into the same destination. The restoration of upper Sophia, the formation of lower Achamoth, the Demiurge's education and the seed's maturation each retain their own time and consequence.

The reported school reads these movements back through Scripture. The lost sheep figures Achamoth wandering beyond the fullness; the lost coin figures upper Sophia's restoration. Bridal language gives conjunction its scriptural expression, and the exposition of John's prologue unfolds the first Ogdoad through named relations. Irenaeus closes that exposition by assigning it to Ptolemy, while contesting the school's handling of the text. His comparison of their exegesis to rearranging a royal mosaic into a fox belongs to his refutation, followed by further rebuttal in I.9 and the Church's rule of faith in I.10. In this [exegetical encounter](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-08) the transmitted readings stand beside the witness's opposing argument: for Irenaeus the same scriptural pieces acquire a different meaning when their original relations are broken, and his confession states the rule of faith by which he rejects that arrangement.

<a id="valentinian-variants"></a>
### Variants that keep the selected telling situated

The surrounding chapters preserve disagreement within the family of teachings. At I.11.1, Irenaeus attributes to Valentinus a first Dyad of Arrhetus and Sige, two Horoi, and a different account of an expelled mother, Christ and the remaining shadow from which the Demiurge proceeds. Secundus differentiates the primary Ogdoad into light and dark tetrads and places the fallen power among the fruits of the thirty. Other teachers differ over whether the first principle has a consort and over the number and arrangement of primary powers. These [different principles, generations and boundaries](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-11) belong to distinct teachers, and their acts and origins cannot all be stages in one generation.

I.12 reports Ptolemy's followers describing thought and will as the Father's two affections, further accounts of simultaneous or reordered generation, and competing origins of the Soter: all Aeons, ten, twelve, Christ and the Spirit, or primordial Anthropos. The collective Soter of the main telling remains intact precisely because these alternatives are not added as successive episodes in his birth. The selected edition flags corrupt readings and uncertain identifications in this material. Irenaeus reports these alternative productions in [his account of the competing productions](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-12), and the selected edition’s uncertain readings and identifications limit how exactly their speakers can be reconstructed.

The Marcosian branch adds a distinct symbolic arithmetic. In I.15.1, Sige's revelation to Marcus explicitly adds two and four to make six, then quadruples six to make twenty-four letter forms. I.17.1 makes the four physical elements images of the Tetrad and combines their four qualities—heat, cold, dryness and moisture—with them as an Ogdoad. These operations belong to the reported Marcosian letter and cosmological correspondences; they neither supply a missing episode in Achamoth's passion nor establish Taylor's particular founding comparison. [The two-plus-four letter formation](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q038) and [the elements with their qualities](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1-q039) preserve these differently counted objects: letter forms produced through two-plus-four and quadrupling, and elements with their qualities making four-plus-four.

The selected context also contains different redemptions. I.21 describes bridal enactments, baptisms with different formulas, oil and water, teachers who reject sensible rites in favour of knowledge itself, and deathbed invocations for passage beyond ruling powers and the Demiurge. Knowledge dissolving ignorance and an ascent beyond local judgment are present, but no one rite represents every account. [The different redemptions](../../../../episteme/sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/irenaeus/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1/irenaeus-1885-against-heresies-book-1.md#telling-21) separate teachers by what they enact, reject or say in passage beyond judging powers. The full I.13–20 context retains Marcus's reported revelation, numerology, exegesis and Irenaeus's allegations without converting them into a single expanded original myth.

<a id="valentinian-authorial-return"></a>
### Solitary reach, sustained distinction and joint contribution

Differentiation becomes consequential when an emerging power can answer the order from which it formed. In [Neumann's account of hero and centroversion](../../../archetypal-ground/neumann-images/WHOLE.md#neumann-hero-centroversion), emancipation from an engulfing origin or arresting parental authority leads toward a differentiated participation whose whole exceeds the ego's attained command, and Taylor proposes the Demiurge's effective but derivative office beside that relation. The Pleroma and the lower cosmos keep their particular religious regions and promised fates, and Irenaeus's report, the English translation and Taylor's reception have distinct historical dates, so that a narrated region does not become a chronological stage of psychic development.

Taylor's [copula comparison, IV.5 and VI.3](../../../../../quilt/copula-derivation-chain-and-resource-pools.md) **sources** the proposed founding relation, which follows Sophia's solitary reach through Horos's supporting distinction to the Aeons' common contribution. [His account of the two logics and differentiated powers](../../../../../quilt/27-07-26-QUILTING-FOR-FULL-ARGUMENT.md) asks how naming, bounded powers and their relations let an ordered plurality act within a more encompassing world, and the telling gives that question different consequences in different acts. Sophia survives within her restored conjunction, her excluded passion persists, the common fruit acts upon it, and Achamoth mediates a world whose maker cannot at first know her. The comparison keeps those acts distinct and puts no named power in an interchangeable office.
