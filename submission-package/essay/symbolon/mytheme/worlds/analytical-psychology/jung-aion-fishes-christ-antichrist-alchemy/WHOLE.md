# Jung's *Aion* — Self, Christ/Antichrist, Fishes, Alchemy, and the Historical Shadow

This record tells *Aion* whole and then lays three layers beside it: the mythemic (the historical drama of a governing image and its contrary), the mathemic (the signed polarity and its collapses) and the epistemic (Jung's text, the records and their sources). Each layer amplifies the others and keeps its own office, so Jung's account is a psychological interpretation of history, the notation is the essay's, and the comparison between them is the essay's too. Its status is Argued, with the appointments marked unratified in the ledger below.

## #0 — A psyche enters the history of its governing image

*Aion* begins with the person who says “I” and ends with a transformed relation to the psychic whole within which that person speaks. Between them Jung follows a Christian image of wholeness through its rejected contrary, its fish symbolism, its historical expectations and the alchemist's attempt to work what the governing image leaves unresolved. Each figure changes its office as the inquiry proceeds. The fish that signifies the redeemer becomes food, monster, elusive substance and an attracting centre; the final return has to carry these encounters within it.

This whole is produced in Jung's twentieth-century analytical psychology. His foreword is dated May 1950; the German work appeared in Zurich in 1951. He writes amid the European crisis of inherited religious orientation, approaching historical symbols through his experience as a physician. His narrated geography is much wider: Jewish and Christian materials of the eastern Mediterranean, Egyptian and Syrian cultic accounts, late-antique Gnostic texts, medieval European prophecy and heresiology, Arabic–Latin alchemical transmission, and the emergence of European natural science. These places are the settings and transmission routes of materials Jung interprets. They retain their differences within a book produced in a particular modern psychological world.

Three times operate together. An individual encounters autonomous psychic contents in a life; communities inherit and alter religious images across centuries; Jung arranges those historical materials through the symbolic time of the aeon and the precession of the equinoxes. He proposes meaningful correspondences among them. A source image, Jung's aeonic interpretation of it and the essay's later use of historical frame-awareness each stand on their own footing.

Jung's [Aion source house](../../../../episteme/sources/psychology/jung/jung-1978-aion-cw9-2/jung-1978-aion-cw9-2.md) **sources** this telling, developed through selected passages of a recovered local Hull translation, a **1979 paperback reprint of the second edition whose copyright page records the additional 1978 corrections**. The house selects the 1978 hardcover printing, and the equivalence of the two printings is uncollated. Chapter, CW paragraph and printed-page locators below refer to the consulted carrier and support paraphrase of Jung's argument; no quotation card has been promoted. Our [whole-story law](../../../../../quilt/final-argument-quilt-2026-08-23/MYTHEME-WHOLE-STORY-AMPLIFICATION-LAW.md) **defines** the direction of reading: the complete interpretive movement precedes any reusable fish, Christ, Antichrist, alchemical or aeonic index.

## #1 — Ego, shadow and the image of perfection

The ego first appears as the centre of reference for conscious contents. Its rootedness in bodily and psychic conditions already exceeds what that centre knows of itself. Jung then brings the person up against the shadow: traits whose recognition requires moral effort because they belong to the person who repudiates them. Affect can seize the initiative. Projection makes an inwardly active factor seem to be a property conclusively encountered outside. The world then gives back the rejected face, while the isolation that follows appears to confirm that the world itself is hostile. A person can inhabit this self-reinforcing enclosure without recognising the activity that makes it. (Chs. I–II, §§1–5, 13–19; pp. 3–4, 8–10.)

The anima/animus movement deepens this problem of relation. In Jung's opening drama a son remains bound to an image of the mother as the giver of effortless fulfilment. Actual encounters disappoint the image; the demand that another person embody it prevents that person from being met. Differentiation requires recognising the image as a psychic reality and loosening its compulsory embodiment in a particular other. Jung's explicitly gendered anima/animus scheme belongs to his historical account; its extension to all persons is not assumed here. His dramatic presentation matters: the symbol acts through longing, loyalty, fear and attraction before the interpreter can make it an abstract object. (Ch. III, §§20–26; pp. 11–13.)

The approach to the Self consequently has a double danger. An ego can be overwhelmed by the larger psychic whole; it can also appropriate that whole as its own magnitude. Jung describes both directions of inflation and insists on discrimination, worldly adaptation and the relative autonomy of unconscious reality. A single formula cannot decide every conflict of duty. Integration must leave someone capable of judgment and responsibility. (Ch. IV, §§43–49; pp. 23–26.)

Christ now enters as a culturally effective symbol of the Self. In the image of the heavenly man, the second Adam and the restored divine image, wholeness has a human face. Jung follows renewal, descent into the depths and the marriage imagery through which human life can participate in that image. Yet the perfection of the image produces a psychological difficulty: an exclusively good and luminous figure does not include all the opposites active in the psyche. In his argument with *privatio boni*, Jung keeps the experience and efficacy of evil in view even when its substantial existence is denied theologically. (Ch. V, §§70–77; pp. 37–42.)

Antichrist belongs to this ensuing drama. A contrary excluded from the representation of totality remains active and acquires a compensatory historical figure. So the Christian aeon bears an opposition within its own field of meaning: its dominant image and the counter-image answer one another. This is Jung's psychological interpretation of the God-image, with a distinct burden from Christian doctrinal judgment. Recognising an evil factor as real does not confer goodness on an evil act. Jung's conclusion returns to the human suffering which a verbal reconciliation of good and evil leaves undiminished.

## #2 — The fishes give the age its temporal image

Jung opens the fish inquiry by noting that Christian interpretation gives Christ and the devil several shared animal symbols. A symbol can carry more than one valuation. Christian fish have their own baptismal, eucharistic and epigraphic settings: the baptised live as little fishes, and the travelling believer of the Abercius inscription is fed a fish offered with bread and wine. Jung sets these alongside older Near Eastern fish traditions and then asks why the image became especially active around Christianity. He proposes astrology as a second source of its activation. Within that proposal the broad distribution of fish imagery and the specific Christian uses stay distinguishable. (Ch. VI, §§127–128; pp. 72–74.)

Pisces lets the double figure acquire duration. Jung assembles planetary conjunctions, Jewish messianic calculations, medieval expectations and the precessional movement of the spring-point. His own notes sometimes correct the astronomy reported by his historical authorities. His calculations, their transmission and the expectations placed on them are parts of his inquiry; an inherited forecast is not an independently confirmed causal law of history. A constellational image makes the contrary tendencies of an age visible together, while the age lives them successively. (Ch. VI, §§128–137; pp. 74–82.)

This doubled life also takes narrative form in Jung's report of the *Pistis Sophia*. A spirit resembling the child Jesus arrives at Mary's house and asks for his brother. Mary, uncertain whether it is a tempting phantom, binds it to a bed and goes to find Jesus and Joseph in the vineyard. On their return the double is released; the two embrace and become one. Jung interprets the meeting through a nature rising from below and a spirit descending from above. Here doubling leads to reunion. It cannot be given the same antagonistic office as Christ/Antichrist merely because two figures appear. (Ch. VI, §§131–133; pp. 78–81; Jung's mediated reading of that work.)

The historical pressure sharpens in the sixteenth-century turn. Jung reads the Church's schism and the outward movement of discovery and conquest of Nature as a crossing of the earlier upward religious aspiration. In his Nostradamus chapter, the letter to Henry II dated 27 June 1558 and the later horizon of revolutionary change make expectation itself a historical actor. Jung's reading places the Christian world's opponent within the European unconscious; projecting it entirely onto an external people would miss his psychological argument. His claims of precessional correspondence remain his interpretive chronology, including the difference between the sixteenth-century turn and the later dates named in prophecy. (Ch. VII, §§150–154; pp. 95–97.)

The subsequent historical fish chapter complicates any simple sequence of one deity replacing another. Jung considers the overlap of shepherd, ram and fish imagery around the beginning of the Christian era. In the account he takes from Epiphanius, worshippers at Alexandria bring a sacred image up from an underground sanctuary after a night of celebration, carry it around the precinct and return it below: the virgin has borne the Aeon. Jung explicitly notes that Epiphanius describes a pagan rite, while using it as testimony to Christian truth. Reception has already changed the image's argumentative office before Jung reads it. (Ch. VIII, §§162–165; pp. 103–104.)

The apocalyptic Lamb likewise refuses a single undifferentiated label. Jung contrasts the sacrificial lamb with the wrathful, many-horned conqueror. He calls the latter a shadow counterpart and distinguishes it from Antichrist. One shared scriptural field can contain a transformed Christ-image without making every darkening of that image the devil's son. His explanation invokes the historical resentment of Jewish Christians under Rome; that explanation is attributed to Jung rather than silently established as independent ancient history. (Ch. VIII, §§166–168; pp. 105–106.)

Ambivalence then becomes an action within the fish itself. In Jung's assemblage of Leviathan traditions, the monster opposed to God divides into a conflict between monsters; at the end the formidable creature becomes food for the devout. He relates this splitting to the differentiation and humanisation of previously undifferentiated psychic contents. Terror, opposition and nourishment are successive offices, not interchangeable dictionary meanings. His comparisons traverse distinct Jewish texts and their later receptions. Converting the monster into a meal does not merge this source narrative with the project's independently authored Meal whole. (Ch. IX, §§181–187; pp. 118–121.)

## #3 — The fish is caught, worked and made to transform

Alchemy alters the position of the human participant. The image has become something to find and work on, and the relation between the worker and the transforming substance becomes part of the transformation. Jung enters through the *Turba* tradition and its associated allegories, placing them within Arabic–Latin transmission. His historical dating and proposed routes belong to his scholarship and require separate controls for independent transmission claims. Within the text he reads, a round sea creature without bones or outer covering is heated slowly and treated with sea water until it shines; the preparation finally becomes an eyewash through which the practitioner can understand the art. What happens to the substance returns as an alteration in the capacity to see. (Ch. X, §§193–197; pp. 126–128.)

Jung's identification of this creature as a jellyfish is an interpretation of the recipe. He also records a sixteenth-century adept reading the round creature as an actual fish, and follows stones reputedly held within another fish's body. Opacity and radiance in the stone lead him through Saturn, lead and the bright mercurial substance concealed within darkness. So the alchemical fish carries both the material with which the work begins and the transformed *lapis* toward which it proceeds. Its relation to the Christian redeemer is mediated through the *filius philosophorum*. Alchemical vocabulary does real work here: a hidden virtue is found within a difficult substance and becomes perceptible through an operation. (Ch. X, §§194–197, 213–216; pp. 127–128, 137–139.)

Jung also follows another, source-distinct pair of fishes through a revelation preserved in heresiological transmission from Carcassonne. John asks Christ about Satan before the fall. Descending through the elements, Satan finds two fishes yoked like oxen beneath the earth, working from west to east at the invisible Father's command. Fire halts his further descent; he returns upward, solicits the angels, falls, and receives a limited time in which to fashion the world. Jung then compares the fishes' ruling office with Augustine's priestly and royal interpretation of the feeding miracle. He expressly distinguishes that Christian pair from the heretical narrative and treats an identification with the elder and younger sons as conjectural. This preserves the story's agents, journey and limits instead of recoding its fish pair as a universal Christ/Antichrist cipher. His proposed Cathar–alchemical connection through Rupescissa remains a historical hypothesis. (Ch. X, §§225–229; pp. 145–147.)

The Echeneis brings the reversal into focus. In the received story this small fish can halt a ship despite its sails and rowers. Its attracting power overcomes the human intention to advance. But the alchemist seeks a magnet capable of attracting the fish itself and drawing the hidden substance to the surface. Jung emphasises this reversal: the practitioner now works with the power that previously held the vessel. A magnet can be taught; it names a doctrine or operative understanding as well as an instrument. *Mercurius* can consequently name substance and philosophy. A worker's changed relation to the elusive centre is integral to the work. (Ch. XI, §§239–241; pp. 154–156.)

Alchemy's aftermath changes the scale again. In Jung's account, the alchemical epoch gives birth to science in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries and then recedes from view as an outlived mother. By the eighteenth-century division of faith and knowledge, each side has lost access to something the earlier practice tried to hold together. The investigating psyche disappears from the account of objective knowledge even while making that knowledge possible. This is the book's explicit route into scientific modernity: the child inherits an operation while forgetting the condition through which it became possible. His historical genealogy and his account of psychology's relation to physics can each be assessed separately. (Ch. XII, §§267–271; pp. 173–174.)

## #4 — The historical descent returns as a dynamic Self

The Gnostic return reaches back into the beginning of the era for earlier images of attraction and integration. Jung reads the Naassene teaching reported by Hippolytus as a water of doctrine that enables each nature to receive what belongs to it. He then distinguishes the Peratic serpent-Son who conveys signs down from the Father and carries their awakened substance back upward. In the first comparison doctrine attracts; in the second, the redeeming figure does. This keeps the local movement within Jung's comparison. These are his readings of reported doctrines, not a replacement text for Hippolytus or a fusion with the separately housed Valentinian Sophia–Horos–Achamoth whole. (Ch. XIII, §§287–291; pp. 184–185.)

The question returns inward through Monoïmos: investigate the one who speaks of “my” spirit, understanding, soul and body, and the affects that arise without the ego's command. Jung compares this with Indian Self language while distinguishing that comparison from a demonstrated route of influence. Now the historical figures lead back to the initial question of the ego's relation to what lives through it. (Ch. XIV, §§347–350; pp. 222–223.)

The concluding quaternio sequence makes the historical interpretation itself answerable. Jung explicitly acknowledges that his inserted **Shadow Quaternio** is a modern reconstruction absent from the Gnostic texts. He supplies the downward balancing movement because a purely upward orientation no longer describes the modern psychic situation. Disappointment at the expected return of Christ, increased human self-consciousness, the chthonic serpent and the approach to Nature form stages in that construction. His account of early natural science even distinguishes a crossing of the religious attitude from a simple reversal of it: the Paradise/lapis movement turns across that orientation. Broad aeonic opposition does not make every transition a 180-degree negation. (Ch. XIV, §§402–407; pp. 254–257.)

Jung then gives the sequence its own symbolic formula. Four major states are Anthropos **A**, Shadow **B**, Physis/serpent **C**, and lapis **D**; lower-case letters articulate the corresponding transformations within the states. The ring returns to its beginning through differentiation and integration. At printed p. 259 the connected diamonds show each whole unfolding into four and passing onward, rather than four unrelated emblems placed side by side. That return changes an unconscious totality into a conscious one. Jung describes a dynamic process within the Self and relates it to *solve et coagula* and the circular *opus*. His subsequent comparison with nuclear processes is explicitly hypothetical in §412. (Ch. XIV, §§408–421; pp. 257–265.)

This is the source-grounded place for the project's encounter with Jung's quaternary diamonds. His **A/B/C/D** schema has its own symbols and psychological assumptions. [Taylor's core theorem field](../../../../episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy.md) **sources** the native **X/x** and all eight determinations, their sequence and inversions. We can read the recurrent local wholes through its established recursive Bimba–Pratibimba operation only by preserving the particular whole unfolded at each stage: the Shadow's field is not the Anthropos field relabelled, and the resulting conscious return is not the unchanged starting condition. The [recursive Bimba–Pratibimba office](../../../../../section-rooms/arguments/concepts/bimba-pratibimba.md) **defines** how an achieved field can ground its own internal determinations while remaining situated within a wider field. That operation is established in QL; the diagram-specific application is a proposed reading whose mapping must preserve each state’s particular transformations. It attributes neither the QL derivation nor a universal six-position scheme to Jung.

Jung's conclusion returns to the clinician's first encounter with the shadow. It includes healthy instincts and creative capacities as well as morally reprehensible tendencies. Anima and animus figures also acquire positive and negative effects. The Self's *complexio oppositorum* requires this differentiated recognition; merely declaring all things good leaves suffering untouched. Jung turns attention back toward one's own error and responsibility. History has altered what the person must be able to acknowledge, and the person must now live that changed relation. (Ch. XV, §§422–427; pp. 266–268.)

## #5→0 — The age becomes visible as an age

<a id="aion-historical-arbitration-return"></a>
The project's operation here is **historical de-monopolisation**. An age inherits a governing image, and the image becomes the apparently immediate background of every judgment. Its exclusions stay active in that background, and contrary images and transforming practices bring the history in it into view. An age can then be seen as an age, and its inherited measure becomes revisable. This is the project's own return on Jung's telling and makes no independent claim that each genealogy Jung proposes has been historically verified.

Laid beside the signed arithmetic, the movement has a precise form. An image of wholeness that is only good takes the whole span of the field for one pole, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the contrary it excludes bears the same span charged from the other end, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`. Denying that evil has any substance, as the doctrine of *privatio boni* does, cancels the contrary instead of holding it, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, and Jung's quarrel with that doctrine keeps the efficacy of the excluded pole in view. What the telling asks in return is the held polarity `(−1)/(+1)`, in which the Christian image and its contrary each have their sense through the other. [Draft 3](../../../../episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-definition-god-draft3/taylor-2026-definition-god-draft3.md) **sources** the reading that identifying the divine only with the good expels the dark from the God-image, and the arithmetic names what that expulsion does. This amplification lays the mythemic action of the aeon, the mathemic operations and Draft 3 beside one another. Its status is Argued, and it assigns no QL coordinate to Christ or Antichrist (see the ledger).

Jung's projection passage gives one psychological sequence of exclusion, apparent confirmation and isolation, and the historical telling gives a different sequence in which an inherited whole-image meets its contrary. This whole **compares** the two with [Complex as Local Arbitration Regime](../../../../../section-rooms/arguments/A19-Complex-as-Local-Arbitration-Regime.md). A complex, a theological image, a protected account and a civilisation's symbolic frame have different substrates and carry different evidential burdens, and in each the return keeps the local power and exposes only the claim to exhaust the containing field. Its containing-field operation keeps its own QL derivation.

<a id="aion-differentiated-return"></a>
The whole **returns-to** [Individuation / Recognition](../../../../../section-rooms/arguments/A21-Individuation-Recognition.md) through the difference between returning unchanged and returning able to receive what could previously only be projected or excluded. At the end the distinction between ego and Self is still needed, and the return into embodied judgment keeps a new universal name from becoming an enlarged persona. Individuation and recognition move as **x→X→x** and **IS→AM**, which are authorial QL operations, and this whole supplies a named psychological-historical refraction of them after their derivation. Separately, the provenance of the phrase "becoming indivisible" is unsettled, since a related passage in *Aion* does not resolve it.

[Neumann's whole](../../../archetypal-ground/neumann-images/WHOLE.md) **compares** the shared archetypal ground: the round, differentiation from parental powers, heroic achievement and its transformation into a relation capable of receiving the transpersonal. Its [source house](../../../../episteme/sources/psychology/neumann/neumann-1954-origins-history-consciousness/neumann-1954-origins-history-consciousness.md) **sources** that morphological account, and Taylor's sibling NOTES disclose his mappings and his encounter with Jung's diamonds, while the copied excerpts remain uncollated leads. *Aion* develops a distinct world from this shared ground: its particular historical image is Christian, its two-fish time is aeonic, its alchemical practice acquires a scientific aftermath, and its modern narrator explicitly reconstructs the shadow missing from the earlier configuration. Neumann's developmental morphology supplies no evidence for those geographical or historical details.

Two kinds of succession are in play here. **Generative succession** asks what further life or capacity a work makes possible, and **historical succession** asks how an achieved image becomes an inherited condition and later meets its contrary or its transformation. Alchemy's forgotten motherhood gives Jung's telling a concrete articulation of the second. [Gebser's source](../../../../episteme/sources/phenomenology-continental-philosophy/gebser/gebser-1985-ever-present-origin/gebser-1985-ever-present-origin.md) **qualifies** the comparison through a distinct relation, that of non-chronological Origin becoming transparent through historical consciousness. Jung's aeonic history and Gebser's Origin are separate wholes, their conjunction is a project comparison, and its exact new symbolic appointment is unratified.

The full-argument quilt's subsection 6, "Aion, Job, and the Assumption — register decision," gives the theological refraction: it places Christ and Antichrist in the register of the **occupied slash**, after the native derivation. The Epi Paper adds the pressure of a tool that mirrors, distorts, amplifies or mediates the spiritual and political constitution of the culture that makes it, and its textual counterpart is representation appropriating the authority of living meaning. A representation becomes protected when it blocks the return through which the life it represents could correct its account. The exact assignments below stay unratified. By the quilt's placement boundary, Antichrist naming, Aquarian eschatology and a final showdown belong to the deep vault, tangential or plate register, and the public manuscript does not rest its historical proof on them. A proposed date for an Aquarian transition needs its own historical and astronomical argument, and the Job–Assumption–Mother movement keeps its separate whole. A technoscientific return concerns a represented culture becoming available to judgment through the agent that interprets it and the means that represent it. In a local inquiry that agent is the functional knower, the representation is known content, and its production and comparison are the means. Within the person's Life the whole apparatus belongs among the means, and the person's lived judgment keeps its own office. That return reaches the warranted consequence: a corrected interpretation, a changed world-model or permission, or a justified continuation under the existing criterion.
