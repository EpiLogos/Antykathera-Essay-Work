# Pauli's egg dream — spontaneous division, conscious participation and the circle

This record tells the dream whole and then lays three layers beside it: the mythemic (a man passing from watching to taking part), the mathemic (a quotient of conjugate expressions, and the signed polarity the essay reads in it) and the epistemic (Pauli's own commentary, the Jung–Pauli correspondence and the essay's records). Each layer amplifies the others and keeps its own office. We paraphrase Pauli, the notation is the essay's, and the new QL appointments carry the status Offered.

## #0 — A physicist meets his concepts in a dream

In the middle of March, within four days of each other, Wolfgang Pauli had two dreams, when his work on Kepler had largely come to an end. The second came as seven pictures in a row: a woman arrives with a bird; an egg is laid and divides; the dreamer finds another egg already in his own hand and takes part in its division; the four eggs become mathematical expressions; and his spoken recognition is followed by a circle. All of it depends on who acts at each stage and on the changes between image, operation, formula and speech.

Its source is Pauli's own account in "Modern Examples of 'Background Physics,'" Appendix 3, section 4, of *Atom and Archetype*, printed pp. 192–196. Its [Jung–Pauli source house](../../../../episteme/sources/psychology/jung/jung-pauli-meier-2001-atom-archetype/jung-pauli-meier-2001-atom-archetype.md) **sources** the work identity and the admitted consulted-carrier paraphrases `jung-pauli-meier-2001-atom-archetype-p001` (telling) and `p002` (commentary), and the local Princeton-imprint translation by David Roscoe supplies the primary text. Collating the house's selected Routledge 2001 carrier is a separate task.

Markus Fierz's accompanying note, Appendix 2, pp. 176–178, describes an undated typescript found among the correspondence and calls it an outline and no finished publication. Pauli introduces "background physics" as physical concepts that appear spontaneously in dreams in a qualitative, symbolic sense, a usage he first finds offensive to his scientific habits. His inquiry then concerns both the psychic activity of such images and the archetypal background of scientific concepts, and he distinguishes it from a formulated body of scientific truths. So the egg enters through a physicist receiving his own disciplinary language in a mode he did not consciously choose. (Appendix 3, pp. 179–180.)

Pauli places the two related dreams within an interval of four days in the middle of March, and the appendix gives no calendar year there. His later **60P, Zürich, 31 March 1953, p.107 and footnote 9**, dates the first dream to 1948 and the background-physics essay to June 1948, followed by the quaternity dream. That [dating passage](../../../../episteme/sources/psychology/jung/jung-pauli-meier-2001-atom-archetype/jung-pauli-meier-2001-atom-archetype.md#jung-pauli-meier-2001-atom-archetype-p006) **sources** that witness, so mid-March 1948 for the pair follows by cross-reference and no exact day is supplied. Fierz's description of the surviving typescript as undated is distinct from this retrospective dating. Pauli's equinox association is his own retrospective account of a time of relative psychic instability and creativity. This egg dream names no city, room or landscape, and its space is enacted by arrival, approach and the discovery of something already in the dreamer's hand. **Göttingen belongs to the companion dream**, in which his first physics teacher announces a fundamental change in the splitting of the hydrogen atom's ground state, speaks of brass tones engraved on a metal plate, and the dreamer then goes to Göttingen. Pauli's later interpretation connects that destination with mathematics as well as physics. In his account the two dreams belong together, and their scenes stay distinct. (Pp. 192, 194.)

This whole is produced in Pauli's physicist-led inquiry within the Jung–Pauli correspondence, later preserved in an edited collection. Its narrated time is the seven-picture succession, its biographical time is the reported mid-March pair, and its later publication and project reception have their own histories. A World Clock is another image and supplies no missing scene here.

## #1 — The first division happens before the dreamer intervenes

**Picture 1.** A woman comes with a bird, and the bird lays a large egg. The account names neither the woman nor the bird's species, so their action brings the egg into the dream without identifying the woman as a particular goddess or mother or the bird as a named mythological creature. A first diagram shows the single egg, whose enclosing outline is still the form of one thing awaiting what will happen to it.

**Picture 2.** The egg divides itself into two. Pauli's grammar matters here, since the division occurs without his reported intervention. A second diagram places two complete egg outlines side by side, so the dream has made a plurality from its first egg before it shows the dreamer's own agency. Neither is a good egg or an evil egg, and no conflict occurs between them. What changes is the field of the image: one has become two while each is still presented as an egg. (Pp. 192–193.)

The woman and the bird do nothing further in the telling. Their contribution stays the arrival and laying through which the scene began, and the later formula gives them no historical identity. Pauli preserves a sequence of events whose significance can be explored without filling its unnamed figures with borrowed narrative.

## #2 — The dreamer finds what is in his own hand

**Picture 3.** Pauli goes closer and notices that he has another egg in his hand, this one with a blue shell. His approach changes what he recognises in the situation: alongside the two that divided spontaneously there is now a third, already held by the observer. Nothing in the account says that he manufactures it or takes it from the first pair. Its diagram keeps the two above and adds the third beneath them, and the additional egg changes both the number of visible terms and the dreamer's position among them.

**Picture 4.** He divides this last egg into two, and miraculously they remain whole, so he now has two blue-shelled eggs. His action differs from the first egg's self-division, because the scene has passed from watching a division to performing one on what he holds. The fourth diagram shows two pairs, and the source records preservation through the division as the marvel: the result is two eggs, with no broken portions needing an invented repair. It gives no hatching, no birth of a creature, no discarded shell or yolk. (P. 193.)

The two pairs have related but distinct histories. One results from a spontaneous change Pauli witnessed, and the other follows his recognition and action. Wholeness survived both passages into plurality. This is the event on which his later commentary concentrates: conscious intervention enters a situation whose first terms were already given. Its force would disappear if the four eggs were introduced at once as an emblem with four fixed meanings.

## #3 — Four eggs become a relation, and the relation becomes a circle

<a id="pauli-egg-seven-pictures"></a>
**Picture 5.** The four eggs change into four mathematical expressions. Pauli's printed arrangement has two rows, each with a cosine term on the left and a sine term on the right:

$$
\begin{matrix}
\cos(\delta/2) & \sin(\delta/2)\\
\cos(\delta/2) & \sin(\delta/2)
\end{matrix}
$$

The duplicated terms keep the paired arrangement while the medium of the scene changes. Pauli reports this transformation directly and does not assign each shell colour to a particular trigonometric function, so no such assignment is added here.

**Picture 6.** The terms give the quotient

$$
\frac{\cos(\delta/2)+i\sin(\delta/2)}
     {\cos(\delta/2)-i\sin(\delta/2)}.
$$

The two rows now form a relation of conjugate expressions: the cosine stays the same while the imaginary sine term changes sign. For real $\delta$ the denominator has modulus one, and the quotient is an ordinary, well-defined complex-number operation. Now the dream's organisation has changed from four displayed terms into a single relation that keeps their opposing signs.

**Picture 7.** Pauli speaks for the first time in this dream. He identifies the expression with $e^{i\delta}$ and names the circle, the formula vanishes, and a circle appears. This replacement completes the reported sequence. Speech enters at the end, after the silent pictures have changed the observer's participation and the form of what is seen. (P. 193; diagrams visually checked in the consulted PDF.)

Pauli's later explanation makes the mathematical step explicit:

$$
\frac{\cos(\delta/2)+i\sin(\delta/2)}
     {\cos(\delta/2)-i\sin(\delta/2)}
=\frac{e^{i\delta/2}}{e^{-i\delta/2}}
=e^{i\delta}.
$$

For a fixed real angle this is a point on the unit circle, and as the angle varies it describes the circle. That distinction keeps the mathematical operation exact while keeping what Pauli recognises in the dream. The final image is circular, with no return to the initial egg outline, and the account reports no return of the woman and bird and no restart of the first picture. Its completion has the history of differentiation and conscious action within it, and no unreported eighth scene is needed.

## #4 — Pauli reads the part played by consciousness

Pauli's commentary joins the two dreams through the intervention absent from the first pair of eggs. He reads that spontaneous pair as a ground state and identifies his participation in Pictures 3 and 4 as the change that produces the quaternity through a reflection of duality. In his account the second dream goes beyond the first, because it makes the intervention pictorially explicit and carries the result into mathematical wholeness. He relates the sequence to the axiom attributed to Maria Prophetissa, mediated here through Jung's *Psychology and Alchemy*, and that comparison is Pauli's and no independently verified ancient attribution. (P. 195.)

He also gives the imaginary unit a symbolic office: introducing it lets the paired opposites combine into the form he reads as wholeness. For him mathematics already takes part in symbolic description, and the question is what such description can embrace. He declines to offer a finished interpretation of the two dreams and writes that further progress in the sciences would be required. That refusal leaves his actual interpretation of conscious intervention, quaternity and mathematical unity fully available, and it limits only the claim that the dreams' whole meaning has been exhausted.

The surrounding essay gives that boundary practical content. Pauli distinguishes a physical process from its psychic symbolic use: a faster process is not thereby more conscious, and a heavier atom has no more consciousness than a lighter one. At the end he separates the limits of physics within life from an alleged defect of quantum theory within physics. Feeling, judgment and the individual case have an office that reproducible quantitative description does not by itself perform, and mathematics also has qualitative resources, including topology, whose symbolic possibilities he considers unfinished. Mathematically the dream's formula is exact, while the scope of its psychological meaning stays an inquiry. (Pp. 190, 195–196.)

[Neumann's round–differentiation–transformation movement](../../../archetypal-ground/neumann-images/WHOLE.md) **compares** how a containing form gives rise to differentiated terms and to a centre capable of participation. His [account of emerging consciousness](../../../../episteme/sources/psychology/neumann/neumann-1954-origins-history-consciousness/neumann-1954-origins-history-consciousness.md) has its own mythological figures and sequence, and Taylor's paper, mark, slash and erasure make differentiation and return available through a further material image. In Pauli's telling the centre enters by noticing and dividing what it already holds. Two spontaneous eggs and two consciously divided blue eggs keep their different histories through the mathematical end, and their particular actions give participation an exact scene, which no assignment of eggs to Neumannian stages could do.

## #5→0 — Conscious participation changes the received image

<a id="pauli-egg-native-refraction"></a>
The dream's difference between reception and participation can now be followed through the whole sequence. Pauli first receives an event placed before him, approach makes what he already holds visible, his intervention changes the field, and mathematical recognition makes the achieved relation articulable. Taylor's [harmonised account](../../../../../quilt/2026-08-02-PARALLEL-HARMONISED-QUILT.md) **extends** that movement after the native theorem, and so does his [psychoid comparison](../../../../../quilt/2026-08-03-PLAIN-ENGLISH-FULL-FLOW.md). Image, noticing, action and recognition stay consequential at each step.

[Taylor's core theorem](../../../../episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy.md) unfolds a determining capacity through its particular, its personed context and its returning horizon. In `X/x` the instance makes the capacity legible and leaves further determination possible. Pauli's egg gives this relation a psychic scene: the received image changes through a participation it first made possible. Seven pictures keep their seven actions, the four eggs keep their two origins, and these narrative differences and the eight native determinations have different cardinalities and generating operations.

Laid beside the matheme, the quotient of Picture 6 is a stroke at work. Dia-ballein holds opposed signs in `(−1)/(+1)`, each having its sense through the other, and the quotient of conjugates holds a term and its opposite-signed twin in one ratio and yields a point on a circle, where cancelling them as a sum would leave no relation to be a circle of. That reading (Offered) lays the mythemic passage from watching to taking part beside the mathemic stroke and the epistemic record of Pauli's commentary, and it adds to Pauli's own comparison a notation he did not use. A further Offered reading, taken from the essay's §4 working draft, counts the four eggs as the explicit terms, two that divided before the dreamer and two that divided through him, held between an implicate source in the woman's arrival and an implicate return in the circle, which is the shape written `4+2`. Seven pictures stay seven and are not counted as six positions.

<a id="pauli-egg-conscious-participation-return"></a>
Participation makes the observer consequential within the scene he first receives, and [A19's local arbitration regime](../../../../../section-rooms/arguments/A19-Complex-as-Local-Arbitration-Regime.md) **qualifies** what that participation can claim: his own noticing and action become part of the history of the resulting whole. That history belongs to this reported dream. Pauli names no particular complex as cured, and the woman and bird keep their initiating actions and gain no newly assigned pathological office. Received form and participating centre stay available together for further interpretation.

The [differential field](../../../../../section-rooms/arguments/A16-Arche-Topos-as-Differential-Field.md) **grounds** the distinction of a retained modulus from a changing angle. For real $\delta$ Pauli's conjugate quotient has unit modulus and phase $\delta$, a point on the circle whose variation describes the circular locus. A toroidal circulation or a quaternionic rotation has further specified structure. Exact phase calculation supplies its own formal relation, and the dream's psychological meaning stays responsive to the changing interpreter.

By the ending the dreamer stands in a different relation to the first appearance. He has received, approached, noticed, divided and finally spoken. What arrived without his choice has become an operation in which he takes part, and the formula that resulted gives way to an image he can name. His next encounter can begin from this altered capacity to recognise and act. In all, the movement keeps both the spontaneity of the first division and the efficacy of the second: conscious participation changes the received field without claiming to have produced everything within it.
