# 1. Chapter 1 — language is entry into a world, not a neutral costume

Fanon's opening treatment of **language** establishes the social depth of the mask problem. For the colonised Antillean, mastery of metropolitan French is not merely acquisition of another instrument. Language carries a civilisation, prestige hierarchy, education, authority and an implied position relative to colonial culture. Speech can therefore change the social world through which the speaker is heard and can also reorganise how the speaker measures himself.

The source-side relation is:

```text
colonial world already distributes prestige / humanity / authority
        ↓
metropolitan language bears that world socially
        ↓
colonised speaker acquires / performs the prestige language
        ↓
access and recognition can increase
        ↓
the speaker is also invited to evaluate self and others through the coloniser's measure
```

The important distinction for Native 020 is already present: **adaptation can be real capability and real alienation at the same time.** The problem cannot be solved by declaring every acquired mask false, because the linguistic capacity genuinely opens domains of action. Nor can the social meaning of the adaptation be ignored, because the field has already attached racial and civilisational value to the mode of speech.

# 2. The lived racial scene — the body is returned through an imposed historical image

In the book's central phenomenological treatment of Black embodiment, Fanon describes a public encounter in which he is racially hailed and objectified through the white gaze. The scene matters as a **whole event**:

```text
Fanon moves through an ordinary public world
        ↓
a white social field identifies him racially
        ↓
existing stories / fears / stereotypes arrive with the identification
        ↓
his lived body is no longer available only through his own practical bodily orientation
        ↓
he is made to encounter himself as the object of an already prepared racial image
```

Fanon uses the scene to revise the phenomenological body-schema problem. His bodily self-relation is not formed only by immediate sensorimotor life. A **historico-racial schema** has been socially prepared from accumulated stories, anecdotes, classifications and colonial history; under the racialising gaze this becomes an **epidermal racial schema**, attaching the inherited field to visible skin and reorganising lived possibility.

The source whole therefore shows exactly why the category can seem to “know” the person before encounter. The category is backed by a historical world which has already decided what the visible body signifies.

This is not merely an error inside another individual's private mind. It is an **objective-internal world** in the ordinary project sense: language, cultural images, institutions, remembered histories, expectations and affects have already been organised before the encounter and enter it as conditions.

That last sentence is a project comparison, not Fanon's terminology.

# 3. Mask — recognition purchased through an alien measure

The book's title gives the mask image its full relation. “White mask” cannot be reduced to conscious deception or theatrical disguise. Across Fanon's analysis, colonial recognition can require the Black subject to approach language, culture, desire and self-image through standards whose authority comes from a white colonial world.

The mask therefore belongs to a field of **recognition and self-administration**:

```text
social field establishes a racial hierarchy of recognisable humanity
        ↓
access / prestige / safety / intelligibility are distributed through that hierarchy
        ↓
colonised subject can learn forms recognised by the dominant world
        ↓
those forms become real capabilities and interfaces
        ↓
the dominant measure can also become internal criterion
        ↓
self and others are judged through a world that first judged them
```

This is stronger and more historically exact than a generic “persona hides the true self” reading. The mask can be an adaptation to a world whose recognition is materially consequential. Its cost is not falsity alone; it is the possibility that the **condition of social appearance has already been racialised by another's measure**.

# 4. Recognition — the problem is relational, not private self-esteem

Later in the book Fanon makes recognition explicit. Colonial racialisation creates a relation in which the Black subject is forced into comparison and into proving value against a hierarchy whose standard is already white. The resulting struggle cannot be cured simply by a stronger private image of the self if the social relation which distributes recognition remains intact.

The whole-source field can therefore be written:

```text
colonial history / racial classification
        ↓
prestige language · images · institutions · white gaze
        ↓
recognition field is pre-weighted
        ↓
Black body / speech appears through inherited racial schema
        ↓
adaptation / mask can open access
        +
mask can internalise the judging measure
        ↓
freedom requires more than changing the local self-image
        ↓
the relation of recognition itself must become revisable
```

This is the source-bearing depth Native 020 needed before using “mask/chameleon” in the wider image/idol argument.
