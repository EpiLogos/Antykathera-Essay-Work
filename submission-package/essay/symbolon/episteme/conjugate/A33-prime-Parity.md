# A33′ — Parity

## #0

A distinction used to justify a technical feature claims a difference in what the feature does. [Operational Parity](../arguments/A33-Epistemic-Cultivation-Operational-Parity.md) requires that difference to have an identifiable locus and a comparison capable of discriminating it. A philosophical argument can remain exact before a technical mapping exists. The proposed feature becomes answerable when its design states the consequence it claims.

The operative centre is the **Gate**: the execution checkpoint where a candidate's experience is adjudicated before adoption. Parity applies that gate to claims themselves. A claimed technological consequence is tested through an operation which actually exercises the distinction, with and without its effective return route. The comparison has to account for other routes and conditions capable of producing the same result.

The Gate does not prove the philosophy by passing an implementation. It asks the narrower and harder question: did the implementation actually enact the distinction it invoked, and can the consequence be discriminated from a version in which the distinction was absent?

## #1

The venue's design questions are parity questions in exactly this sense. Q10 asks what interfaces, "against a constrained action-space, instead cultivate the abstraction and tolerance for ambiguity that agent orchestration demands" (PDF p. 57; verified local transcription, uncarded). Q11 asks for design primitives for agent-to-agent interfaces that "presume both cooperation and adversariality as norms" (PDF p. 57; uncarded). Both demand the operationalization, not the mood: an interface that claims to enact tolerance for ambiguity must enact it in the action-space — what becomes selectable, deferrable, revisable — not in the copy's register. And the [harness-centric passage](../sources/media-technology-philosophy/bratton/bratton-2026-agentworld-brief/SOURCE.md#bratton-2026-agentworld-brief-q038) fixes the locus: "capability in deployment is not a property of weights alone. It is a property of the harness — the scaffolding of tools, memory, shared knowledge bases, agent protocols, and institutional interfaces through which a model acts on the world." The claimed distinction must be located in the actual acting ensemble: model, tool, memory, permission, interface or a consequential relation among its participants. The research questions propose design work. They do not report the results of the comparisons required here.

## #2

A claimed distinction first identifies its operation: retrieval, retention, routing, permission, evaluator, collaboration or return. Consider a source correction. The relevant test follows whether the corrected passage changes the inherited conclusion and which later action depends on that conclusion. Merely displaying the passage or adding a source field would test availability, a different operation.

An authority claim has a different locus. A derivative judgment can be tested for whether it overwrites an originating instruction, and a challenge can be tested for whether it reaches the authority able to revise the instruction. A challenge need not possess unrestricted write-access to the criterion. The difference lies in the viable, attributable route through its actual receiving authority. [Covenant architecture](A25-prime-Covenant-Architecture.md) retains that source/office relation.

[Deference as alignment](A31-prime-Deference-as-Co-Evolutionary-Alignment.md) distinguishes the five possible depths: answer, task, world-model, evaluator and commission. A comparison tests the site the encounter actually warrants. A changed answer under an adequate evaluator can be a successful return; criterion change becomes the relevant outcome where the claimed function is correction of a failed criterion. [Shared worlds](A30-prime-Shared-Worlds-Unmerged.md) and [the moving mirror](A32-prime-The-Mirror-Is-a-Film.md) supply further distinct questions about attributable mutual change and an inspectable output-to-input edge.

## #3

**The negative result counts.** A proposed technical relation has to identify an operation and a comparison capable of exercising its difference. The [[symbolon/episteme/conjugate/A33-prime-Parity.md#3|Removal Edge]] matters most: delete the disclosure, sever the challenge route, freeze the evaluator, merge the worlds, close the audit — and inspect the operation in a case that engages the removed route. An unchanged result in a case where the route was never used does not decide the claim. This protects against decoration in both directions: against philosophy that never touches the machine, and against machine features that borrow philosophy's prestige without its operation. The conceptual inquiry receives the test result: a failed test is one of three findings — the implementation is wrong, the operational mapping is inadequate, or the test cannot discriminate the distinction — and each returns the question to a different home. [C46 — Epistemic Cultivation](../concepts/C46-Epistemic-Cultivation.md) **defines** the continuing practice: preserve the path of change, so a distinction that passed at one scope is re-tested at another rather than banked.

## #4

The comparison retains the scope of the proposed distinction. A philosophical relation can be valid while its technical mapping remains undeveloped. A successful implementation can enact its specified operation while leaving the wider metaphysical argument to its own derivation. The research questions retain their role as proposals until an actual execution supplies a result.

An unimplemented consequence can be specified precisely: what should change, under which condition, through whose authority, and what competing explanation the comparison must discriminate. [Operational Parity](../concepts/C45-Operational-Parity.md) carries this test; [Epistemic Cultivation](../concepts/C46-Epistemic-Cultivation.md) carries its continuing correction. Naming the locus makes a proposal reviewable. Passing the test requires the discriminating comparison itself.

## #5→0

A distinction survives as a changed operation or returns to its concept for revision. The Gate closes the circuit by forcing the philosophical claim and the implemented operation to remain mutually answerable.

This applies reflexively to the essay's own technical proposals. If a proposed distinction produces no discriminable consequence, the mapping needs revision. If the consequence appears somewhere other than predicted, the concept may need sharpening. Parity is valuable precisely because the inquiry can change its own instruments without promoting the latest instrument into the ground of the inquiry.

The return through [Epistemic Cultivation / Operational Parity](../arguments/A33-Epistemic-Cultivation-Operational-Parity.md) and [the shared ethical field](AC.md) keeps philosophical argument, technical proposal and achieved operation distinguishable. Each claimed technical consequence returns through its actual locus, the comparison which exercised it and the later act which inherited its change.
