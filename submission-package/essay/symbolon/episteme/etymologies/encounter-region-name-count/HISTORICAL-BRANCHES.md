# Encounter and Account — Historical Branches and Relational Returns

In the [six-term encounter relation](WHOLE-FIELD.md), a meeting becomes situated, its participant discriminable, its name repeatable and its account answerable to another encounter. ## Four con-histories

The apparent English cluster crosses separate histories. Merriam-Webster traces [encounter](../../sources/language-literary-studies/merriam-webster/merriam-webster-online-dictionary/SOURCE.md#merriam-webster-online-dictionary-q001) through Anglo-French *encuntrer*, Medieval Latin *incontrare* and Late Latin *incontra*, with *in-* and *contra*. Its senses retain adversarial meeting and coming upon someone or something. The same dictionary traces [country](../../sources/language-literary-studies/merriam-webster/merriam-webster-online-dictionary/SOURCE.md#merriam-webster-online-dictionary-q002) through Anglo-French forms and Medieval Latin *contrata* to *contra*. Facing and coming against are actions within these reported descents; they do not derive numerical count.

The reckoning line runs through Anglo-French *cunter/counter* to Latin *computare*. Merriam-Webster separately derives the title [count](../../sources/language-literary-studies/merriam-webster/merriam-webster-online-dictionary/SOURCE.md#merriam-webster-online-dictionary-q003) through *comes/comitis*, companion and member of the imperial court. Their English convergence lets counting and entrusted office meet in Taylor's comparison while the homographs retain different descents. The companion-to-office-to-county institutional sequence has no dated textual collation here; this dictionary's etymology is not a full history of territorial government.

The bearing line reaches [countenance](../../sources/language-literary-studies/merriam-webster/merriam-webster-online-dictionary/SOURCE.md#merriam-webster-online-dictionary-q004) through Anglo-French and Medieval Latin *continentia*, from the Latin restraint/holding-together family of *continere*. Facial expression, bearing, composure and approval or support give different ways a presence can become available to another. The attested semantic field permits this comparison without making a face a number.

Facing against, reckoning together, holding oneself and going with another each articulates a different with-relation. Taylor's [Count-through-Countenance](WHOLE-FIELD.md#count-through-countenance) lets a person enter a count as distinct while remaining able to answer its terms. The English sounding recurrence makes this movement audible across the words. It does not require every visible *con-* to carry an identical ancient essence, or every act of holding to descend from one root.

## Six ways of counting

Six branches differentiate how a field becomes countable. Their number belongs to this authorial comparison, rather than proving a universal classification of languages. The particular operations remain intelligible while their historical warrants differ.

1. **Fitting and order.** Arrangement makes units comparable within a pattern; rhythm and proportion make recurrence intelligible. The proposed *number* genealogy has not been established through each language's forms and senses here. The operation concerns recurrence and ratio: a result is exact through the relation in which it fits, without every sense of fitting becoming one historical root.
2. **Straightening and reckoning.** A line or rule permits repeated comparison. The *regere/reckon/rechnen* grouping remains historically unverified here; similar offices do not establish cognacy. The philosophical relation exposes the selected criterion together with what it orders, so the rule can be retained, corrected or revised through an answerable comparison.
3. **Pruning and computing.** The *computare* ancestry of numerical count is independently supported above. The stronger *putare* pruning/clearing/reckoning history lacks a collated Latin entry and attested uses here. Operationally, selecting material makes a reckoning possible while leaving a consequential remainder. The retained exclusion concerns what materially constituted the result.
4. **Telling and succession.** *Tell/tale* and *Zahl/zählen/erzählen* remain distinct uncompleted lexical comparisons. Narration makes a succession available, while a numerical total can discard its order. An actual trajectory matters wherever the sequence changed the next field of possibilities; tally and history then answer different questions.
5. **Gathering and saying.** In his [Logos essay](../../sources/phenomenology-continental-philosophy/heidegger/heidegger-1975-early-greek-thinking/SOURCE.md), Heidegger interprets *legein* as gathering and laying. This is his philosophical articulation, with contested Heraclitus philology; it is not a settled Greek lexical descent. Taylor's returning account keeps what is gathered distinct, so gathering can compose without arresting its terms.
6. **Declaring together.** Monier-Williams distinguishes [enumeration, deliberation and appellation](../../sources/classical-philology/monier-williams/monier-williams-1899-sanskrit-english-dictionary/SOURCE.md#monier-williams-1899-sanskrit-english-dictionary-p001) within the saṃ-√khyā/saṃkhyā group. Its separate [sāṃkhya entry](../../sources/classical-philology/monier-williams/monier-williams-1899-sanskrit-english-dictionary/SOURCE.md#monier-williams-1899-sanskrit-english-dictionary-p002) offers discrimination or enumeration of twenty-five principles as alternative explanations of the philosophical name, with its own qualified preference for the latter. The [Kārikā’s classification](../../sources/indian-philosophy/isvarakrishna/isvarakrishna-ruzsa-1998-samkhyakarika/SOURCE.md#isvarakrishna-ruzsa-1998-samkhyakarika-p001) counts root prakṛti, seven principles both productive and produced, sixteen products and puruṣa as neither: 1+7+16+1 preserves different causal offices rather than flattening them into equivalent objects. Its [knowledge means](../../sources/indian-philosophy/isvarakrishna/isvarakrishna-ruzsa-1998-samkhyakarika/SOURCE.md#isvarakrishna-ruzsa-1998-samkhyakarika-p002) make perception, inference and trustworthy testimony answer how those principles are known. These selected online witnesses establish the respective lexical and doctrinal operations; print collation, all cited lexical contexts and the school’s institutional origins remain separate historical warrants. Taylor’s comparison makes a relation explicit enough to be recounted and revised, without making QL descend from Sāṃkhya or assigning reciprocal revision to puruṣa.
