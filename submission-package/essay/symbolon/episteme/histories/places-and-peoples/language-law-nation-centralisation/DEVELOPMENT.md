# Language, Law, Nation and Centralisation — Twelve Situated Histories

<a id="historical-trunk"></a>
## #0 — A common order is made through different histories

Territory, language, law and people acquire their relations through acts that take time. Conquest changes jurisdiction; a court prescribes a documentary language; a school makes competence in that language ordinary; a later generation receives the achieved arrangement as its native world. The [twelve situated histories](HISTORY.md) make these different acts and bearers concrete. Incorporation, transmission, educational formation and renewed judgment meet through six relations, while the twelve branches keep their own chronologies.

The complete movement **Fides→Topos→Logos→Nomos→Natio→Credere→Fides** joins reliance becoming situated, articulated and administered, an arrangement inherited as belonging, and examination which makes [renewed entrustment](../../../etymologies/trust-place-logos-nomos-natio-credere/WHOLE-FIELD.md#relational-offices) possible. Its inner **Topos→Logos→Nomos→Natio** names the recurrent attempt to align place, language, law and people. The [Greek lexical witnesses](../../../sources/classical-philology/liddell-scott-jones/lsj-1940-greek-english-lexicon/SOURCE.md) and [Latin lexical witnesses](../../../sources/classical-philology/lewis-short/lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary/SOURCE.md) contribute their distinct attested meanings; their different word histories do not establish one chronology of civilisation.

Centralisation makes arbitration consequential at a wider scale. More persons must have their acts recognised through a common jurisdiction, and that jurisdiction needs sufficiently shared forms of naming and recording. Yet a common form does not make every speaker equally able to use it. The person who translates, the official who admits a document, the institution that teaches the accepted language and the authority that can revise its rules occupy different positions in the same process. Their differences are where the history becomes more exact than a general story of increasing unity.

Continuity-in-Indeterminacy lets a judgment belong to a history whose origin its measure cannot exhaust. Criterion-through-Distinction makes a comparison possible within a limit; Delineation-through-Difference gives that comparison a perspective and frame. A consequential decision can then settle a crisis or usurp the authority under which it decides. [Arbitration-in-Crisis](../../../etymologies/arbitration-hybris-regard-anamnesis/WHOLE-FIELD.md#arbitration-in-crisis) is the deciding relation; hybris is its closure when a local determination assumes the office of its determining condition. Con-text-through-Diaphaneity makes the affected field available to Regard, and Resolution-in-Reconciliation carries settlement through its history into a later judgment. [Apportionment](../../../etymologies/apportionment-and-economy/WHOLE-FIELD.md#canonical-operations) distributes finite capacity, criteria, delegated labour, local governing powers and the wider economy sustaining an act. A national language consequently acquires authority through capacities, costs and exclusions which can themselves become answerable.

The historical and archetypal accounts meet without sharing evidential authority. A marriage transmits a particular claim; a motherland image can give belonging an affective body; a law creates an administrative distinction. The document, image and enacted consequence require different witnesses. Their common field does not make one people's history a stage in another people's development, nor does a shared psychic morphology establish a route of textual transmission. A document can answer a claim about jurisdiction, an image a claim about psychic investment, and affected testimony a claim about lived consequence; each judgment receives the witness appropriate to its act.

<a id="regional-incorporation-and-transmission"></a>
## #1 — A region enters a kingdom while its texts travel elsewhere

<a id="branch-i-occitania"></a>
### I — Occitania: incorporation, legal French and national schooling

The Albigensian Crusade begins in **1209**; the **1229** Treaty of Paris changes the political settlement; Toulouse enters the royal domain in **1271**. Crusade, settlement and territorial incorporation precede the much later alignment of royal jurisdiction with administrative French and national schooling. They do not themselves determine what language a household spoke or how a local court recorded every dispute. Particular treaty clauses and local linguistic effects have their own documentary witnesses, distinct from these dates of political incorporation.

The **1539** ordinance gives one later intervention an exact textual body. In articles **110–111**, François I requires clarity in judgments and prescribes French for specified judicial acts, procedures, registers and instruments, addressing difficulties associated with Latin wording. The [judicial articles](../../../sources/political-theory-institutions/francois-i/francois-i-1539-villers-cotterets/SOURCE.md#francois-i-1539-villers-cotterets-q001) establish that prescription. It does not show that everyone immediately spoke French or that all regional languages were abolished by those articles. The operative change concerns the language in which an act can travel through the jurisdiction and reach its parties with recognised force.

In **1794**, the scale and justification change. Grégoire's intervention before the Convention joins accurate public acts and access to office to a programme of national linguistic uniformity. His proposed answer to exclusion through insufficient French is also an answer that would eliminate regional idioms. In the [selected intervention](../../../sources/political-theory-institutions/gregoire/gregoire-1794-unite-de-langue/SOURCE.md#gregoire-1794-unite-de-langue-q001), Grégoire answers southern attachment and literary traditions through this same combination of public access and proposed elimination. Its omissions remain visible: this is neither the complete report nor the annexed decree, and its reported language counts are the speaker's claims.

Nineteenth- and twentieth-century schooling form the next institutional phase. The movement now passes from prescribing a record's form to forming the future speaker: an acquired national standard can become a child's inherited language. The word *patois* belongs inside the process. Characterising a speech-world as deficient can justify policy whose effects then seem to confirm the characterisation. This feedback connects a judgment to the world it helps produce; its actual force requires school instructions, teaching practice and testimony from affected speakers. Access and assimilation remain held together, and an affected speaker's evidence can challenge the deciding language policy.

<a id="branch-ii-languedoc-jewish"></a>
### II — Posquières and Lunel: learning exceeds territorial incorporation

**Abraham ben David of Posquières, RABaD III**, and **Isaac the Blind** belong to the southern French scholarly and early Kabbalistic field. **Lunel**, **Maimonides** and the **ibn Tibbon** translations connect learned communities through a different relation. Regional proximity makes encounters possible without making philosophical translation and Kabbalistic teaching one transmission chain, or either history a derivative of incorporation into France.

A particular letter now supplies a recoverable contact. The Princeton Geniza Project identifies **Bodleian MS heb. b 11/33** as a letter from **Yonatan ha-Kohen of Lunel to Maimonides**, dated by inference within **1168–1204**. Its description and displayed Stern translation name **Samuel ibn Tibbon**, his father's instruction in Arabic, the community's reception of translated works and its request for the remaining *Guide*. The transcription is excerpted, and the later date on the manuscript is not the date of composition. This is a bounded witness to requesting, translating and circulating learning across **Provence and Egypt**. It establishes no itinerary from Isaac the Blind to Pico. [Letter, description and translation](https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/19297/).

The act of translation changes access without making the translated work originate in the receiving language. A community can address a distant authority, depend upon a particular translator and incorporate the resulting text into its own learned life. Political geography and intellectual geography therefore remain partially non-coincident. Their intersection becomes historically consequential wherever migration, exclusion or institutional disruption changes who can teach, read or transmit.

Manuscript descent, family movement, teaching and patronage can carry learning toward **Catalonia/Spain, Provence and Italy**, and onward into **Christian Kabbalah**. Each connection needs its own carrier. Occultation likewise has several possible historical bodies: destruction of a school, displacement of a community, loss of a language, restricted teaching, or later refusal to recognise a practice as knowledge. The recovered letter makes one positive relation available while those other mechanisms remain distinguishable. An articulated teaching crosses jurisdictions through a translator's entrusted labour, and a community's access depends upon that work. The letter does not complete the further transmission routes.

<a id="renaissance-and-historical-reading"></a>
## #2 — Transmission changes what can count as knowledge

<a id="branch-iii-renaissance"></a>
### III — Florence, Venice and Milan: patronage, dynastic claim and mathesis

The Renaissance field connects different kinds of movement. In **Florence**, **Cosimo de' Medici's patronage**, **Ficino's Hermetic translation before his return to Plato**, and the learned world of **Pico** give acquisition and commission concrete consequences: they determine which texts can be read and in which language. Patronage distributes time, access and authority. Philosophical availability changes through those decisions, while the exact commission and downstream textual network still require their own edition-specific documents.

The **Visconti–Orléans** relation moves another kind of claim. **Valentina Visconti's marriage to Louis I of Orléans** carries a Milanese dynastic claim into the French royal lineage; **Louis XII** later makes that inherited claim politically consequential in the Italian Wars. Marriage is part of the infrastructure of succession. Its office differs from translating a manuscript, even when people, household objects, heraldry and learned practices travel through overlapping courts. This particular dynastic conduit gives a means of transmission; it does not explain every visual resemblance encountered along a possible route.

The **Minervois Biscione** remains an unidentified local serpent-child object with a proposed heraldic relation. Its exact site, date, patronage and renovation history would decide whether the image belongs to a Visconti transmission path. The prospective visual and mythemic comparison survives while the object and its route remain unestablished.

**Pacioli and Leonardo** give the branch its mathematical hinge. The **1494 Summa**, with its printed accounting exposition, belongs to a practice of double entry that precedes its publication. The proportion and geometrical work associated with **Milan** has another public event in the **1509 Venetian edition of Divina proportione**. The Metropolitan Museum's copy, object **19.50**, is catalogued as a book with woodcuts after Leonardo, published by **Paganinus de Paganinus in Venice on 1 June 1509**. The object record verifies that printed carrier, not a complete reading of its argument. [Museum object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/336656).

The **undivided hinge of mathesis** names the proximity of calculative account and qualitative/proportional geometry within this field. Florence's philosophical patronage, Venice's commercial and printing activity, and Milan's mathematical collaboration remain geographically differentiated. Their later institutional futures need not be the same. **Differential occultation of qualitative mathesis** is a research hypothesis about that divergence; neither the book's existence nor its divine title proves a later programme of suppression. Different uses of account and measure become communicable and authoritative through particular institutions.

<a id="branch-iv-rejected-knowledge"></a>
### IV — Yates and Hanegraaff: the historian reopens an exclusion

**Frances Yates** brought Hermetic and occult materials into the account of early modern science. Re-admitting that material restores lives and institutions in which theology, alchemy, astrology, magic and natural philosophy had not yet acquired their later disciplinary separations. It does not establish the strongest causal claim that one esoteric tradition generated modern science. The exact selected passages and later criticisms need their own collation; their disagreement is preserved rather than resolved by a declaration that everything was connected.

**Wouter Hanegraaff** gives the historiographic turn a different emphasis. In his own account of **rejected knowledge**, traditions become marginal partly through the criteria by which modern academic culture recognises serious inquiry. Reputational dismissal can remove a speaker's capacity to be heard without physically destroying the surviving text. His account makes the historian's own inherited categories part of the inquiry. This is a consulted authorial statement of his research programme, not a claim to have read the whole 2012 monograph. [Hanegraaff, “Rejected Knowledge”](https://www.wouterjhanegraaff.net/rejected-knowledge).

The chronological distinction is between a practice's earlier life, its later classification and a subsequent scholarly recovery. A text can survive materially while ceasing to count intellectually. Conversely, a lost text need not have been deliberately suppressed. Censorship, strategic secrecy, patronage, accidental loss, professionalisation and disciplinary exclusion remain different mechanisms. Regard becomes concrete when previously excluded material can answer the historiographical judgment made upon it: it can correct a case, alter a governing category where warranted, or give reasons to retain a fitting account. The achieved history then turns upon its own selections. Rejection does not make a claim true merely because it was rejected.

<a id="branch-v-aion"></a>
### V — Jung's Aion: the historical image and its limits

Jung's **Aion** reads historical time through **Christ/Antichrist, Pisces, Gnostic and alchemical images of the Self**, and a movement of opposition and reversal within the Christian aeon. He joins **sixteenth-century schism** to an outward or horizontal movement of consciousness, discovery and the conquest of nature, and places alchemy in relation to scientific modernity. These historical-psychological constructions make images and events act within one psychic interpretation. Their symbolic force does not independently establish the causal history of the Reformation or scientific institutions.

In [Aion's sixteenth-century sequence](../../../sources/psychology/jung/jung-1978-aion-cw9-2/SOURCE.md#passages), VII, §§150–154, pp.95–97, the schism and outward turn place European consciousness under altered historical pressures. XII, §§267–271, pp.173–174, follows alchemy, science and the later division of faith from knowledge, with the knowing psyche omitted from the resulting separation. Jung gives these events their relation within the knowing psyche's historical situation.

An image can organise what a historical community fears, expects or recognises in an event. A polarity can give a sequence affective direction; a later account can receive an earlier figure under changed conditions. That reading needs the determinate history through which the image acted. It cannot establish a marriage, a treaty, a translation or an institutional decision by symbolic resemblance. Historical and archetypal Logos meet through these different questions. Shared Neumann structuration does not make European religious time the developmental measure of the other branches. Recollection can resituate an account while the complete symbolic telling retains its own source and historical scope.

<a id="national-language-and-jurisdiction"></a>
## #3 — A language becomes a condition of belonging and action

<a id="branch-vi-italy"></a>
### VI — Italy: literary prestige becomes a public language project

The Italian branch begins with a literary prestige that precedes unified state administration. Tuscan and Florentine writing provide an inherited resource; **unification in 1861** creates a different practical demand for communication across the national field. **Manzoni's 1868 report** belongs at this transition. The commission joins a writer's linguistic position to public educational policy without making literary recognition equivalent to widespread ordinary speech.

The report argues for a **living Florentine** language and a dictionary representing its use. Manzoni answers four objections: that a national dictionary cannot rest upon one city, that Florentine is merely a dialect, that its adoption would impose a law, and that the dictionary must also explain writers of every period. He distinguishes providing a communicative means from a penal code, and a living-language dictionary from a historical one. He then gives regional comparative dictionaries a mediating role: speakers approach the proposed common language through words they already know. The consulted transcription is marked complete but not fully proofread; its argument is paraphrased, not quoted as a collated edition. [Manzoni, complete report](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Dell%27unit%C3%A0_della_lingua_e_dei_mezzi_di_diffonderla).

A selected living usage becomes a reference; lexicographic work makes it teachable; government support and education give it possible reach. The subsequent history of schooling, literacy and ordinary spoken Italian requires evidence beyond Manzoni's proposal. His distinction between a useful means and compulsion remains available when later policy is judged. Literary articulation becomes an administered resource through teachers, dictionaries and educational capacities; whether that resource became usable is a further historical question.

<a id="branch-vii-germany"></a>
### VII — Germany: linguistic commonality and the later racialising cut

Language and culture have three distinct intellectual uses before their later racialised administration. **Herder's** language-cultural relation retains its pluralistic and anti-imperial dimensions. **Fichte's Addresses of 1807–08** intervene under Napoleonic domination. **Jacob Grimm's** philology, grammar, mythology and legal-historical work contribute to a field whose cultural relations exceed fragmented state jurisdictions. Their coexistence does not make them authors of one doctrine or turn later racial law into an inevitable consequence of studying language.

The consulted excerpts from Fichte's seventh and eighth addresses claim German originality and connect national continuation to education. They also distinguish state from nation and describe a person as belonging both to a particular state and to a wider German cultural fatherland. These are claims within Fichte's political-philosophical address, including its exclusionary claim to originality; his own etymological assertion about *deutsch* is not independently adopted here. [GHI edition, addresses VII–VIII, excerpted](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/the-holy-roman-empire-1648-1815/ghdi:document-3596).

The later transition becomes legally inspectable in **1935**. The **Reich Citizenship Law of 15 September** reserves full political rights to a racially defined class of subjects; its **First Regulation of 14 November** specifies classifications through grandparents and religious-community membership, with additional conditions for people assigned two Jewish grandparents. It excludes those classified as Jews from Reich citizenship, voting and public office. The distinction between state subjecthood and Reich citizenship is retained; the text is not described as immediate cancellation of every form of nationality. [GHI Citizenship Law, §§1–3](../../../sources/political-theory-institutions/german-reich/reich-1935-citizenship-law-first-regulation/SOURCE.md#reich-1935-citizenship-law-first-regulation-q001); [First Regulation, §§2–5](../../../sources/political-theory-institutions/german-reich/reich-1935-citizenship-law-first-regulation/SOURCE.md#reich-1935-citizenship-law-first-regulation-q002).

The rule turns a record about ancestors into a present distribution of rights. This is **arbitration hardening into ontology**: an administratively instituted racial category acts as if it disclosed the person's natural being. The longer chain **linguistic relation→people→ancestry→biological race→juridical category→entitlement/exclusion** requires a historical carrier at each transition. The two consulted endpoints do not establish every intervening conduit, nor a simple Fichte-to-Nazism genealogy. The [consequential category and its deciding office](../../../arguments/A24-Arbitration-and-the-Usurpation-of-Measure.md) make belonging administratively exclusive through the concrete offices and capacities taken away.

<a id="branch-viii-anglo-celtic"></a>
### VIII — Wales, Ireland and Scotland: jurisdictional capability has three histories

In **Wales**, legal incorporation extends English jurisdiction and legal language through the Laws in Wales Acts before the nineteenth-century educational inquiry. The **1847 Blue Books** then make schooling, language, religion and social standing objects of official evaluation. Jurisdictional prescription and educational judgment act through different institutions. The education report supplies no missing statutory clauses or evidence of their enforcement; those questions depend upon the statutes and the acts performed under them.

One local observation complicates the general characterisation. A later Conwy history reproduces a passage identified as **Part III, pp.25–26**, recording an examination on **12 March 1847**. The examiner credits the master with English and Welsh competence and a successful method of interpretation while criticising organisation, training and material provision. This is a secondary reproduction of a named primary report, not an independently collated original. The credited linguistic competence and interpretation coexist with organisational and material deficiencies. Those different conditions bear upon the examination's outcome; a judgment upon Welsh has to answer them rather than treating every deficiency as an effect of the language. [Conwy history, PDF pp.20–21](https://www.peoplescollection.wales/media/6108/download?attachment=).

**Ireland** has a different institutional sequence: plantation and confiscation reorganise landholding; Anglicisation and the Protestant Ascendancy connect office, property and jurisdiction to a changed social order. A language's practical value increases where the institutions through which a person petitions, contracts, holds land or advances use it. The histories of those acts cannot be replaced by the Welsh school report. Property, religious office and administration give distinct mechanisms whose particular grants, testimonies and school orders remain to be recovered.

**Scotland** likewise retains its own Gaelic, educational and administrative history. Centralising institutions can change the language in which advancement is possible while migration, commerce and bilingual adaptation change speakers' choices through other pressures. The Scottish chronology cannot be supplied by Ireland's land history or Wales's incorporation. Its legislation, school records and local testimony remain separately required before more precise causal attribution.

Across the three branches, **English as jurisdictional capability** is a real operation rather than a verdict on every instance of learning English. Acquisition can enlarge a person's reach while the conditions rewarding it marginalise another speech-world. Capability is distributed through institutions and judged under a criterion. When the history of that judgment disappears, a language's acquired social authority can appear to be its inherent superiority.

<a id="revival-and-transregional-formations"></a>
## #4 — Sacred and national languages distribute people and place differently

<a id="branch-ix-hebrew"></a>
### IX — Hebrew revival, Zionism and Palestinian counter-history

Hebrew enters the modern revival with sacred, textual, literary and scholarly continuities already present. The new movement is its formation as an ordinary vernacular within a multilingual **Yishuv**, through households, schools, literature, journalism, theatre, song and lexicography. The historical question concerns how those activities acquire a mutually sustaining public reach. Neither ancient textual continuity nor one successful school alone establishes the resulting national configuration.

The **1913 Language War** makes one disputed office visible. A protest against German instruction at the planned Technikum and Reali institutions treats the teaching language as constitutive of national revival. The National Library of Israel's presentation of the notice identifies **Yosef Lurie and Eliezer Ben-Yehuda** among its signatories. This is a particular nationalist intervention in technical education; the catalogue's description is not an independent collation of every signatory or the trustees' minutes. [Notice and institutional description](https://www.nli.org.il/en/education/teaching-resources/primary-sources/nnl_edu997013313315805171).

The **1922 Mandate** supplies a later and different distribution of authority. Articles **2, 6 and 7** charge the administration with a Jewish national home, facilitated immigration and a nationality law while including protections for other inhabitants. Article **15** states community educational-language rights; **22** names English, Arabic and Hebrew as official languages. These are provisions of the Mandate, not evidence of fulfilment or equal political power. [Complete Avalon transcription, selected articles](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/palmanda.asp).

The branch therefore contains an attempted coincidence of ancestral people, territory, national language, sovereign law and citizenship, together with a population whose own place and standing that coincidence changes. **Palestinian Arabic, landholding, displacement and citizenship histories are constitutive counter-histories**, not an appendix to a completed national account. The Mandate text cannot narrate their lived consequences by itself. Ottoman, Mandate and subsequent state arrangements retain distinct laws and actors, and the transition between them requires Palestinian records and testimony as well as the national institutions' accounts.

Diaspora rabbinic Judaism gives another arrangement: peoplehood persists through text, ritual, communal law and dispersed institutions without a coincident sovereign territory. Modern territorialisation and vernacularisation reconfigure those existing relations. Land, schooling and civic capacity become consequential distributions, while an affected person's capacity to contest the terms of belonging remains part of how that reconfiguration can be judged.

<a id="branch-x-abrahamic"></a>
### X — Abrahamic formations: Word, law and people have different geometries

The comparative **F. E. Peters** branch opens the relation **revelatory Word↔normative Law↔collective identity↔territorial/political order**. Its chronology is plural: it begins before modern national language programmes and continues within them, through traditions whose institutions repeatedly change their relation to territory. The resulting comparison follows those distinct distributions rather than assigning one constitutional sequence to all three traditions. Selected Peters passages remain unacquired.

In the **Jewish** branch, Torah and halakhic life sustain communal continuity through changing relations to divine obligation, communal authority and host-state law. Dispersion changes the institutional conditions without erasing peoplehood. The Lunel letter above gives one local textual relation within that wider world; it cannot establish a complete history of rabbinic jurisdiction.

The **Christian** branch carries a different expansion across peoples and languages. Logos becomes a theological centre; Latin provides a transregional learned and ritual medium; vernacular scripture later enters the Reformation and national-language histories. These phases distinguish the universality claimed by a teaching from the languages and institutions through which it is transmitted. Translation changes access and the distribution of interpretive authority, but a particular church, court or printer must supply the evidence of that change.

The **Islamic** branch joins the privilege of Qur'anic Arabic to a multilingual umma. Sharia and fiqh retain their different offices as legal traditions develop across empires, schools and local orders. Sacred-language authority does not imply that every subject of an empire speaks one language or that a single state owns the community. The relevant historical actor can be a jurist, school or local institution rather than a national administration.

Word, people and place have three distributions which prevent the fourfold from becoming a universal Word-to-nation sequence. Administration is received and exercised through an office whose purpose is not exhausted by its holder. [Covenant and mediating authority](../../../arguments/A25-Covenant-Mediating-Office-Source-Authority.md) concern a source, interpreter, obligation and jurisdiction which each tradition distributes differently. Their comparison gives no common Abrahamic constitutional chronology and supplies no missing scriptural witness.

<a id="institutions-and-executable-return"></a>
## #5→0 — An inherited category can enter a new institution and act again

<a id="branch-xi-postwar"></a>
### XI — Post-war recombination: rupture and retained capacities

The defeat of Nazi Germany in **1945** is a rupture in political rule. Personnel, techniques, scientific and intelligence institutions, administrative forms and racial classifications nevertheless cross it through particular acts of recruitment, retention and reorganisation. The preceding study of **United States racial law by Nazi jurists** is a different transnational conduit, requiring its own legal and deliberative documents. The later recruitment programmes do not demonstrate that earlier route.

A **1946 Paperclip policy document**, approved by Truman on **3 September**, gives recruitment an explicit administrative body. It expands exploitation of German and Austrian specialists through contracts, military custody, family movement, screening and possible later immigration status. Its screening clause excludes more-than-nominal Nazi participation and active support, while treating technical honours differently and permitting further screening in doubtful cases. This is the programme's stated rule, not evidence that every appointment complied. The document makes technical value, political admissibility and the authority to decide between them distinct. [FRUS1946V448, cover and approval](../../../sources/political-theory-institutions/united-states-government/us-1946-paperclip-policy-frus448/SOURCE.md#us-1946-paperclip-policy-frus448-q001); [enclosure2a–b](../../../sources/political-theory-institutions/united-states-government/us-1946-paperclip-policy-frus448/SOURCE.md#us-1946-paperclip-policy-frus448-q002).

The **Gehlen** conduit has a different institutional history. The National Archives' account of the CIA's **2000 acknowledgement and promised release of records** describes the network's post-war Army Intelligence relation and subsequent CIA responsibility through **1956**. It identifies former Nazi and collaborationist backgrounds within the network. The consulted archival notice establishes that disclosed relationship and its record programme; it is not a full personnel audit. [National Archives, 5 October 2000 announcement](../../../sources/political-theory-institutions/national-archives/nara-2000-gehlen-disclosure/SOURCE.md#nara-2000-gehlen-disclosure-q001); [disclosed institutional relationship](../../../sources/political-theory-institutions/national-archives/nara-2000-gehlen-disclosure/SOURCE.md#nara-2000-gehlen-disclosure-q002).

[Selective repudiation can coexist with recruitment, changed mission and retained expertise](../../../arguments/A35-Compassion-Sensitivity-to-Origins-Epi-Logos-as-Vocation.md). **Incomplete denazification** is a person- and institution-specific investigation: appointment files, screening decisions and returned authority matter more than a general claim that a profession continued. Financial, imperial and technical continuities likewise need their own conduits. An office admits a person or technique under a new criterion; that decision transfers capacities and apportions costs; the receiving institution's renewed self-description remains answerable to the history of what it received. These relations do not establish one continuous hidden regime.

<a id="branch-xii-computation"></a>
### XII — Computation: classification returns as an executable condition

Naming becomes materially operative through a repeatable circuit: **mark→name→policy/allocation→changed world→new evidence→stronger or revised name**. [A classification enters a database or model](../../../arguments/A28-Authored-Ground-Positional-Delegation.md); an institution acts through it; the changed opportunities of those classified become part of what later appears as evidence. The return can expose the original cut or conceal it by treating its effects as independent confirmation. A particular deployed circuit must identify its actual acts and received consequences.

A dated technical formulation appears in **Perdomo and colleagues' 2020 Performative Prediction**. Their abstract explicitly treats predictions that alter their targets and introduces performative stability in relation to outcomes produced by acting on predictions. Predictions can thus change the future conditions under which their own performance is judged. This particular research problem gives classification a named technical neighbour; it supplies no general proof of every social feedback mechanism. [PMLR119, pp.7599–7609, abstract](../../../sources/computer-science-ml/perdomo/perdomo-et-al-2020-performative-prediction/SOURCE.md#perdomo-et-al-2020-performative-prediction-q001).

In **2023**, [DPO expresses pairwise preference](../../../sources/computer-science-ml/rafailov/rafailov-et-al-2023-dpo/SOURCE.md#rafailov-et-al-2023-dpo-q001) relative to an optimal and reference policy. A reference available from supervised fine-tuning differs from an estimated proxy when that source is unavailable. The policy's mathematical office does not itself establish a political defect. The historical and institutional question concerns [who selects the preference data, proxy and use of the result](../../../arguments/A29-Power-Delegated-Labour-Return.md), and which later evidence can reach that selection.

Execution distributes finite capacity under an apportioned criterion, delegates labour, enters a wider commons and sustains a planetary economy. Resources are committed, a course is authorised, an affected other bears consequence and completed work returns with costs and correction rights: **Name/Power** gives these four consequences within those five operations, without identifying political power with a numerical score. A local reference is real **Bimba** for its downstream comparisons while remaining **pratibimba** in a wider source relation. In a particular comparison, the judging agent is a functional knower, memory, tools and rules are means, and the available alternatives are known. The complete apparatus is in turn means within a person's containing inquiry and Life. A returned encounter can correct an answer, task or world-model, challenge an evaluator, source, lens, gauge, permission or commission, or give reasons to retain a fitting condition. Its next use inherits that disposition. Calling every reference merely derivative would conceal the actual office through which this return can act.
