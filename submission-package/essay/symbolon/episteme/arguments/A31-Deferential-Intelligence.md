## #0

Deferential Intelligence is **model-revising encounter**. A determination remains strong enough to act while the Other or world can change the model under which its encounter is interpreted. [[A27-Self-and-Other-Unity-without-Possession|A27]] grounds the Other's excess over my account; [[A30-Objective-Co-Internality|A30]] grounds the shared field in which that excess becomes consequential. A31 gives their relation an operational virtue: fidelity can require changing the terms on which the first answer was made.

The metaphor names a structural relinquishment of exhaustive source-claim. It does not establish that an implemented system already possesses this capacity.

## #1

The [[section-rooms/arguments/08-deferential-intelligence|direct mature carrier]] specifies what must be available **before consequential action**: origin, aperture, exclusions, confidence, counterclaim and undecidable remainder. These conditions tell another participant where a disagreement can act. A contrary source can challenge provenance; an excluded case can challenge scope; a changed permission can challenge the next act's authority.

The account can remain exact where its warrant is strong. Deference does not require lowering every confidence or replacing judgment with assent. A new assertion can be rejected after examination; the rejection must then include the encounter in its warrant rather than treating contradiction as evidence of the account's infallibility. [[symbolon/episteme/concepts/C47-Deferential-Intelligence|C47 Deferential Intelligence]] defines this reusable distinction.

## #2

The [[quilt/2026-08-02-PARALLEL-HARMONISED-QUILT|R16.5 ratification]] sources five possible sites of revision: answer, task interpretation, world-model, evaluator/gauge and terms of commission. These are different depths of return.

That analytic case identifies the operation to test; it reports no observed run. Literal compliance which removes the encountered contradiction would betray the commission's epistemic purpose.
