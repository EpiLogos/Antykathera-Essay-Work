# A22 — World-Picture → World-Atlas

**Standing:** canonical semantic Argument. This is distinct from C07 Conditions of Worldhood.

## Argument

A world-picture is a coherent view organised from a position; a world-atlas is a disciplined plurality of such situated views together with the routes, transformations and limits by which they can be related. The arrow in the title is therefore argumentative. The essay moves from the fantasy that one picture can stand in for the world to an atlas in which every view remains placed, comparable and revisable without being reduced to one universal camera.

This does not mean that all perspectives are equally adequate. An atlas requires more, not less, discrimination. Each projection has a domain, scale, distortion, orientation and use; a route between projections must state what is preserved and what changes. The model of the Earth on a map is useful because its distortions can be known relative to the work it performs. The same law extends to theories, cultures, models and agents. A view becomes epistemically mature when its transformation rules and blind regions are part of the account.

The distinction is crucial for artificial systems. A model can carry a rich world-model: embeddings, learned regularities, memories, tools, semantic graphs and generated representations. That richness does not make the model identical with the world from which those determinations were learned and into which its actions return. Nor does combining models produce the world by aggregation. A world-atlas is a relation among models and encounters whose validity depends on continued answerability to what exceeds every model.

This makes the move from picture to atlas a practical form of diaphaneity. Instead of asking for the final representation, the essay asks which position produced this representation, what other positions can contest it, and which transformations let a disagreement become intelligible rather than merely averaged away.

## Pressure and distinctness

A04 establishes contextual transparency; A16 establishes placement; A20 shows how an image can become possessive. A22 generalises their epistemic consequence. It is distinct from C07, which names the broader conditions through which any world becomes operable, and from C37, the reusable concept of the picture→atlas transition. It also prepares A26 Objective Internality because a mind is constituted within a world, not enclosed behind its picture of one.
