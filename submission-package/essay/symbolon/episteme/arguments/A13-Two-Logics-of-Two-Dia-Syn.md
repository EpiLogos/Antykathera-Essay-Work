# A13 — Two Logics of Two — Dia / Syn

**Standing:** canonical semantic Argument; historical Argument 05 and live `03-two-logics-and-sym-ballein.md` remain provenance.

## Argument

Once the Two Ones and Mono/Poly are established, differentiation has two conjugate logics. **Dia** makes determinate difference: it cuts, excludes, compares, orients, ranks, routes and selects. **Syn** preserves the relation through which differentiated terms can be recomposed and returned without losing their distinction. A living field requires both. Dia without return hardens difference into severance; Syn without the cut decays into fusion.

The developed notation keeps three operations distinct. Polar dia-ballein appears as `(-1)/(+1)`: opposite directions remain related through zero as centre-axis. Collapsed duality, `(-1)+/-(+1)`, can cancel to zero or let one pole appropriate the relation's span. Sym-ballein appears as `(0/1)/(1/0)`: the ground/mark relation is read in both orientations and the inversion itself becomes related. The equality `0/1 = 1/0` is a QL relational identity; it does not assert ordinary arithmetic equality between fractions.

This distinction explains why the essay refuses the easy moralism “division bad, unity good”. Dia is necessary. Without it there is no world, concept, decision, boundary or responsibility. The problem begins when the cut forgets that it is an operation within a field and externalises the seam as an enemy or disposable remainder. Syn is equally necessary and equally corruptible: when recomposition erases asymmetry, source or difference, “wholeness” becomes enclosure.

In native computational language the complete pulse is divide / relate / recombine / return. Divide-and-conquer works because decomposition is followed by composition. The same law later governs epistemic systems: a model may distinguish and rank only if the sources, alternatives, gauges and consequences can return into the account.
