# A24 — Arbitration and the Usurpation of Measure

**Standing:** canonical semantic Argument. The mature Arbitration / Hybris / Regard / Anamnesis whole-field is a first-class Etymology reasoning office consumed here; it is not replaced by this Argument.

## Argument

Arbitration is necessary wherever a finite agent must decide under conditions that do not decide themselves. The danger is not decision. It is the usurpation of measure: a derivative office mistakes the criterion by which it presently judges for the source by which the whole field is measured. Hybris names this error at its sharpest. A local measure ceases to know itself as local.

The mature relational exemplar is exact:

The conjugate forms generate six determinations:


The generated relation must not be confused with its semantic flowering. **Arbitration-in-Crisis** is the relational determination; **Hybris** is one flowering that becomes visible when decision forgets the crisis and installs its measure as self-authorising. **Con-text-through-Diaphaneity** generates a relation from which Regard can flower: the field is looked through and the conditions/others that constitute the view become visible. **Resolution-in-Reconciliation** can flower as anamnesis or recognition: the settled account returns to the sources and differences it had temporarily gathered.

The philosophical claim follows. Every finite judgment has an arbitral moment, because action requires a cut. A complex, institution, model, judge, metric, market or state can therefore be assessed by whether its derivative criterion remains answerable to a wider field. Usurpation occurs when the criterion protects itself from the return that could reveal its locality. A score becomes “what the person is”; a market price becomes “what value is”; a model preference becomes “what users want”; a political office becomes “the people”. The error is office-confusion, not simply bad intention.

The positive alternative is not endless indecision. Deferential intelligence later makes decision revisable by encounter. Covenant makes source/office relations explicit. Operational parity makes hidden criteria inspectable where they are implemented in software. A just arbitration is therefore one whose authority is real enough to act and bounded enough to return.
