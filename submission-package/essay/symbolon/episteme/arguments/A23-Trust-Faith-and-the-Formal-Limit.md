# A23 — Trust, Faith and the Formal Limit

**Standing:** canonical semantic Argument; live `18-trust-faith-formal-limit.md` is direct developed provenance.

## Argument

Faith begins where an account remains answerable to a source it cannot possess. This is not a defence of belief without evidence. A03's formal limit establishes that no finite determination can include the determining condition as one more item without generating a further edge. A23 asks what epistemic relation remains possible once that limit is acknowledged. The answer is trust: committed dependence under conditions in which complete possession is structurally unavailable.

Trust is unavoidable in ordinary knowledge. We depend on memories we cannot re-run, instruments whose manufacture we did not personally witness, people whose interiors are not objects in our possession, institutions whose records mediate distant events, and worlds that continue beyond our present model. The relevant distinction is therefore not trust versus reason but faithful versus unfaithful trust. Faithful trust makes its grounds, offices and revision conditions visible. Unfaithful trust hides dependence and presents a mediated account as self-grounding certainty.

This is why formal limit strengthens rather than weakens epistemic responsibility. If certainty cannot be manufactured by installing an account in the source-office, the task becomes to build relations that remain corrigible by encounter. Trust can be graded by provenance, competence, consistency, openness to correction and the consequences of error. Faith names the existential form of the same structure when commitment must precede exhaustive proof.

The argument returns directly to A01. A faithful definition does not withhold determination; it determines while preserving answerability. Trust is that structure extended through time: an account is acted upon without being exempted from the possibility that its source, context or Other will revise it.
