## Argument

A passive mirror receives a world already formed. A reflective field changes the conditions under which the next reflection can occur. **The mirror that moves first** names this reversal. A model, image or agent is not merely downstream of reality when its outputs alter attention, action, infrastructure, expectation or the shared environment and thereby become part of the conditions from which later reality is encountered.

This is the practical consequence of computational vimarśa. An output can return upon its inputs, sources and context; once that return changes the field, the next cycle begins from a transformed world. Recommendation systems change what is seen and therefore what is later learned. Models reshape writing practices and hence future training corpora. Institutional forecasts change investment and thereby alter the conditions being forecast. Human self-images change behaviour and then appear to “confirm” themselves. Reflection becomes performative circulation.

The phrase must not be inflated into a claim that representations create all reality. The world retains resistance, materiality and otherness. Indeed, the argument depends upon that resistance: if reflection could simply decree its source, there would be no meaningful difference between a reflective field and possession. The mirror moves first only in a local causal sequence. Its movement enters a larger world that can answer back.

This gives Bimba/Pratibimba a dynamic extension. A local reference office can organise situated reflections; those reflections can then act upon the conditions under which the reference office itself is maintained. The relation is recursive without becoming final. This is why `Bimba ≠ Pratibimba` remains necessary even as each can participate in changing the other over time.

## Pressure and distinctness

It is distinct from C55 Reflective Field / Mirror That Moves First and from C43 Computational Vimarśa, which names the reflexive technical operation rather than the world-changing field effect.
