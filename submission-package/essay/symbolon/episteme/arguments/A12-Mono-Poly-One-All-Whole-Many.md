# A12 — Mono/Poly — One / All, Whole / Many

**Standing:** canonical semantic Argument; historical Argument 04 remains provenance, with Alpha/Delta matheme residue preserved rather than silently absorbed.

## Argument

Mono/Poly is the ontological consequence of the Two Ones. Reality is read as one relational whole differentiating through many real expressions. The whole is prior in the limited structural sense that no part creates all of the relations and conditions through which it becomes this part; the many are real because differentiation is not an error to be cancelled on return. “One / All” and “Whole / Many” therefore name one field from two directions rather than a contest between monism and pluralism.

Apoha makes this concrete. A determination is constituted through exclusions; its apparent boundary already depends on a wider field. The local `1` is consequently empty of self-subsistence without being empty of reality. This is why Mono is not a giant entity made by adding the Poly together, and why Poly is not a heap of independently grounded atoms.

The argument acquires political and technical force once that relation is forgotten. Monopoly is the attempt by a local `1` — ruler, platform, institution, model, theory or account — to occupy the source-office of the field that sustains it. Conversely, an undifferentiated rhetoric of wholeness can erase local responsibility and difference. Mono/Poly requires both structural priority of relation and irreducible plurality of situated expressions.

This supplies a clear institutional grammar. Commons, protocols or shared fields can provide common conditions without becoming sovereign embodiments of the whole. Local contexts can remain genuinely authoritative within their scope without pretending that local authority generates the shared world. The later Power and Commons arguments depend on this distinction.

## Pressure and distinctness

A11 is the necessary prior: `0 = One`, `1 = All`. A12 expands those offices into whole/many ontology. It is distinct from A13 because Dia/Syn are conjugate operations by which the field differentiates and returns; Mono/Poly names what kind of field those operations inhabit. It is distinct from C54 Commons / Non-Monopoly, which is one institutional consequence rather than the ontology itself.
