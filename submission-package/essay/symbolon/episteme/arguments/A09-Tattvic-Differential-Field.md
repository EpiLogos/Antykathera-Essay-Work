## #0

The tattvas give differentiation a genealogy: a luminous field becomes a subject/object seam, a bounded horizon, an inner instrument and a material world without creating a second reality outside itself. Anuttara names the field for which no external measuring object stands outside. This beginning matters to every subsequent contraction: what becomes limited is a power of the field, and the limit remains effective within it.

The genealogy discloses **paradigmatic existence before explicit paradigm**. A Life does not first stand outside a complete world and then adopt a worldview about it. Its powers, limits, means of discrimination, inner instrument, temporal order and material situation participate in the very form in which a world becomes available. [Objective Internality](../concepts/C41-Objective-Internality.md) develops this relation as lived/enacted paradigm. The tattvic genealogy makes its constituent powers available through a specifically Śaiva account of manifestation.

## #1

Śiva–Śakti distinguishes luminosity and its self-articulating power. Prakāśa is luminous appearing; vimarśa its active self-presence, through which appearances are known, distinguished, joined and withdrawn. The [Prakāśa–Vimarśa argument](A05-Prakasa-Vimarsa.md) develops Dyczkowski's mirror contrast: the reflected display of an inert crystal does not know its display. The tattvic descent depends on active self-articulation, not a succession of objects deposited before an unchanged spectator.

[Dyczkowski](../sources/indian-philosophy/dyczkowski/dyczkowski-2000-doctrine-vibration/SOURCE.md) p.75 distinguishes two axes of this activity. Vertically, manifestation and withdrawal articulate the movement between unity and diversity. Horizontally, awareness goes towards and returns from its object in each perception. Will, knowledge and action generate, sustain and annul dimensions of experience; they are phases of the living movement through which the levels operate. An effect remains within the power that differentiates it, and an act of perception recapitulates the relation rather than merely looking at a finished cosmological inventory. A local act can therefore bear the whole movement: a possibility takes form, becomes a particular encounter, leaves an effect and returns into the conditions of another act. The relation is internally differentiated at each scale.

## #2

Sadāśiva and Īśvara give the field inverse universal readings: “I am this” and “This am I”, `0/1 ↔ 1/0`. The reversal changes the direction of apprehension within one field. Ahaṃkāra can later say “I am this” while appropriating a particular local form. The grammar repeats, but its scope has contracted: a universal orientation and identification with a finite body, role or thought do different work. Keeping the scope explicit explains how the whole becomes locally inhabitable and how local identity can forget its provenance.

## #3

Sadvidyā holds I and this in balanced identity; Māyā makes their separation operative. Māyā is the horizon at which this appears while that is withheld; the withholding lets an agent discriminate and act. Mahāmāyā remains Śakti: revealing and concealing are powers of the same field. The horizon is therefore neither the unobjectifiable Subject nor the hole of a topological picture. In Dyczkowski's source-matched pp.78–79, inner/outer separation is produced through consciousness's creative activity, and external manifestation preserves its internal nature. The experienced world has efficacy.

The [sleeper relation](../../../quilt/27-07-26-QUILTING-FOR-FULL-ARGUMENT.md) gives the lived operation through forgetting sleep: that forgetting lets a dream count as immediately real. Dream shows why objective need not mean externally material: its objects have an experienced reality whose relation to the sleeper can subsequently be recognised. Recognition changes the relation to the horizon through which experience occurs.

## #4

The five kañcukas contract powers into the finite parameters of a context: *kalā* apportions efficacy, *vidyā* knowledge, *rāga* desire through incompletion, *kāla* time, and *niyati* order. Efficacy and time are distinct contractions despite the closeness of their names. Puruṣa is the bounded experient within this field; Prakṛti and the inner instrument articulate its world of experience. Buddhi determines, ahaṃkāra appropriates under I and mine, and manas coordinates presentations and alternatives.

The further differentiation reaches the capacities of sensing and acting, the subtle objects of sense and the gross elements. Judgment and action thus receive an effective body and a world in which their differences have consequences. The descent does not end with an interior observer looking at a diagram: hearing, touching, seeing, tasting and smelling, the powers of action and their material field belong to its completion. The universal orientations remain prior to the appropriation of a local body or role. The [Sāṅkhya source](../sources/indian-philosophy/isvarakrishna/isvarakrishna-colebrooke-wilson-1837-sankhya-karika/SOURCE.md), kārikās XXIII, XXIV, XXVII and XXIX, supplies the functional triad; XIX qualifies it with the passive witness. That witness distinguishes the instrument's activities from its passive experient; the Śaiva descent begins instead from the self-articulating power of awareness.

The interpretation does not attribute this square construction historically to Abhinavagupta or supply a completed thirty-six-cell assignment; its yield is the disclosed generating relation.
