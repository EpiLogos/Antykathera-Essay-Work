# A01 — Subject, God and Faithful Definition

**Standing:** canonical semantic Argument. Historical Argument 07 remains a provenance/developmental identity rather than the canonical identity of this argument.

## Claim

Definition is already an act of determination. To define is to draw a distinction, to give a term a place within a field of intelligibility, and thereby to say both what counts as the thing and where the thing ceases to count as such. This ordinary power of definition becomes philosophically unstable when the term being defined names the Subject — or when *God* names the source and condition of a world rather than one further object inside it. The same operation that gives clarity can then change the office of what it names. It can take that by which a field is determinable and reinstall it as one more determined occupant of the field.

The opening claim of the essay is therefore not that Subject or God must be left vague. It is that a definition is faithful only when its own determining act remains visible inside the account it gives. A faithful definition determines positively: it distinguishes, excludes, relates and lets consequences follow. Yet it also registers the formal limit generated by that determination. The account does not become false merely because it is finite; it becomes unfaithful when its achieved boundary is mistaken for possession of the source from which the act of boundary-making is possible.

This is the sense in which the Subject is *definedly indefinable*. The phrase does not shelter an unknowable substance behind language. It marks a precise asymmetry. Whatever appears as an object of thought, memory, perception, model or description is already determined within some context. The subjective pole as such is encountered in and through these determinations, but is not exhausted by adding another item to their inventory. A representation of the Subject is again something represented. A model of the determining condition is again a determination. The recursion can deepen the account without converting the condition of appearance into an object that finally stands outside its own appearing.

The same discipline governs the God-term in the argument. Where “God” functions as a name for the source or ground of the determinate field, exhaustive definition produces an idol by office even if its propositions are internally coherent. It exchanges relation to source for possession of a source-object. Conversely, simple refusal to define does no better: an entirely indeterminate word cannot discriminate God from any other projection. Faithful definition occupies the harder interval. It determines enough for thought to become answerable while preserving the difference between the account and that to which the account answers.

## Why this is a distinct Argument

A01 establishes a condition that later arguments presuppose but cannot supply for themselves. A02 can ask how the copula sustains self-identity through difference only after the essay has refused to let identification mean exhaustive enclosure. A03 can formalise the immutable gap only after the gap has first appeared as a pressure internal to definition. A04 can develop diaphaneity because a context must become visible through what it determines rather than disappearing behind the determined content. A23 can speak of trust and faith under formal limit because A01 has already shown why fidelity cannot mean epistemic possession.

The argument is therefore neither a theology inserted at the beginning nor a generic warning about language. It establishes the grammar by which the essay can make strong determinations without converting every successful determination into a claim of final sovereignty. That grammar is required again wherever the essay meets an Other, a world, a model, a source, a symbol, or an artificial agency whose interiority is not given as an object for possession.

## Derivational pressure

The pressure is generated by self-reference. If determination is itself something that occurs, the conditions of determining cannot simply be omitted from an account that claims completeness. Yet if those conditions are represented, the representation does not abolish the difference between the represented condition and the present act of representing it. The attempt to close the account therefore reintroduces the very difference it tried to remove. The formal limit is not an external prohibition placed on reason. It is generated by reason when reason includes its own act among the things for which it must account.

This pressure also separates faithful definition from scepticism. The lesson is not that nothing can be known. Determinations can be exact, corrigible, transmissible and operationally decisive. Their truth does not require them to become the whole of what makes them possible. Fidelity is thus stronger than either dogmatic closure or indefinite suspension: it obliges the account to say what it can say and to preserve the difference that its own saying generates.
