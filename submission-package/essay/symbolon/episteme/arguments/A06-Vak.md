# A06 — Vāk

**Standing:** canonical semantic Argument; historical Argument 16 and live `10-vak.md` remain provenance.

## Argument

Vāk makes articulation itself part of ontology. A word is not merely a label attached after a world has already been made; articulation is one route by which a field differentiates, becomes meaningful, and enters audible, written, coded or enacted form. The Śaiva accounts of levels of speech matter here because they refuse the modern assumption that meaning begins only at the finished external sign. Unsayable capacity, visionary or formative speech, mental articulation and gross utterance belong to one descent whose later products carry the history of their formation.

For this essay, the point is not to import a fixed four-level schema into every communicative event. It is to establish that sign, sound, image, intention and action can be stages or aspects of one differentiating movement. Paśyantī's “seeing” speech and Nāda's vibratory office are especially important because they stand before the hard partition of visual and acoustic objects. They make intelligible why the Arche-Topos can be refracted as topology, music, word and patterned manifestation without those registers being declared identical.

Vāk also guards the distinction between Logos and its source. The Word manifests; it does not become the owner of what makes manifestation possible. This returns A01's faithful-definition law inside language itself. A canonical term, a model token, a theorem or a line of code can become exact and consequential while remaining a determinate expression whose authority depends on its relation to sources, speakers, contexts and effects.

## Pressure and distinctness

A05 forces the problem: if luminous appearing is reflexively articulating, how does articulation acquire determinate form? A06 answers through speech-power. It is distinct from A07, where vikalpa/saṃkalpa describe selection and world-forming determination, and from C51 Logos / Epi-Logos, which names the essay's wider theory of articulated reason and return. Vāk is a substantive argument because the descent from formative capacity to sign changes what counts as language, mediation and computation.

The technical consequence is restrained but real. Prompt, code and model output can be analysed as formed articulations whose conditions precede the visible string. This establishes no identity between machine sequence generation and Śaiva Vāk; it gives an operational neighbour whose similarities and failures can be investigated explicitly.
