# C42 — Objective Co-Internality

## #0

Objective Co-Internality names the reciprocal constitution of non-identical operative interiors through a shared world and one another. A document, action, expectation, permission or changed environment can enter the conditions from which several participants next perceive and act.

## #1

The participants therefore do more than exchange messages after being fully formed. Their records, tools, language and institutions participate in each other's possible trajectories. Symbolon Dynamics distinguishes the currently available landscape from the actual history that changes it: products of one circuit can enter the next inherited field, including another participant's field.

## #2

Reciprocity requires difference. Q27's Other-within-Mono distinction prevents common reality from becoming possession by a dominant local model. Each contribution remains answerable to its own sources and world, not merely useful to the receiver. A shared state without independent grounds can be an instrument of assimilation rather than co-internality.

## #3

The federated Bimba design makes sites of disagreement explicit: source, world-object, lens, gauge, reference field, permission and inherited context. Distinct communities can keep locally authoritative maps. Translation exposes enough of their grounds for comparison without declaring one map the final world or reducing divergence to an average output.

## #4

Returned evidence has several possible effects. It can change one local judgment, revise a reference map, alter a relation between maps, or change the rules by which contributions circulate. These are different operations and require different records. A conversation demonstrates co-internal transformation only where some participant's future conditions become materially different through the encounter.

## #5→0

The shared capacity belongs to the coupled run. No participant possesses its complete field, and no collective phenomenal super-subject follows from coordination. C47 asks how models change through encounter; C55 asks how the reflection and the institution holding it can turn first toward source. The return preserves distinct authority, revision and responsibility across the field it has helped constitute.
