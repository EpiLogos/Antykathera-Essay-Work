# C10 — Mediation / Pramāṇa

## #0

**Pramāṇa is Objective Internality as means**, between Subjective Immediacy as knower and World as known; Life / Mind is the whole relation. [Objective Internality](C41-Objective-Internality.md) names the constituted interior through which a world becomes available. Mediation specifies how this disclosure occurs: what becomes salient, what transformations it undergoes, and how the disclosed can be known and acted upon.

The means participates in the determination of its object. A recollection presents an event as past; a perception presents what is being encountered; a testimony carries another's account. Their difference can matter even when the resulting sentence is identical. Knowledge does not require the means to vanish. It requires an adequate relation to what is known through it. The [immutable gap](C05-Immutable-Gap.md) makes that relation necessary without abolishing its reality.

## #1

[[symbolon/episteme/sources/indian-philosophy/dyczkowski/dyczkowski-2000-doctrine-vibration/SOURCE|Dyczkowski — The Doctrine of Vibration]] **sources** a determinate Śaiva articulation in the locally held discussion, pp.63–64. Subject, object and means of knowledge attend the cognitive act together. The means is not a neutral addition inserted after an independently complete subject and object have been established. Their differentiation belongs to the one act whose articulation makes an object available.

The three luminaries preserve that relation as an image. The Sun figures the illuminating means, the Moon the object shining by reflected light, and Fire the subject which takes the perceived into itself. The illuminating Sun, reflected Moon and assimilating Fire belong to one cognitive scene. Remove the means and the account loses the act of disclosure; remove the knower and it loses the act's first-person fact; remove the known and it loses what the act differentiates. The image teaches their relation rather than presenting three self-sufficient things.

## #2

The relation must preserve the difference it renders intelligible. Dyczkowski's pp.65–66 press this through colour: if consciousness were entirely homogeneous in its knowing, blue and yellow could not appear as distinct. Formless availability and determinate appearance must be thought together. At pp.70–72, the distinction between a mirror or crystal and conscious reflexivity adds an active power of recognition, memory and presentation to the account. A mechanically reflected appearance does not by itself supply that whole operation.

[Prakāśa–Vimarśa](../arguments/A05-Prakasa-Vimarsa.md) grounds this illumination and reflexivity. The epistemic middle has an active office: it selects, discloses, retains and presents. Remembering an earlier perception does not simply repeat the earlier event, because its relation to time has changed. The means participates in that change while retaining what makes this a recollection of that event.

## #3

A symbol makes the same demand on an account. [[symbolon/episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-definition-god-draft3/SOURCE|Taylor — Definition of God, Draft3]] **grounds** the authorial movement in which an unavailable whole nevertheless orders its partial expressions. Its image of iron filings patterned around a magnet makes mediation legible: the pattern answers to a relation whose source it does not contain. The visible pattern makes the relation to an uncontained condition encounterable.

A determinate sign fixes something; a living symbol carries that determination in a relation exceeding it. [Diaphaneity](C09-Diaphaneity.md) **extends** this into reading the shaping conditions through what appears. [Vāk](../arguments/A06-Vak.md) **extends** it through articulation: speech is one developed mediation, while feeling, perception, intention, contact and attention prevent mediation from being restricted to fully formed verbal statements.

## #4

Suppose the sentence is “the door is locked.” Seeing the bolt engaged, recalling that it was locked yesterday, and repeating another person's report can produce the same words. They expose different routes of correction. Renewed perception can discover that the bolt has moved; a dated record can disclose that the recollection concerns yesterday rather than now; tracing testimony can identify who checked and when. The sentence does not carry these distinctions on its surface, but they change the reason for accepting it and the action it warrants.

The example makes the difference between source, means and warrant concrete. A generated answer drawn from a record and a conjecture about an unobserved state cannot exchange authority simply because they sound alike. Returning through the means requires recovering the source relation and the transformation that made this answer available.

The [Sanskrit enumeration case](../etymologies/symbol-account-and-trust/HISTORICAL-BRANCHES.md#sanskrit-enumeration-and-discriminative-practice) historicises a particular taxonomy: Sāṃkhya Kārikās 4–6 distinguish perception, inference and trustworthy testimony. The Śaiva source above concerns the co-presence of knower, means and known; this Sāṃkhya case supplies its own account of valid cognition. The essay's broader appointment of Objective Internality to the office of means preserves, rather than erases, such differences of warrant.

[[symbolon/episteme/etymologies/symbol-account-and-trust/WHOLE-FIELD|Symbol / Account / Trust]] carries the operational relation: the account states how it answers to source. The [mediating office](C29-Mediating-Office-Derivative-Sovereignty.md) raises the connected question of authority to act or speak. Being appointed to convey an account does not make its bearer the source of what it conveys.

## #5→0

The return reaches the constitution of the means. [Deferential Intelligence](../arguments/A31-Deferential-Intelligence.md) distinguishes endlessly varying answers from changing what counts as an adequate answer. A system could keep recommending that the supposedly locked door be avoided while consulting the same outdated record. Retrieving a current observation changes the available evidence; changing a rule that treats old observations as current changes how the means will work next time. Those are different, consequential repairs.

An answerable mediation preserves the path through which a result can reach its sources and governing criteria. Recording disagreement keeps an encounter available. Revising a criterion changes how later encounters can count. The next act is where that return becomes effective.

[Trust and faith](C48-Trust-Faith-under-Formal-Limit.md) act through this relation without waiting for complete possession of its source. The [context frame](C08-Context-Context-Frame.md) situates the act, and [diaphaneity](C09-Diaphaneity.md) lets its shaping conditions become readable. What is learned through a means can then renew the means itself.
