# Simulation

## Definition

Simulation is a running representation of a world — agents, economies, trajectories, futures — synthesised "in miniature" to describe, predict, or speculate about the processes it stands in for. It lives at **#1** (What? / Definition, Square B), refracted through **L1** (Causal: what it stands for, and by what provenance) and **L4'** (Scientific: the descriptive → predictive → speculative loop the brief names at §5.2). The brief's axis runs from *multiagent simulation* to *simulated agency and back again*; where the membrane perforates, a simulation becomes a real-world event about which further simulations are run.

## In the brief

The history is double: "Before contemporary AI, software agents were long used in multi-agent simulations of social and economic scenarios," while today generative inference lets users "mobilize legions of agents" acting directly on the physical world; "in a simulation, past, present, and future are synthesized in miniature" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 25|p. 25]]). The time-axis divides into three modalities: descriptive simulations are "lower-order functional equivalents of physical processes" judged by correspondence; predictive simulations "anticipate events and patterns in the future"; and the most interesting, speculative forms, "based on generative back-and-forth between simulation and simulated," surface "potential courses through the articulation of counterfactual possibilities" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 25|p. 25]]).

The decisive claim: "For Agentworld, simulations have meaningful agency in the world. They directly cause things to occur; the membrane between simulation and simulated perforates," yielding "a growing relay that can extend many orders deep: real-to-sim-to-real-to-sim-to-real, etc." These relays "are economies unto themselves," engineered by one actor or many, collaborative or adversarial: "Friends and enemies formally simulate and counter-simulate one another in oscillating sequences that attempt to model the models of the models" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 25|p. 25]]) — the scenario adds that some models in these cascading economies "are engineered to deceive" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 24|p. 24]]). The chain of refraction then mixes nesting and resolution — "Matryoshka realities nested inside one another," where some simulated events are themselves simulations of realities "with far greater informational complexity than their own" — so that description, prediction, and speculation "cascade through the chain of refracted simulations, blurring the modeling of past, present, and future into kaleidoscopic translations" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 26|p. 26]]). The FROM → TO table compresses the axis: "Agents remain purely sims → Agent sims have increased recursive agency" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 56|p. 56]]).

## In the argument

The essay refuses simulation-as-innocent-modelling. Along the [[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 25|real-to-sim-to-real relays]], representation and represented shift back and forth until a higher-order sim recursively *governs* the field it claims only to describe — Q9's "simulation as political force." The essay's response (Argued): a simulation is a pratibimba whose bimba, gauge, exclusions, and permissions must be disclosed. [[39-s5-p2-j-space|J-Space]] makes its judgments measurable; [[40-s5-p3-preference-hidden-zero|Preference Models and the Hidden Zero]] exposes the socially occupied baseline a Bradley–Terry gauge hides. Primary stations: §1 ([[13-s1-p0-sign-migrates|A Sign Migrates Between Worlds]] — the sign that reorganises its own field) and §5. Failure mode: [[j-space|circumscription-without-circumstance]] — a boundary hiding the horizon that produced it.
