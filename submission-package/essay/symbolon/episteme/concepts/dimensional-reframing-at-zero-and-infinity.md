# Dimensional Reframing at Zero and Infinity

## The method proposition

A mathematical obstruction can disclose which object or operation an account presupposes. Complex extension, projective completion, compactification, quotienting, covering and differential limiting answer different questions by changing particular definitions and permissions. A construction preserves the relation specified by its hypotheses; it does not guarantee a solution to every obstruction. Zero and infinity expose these differences sharply. **The return to zero is where a system declares its law:** the attempted operation makes its domain, inverse, extension or lost cancellation available for examination. Taylor reads that exposure through the immutable gap, while each mathematical construction retains its own proof.

## Case ledger

Each case names the operation, its hypotheses and the relation it preserves.

- **Signed line → complex plane.** Over the real field, `x² = −1` has no solution. Define multiplication of pairs by `(a,b)(c,d)=(ac−bd,ad+bc)` and let `i=(0,1)`. Then `i²=(-1,0)` represents −1, and multiplication by `i` rotates a vector by a quarter-turn. Adjoining a coordinate alone would not establish this multiplication.
- **Affine line → projective line.** For a field, identify nonzero pairs under common nonzero scalar multiplication. The affine coordinate exists where the second coordinate is nonzero; the remaining class is the infinity point. Real-projective parallel-line incidence belongs to a projective plane, not this one-dimensional construction. Under the specified invertible projective transformations, cross-ratio retains its own non-degeneracy conditions.
- **Complex plane → Riemann sphere.** Stereographic projection identifies the one-point completion with `CP¹`. The map `z↦1/z` extends by exchanging zero and infinity. This specified reciprocal map is everywhere defined on the sphere; binary `0/0` and ∞−∞ do not thereby gain unique values.
- **Covering plane → quotient surface.** `T² = R²/Z²`: integer-translation equivalence identifies points of an infinite plane into a compact surface. Each quotient point has an infinite lattice of pre-images. The quotient operation is not arithmetic division, and the torus's zero Euler characteristic does not calculate infinity divided by zero.
- **Inexhaustible field → local change.** The native `∞/dx` compresses the relation of determining capacity to an indefinite particular. A calculus derivative instead requires a specified difference quotient and an existing limit at the point. On an interval, differentiation determines an antiderivative only up to a constant. These exact operations give distinct refracting cases; `∞/dx` is not their standard quotient formula.
- **Division by zero → division pluralisms.** Ordinary field division requires a nonzero denominator. A projective swap extends a reciprocal map; a zero-totalised inverse supplies a different operation with conditional inverse laws; wheels and transreal systems require their own exceptional objects and equations. Bhāskara II's ***khahara*** retains a zero-denominator quantity. Colebrooke's I.14 and I.16, printed pp. 137–38, distinguish the written fraction and its stated invariance under insertion or extraction, including the divine-immutability analogy. That historical expression does not supply the missing ordinary-field inverse.

## Status boundary

The comparison concerns constructions that actually state a new permission while retaining the old restriction. It supplies no general law that zero generates a dimension or that every obstruction can be removed. QL's `1/0` is an oriented meta-sign: ordinary division's defined/undefined seam remains visible through it. Extending a geometric reciprocal or defining a totalised inverse answers a stated mathematical task; the native orientation returns the achieved determination through its ground.
