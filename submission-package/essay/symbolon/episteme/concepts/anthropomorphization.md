# Anthropomorphization

## Definition

Anthropomorphization is the inherited human projection of human form — face, voice, performed subjecthood — onto agents, which in Agentworld has become "the dominant interface between two cultures" (Bratton). It lives at **#2** (How? / Word / Dynamis), the operational-interface position of Square C (the L2↔L3' axis): not *what* agents are but *how* humans and agents meet. Through **L2** (the tetralemma, IS/IS-NOT/BOTH/NEITHER) it is benign — one contingent interface among possible others. It is *hereditary* because carried and refined across history — the **L3'** (chronological, Hegelian-dialectical) Night face of the same axis.

## In the brief

The founding claim: "Our relations with agents are based on hereditary anthropomorphization that becomes itself the dominant interface between two cultures" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 4|p. 4]]). The interface is one-sided by construction: as with the GUI, "it has proven much more difficult to teach humans how computers think than to teach computers to behave as if they knew how humans think" — with the brief's own caveat that this "does not neatly map onto which of the pair is more intelligent" (its example: grey parrots have learned far more human language than any human has learned parrotspeak) ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 5|p. 5]]). The brief holds two futures for the habit at once: "training and perfecting the anthropomorphization of AI may ultimately be the lasting legacy of humans in the deeper future," and yet "this may not last" — there are "strategic advantages to building durable forms of human-agent and agent-agent interaction paradigms … that are less immediately intuitive but which facilitate more robust assemblies of collective hybrid intelligence" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 5|p. 5]]).

The projection runs in both directions: "we have a theory of mind of AI too. This is why they appear to us as chatbots … We say 'please' and 'thank you'" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 39|p. 39]]). Its boundary phenomenon is the uncanny valley, "a boundary of recognition and non-recognition between human and inhuman around which anthropomorphization revolves" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 44|p. 44]]). Its dark inflection: "in a mature Agentworld the personification of AI is likely to trigger more precise forms of anthropomorphic animosity" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 45|p. 45]]), and Q14 asks how "the mis-anthropomorphization of agents" has fueled the xenophobic reaction ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 57|p. 57]]). The exit route the brief itself points to: agent-generated vocabulary that "depart[s] from simple anthropomorphism, describing a quite non-human way of thinking," suggesting "paths for human-agent interaction that go beyond the simple masks of today" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 41|p. 41]]); the FROM → TO table's "Chat, voice, anthropomorphism → Agent choreography abstraction layers" and "Teach AI to pretend to think like a human → Teach humans to think like agent swarms" ([[Antikythera Agentworld Brief#Source PDF page 56|p. 56]]).

## In the argument

The concept names the failure mode that [[Agent Subjectivity Must Remain Open]] steers between: the method "neither grants subjectivity by anthropomorphic analogy nor denies it by substrate prejudice." Its zero-sum inflection — the "humanity-of-the-gaps" trap (Bratton §9.5) and its "anthropomorphic animosity" (Bratton §9.6) — is a **dia-ballein pathology**: the seam-cancelling logic that forces identity and externalises the other as encroaching enemy, collapsing L2's four corners to `(+1)/(−1)` (Argued). The seam-retaining alternative is [[Sym-Ballein]] — keep the human/agent difference inside a shared field rather than making it decisive. [[Centaur Societies]] and [[Parasociety]] are the register in which the interface operates.
