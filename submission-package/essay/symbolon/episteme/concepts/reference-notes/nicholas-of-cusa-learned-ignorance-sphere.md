# Nicholas of Cusa — Learned Ignorance, Coincidence, and the Infinite Sphere

> [!summary]
> Cusa makes mathematical limit an instrument of learned ignorance. Polygon and circle, maximum and minimum, centre and circumference become exact images through which finite reason discovers the form of its relation to what it cannot exhaust.

## Attribution correction

“God is an infinite sphere whose centre is everywhere and circumference nowhere” originates as the second definition in the anonymous medieval *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers*. It was widely transmitted and later taken up in related forms by Cusa and other writers. The essay should not present the aphorism as Cusa's coinage.

Cusa's own *On Learned Ignorance* nevertheless gives the image a decisive mathematical and cosmological role. God is absolute maximum beyond comparative measure; the universe is a contracted maximum without a fixed physical centre or containing circumference. Cusa's formula that God is the universe's centre and circumference, everywhere and nowhere, loosens the inherited closed cosmos without turning infinity into one more measurable object.

## Mathematical images

### Inscribed polygon and circle

As the number of sides of an inscribed polygon increases, it approaches the circle while remaining a polygon at every finite stage. The image gives learned ignorance a rigorous form: increasing precision deepens approximation and simultaneously discloses the non-identity between finite measure and its limit.

### Maximum and minimum

At the infinite limit, familiar oppositions can coincide. An infinite circle's curvature tends toward straightness; maximum and minimum cease to behave like ordinary comparative magnitudes. The *coincidentia oppositorum* is not a verbal celebration of contradiction. It is reason following its own operations to the point where the coordinate distinctions that secured finite comparison no longer retain the same meaning.

### Centre everywhere, circumference nowhere

The image places the centre at every determinate point while withholding an enclosing edge. It gives the subject-at-the-limit a spatial figure: every `1` can function as a local centre of disclosure, while no local centre owns the whole circumference or converts the unbounded condition into its object.

The strongest sentence is positive: **finite reason runs true when the growth of its exactness also renders its mode of limitation exact.**

## Claim boundary

Cusa's mathematical examples are speculative mathematics used philosophically, not modern proofs of QL, projective geometry, non-Euclidean cosmology, or physical infinity. Their function is methodological and artistic: they show mathematical form carrying apophatic pressure without surrendering precision.
