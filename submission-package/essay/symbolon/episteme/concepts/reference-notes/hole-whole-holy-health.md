# Hole, Whole, Holy, Health

> [!summary]
> *Whole*, *holy*, *heal*, and *health* belong to the Old English *hāl* family: sound, uninjured, entire. *Hole* derives from a separate hollow-root. The power of the phrase is structural chiasm, not shared etymology.

Dictionary checks: [Merriam-Webster — whole](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/whole), [holy](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/holy), [hole](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/hole).

## Permitted line

> The hole is the false cognate that tells the structural truth: some wholes remain whole only by keeping their opening open.

The torus is the exact mathematical image—a surface whose genus depends on its hole. Do not claim the four English words share one historical root.
