# SEED Language of Spirit Dialogues

> [!summary]
> The Language of Spirit dialogues provide an institutional precedent for shared intelligence across genuinely different knowledge-worlds. Their form joined Bohmian dialogue with Indigenous talking-circle practice while refusing an adversarial contest for one master cosmology.

## Historical sequence

The timeline must remain exact:

1. In 1992, Blackfoot scholar and elder Leroy Little Bear and physicist F. David Peat approached David Bohm with a proposal to bring theoretical physicists, Native American elders, and linguists into deep dialogue about the cosmos.
2. Bohm died in 1992. He did not work inside the later SEED Institute.
3. SEED was founded in 1996 to bridge Indigenous wisdom and Western ways of knowing.
4. SEED began sponsoring the dialogues in 1999.
5. The resulting practice combined elements of a Native talking circle with Bohmian dialogue.

The accurate formulation for the essay is: **a dialogue project Bohm agreed to help initiate with Leroy Little Bear and F. David Peat was later carried forward by SEED.**

## Argumentative function

The dialogues make a collective intelligence claim without presuming commensurability. Indigenous and Western scientific participants do not enter as interchangeable data sources. Each brings a world of practice, history, ontology, language, authority, and relation to place. The shared field is produced through respectful circulation rather than by assigning one vocabulary the right to translate all others.

This supplies a concrete antecedent for the Agentworld problem of “same decision-procedural grammar, sufficiently divergent world models.” The common procedure is not a common ontology. Participants share the disciplines of turn, listening, suspension, response, memory, and return while retaining the difference between their epistemic grounds.

## QL/MEF and mono–poly reading

- **Dia-ballein** differentiates real disagreements, incompatible assumptions, and distinct authorities.
- **Sym-ballein** keeps the dialogue, circle, place, and shared question present as the field through which those differences can generate meaning.
- **MEF** makes lenses, source traditions, warrants, translations, and untranslatable remainder explicit.
- **Mono–poly** names one dialogical commons carried through many locally sovereign knowledge-worlds.
- **Compassion as sensitivity to origins** prevents a statement from being detached from the land, community, ceremony, wound, or purpose through which it speaks.

The social topology is toroidal in the limited operative sense: speech circulates through a common field and returns to speakers changed by the path, while no single seat becomes the permanent centre. This is an **Argued** institutional image, not a claim that talking circles instantiate a topological torus.

## Agentic use

The dialogue lineage can ground an Epi-Logos interaction protocol for humans and artificial agents:

- disclose the world-model and evidential tradition from which a contribution arises;
- preserve local terms where translation would destroy their operative force;
- circulate a claim through distinct lenses before synthesising it;
- return the synthesis to the participants and affected contexts for correction;
- keep the shared Bimba map inspectable without letting it appropriate local interiority or sovereignty.

This is the spirit in which a cross-civilisational language of intelligence should be built. East and West, Indigenous and scientific, human and artificial are not input categories for a machine-readable perennialism. They are participants whose differences must remain capable of changing the shared field.
