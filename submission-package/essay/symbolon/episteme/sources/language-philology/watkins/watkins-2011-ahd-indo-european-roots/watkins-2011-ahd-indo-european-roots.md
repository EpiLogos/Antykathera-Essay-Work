## Source scholarship

A reader's dictionary of Indo-European roots for English etymology, derived from the appendix of the *American Heritage Dictionary*. Entries give a root, its meaning, derivatives by branch and a Pokorny reference. It states reconstructions without the hedging of a specialist Latin dictionary; it is the standard English-language route to the root *skei-*.

## Passage card — `watkins-2011-ahd-indo-european-roots-q001` — skei-: to cut, split; scire and scindere among the descendants

**Paraphrase:** The entry *skei-*, glossed “To cut, split” and described as an extension of *sek-*, lists among its descendants (a) under the Latin verb *scire*, “to know,” explained as originally “to separate one thing from another,” hence “discern,” English *science*, *conscience*, *conscious*, *nescience*, *omniscient*, *prescient*, *plebiscite*, *scilicet*, *sciolism*; (b) under the nasalized zero-grade form, Latin *scindere*, “to split,” English *scission*, *rescind*, *exscind*, *prescind*; (c) under a zero-grade suffixed form, Greek *skhizein*, “to split,” English *schism*, *schist*, *schizo-*. A Pokorny reference follows. The entry presents the *scire* link without a hedge.
