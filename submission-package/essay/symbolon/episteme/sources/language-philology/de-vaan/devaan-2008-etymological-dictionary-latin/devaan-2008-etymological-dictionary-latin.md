## Source scholarship

A specialist historical-comparative dictionary of Latin (and Italic) etymology; each entry gives the Proto-Italic form, the PIE reconstruction, cognates, discussion and bibliography. Its reconstructions are specialist and revisable; entries are often explicitly hedged. It is the fitting corrective to the older comparisons recorded in Lewis and Short.

## Passage card — `devaan-2008-etymological-dictionary-latin-q001` — scio: back-formation, root meaning "to incise"

**Paraphrase:** The entry for *scio*, *scire* “to know” lists as derivatives *scientia*, *nescire*, *conscius*, *consciscere* and others; reconstructs Proto-Italic *skije/o-* from a PIE root meaning “to incise,” with Sanskrit and Greek cognates for cutting; and is explicitly guarded: *scio* is probably a back-formation on *nescire*; if “to know” developed from “to distinguish, make a decision,” *-scire* can belong to a root meaning “to cut off, incise”; the *LIV* derivation of *-scio* from *\*sekH-* is reported, together with the argument that a simplex would not have given Latin *-scire*. The entry does not reconstruct the root as Watkins's *skei-*.

- **Locator:** p. 545, s.v. *scio, scire*, from the derivatives list through the closing bibliography (page confirmed against the book's own index entry “scio scire 545” in the scan).
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only.
- **Verification:** read 2026-10-04 in the Internet Archive scan (OCR text layer; the PIE notation is garbled in the OCR and is deliberately not reproduced here). Reading: the entry's own hedges (“probably,” “if … developed”) are preserved. No quotation promoted.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased.
- **Evidential action:** qualifies the claim that *scire* “lies behind” the root *skei-*: the connection is probable, not settled, in the specialist dictionary.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-scire.

## Passage card — `devaan-2008-etymological-dictionary-latin-q002` — scindo: nasal present of a root meaning "to split"

**Paraphrase:** The entry for *scindo*, *-ere* “to split, cleave” (derivatives include *discidium*, *rescindere*, *scindula*) reconstructs a nasal present, PIE *\*ski-n-d-* “to split, tear off,” with Sanskrit *chindhi*, Greek *skhizō* and Baltic cognates, and discusses the absence of Lachmann's Law in the participle. The entry for *scūtum* (p. 548) remarks that a root *\*ski-* might be an unenlarged variant of the *\*skid-* “to split” found in *scindo*.

- **Locator:** p. 544, s.v. *scindo, -ere* (page confirmed against the scan's index entry “scindo -ere 544”); cf. p. 548, s.v. *scūtum*, for the *\*ski-* variant remark (page from the scan's index entry “scutum 548”).
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only.
- **Verification:** read 2026-10-04 in the Internet Archive scan (OCR; notation not reproduced). No quotation promoted.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased.
- **Evidential action:** supports *scindere* “to split” and the relation of the verb to a splitting root; de Vaan does not unite it with *scio* under one root without qualification.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-scire.
