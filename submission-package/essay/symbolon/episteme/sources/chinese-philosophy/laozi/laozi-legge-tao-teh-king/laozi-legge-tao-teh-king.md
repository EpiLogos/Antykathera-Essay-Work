# Laozi — Tao Teh King, translated by James Legge

The selected English carrier is Project Gutenberg ebook 216, *The Tao Teh King, or The Tao and Its Characteristics*, attributed to Lao-Tse and translated by James Legge. Its header records release 12 July 2008 and update 4 February 2013. The underlying print impression is not identified here. Cite this electronic translation and chapter; no modern Chinese critical edition or variant collation is claimed.

## Chapter 25

**Paraphrase:** Tentative designation follows the unnamed. The four greats include the king; the sequence returns human law through earth and heaven to Tao's own character.

**Locator:** 25.1–4.

**Verification:** complete chapter read; paraphrase-ready. King/human variants require separate Chinese collation.

## Chapter 28

**Paraphrase:** Paired differences return to simplicity; material divided into vessels is placed beside governmental office and nonviolent rule.

**Locator:** 28.1–2, including all three verse stanzas and closing prose.

**Verification:** complete chapter read; paraphrase-ready. Legge's nonviolence does not certify the protected history's stronger non-severing gloss.

## Chapter 32

**Paraphrase:** Nameless simplicity passes into named action; rest limits error, and streams relate to rivers and seas.

**Locator:** 32.1–5.

**Verification:** complete chapter read; paraphrase-ready. Chang's reception, alternative translations and Chinese wording remain independently collatable; QL alignment belongs to Taylor.

Re-read 2026-10-04 (§0/1 M02): 25.1–2 (something undefined and complete before Heaven and Earth; "I do not know its name, and I give it the designation of the Tao ... Making an effort (further) to give it a name I call it The Great"); 28.2 ("The unwrought material, when divided and distributed, forms vessels"); 32.1 and 32.4 ("The Tao, considered as unchanging, has no name"; "As soon as it proceeds to action, it has a name"). Legge's "unwrought material" renders *pu*, commonly "uncarved block"; the Chinese is not collated. The Gutenberg text is Legge's 1891 translation as digitised; its title line reads "THE TAO TEH KING, OR THE TAO AND ITS CHARACTERISTICS ... Translated by James Legge."
