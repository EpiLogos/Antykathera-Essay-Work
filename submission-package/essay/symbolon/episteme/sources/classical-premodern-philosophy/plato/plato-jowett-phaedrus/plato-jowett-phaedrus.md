## q001 — Two kinds and four divine forms

**Paraphrase:** Socrates distinguishes illness from divine release, then names prophetic, initiatory, poetic and erotic madness.

- **Locator:** recap following the two speeches, web paragraphs beginning “And of madness there were two kinds” and “The divine madness was subdivided”.
- **Status:** paraphrase-only.

## q003 — Stephanus locators for the fourfold madness and its recapitulation

**Paraphrase:** Socrates opens the palinode by saying the earlier speech was Phaedrus's and that his own would be Stesichorus's, and that madness is not simply an evil but the channel of the greatest goods when divinely given. He names prophecy first, with Delphi and Dodona, then relief from inherited affliction through prayer, purification and rites (*katharmoi*, *teletai*), then the Muses' possession in poetry; he announces love as the further kind. At 265a–b he recapitulates the two kinds of madness and the four divine kinds, with their four gods.

- **Locator:** palinode opens 244a; prophecy 244a–d; purification and rites 244d–e; poetry 245a; love announced 245b–c and developed from 245c on; recapitulation 265a (two kinds) and 265b (four kinds: Apollo, Dionysus, the Muses, Aphrodite and Eros). Stephanus numbers per Burnet's Greek (Perseus).
- **Jowett wording for orientation (web text, no numbers):** “there is also a madness which is a divine gift, and the source of the chiefest blessings granted to men”; “The divine madness was subdivided into four kinds, prophetic, initiatory, poetic, erotic.”
- **Status:** paraphrase-only for the essay; locators `locator-verified` against the Greek.
- **Verification:** Perseus Burnet text for each section read 2026-10-04 (g5a): 244a “νῦν δὲ τὰ μέγιστα τῶν ἀγαθῶν ἡμῖν γίγνεται διὰ μανίας”; 244d–e καθαρμῶν τε καὶ τελετῶν; 245a Muses' possession; 265b τέτταρα μέρη … μαντικὴν … τελεστικήν … ποιητικήν … ἐρωτικὴν μανίαν.
- **Correction recorded:** the former locator “244a–245a” for “the four divine madnesses and the palinode” covered only three of the four kinds; the fourth is announced at 245b–c, so the span for the fourfold list is 244a–245c.
- **Consumers:** current M04, `s01-phaedrus`. The prose's “Delphi … initiatory purification, poetry and the madness of love” matches these loci; “what he first condemned as madness becomes something through which a person may receive more than established understanding” matches the retraction at 244a and the claim that the greatest goods come through divinely given madness.

## Stephanus check — 2026-10-04 (§0/1 M02)

The Perseus Digital Library's Phaedrus (Fowler translation, with Stephanus section links) was opened: at 265a–b Socrates distinguishes madness from human infirmity from a divine release from customary habits and then names its four kinds (prophetic, mystic or initiatory, poetic, erotic), and 244a–245a covers the prophetic, purificatory/initiatory and poetic inspiration that Socrates praises in his second speech. The conventional Stephanus cross-references 244a–245a and 265a–b used in the M02 note are therefore confirmed as the sections that correspond to the Jowett web paragraphs this house records (the recap "And of madness there were two kinds ..." is 265a–b). Stephanus numbers are edition-independent; the Jowett web object remains the printed-text witness.
