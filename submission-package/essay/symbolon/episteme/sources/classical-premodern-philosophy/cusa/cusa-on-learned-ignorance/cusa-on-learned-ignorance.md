## Essay use

Primary source for learned ignorance, mathematical limit-images, maximum/minimum, polygon/circle approximation, contracted infinity, and coincidence of opposites.

**Attribution control:** “God is an infinite sphere whose centre is everywhere and circumference nowhere” derives from the anonymous *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers* and has a transmission history. Attribute Cusa's use and transformation of the image separately from its coinage; create the source text as a separate record when an edition is selected.

**Claim boundary:** Cusa's mathematical images are rigorous speculative instruments within learned ignorance. Modern claims in projective geometry, cosmology, or QL require their own proofs and sources.

## Passage card — `cusa-on-learned-ignorance-q003` — intellect and truth as polygon and circle
^cusa-on-learned-ignorance-q003

> For the intellect is to truth as [an inscribed] polygon is to [the inscribing] circle. The more angles the inscribed polygon has the more similar it is to the circle. However, even if the number of its angles is increased ad infinitum, the polygon never becomes equal [to the circle] unless it is resolved into an identity with the circle.

- **Locator:** Book I, ch. 3, margin no. 10, printed p. 8 (the sentence beginning “Therefore, it is not the case that by means of likenesses a finite intellect can precisely attain the truth about things” is margin no. 10; the polygon sentences fall in its second half, carrying Hopkins's note 19, which records that the inscribed-polygon example recurs in III, 1 and III, 4). Margin no. 9 (the finite/infinite sentence, q002) runs from p. 7 onto p. 8.
- **Status:** quotation-ready.
- **Verification:** Agent G5b, 2026-10-04; the University of Minnesota-hosted Hopkins Book I PDF (Internet Archive capture of 2006-02-10), printed p. 8 and margin number 10 read from the page layout; wording matches the essay's quotation word for word, including both bracketed insertions, which are Hopkins's.
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports.
- **Argument function:** limit-image — approximation by added sides never reaches identity; only a resolution into identity (a change in kind) does.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-cusa-polygon`.
- **Use boundary:** licenses Cusa's polygon/circle image for the intellect and truth. The comparison with the strengthening of formal theories under Gödel and with the identification of a square's edges into a torus is the essay's own.

## Passage card — `cusa-on-learned-ignorance-m02-q001` — the world-machine's centre everywhere, circumference nowhere (Book II, chap. 12, n. 162; chap. 11, nn. 156–157)

Paraphrase: the world-machine has its centre everywhere and its circumference nowhere, so to speak, because God, who is everywhere and nowhere, is its circumference and centre; the earth is not the fixed centre of the world, nor the sphere of the fixed stars its circumference, because with regard to motion there is no unqualified minimum, so that the centre of the world coincides with the circumference.

- **Locator:** *De docta ignorantia* II.12, margin number 162 (the sentence about the world-machine's centre and circumference); II.11, nn. 156–157 (the world has no fixed centre; the earth and spheres have no true centre; God as the centre of all and the infinite circumference). Page numbers: in Hopkins's web edition of Book II (2000), nn. 156–157 fall on pp. 89–90 and n. 162 on p. 93; those pages belong to that web edition and are **not** asserted for the 1990 Banning printing cited in the essay.
- **Status:** locator-verified (chapter and margin number); paraphrase-only. This corrects the earlier working note, which cited the passage as bk. 2, chap. 12 without margin number: the earth/centre argument is in chap. 11 and the "everywhere and nowhere" sentence is in chap. 12.
- **Verification:** agent s01-lock M02, 2026-10-04; Hopkins's Book II text (web edition, University of Minnesota, preserved at the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine, `cla.umn.edu/jhopkins/DI-II-12-2000.pdf`, snapshot 2006) read with margin numbers, running heads and Hopkins's note 131, which refers to the *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers*, proposition II, and to Harries's article on the infinite sphere.
- **Source relation:** paraphrased; Cusa receives and transforms the sphere image.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M02, marker `s01-cusa`.
- **Use boundary:** supports the statement that Cusa applies the image to the world and denies the earth a fixed centre. The reading through `0/1` is the author's.
