## Essay use

### Source's relevant contribution

- A sustained philosophical reading of Eckhart's Latin and German works, including the argument of the *Opus tripartitum*, the Parisian questions, the Johannine commentary, the German sermons, and the *Book of Divine Consolation*.
- A treatment of Eckhart's primary determinations, analogy and qualified predication, apophatic movement, the birth/ground material, and the metaphysics of the *verbum*.
- A historically specific account of why Eckhart's theological language functions as philosophical argument rather than as a portable, decontextualised spirituality.

### Working content map

- Chapters 6–11 follow the Latin project: its prologues, Parisian debates, programmatic speeches, Genesis, *In Sapientiam*, and *In Exodum*.
- Chapter 13 (pp. 166–76) treats the Johannine commentary's method and its metaphysics of the *verbum*; chapter 14 continues the commentary's account of unity and likeness.
- Chapters 15–17 return to the German sermons and the *Book of Divine Consolation*, including primary determinations, incarnation, and positive/negative theology.
- The prior Eckhart note's leads—predicate and reversal, *in quantum* / “insofar as,” Word, image, ground, desert, and silence—belong in this book's content map. They await page-level extraction here; their previous omission from the record described a gap in the bank, not an absence from Flasch.
