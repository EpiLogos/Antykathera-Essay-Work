# Leibniz — *Essais de Théodicée* (Amsterdam, 1710)

## Bibliographic identity

*Essais de Théodicée sur la bonté de Dieu, la liberté de l'homme et l'origine du mal*, published anonymously at Amsterdam by Isaac Troyel in 1710; Leibniz acknowledged authorship in the second edition (1712) (Feeney, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, “Leibniz on the Problem of Evil”, first published 12 January 2026, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/leibniz-evil/, read 2026-10-04, g5a). The modern critical text is C. J. Gerhardt (ed.), *Die philosophischen Schriften von G. W. Leibniz* (Berlin: Weidmann, 1875–90), vol. 6, from which the standard English translation was made. The selected English translation is E. M. Huggard, *Theodicy: Essays on the Goodness of God, the Freedom of Man, and the Origin of Evil*, ed. with an introduction by Austin Farrer (first published London: Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1951; Open Court, La Salle, Illinois, 1985); the Gutenberg eBook 17147 reproduces the Open Court text (title page and imprint read 2026-10-04). A Gerhardt page or Huggard page for the coinage is not given because Leibniz's own statements of the coinage are outside the Essais proper (see below).

## Source scholarship

The coinage claim is secured from Feeney (SEP, 2026): “theodicy” was Leibniz's own coinage from Greek *theos* (God) and *dikē* (justice), meaning “the doctrine of the right and justice of God” (Gerhardt II.428, cited there from Lodge 2020, 173), and he had used the term in French and Latin forms in the mid-1690s, in letters and fragments (Feeney citing Antognazza 2009, 480–81). The OED entry was not used; SEP is the independent lawful source.
