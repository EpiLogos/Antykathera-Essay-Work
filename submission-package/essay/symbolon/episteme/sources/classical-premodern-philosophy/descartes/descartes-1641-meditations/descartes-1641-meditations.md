## Source scholarship

The *Meditations* establish the doubting context (Meditation I: the senses deceived, the dream argument, the deceiving deceiver), reach the indubitable occurrence at the threshold of Meditation II (in thinking, doubting or being deceived, there is presence; the "let him deceive me as much as he can" movement), and then perform the move this house exists to index: from the occurrence to the thinking-thing — *res cogitans* — "a thing that doubts, understands, affirms, denies, is willing, is unwilling, and also imagines and has sensory perceptions." The essay's reading, already argued on its own ground, receives this double movement exactly: the finding is heard correctly; the inference gathers the I into the act too quickly, and the essay's crossed zero names what that gathering did — the slash treated as the I, the zero behind it covered over, `Ø` at position #1 of the recognition-matheme (Taylor, core theorems §IX; see the [core-theorems house](../../../internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy.md) for the fuller accounting). **Hume sits at the same position** (finding only the impressions the slash produced and calling the zero empty); Descartes and Hume differ over which side of `Ø` they fixate on.

This summary is orientation, not quotation; the wording of every named site is fixed only at extraction from the edition in hand.

## Passage card — `descartes-1641-meditations-q004` — not a sailor in a ship (Meditation VI)
^descartes-1641-meditations-q004

> Latin (AT VII 81): “Docet etiam natura per istos sensus doloris, famis, sitis, etc. me non tantum adesse meo corpori ut nauta adest navigio, sed illi arctissime esse conjunctum, et quasi permixtum, adeo ut unum quid cum illo componam; alioqui enim cum corpus laeditur, ego, qui nihil aliud sum quam res cogitans, non sentirem idcirco dolorem, sed puro intellectu laesionem istam perciperem, ut nauta visu percipit, si quid in nave frangatur”

*(English, CSM II: not transcribed, the translation is in copyright. In substance: nature teaches by the sensations of pain, hunger, thirst and so on that I am not merely present in my body as a sailor is present in a ship, but am very closely joined and, as it were, intermingled with it, so that I and the body form a unit; otherwise, being only a thinking thing, I should not feel pain when the body is hurt, but should perceive the damage by the pure intellect, as a sailor sees by sight if anything in his ship is broken.)*

- **Locator:** Meditation VI, AT VII 81 / CSM II 56. AT VII 81 is confirmed by two independent scholarly citations of the same clauses (“as a sailor is present in a ship,” “form a unit,” the confused modes of thinking arising from the union), and CSM II 56 by a citation giving AT VII 81 / CSM II 56 for the neighbouring sentences of the same passage; neighbouring pairs AT VII 83 / CSM II 57 and AT VII 76 / CSM II 52 in the same literature are consistent with it. The Latin wording is from the Project Gutenberg Latin text (ebook 23306), where the passage falls within the Sixth Meditation.
- **Status:** locator-verified (AT VII 81 / CSM II 56); Latin wording quotation-ready from the public-domain Gutenberg text; the CSM English was not transcribed from a copy.
- **Verification:** Agent G5b, 2026-10-04; Latin passage read in Gutenberg #23306; locators from secondary scholarship (J. Gottlieb and S. Parvizian, “Cartesian Imperativism,” citing AT VII 81 / CSM II 56; a Roczniki Filozoficzne article citing AT 7:81 for the sailor clause); CSM II pagination not collated against a physical copy.
- **Source relation:** extracted (Latin); paraphrased (English).
- **Evidential action:** supports (lived union; the sailor model of a pilot who perceives damage by the intellect alone is rejected).
- **Argument function:** the pain, hunger and thirst argument against a merely external presence of the mind in the body.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-descartes-sailor`.
- **Use boundary:** licenses the Sixth Meditation claim of a close union as taught by sensation; the essay's sentence that Descartes “insisted he was not in his body as a sailor is in a ship, observing its damage from outside” is within the passage (the sailor sees damage to his ship; the thinking thing alone would perceive it by intellect). The essay's larger claim about the later inheritance of his distinction is its own.

## Locator corroboration — 2026-10-04 (§0/1 M02)

No copy of CSM II was opened. The locators for the Second Meditation were checked against a scholarly secondary source that quotes the CSM text with both pagination systems: G. J. Mattey, "Lectures on Meditation Two" (UC Davis, Philosophy 168, 2011), which cites the supposition of a deceiver and the proposition "I am, I exist" at AT VII 25 / CSM II 17; the question what the "I" is, and the unresolved identity of the "I" with things supposed to be nothing, at AT VII 27 / CSM II 18; the claim that knowledge of the "I" does not depend on what is invented in imagination at AT VII 27–28 / CSM II 18–19; and the turn from the imagination at AT VII 28 / CSM II 19. The list of the thinking thing's activities (doubts, understands, affirms, denies, wills, refuses, imagines, senses) immediately precedes AT VII 29 / CSM II 19 and is cited as AT VII 28 / CSM II 19. These support the existing q001 and q002 locators (AT VII 25 / CSM II 16–17; AT VII 27–28 / CSM II 18–19) without raising their status: both stay "locator recorded; unextracted" until collated against the CSM copy.

The Third Meditation passage in which God is shown not to be a deceiver and the Sixth Meditation passage that reasons from that to the existence of bodies were not located by page; the essay's note therefore names the Meditations only.
