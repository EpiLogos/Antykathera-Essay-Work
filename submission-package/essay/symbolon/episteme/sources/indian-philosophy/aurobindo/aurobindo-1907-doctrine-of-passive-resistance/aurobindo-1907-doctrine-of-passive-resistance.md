### aurobindo-1907-doctrine-of-passive-resistance-p001 — The programme: boycott, Swadeshi, national education, arbitration courts

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition status: volume-and-page-open; URL https://sri-aurobindo.co.in/workings/sa/01/0024_e.htm; accessed 2026-10-04.

**Locator:** Part 4, "Its Methods," April 17, 1907; parts 3 and 5 (April 13 and 18–19).

**Located operation:** Part 4 states the programme the M05 sentence describes: refusal to buy foreign and especially British goods; refusal to send boys to government schools and the need for schools of their own giving a thorough national education; refusal to resort to the alien courts and the need for arbitration courts of their own; and no industrial boycott without Swadeshi and the expansion of indigenous industries. Every element of the essay's sentence is supported. Nothing in these parts speaks of compassion toward the British (see the Karmayogin text carded in the M05 return).

**Status:** paraphrase-only

**Source relation:** Paraphrased.

## Open acquisition and verification

Confirm the CWSA volume and page numbers in the printed volumes or at incarnateword.in (the site served only a navigation shell to the research pass). On the claim that Aurobindo spoke of extending compassion to the British: not found. The closest primary text is "An Open Letter to My Countrymen," *Karmayogin*, July 31, 1909 (CWSA vol. 8), which says a struggle for rights does not involve hatred of those who mistakenly deny them and that the ideal of Swaraj involves no hatred of any nation or of the administration; the Uttarpara speech (May 30, 1909) sees Vasudeva in the magistrate and Srikrishna in the prosecutor. The 15 August 1947 message and 1940s statements were not read.
