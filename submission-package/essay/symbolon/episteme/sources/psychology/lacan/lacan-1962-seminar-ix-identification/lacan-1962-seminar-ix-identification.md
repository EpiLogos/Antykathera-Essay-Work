## Source scholarship

From session 12 (7 March 1962) Lacan introduces the torus as a surface for the subject. Full circuits around the body of the torus stand for the repetition of demand; the empty circle around the central hole is related to the object, not to desire itself; the later sessions superimpose two tori to depict an inversion of demand and desire in the neurotic.

## Passage card — `lacan-1962-seminar-ix-identification-q001` — the torus introduced; full and empty circles

> Paraphrase only: the torus is introduced as a surface for the subject. Lacan separates two kinds of irreducible circuit, the full circles that repeat around the body of the torus and the empty circle that rings the central hole, and says the second relates to the object and is not itself desire.

- **Locator:** session 12, 7 March 1962 (Gallagher typescript, XII 119–23; the two kinds of circle at XII 123).
- **Status:** locator-verified.
- **Verification:** read in the typescript text layer, 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** paraphrased.
- **Evidential action:** supports and qualifies the essay’s sentence (see Use boundary).
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker s01-lacan-identification.
- **Use boundary:** supports the two-circuit use of the torus. It does not say that the empty circle is desire; Lacan says it stands for the object that is opposed to desire. The essay’s phrase “the desire traced through their repetition” is the author’s gloss.

## Passage card — `lacan-1962-seminar-ix-identification-q002` — demand and desire on two tori
