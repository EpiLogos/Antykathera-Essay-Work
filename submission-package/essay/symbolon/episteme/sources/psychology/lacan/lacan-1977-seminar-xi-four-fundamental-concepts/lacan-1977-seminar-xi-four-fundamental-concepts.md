## Source scholarship

In these chapters Lacan rereads Freud’s four components of the drive (pressure, source, object, aim), separates the aim from the goal, and describes satisfaction as the completion of the drive’s circuit around its object, out of and back to the erogenous zone.

## Passage card — `lacan-1977-seminar-xi-four-fundamental-concepts-q001` — drive, aim, goal and circuit

> Paraphrase only: the drive does not reach a goal but follows its aim, which is to circle the object and return; satisfaction lies in that circuit.

- **Locator:** chap. 13, “The Deconstruction of the Drive,” and chap. 14, “The Partial Drive and Its Circuit” (chap. 14 begins at p. 174).
- **Status:** source-matched (chapter titles and numbers agree across two search-seen contents lists, the Routledge record and a TOC relayed by search; the start page 174 is single-witness; no wording seen).
- **Verification:** Routledge contents list for the Hogarth edition (read 2026-10-04) gives the titles; a second contents list seen through search gives chap. 14 = “The Partial Drive and Its Circuit,” p. 174, chap. 16 = “The Subject and the Other: Alienation,” p. 203, chap. 17 = “…Aphanisis,” p. 216. This corrects the prior working note that placed the partial-drive chapter at 13: chap. 13 is “The Deconstruction of the Drive.”
- **Source relation:** paraphrased.
- **Evidential action:** supports the circuit and its satisfaction; the word *jouissance* is the essay’s (and later Lacanian) gloss on satisfaction and needs a text check in these chapters.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker s01-lacan-drive.
