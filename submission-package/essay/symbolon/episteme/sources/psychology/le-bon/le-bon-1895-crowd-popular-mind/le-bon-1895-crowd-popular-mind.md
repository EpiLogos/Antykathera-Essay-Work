# Le Bon — The Crowd: A Study of the Popular Mind (1895)

## Essay use

Le Bon supplies a historically situated early account of the psychological crowd as an emergent collective formation rather than a numerical aggregate. It supports the narrow claim that a crowd can organise perception, feeling, and action differently from its isolated participants.

**Claim boundary:** his racialised evolutionary vocabulary and generalising psychology are not adopted. He does not supply QL, a theory of institutions, or a warrant for treating every political collectivity as pathological.

## Verified passages

- Book I, ch. 1: a psychological crowd makes its participants "feel, think, and act" differently than in isolation. The formation is a combination that produces new characteristics, not an average of its members.
- Book I, ch. 1: an entire nation may become a crowd "under the action of certain influences," even without visible agglomeration.

The complete passage sequence—distributed formation, collective transformation, and emergent combination—is in [Quotes — Le Bon, The Crowd (1895)](#passages).

## Passage sequence: a crowd is a formation, not an aggregate

<a id="le-bon-1895-crowd-popular-mind-lb-01"></a>
### LB-01 — No physical agglomeration is required

**Locator:** book I, ch. 1.

> “an entire nation ... may become a crowd”

Le Bon's point is formally important: psychological crowd formation is not reducible to people being physically gathered. Shared passions and influences can produce the formation at a distance.

**Use in the essay:** Dia-ballein can operate through media, institution, and distributed narrative; it does not require a literal mob.

<a id="le-bon-1895-crowd-popular-mind-lb-02"></a>
### LB-02 — Collective transformation

**Locator:** book I, ch. 1.

> “feel, think, and act in a manner quite different”

Le Bon describes a psychological crowd as a temporary collective form in which its members are transformed relative to isolation. His model is not a sociology of all collective life; it is a diagnostic account of a specific collective psychic condition.

**Use in the essay:** The line establishes the phenomenological shift that Desmet later tracks as narrative fixation. It does not make the crowd into an ontological person.

<a id="le-bon-1895-crowd-popular-mind-lb-03"></a>
### LB-03 — Combination, not summation

**Locator:** book I, ch. 1.

> “a combination followed by the creation of new characteristics”

Le Bon rejects the idea that a crowd is simply the average or sum of its constituents. This is the decisive structural resource: collective formation has emergent effects, but those effects remain formed through its constituent relations.

**Use in the essay:** The political field can be emergent without becoming a sovereign Whole. Sym-ballein therefore does not dissolve persons into collective identity; it restores the relations through which a collective may become accountable.

<a id="le-bon-1895-crowd-popular-mind-lb-04"></a>
### LB-04 — Prestige as domination of the critical faculty

**Locator:** book II, ch. 3, §3 “Prestige” (the third numbered section of the chapter; the chapter's sections are 1 The Leaders of Crowds, 2 The Means of Action of the Leaders: Affirmation, Repetition, Contagion, 3 Prestige).

> “Prestige in reality is a sort of domination exercised on our mind by an individual, a work, or an idea. This domination entirely paralyses our critical faculty, and fills our soul with astonishment and respect.”

Status: quotation-ready (Project Gutenberg eBook 445, text read 2026-10-04). Source relation: extracted. Evidential action: supports.

**Use in the essay (M04, marker `s01-le-bon`):** warrants the sentence that prestige lends a person, title or idea the force to command assent before its claims are examined. Le Bon names an individual, a work or an idea; acquired prestige attaches to name, fortune and titles (“A soldier in uniform, a judge in his robes, always enjoys prestige”).

<a id="le-bon-1895-crowd-popular-mind-lb-05"></a>
### LB-05 — Prestige ends when it is called in question

**Locator:** book II, ch. 3, §3 “Prestige,” near the end of the section.

> “From the moment prestige is called in question it ceases to be prestige.”

Status: quotation-ready. Source relation: extracted. Evidential action: supports and qualifies (prestige works only while unexamined; it is also lost with failure: “Prestige lost by want of success disappears in a brief space of time”).

**Use in the essay:** the limiting passage paired with LB-04, which confirms that the force of prestige lies in assent given before examination.

<a id="le-bon-1895-crowd-popular-mind-lb-06"></a>
### LB-06 — Affirmation, repetition, contagion

**Locator:** book II, ch. 3, §2 “The Means of Action of the Leaders: Affirmation, Repetition, Contagion.”

> “Affirmation pure and simple, kept free of all reasoning and all proof, is one of the surest means of making an idea enter the mind of crowds.”

> “The thing affirmed comes by repetition to fix itself in the mind in such a way that it is accepted in the end as a demonstrated truth.”

Status: quotation-ready. Source relation: extracted. Evidential action: supports.

**Use in the essay:** Le Bon names three means (affirmation, repetition, contagion; “suggestion” is his word for the crowd's receptivity, e.g. “rapid suggestion” in the same section). The essay's sentence about repetition and suggestion as the method and magic of media and propaganda is the author's; Le Bon's own examples are advertising (“the astonishing power of advertisements”), political cause-defending and religious and legal texts.

## Argument boundary

Le Bon's racial hierarchy, evolutionary language, and sweeping generalisations are excluded. The essay uses only the narrow structural insight that a collective formation can reorganise its members' thought and action.
