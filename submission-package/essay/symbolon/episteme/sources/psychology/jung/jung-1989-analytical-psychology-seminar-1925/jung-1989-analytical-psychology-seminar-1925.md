### jung-1989-analytical-psychology-seminar-1925-p001 — Elijah and Salome as Logos and Eros (Lecture 11, 1 June 1925)

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition detail: Bollingen Series XCIX; text edited from the multigraphed transcript prepared by Gary F. de Angulo (privately issued 1926). The consulted copy is a later Princeton reprint of the 1989 text, whose copyright page reads 1989.; local copy recorded in this house; accessed 2026-10-04.

**Locator:** Lecture 11 (1 June 1925), p. 89; diagram of Elijah (Rational/Thinking), Salome (Feeling), the snake and the inferior and superior irrational functions, p. 90.

**Located operation:** Jung describes Salome as an anima figure and Elijah as the personification of the cognitional element, Salome of the erotic; says one could speak of the two as personifications of Logos and Eros "very specifically shaped"; and adds that, Logos and Eros being speculative and irrational terms, it is better to leave the figures as events and experiences. The black serpent between them is the counterpart of the hero (p. 89).

### jung-1989-analytical-psychology-seminar-1925-p002 — Elijah the wise old man (Lecture 12, 8 June 1925)

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition detail: Bollingen Series XCIX; text edited from the multigraphed transcript prepared by Gary F. de Angulo (privately issued 1926). The consulted copy is a later Princeton reprint of the 1989 text, whose copyright page reads 1989.; local copy recorded in this house; accessed 2026-10-04.

**Locator:** Lecture 12 (8 June 1925), pp. 92–94.

**Located operation:** Salome is the anima and Elijah the wise old man; the prophet is compensatory to the blind anima (p. 92). Elijah and Salome are together as a pair of opposites; Elijah is the man of prestige, wise man and "mana man," who touches archetypes (p. 93). Nothing in lectures 11–12 mentions Prometheus; the only Prometheus in the body text of the seminar is the editor's footnote on Carl Spitteler (lecture 2, p. 13, n. 3), which names his epic *Prometheus and Epimetheus* and Jung's treatment of it in *Psychological Types*. The seminar therefore supports the Elijah/Logos side of the chain only, not Prometheus.
