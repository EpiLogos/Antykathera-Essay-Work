## Source scholarship

“On the Psychology of the Unconscious” (1943 version, from essays of 1912 and 1917) compares Freud’s psychology of Eros (ch. II) with Adler’s psychology of power (ch. III), introduces the attitude types (ch. IV) and moves to the personal and collective unconscious (ch. V). Two passages bear on the essay’s two logics. In ¶78 Jung derives psychic energy from the tension of opposites and names the psychological opposite of Eros as the will to power. In ¶¶111–12 he gives Heraclitus’s *enantiodromia* as the regulative function of opposites and names the only escape from it: separation from the unconscious by placing it before oneself “as that which he is not.” In ¶113 enantiodromia is “being torn asunder into pairs of opposites,” with the image of Zagreus, the dismembered Dionysus. Jung speaks as a psychologist of the conscious attitude and its compensation; he does not formulate the essay’s dia-ballein and syn-ballein.

Paired passages: ¶78 supports the claim that the opposites belong to one energy; ¶¶111–12 qualify it, since a one-sided attitude does not reach its opposite as a relation but swings into it, and the swing offers “no solution.”
