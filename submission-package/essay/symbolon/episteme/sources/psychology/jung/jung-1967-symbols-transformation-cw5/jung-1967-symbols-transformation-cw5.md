## Source scholarship

Part Two, chapter "The Origin of the Hero" closes its discussion of faith, myth and symbol with ¶¶342–345: religious myth as one of humanity's greatest achievements, psychologically true as the bridge to what is best in humanity (¶343); psychology holds aloof from metaphysical assertion yet treats the psyche and its contents as real because they work; there is no Archimedean point outside the psyche from which to observe it, since all knowledge of the psyche is itself psychic, yet the soul is the only experient of life and existence; symbols are grounded in the unconscious archetype, shaped by conscious ideas, and act as transformers of libido (¶344); legitimate faith rests on experience, and tradition alone risks inertia (¶345).
