### jung-1975-letters-vol2-1951-1961-p001 — The January 1960 *Listener* letter is not printed; its two sources are (pp. 524-526)

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition detail: Selected and edited by Gerhard Adler in collaboration with Aniela Jaffé, translations from the German by R. F. C. Hull. Princeton University Press (Bollingen Series XCV:2), 1975. The consulted text is the Routledge & Kegan Paul edition (London, 1976; reprinted by Routledge, 1990, ISBN 978-0-415-05171-2; digital printing 2011), whose pagination matches the Princeton citation of the Brooke letter (p. 521) and the index entry for The Listener (p. 524 n.).; local copy recorded in this house; accessed 2026-10-04.

**Locator:** To Hugh Burnett, 5 December 1959, pp. 524-525 (the BBC producer of the Freeman interview), with the editorial note on p. 524 (n. 1) stating that a letter published in *The Listener* (London), 21 January 1960, is a combination of the letters to Burnett and to Leonard and has for that reason been omitted; and To M. Leonard, 5 December 1959 (Leonard of King's College, Newcastle upon Tyne), pp. 525-526.

**Located operation:** In the Leonard letter Jung says that what he meant by saying he does not need to believe in God because he knows is that he is confronted with a factor unknown in itself that he calls "God"; that the name designates whatever crosses his will and changes the course of his life; that this power approaches him as conscience, a *vox Dei* with which he can converse and argue; and that he would count it an intellectual immorality to take his view of a God for the universal metaphysical Being of the confessions (pp. 525-526). The Burnett letter says that many letters have emphasised his statement about "knowing" (God) and encloses his answer (p. 524). These are the passages the essay's prose paraphrases in M04 (power unknown in itself, interrupts intentions, conscience, no identification with metaphysical reality).

**Status:** locator-verified. The *Listener* text itself (the combined letter) was not seen; its issue, volume and page are not in this volume. No quotation admitted.

**Source relation:** Paraphrased by the essay; the essay's reading of knowing as knowledge of an encounter is the author's.

**Consumer:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-jung-know`.

### jung-1975-letters-vol2-1951-1961-p002 — Editorial note on the Freeman interview (To Valentine Brooke, 16 November 1959, p. 521, n. 1)

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition detail: Selected and edited by Gerhard Adler in collaboration with Aniela Jaffé, translations from the German by R. F. C. Hull. Princeton University Press (Bollingen Series XCV:2), 1975. The consulted text is the Routledge & Kegan Paul edition (London, 1976; reprinted by Routledge, 1990, ISBN 978-0-415-05171-2; digital printing 2011), whose pagination matches the Princeton citation of the Brooke letter (p. 521) and the index entry for The Listener (p. 524 n.).; local copy recorded in this house; accessed 2026-10-04.

**Locator:** To Valentine Brooke, 16 November 1959, pp. 520-523; n. 1 on p. 521 identifies the television interview by John Freeman in the series "Face to Face" and gives Freeman's question and Jung's answer; the letter itself says at p. 522 that when he says he does not need to believe in God because he "knows" he means he knows of the existence of God-images.

**Status:** locator-verified. Corroborates the interview and answer independently of *C. G. Jung Speaking*.

**Consumer:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-jung-know` (supporting only).
