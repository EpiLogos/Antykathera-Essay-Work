## Source scholarship

Csikszentmihalyi describes optimal experience as a state of absorption in a task matched to skill, with clear goals and immediate feedback, in which action and awareness merge and self-consciousness recedes. The elements of that state are set out in the chapter on enjoyment and the quality of life (chap. 3); athletic, musical and other bodily activities are the subject of "The Body in Flow" (chap. 5). The standard formulations of the "merging of action and awareness" and the "loss of self-consciousness" are in the book; the exact pages in the 1990 impression are not yet pinned.

Claim boundary: the book speaks of loss of (reflective) self-consciousness and of attention absorbed in the activity. The essay's wording, that verbal thought "falls quiet," is the author's gloss on that description, and the essay's step from flow to an "unworded regard" is the author's.
