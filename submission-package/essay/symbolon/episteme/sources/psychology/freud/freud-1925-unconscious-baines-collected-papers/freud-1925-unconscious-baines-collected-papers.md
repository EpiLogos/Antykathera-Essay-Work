## Passage card — `freud-1925-unconscious-baines-collected-papers-q001` — the assumption is necessary and legitimate, because the data of consciousness are defective

> To this we can reply that our assumption of the existence of the unconscious is *necessary* and *legitimate*, and that we possess manifold *proofs* of the existence of the unconscious. It is necessary because the data of consciousness are exceedingly defective; both in healthy and in sick persons mental acts are often in process which can be explained only by presupposing other acts, of which consciousness yields no evidence.

- **Locator:** Baines trans., *Collected Papers*, vol. 4 (1925), "The Unconscious," sec. I, p. 99. SE parallel: SE 14, sec. I, from p. 166 (Strachey wording not seen).
- **Status:** quotation-ready (Baines translation, 1925 edition only).
- **Verification:** transcribed from the archive.org OCR text of `freud-1925-cp-5` and collated by eye against the page image (leaf n102, printed p. 99), https://archive.org/details/freud-1925-cp-5/page/99. Italics as printed. Typographic normalisation, stated here: the print sets a space before the semicolon after "defective"; that space is removed. Verifier: agent, 2026-10-05.
- **Source relation:** Extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports.
- **Consumers:** §4 · #0 (reworked draft, 2026-10-05).
- **Use boundary:** licenses the claim that Freud argues for the unconscious as a necessary and legitimate assumption, on the ground that consciousness alone leaves mental acts unexplained. Cite as Baines's translation with the Collected Papers note, not as the SE.

## Passage card — `freud-1925-unconscious-baines-collected-papers-q002` — interpolated unconscious acts give demonstrable connection

> All these conscious acts remain disconnected and unintelligible if we are determined to hold fast to the claim that every single mental act performed within us must be consciously experienced; on the other hand, they fall into a demonstrable connection if we interpolate the unconscious acts that we infer. A gain in meaning and connection, however, is a perfectly justifiable motive, one which may well carry us beyond the limitations of direct experience.

- **Locator:** Baines trans., *Collected Papers*, vol. 4 (1925), "The Unconscious," sec. I, p. 99.
- **Status:** quotation-ready (Baines translation, 1925 edition only).
- **Verification:** as q001, same page image (leaf n102, p. 99). The print's spaced semicolon after "experienced" is normalised. Verifier: agent, 2026-10-05.
- **Source relation:** Extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports.
- **Consumers:** §4 · #0 (reworked draft, 2026-10-05).
- **Use boundary:** Freud's warrant is inferential: unconscious acts are inferred and interpolated because they restore connection and meaning, and the next sentences on the same page add the success of the therapeutic method as proof. Do not present this as direct observation of the unconscious.

## Passage card — `freud-1925-unconscious-baines-collected-papers-q003` — the unconscious is known only as something conscious (qualifying)

> How are we to arrive at a knowledge of the unconscious? It is of course only as something conscious that we know anything of it, after it has undergone transformation or translation into something conscious.

- **Locator:** Baines trans., *Collected Papers*, vol. 4 (1925), "The Unconscious," opening paragraphs before sec. I, p. 98.
- **Status:** quotation-ready (Baines translation, 1925 edition only).
- **Verification:** collated against the page image (leaf n101, printed p. 98), https://archive.org/details/freud-1925-cp-5/page/98. The print sets a space before the question mark after "unconscious"; it is removed. The same page carries the translator credit "[Translated by Cecil M. Baines.]" in the first footnote. Verifier: agent, 2026-10-05.
- **Source relation:** Extracted.
- **Evidential action:** qualifies.
- **Consumers:** §4 · #0 (reworked draft, 2026-10-05).
- **Use boundary:** the pairing limit for q001–q002: Freud's unconscious is reached only through its translation into consciousness. The same page also states that "the repressed does not comprise the whole unconscious," so the essay should not equate Freud's unconscious with the repressed.
