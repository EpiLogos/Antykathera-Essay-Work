## Source scholarship

A classic handbook of Maya calendrical and numerical writing. It sets out the vigesimal place-value count (with the irregular 18 × 20 = 360-day *tun* in the calendar), the forms of the zero sign in codices and inscriptions, the reading of zero as completion or ending in Period-ending dates, and the Maya practice of numbering the first day of a month as zero ("0 Pop"). Its interpretations of glyph origins (e.g. Maudslay's tonalamatl derivation, the hand as "ending") are 1915 judgements; later epigraphy (e.g. Anna Blume, "Maya Concepts of Zero," *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society* 155, no. 1 (2011): 51–88, not read this pass) refines them. The essay uses Morley for the counting practice and the completion reading, stated as his.

## Passage card — `morley-1915-maya-hieroglyphs-q003` — a sign for zero indispensable to the positional count (paraphrase)

Morley argues that, unlike a sign for twenty, a sign for zero was indispensable to the Maya count, because any numerical system rising to a second order of units needs a character to say that no units of a given order are present; signs for zero occur in both codices and inscriptions (lenticular forms in the codices; normal forms and head variants in the inscriptions).
