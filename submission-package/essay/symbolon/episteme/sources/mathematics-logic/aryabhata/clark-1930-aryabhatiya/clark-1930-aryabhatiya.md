## Source scholarship

The *Āryabhaṭīya* (499 CE) states decimal place value in words (Gaṇitapāda 2: the classes of number "from place to place each ten times the preceding"). Clark's note on the Gītikā alphabetic notation argues that *kha* there is best taken as "space" or "place", records that *kha* later becomes one of the commonest words for zero, and that whether a written zero symbol was in use in Āryabhaṭa's time is disputed: computation may have used a board ruled in columns with a blank column for zero. The note is a 1930 scholarly judgement, used here as such.

## Passage card — `clark-1930-aryabhatiya-q001` — *kha* as place; a blank column for zero

> It seems to me, therefore, preferable to take the word kha in the sense of "space" or better "place." Later the word kha is one of the commonest words for "zero," but it is still disputed whether a symbol for zero was actually in use in Āryabhaṭa's time. It is possible that computation may have been made on a board ruled into columns. Only nine symbols may have been in use and a blank column may have served to represent zero.

> Compare II, 2, where in giving the names of classes of numbers he uses the expression sthānāt sthānaṁ daśaguṇaṁ syāt, "from place to place each is ten times the preceding."
