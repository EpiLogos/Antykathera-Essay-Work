# al-Khwārizmī (Latin) — Algoritmi de numero Indorum (Boncompagni, 1857)

## Bibliographic identity

The unique Latin text of al-Khwārizmī's treatise on Indian reckoning (the Arabic original, c. 825, is lost), edited by Baldassarre Boncompagni from Cambridge University Library MS Ii.6.5, fols. 102r–109v, as no. I of *Trattati d'aritmetica*, vol. 1 (Rome, 1857), pp. 1–23. Internet Archive item `bub_gb_Jyat9f1pSIYC` (Google digitisation, Biblioteca Provinciale di Napoli copy); page images checked, agent, 2026-10-05. The text opens "Dixit algoritmi" (the spelling "algorizmi" also occurs, p. 2). An English translation of the same manuscript: John N. Crossley and Alan S. Henry, "Thus Spake al-Khwārizmī: A Translation of the Text of Cambridge University Library Ms. Ii.vi.5," *Historia Mathematica* 17, no. 2 (1990): 103–31, https://doi.org/10.1016/0315-0860(90)90048-I (metadata confirmed via Crossref, 2026-10-05; its pages not collated). The essay's translations from the Latin are the author's.

## Source scholarship

The text that gave Europe the word *algorism* (from the Latin form of the author's name, "Dixit algoritmi", "Algoritmi says"). It teaches writing and reckoning with the nine Indian figures and a small circle for the empty place. Two passages bear on §1: the account of the circle placed in an empty "differentia" (place) "in the likeness of the letter o" so that readers know the place is empty; and the passage calling one the root of all number and "outside number".
