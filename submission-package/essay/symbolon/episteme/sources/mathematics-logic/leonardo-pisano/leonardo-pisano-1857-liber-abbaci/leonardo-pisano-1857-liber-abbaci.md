# Leonardo Pisano — Liber abbaci (Boncompagni, 1857)

## Bibliographic identity

Boncompagni's edition of the *Liber abbaci* from the Florentine manuscript Magliabechiano C. I, 2616 (Badia Fiorentina 73), printed as volume 1 of *Scritti di Leonardo Pisano* (Rome, 1857). The text is the second redaction, dedicated to Michael Scot; the incipit dates the work "In Anno M° cc° ij°" (1202). ## Source scholarship

The book that did most to carry Indian reckoning into Latin Europe. Its prologue gives Leonardo's own account of learning the "nine Indian figures" as a boy at the customs house of Bugia (Béjaïa), where his father served as public notary for the Pisan merchants, and of preferring the Indian method to "algorism" and the "arcs of Pythagoras" (the counting board). Chapter 1 sets out the nine figures and then the sign 0 with its Arabic name *zephirum*. The figures are *figure*; the zero is introduced separately as a *signum*. The English translation by L. E. Sigler (Springer, 2002) is the standard modern rendering; translations given in the essay are the author's.
