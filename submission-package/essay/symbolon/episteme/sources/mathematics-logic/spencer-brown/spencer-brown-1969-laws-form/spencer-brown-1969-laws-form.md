## Essay use

Primary source for drawing a distinction, the mark, marked/unmarked asymmetry, and re-entry. It is a formal neighbour of seam-retaining distinction and recursive participation.

**Passages needed:** opening injunction; the two primary equations; re-entry; any claim about time, oscillation, memory, circuits, or imaginary values.

**Claim boundary:** Spencer-Brown defines the unmarked state within the calculus of indications. QL's `0`, the two logics, tattvas, and MEF are explicit in-house developments whose relation to that calculus must be argued rather than attributed to the source.

## Passage card — `spencer-brown-1969-laws-form-q001` — a re-entering expression has an imaginary state in time

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition status: selected.

- **Paraphrase:** Chapter 11 takes an expression that re-enters itself and finds that it fits neither the marked nor the unmarked value; it allows the equation a solution in an “imaginary state” of the form, and the state it envisages is one of time rather than space.
- **Locator:** chap. 11, “Equations of the second degree” (from p. 54), sections “Re-entry” and “Imaginary state”; the sentence that places the state “in time” stands at the foot of p. 57 and carries over to p. 58 (page head “58 IMAGINARY STATE”); the succession of square waves follows (later in the chapter). The pages of the “Re-entry” and “Imaginary state” headings themselves were not read separately.
- **Status:** locator-verified (chapter, section names, and the page of the “in time” sentence read from page heads); paraphrase only; no quotation admitted.
- **Source relation:** paraphrased.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03 marker `s01-spencerbrown` (the clause “a distinction that re-enters its own space alternates in value through time”) and M04 marker `s01-spencerbrown` (the sentence on re-entry, “no stable value … alternates between the marked and the unmarked state”).
- **Use boundary:** the source makes the point about the form's imaginary value being a state of time; “alternates between the marked and the unmarked state” is the standard reading of that passage, to be read once more on p. 57–58 before printing a quotation. The identification of the re-entering mark with `5→0`, and the reading of hardened opposition as arrested alternation, are the author's.

## Passage card — `spencer-brown-1969-laws-form-q002` — preface to the first American edition on the theory of types

**Provenance:** Status:** locator-verified; the wording was read only in short search-result snippets, so the paraphrase above is not a transcription and no quotation is admitted.

- **Paraphrase:** In the preface to the first American edition Spencer-Brown says that Whitehead and Russell introduced the theory of types as a special rule to disallow certain self-referential paradoxes, that this was a mistake, and that those paradoxes are no worse than similar self-referential cases already accepted in ordinary mathematics (he draws the parallel with imaginary roots). He recalls that Russell, shown the calculus, called the theory of types the most arbitrary thing he and Whitehead had ever had to do, not really a theory but a stopgap.
- **Locator:** preface to the first American edition (Julian Press, 1972), pp. vii–viii (the sentence on Russell's connexion with the theory of types breaks across the page turn to p. viii); in the 1979 Dutton printing, pp. xii–xiii. The 1969 Allen & Unwin text has no preface; its introduction and notes also refer to the Whitehead–Russell theory of types (introduction, citing *Principia mathematica* vol. 1, 2nd ed., pp. 37 ff.), which were not located by page.
- **Status:** locator-verified; the wording was read only in short search-result snippets, so the paraphrase above is not a transcription and no quotation is admitted.
- **Source relation:** paraphrased.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04 marker `s01-spencerbrown` (the preface sentence in the note).
- **Use boundary:** the preface presents the theory of types as a mistaken exclusion and the calculus as a place where the excluded paradoxes can stand. “Presents the calculus as an answer to the theory of types” overstates it; the note was reworded accordingly.
