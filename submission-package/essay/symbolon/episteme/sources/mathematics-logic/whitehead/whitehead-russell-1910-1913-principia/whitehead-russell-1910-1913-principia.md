## Essay use

The work establishes the actual closure project to which Russell's type theory and Gödel's title belong. It also makes the Russell–Whitehead narrative historically concrete: both participated in the logical enterprise before Whitehead's later process metaphysics.

**Passages needed:** only if the essay discusses a specific proposition, type-theoretic rule, or the work's stated aim. The general narrative may be supported by the work plus an intellectual history.

**Claim boundary:** *Principia* warrants claims about the joint logical project. Whitehead's later philosophical divergence requires his later works and responsible biographical or historical support.

## Passage card — `whitehead-russell-1910-1913-principia-q001` — inductive warrant of the premises

**Provenance:** Status:** quotation-ready (second-edition scan); first-edition page unconfirmed.

> The justification for this is that the chief reason in favour of any theory on the principles of mathematics must always be inductive, i.e. it must lie in the fact that the theory in question enables us to deduce ordinary mathematics.

- **Locator:** vol. 1, preface, p. v (second paragraph). The next sentences, on the same page, say that the greatest self-evidence usually lies not at the beginning but at a later point, so that the early deductions give reasons for believing the premisses because true consequences follow from them.
- **Status:** quotation-ready (second-edition scan); first-edition page unconfirmed.
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-principia` (third use, the sentence beginning “They were frank, too…”).
- **Use boundary:** the preface speaks of “any theory on the principles of mathematics”; the essay's “their premises” is the same claim applied to their own. A separate passage of similar sense, on the axiom of reducibility (“The reason for accepting an axiom, as for accepting any other proposition, is always largely inductive,” Introduction, chap. 2, §VII), is not the passage the essay uses.

## Passage card — `whitehead-russell-1910-1913-principia-q002` — months of work on failed hypotheses; types not logically indispensable

**Provenance:** Status:** quotation-ready (second-edition scan); first-edition page unconfirmed.

> Sometimes it has cost us several months' work to convince ourselves that a hypothesis was untenable.

> The particular form of the doctrine of types advocated in the present work is not logically indispensable, and there are various other forms equally compatible with the truth of our deductions.

- **Locator:** vol. 1, preface, p. vii (first paragraph: the examination of “a great number of hypotheses for dealing with these contradictions,” the months of work, the conclusion that “some form of the doctrine of types must be adopted”; then the passage on other forms, ending that “hardly anything in our book would be changed by the adoption of a different form of the doctrine of types”).
- **Status:** quotation-ready (second-edition scan); first-edition page unconfirmed.
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports (months of work; alternatives) and qualifies (the authors hold that some form of the doctrine of types is needed; what they leave open is the particular form).
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-principia`, the sentence “Arriving at the theory of types took prolonged trial …”.
- **Use boundary:** the preface supports both halves of the essay's sentence: the months of work, and the openness of the form of types (“supposing some other way of avoiding the contradictions to exist, not very much of our book, except what explicitly deals with types, is dependent upon the adoption of the doctrine of types in any form,” p. vii). It also says that “some form of the doctrine of types must be adopted” if the contradictions are to be avoided, so the openness is of the form, not of the need for a restriction. Russell's *Autobiography* is not needed for these claims and no page in it has been consulted.

## Passage card — `whitehead-russell-1910-1913-principia-q003` — the symbolism developed in practice

**Provenance:** Status:** quotation-ready (second-edition scan); first-edition pages unconfirmed.

> The symbolic form of the work has been forced upon us by necessity: without its help we should have been unable to perform the requisite reasoning. It has been developed as the result of actual practice, and is not an excrescence introduced for the mere purpose of exposition.

> A great deal of the symbolism, however, has had to be new, not so much through dissatisfaction with the symbolism of others, as through the fact that we deal with ideas not previously symbolised.

- **Locator:** vol. 1, preface, p. vii (first sentence pair, penultimate paragraph) and p. viii (second quotation, “Detailed acknowledgments” paragraph).
- **Status:** quotation-ready (second-edition scan); first-edition pages unconfirmed.
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports a weaker claim than the essay's wording (see use boundary).
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-principia`, the sentence “Whitehead and Russell explain in their preface that their symbolism grew as they worked …”.
- **Use boundary:** the preface says the symbolism was developed in practice and that much had to be new because the ideas had not been symbolised before. It does not say that the relations “outran what the existing signs could distinguish”; that is the essay's gloss. See the prose-issue return.
