## Passage card — `harper-etymonline-online-etymology-dictionary-q001` — con (adj.): swindling, 1889, from confidence man (1849)

**Paraphrase:** The entry *con* gives the adjective “swindling” as attested in 1889 in *con man*, American English, from *confidence man* (1849), from scams in which the victim hands over money as a token of confidence; it adds *con artist* by 1910.

- **Locator:** s.v. *con*, the third sense block (https://www.etymonline.com/word/con)
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Entry read 2026-10-04; no quotation promoted. The 1849 date agrees with the New-York Herald report.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Dates the clipped form *con* (1889) separately from *confidence man* (1849).
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-con

## Passage card — `harper-etymonline-online-etymology-dictionary-q002` — manipulation: by 1730, French manipulation from manipule “handful”

**Paraphrase:** *manipulation* is attested by 1730 in a method of digging ore, from French *manipulation*, from *manipule*, “handful” (a pharmacists’ measure), from Latin *manipulus*, “handful, sheaf, bundle,” itself from *manus* and a root of *plere*; the sense of skilful handling is attested by 1826 and handling of persons to one’s advantage by 1828.

- **Locator:** s.v. *manipulation* (https://www.etymonline.com/word/manipulation)
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Entry read 2026-10-04; no quotation promoted. The American Heritage entry gives a Spanish stage not found here.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Supports descent from *manipulus*, “handful,” through French.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-digit

## Passage card — `harper-etymonline-online-etymology-dictionary-q003` — digit: numeral below 10, from digitus “finger or toe”

**Paraphrase:** *digit* is attested in the late fourteenth century as “numeral below 10,” from Latin *digitus*, “finger or toe,” the numerical sense arising because numerals under ten were counted on the fingers; the “finger or toe” sense in English dates from the 1640s.

- **Locator:** s.v. *digit* (https://www.etymonline.com/word/digit)
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Entry read 2026-10-04; no quotation promoted. Lewis and Short, s.v. *digitus*, independently attest counting on the fingers in Latin (*computare digitis*).
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Supports the finger-to-numeral sense of *digit*.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-digit

## Passage card — `harper-etymonline-online-etymology-dictionary-q004` — savor: Old French savor from Latin sapor

**Paraphrase:** *savor* (noun) is attested around 1200, from Old French *savor*, “flavor, taste; sauce, seasoning; delight, pleasure,” from Latin *saporem* (*sapor*), “taste, flavor,” related to *sapere*, “to have a flavor.”

- **Locator:** s.v. *savor* (https://www.etymonline.com/word/savor)
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Entry read 2026-10-04; no quotation promoted.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Supports *savour* from *sapor* through Old French.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker s01-savoir
