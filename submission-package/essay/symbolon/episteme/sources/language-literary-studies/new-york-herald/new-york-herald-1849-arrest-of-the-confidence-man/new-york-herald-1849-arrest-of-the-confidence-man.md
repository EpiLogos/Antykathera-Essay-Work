## Source scholarship

The short police-court item reports the arrest of William Thompson, an “old offender,” who had for some months approached strangers in the street, asked whether they had “confidence” in him to trust him with a watch until the next day, and kept it; the Herald calls him “the Confidence Man.” Karen Halttunen’s *Confidence Men and Painted Women* (Yale University Press, 1982) is the standard historical study cited by the Lost Museum introduction (not consulted here).

## Passage card — `new-york-herald-1849-arrest-of-the-confidence-man-q001` — The “Confidence Man” and the request for “confidence”

**Paraphrase:** The Herald item describes a genteel man who accosts a stranger, asks whether the stranger has “confidence in me to trust me with your watch until to-morrow,” and walks off with it; it calls him the “Confidence Man” and reports his arrest on a charge of stealing a gold lever watch taken on the 12th of May.
