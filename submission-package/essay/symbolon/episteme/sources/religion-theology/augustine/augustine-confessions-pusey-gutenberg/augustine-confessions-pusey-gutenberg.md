# Augustine — *Confessions* 10.2.2 and 10.5.7 (Pusey; Latin numbering checked)

## Bibliographic identity

The *Confessions* are cited by the standard book.chapter.section division. The selected lawful English witness is E. B. Pusey's translation as published by Project Gutenberg (eBook 3296, release date 1 June 2002, credits Robert S. Munday). Pusey's translation first appeared in 1838 (*The Confessions of S. Augustine*, Library of the Fathers, vol. 1; Oxford: John Henry Parker — confirmed through a library catalogue record, 2026-10-04); the Gutenberg file does not say which Pusey printing it follows and carries no section numbers, so the numbering is pinned from the Latin text at The Latin Library (https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/augustine/conf10.shtml), which prints “10.2.2” and “10.5.7” as section headings. Henry Chadwick's Oxford World's Classics translation (1991), the other standard edition, was **not** consulted; its page numbers for these two sections remain to be added from a print copy.

### Passage card — `augustine-confessions-pusey-gutenberg-q002` — something of man the spirit of man does not know

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition status: selected-public-domain-translation; print-edition-not-collated; URL https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3296; accessed 2026-10-04.

> For Thou, Lord, dost judge me: because, although no man knoweth the things of a man, but the spirit of a man which is in him, yet is there something of man, which neither the spirit of man that is in him, itself knoweth. But Thou, Lord, knowest all of him, Who hast made him.

- **Locator:** *Confessions* 10.5.7 (Latin: “tu enim, domine, diiudicas me, quia etsi nemo scit hominum quae sunt hominis, nisi spiritus hominis qui in ipso est, tamen est aliquid hominis quod nec ipse scit spiritus hominis qui in ipso est. tu autem, domine, scis eius omnia, quia fecisti eum.”).
- **Status:** quotation-ready on the same terms as q001.
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports (the passage sets the knowledge of the human spirit, 1 Cor. 2:11, against what remains unknown to it).
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03 marker `s01-augustine`.

## Essay uses

M03, marker `s01-augustine`: the prose says that in Book X Augustine “addresses a God before whom the depths of human conscience lie open even when the speaker would hide them, and he admits that something of a person remains unknown to the person's own spirit while God knows it entire.” q001 (“what could be hidden in me though I would not confess it?”) and q002 support both clauses; the locators 10.2.2 and 10.5.7 in the note are correct.
