# Hebrew Bible — Mechon Mamre Hebrew and JPS 1917 English, selected passages

## Identity, selected edition and historical scope

Selected bilingual online witness: Mechon Mamre’s Hebrew Bible with English based on the Jewish Publication Society 1917 edition. Its English-edition page identifies the edition and subsequent electronic corrections; this house cites the actual online state accessed 8 September 2026, not a newly collated 1917 printed copy. Verse numbering is the selected Hebrew/JPS numbering: 2 Kings 12:11 and 12:16 correspond to 12:10 and 12:15 in many Christian English Bibles. Read scope is the complete four saved chapters, with cards limited to the named passage units.

Chronology has three separate offices. The selected English edition is dated 1917. The temple-repair narrative places the unrepaired building in Jehoash’s twenty-third regnal year (12:7); the Shaphan/book episode is set in Josiah’s eighteenth year (22:3). These are explicit narrative dates, not an independent archaeological dating of the practices or a date of final composition. Genesis 15 and 2 Kings 8 are used as textual occurrences without assigning an invented absolute date to their events.

This is a Hebrew/JPS source identity distinct from the existing KJV Job carrier. BDB’s lexical parsing and this primary-language context retain separate houses. English renderings of counting, reporting and reckoning do not all translate SPR.

### hebrew-bible-mechon-mamre-jps1917-p001 — A count and an undertaking

**Paraphrase:** Genesis 15:1–6 moves from Abram’s question about an heir to the invitation to count the stars if he can, then to his belief in the promise. The Hebrew count forms in 15:5 belong to SPR; believing in 15:6 belongs to the separate ʾMN family, and the following reckoning uses ḤŠB. The passage joins distinct acts within a promise narrative.

- **Locator:** Genesis 15:1–6, especially 15:5–6, Mechon Mamre Hebrew/JPS.
- **Verification:** Read against the saved selected carrier on 2026-09-08; English explanation is a paraphrase, not a claimed transcription.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased.
- **Status:** citation-ready for the scoped online passage; no exact quotation admitted.
- **Use boundary:** A lexical occurrence and narrated reliance, not an observed demonstration that trust follows from an impossible calculation; no common root for all three verbs.

<a id="hebrew-bible-mechon-mamre-jps1917-p002"></a>
### hebrew-bible-mechon-mamre-jps1917-p002 — The narrated person answers and receives a consequential order

**Paraphrase:** After seven years away during famine, the woman petitions for her house and land. The king asks Gehazi to recount Elisha’s deeds; during his telling she appears. He identifies her, the king asks her directly, she recounts, and the king commissions an officer to restore her possessions and the field’s produce since her departure. The SPR forms in 8:4–6 connect telling to a named hearer and then to a material order.

- **Locator:** 2 Kings 8:1–6, particularly sappərāh (8:4), məsappēr (8:5) and wattəsapper (8:6).
- **Verification:** Read against the saved selected carrier on 2026-09-08; English explanation is a paraphrase, not a claimed transcription.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased.
- **Status:** citation-ready for the scoped online passage; no exact quotation admitted.
- **Use boundary:** The narrative records the restoration order; it does not independently document its execution. The woman confirms the account here; she is not reported as refuting Gehazi. No universal model of equitable royal jurisdiction follows.

<a id="hebrew-bible-mechon-mamre-jps1917-p003"></a>
### hebrew-bible-mechon-mamre-jps1917-p003 — Counted resources, repair and trusted work

**Paraphrase:** In Jehoash’s twenty-third year the repairs remain undone. The king changes the arrangement; a chest receives money, the royal scribe and high priest count it, and funds are assigned to labour and materials. The narrative explicitly says they did not reckon with the entrusted workmen because they acted faithfully. Sōfēr names the SPR office at 12:11; the counting verb there is MNH, the reckoning at 12:16 is ḤŠB and faithfulness is ʾMN.

- **Locator:** 2 Kings 12:5–17, especially 12:7–8,11–13,15–16 in this edition; many English editions number the last pair 12:14–15.
- **Verification:** Read against the saved selected carrier on 2026-09-08; English explanation is a paraphrase, not a claimed transcription.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased.
- **Status:** citation-ready for the scoped online passage; no exact quotation admitted.
- **Use boundary:** The passage distinguishes management, material repair and reliance. It cannot be cited as a claim that more auditing always creates trust or that every English account-word is SPR.

<a id="hebrew-bible-mechon-mamre-jps1917-p004"></a>
### hebrew-bible-mechon-mamre-jps1917-p004 — A book reaches an office and changes its question

**Paraphrase:** In Josiah’s eighteenth year, Shaphan the scribe is sent about repair funds. Hilkiah gives him the found law-book, which Shaphan reads and then reads before the king. Hearing it changes the king’s response into mourning and an instruction to inquire; the delegation goes to Huldah. Sōfēr and sēfer occur together, while reading is QRʾ and the reporting verb in 22:10 is NGD, not SPR.

- **Locator:** 2 Kings 22:3–20, especially 22:3,7–14; selected Hebrew/JPS witness.
- **Verification:** Read against the saved selected carrier on 2026-09-08; English explanation is a paraphrase, not a claimed transcription.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased.
- **Status:** citation-ready for the scoped online passage; no exact quotation admitted.
- **Use boundary:** Narrated authority and consultation, not proof of the book’s composition date or provenance. Do not silently identify this found object with a modern edition or infer that all subsequent reform is non-coercive.
