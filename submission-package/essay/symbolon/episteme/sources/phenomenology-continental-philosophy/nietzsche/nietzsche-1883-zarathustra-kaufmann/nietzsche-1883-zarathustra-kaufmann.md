# Nietzsche — Thus Spoke Zarathustra, part 2 (Kaufmann, 1954)

## Bibliographic identity

Selected edition: Friedrich Nietzsche, *Thus Spoke Zarathustra: A Book for All and None*, in *The Portable Nietzsche*, ed. and trans. Walter Kaufmann (New York: Viking Press, 1954). German original: *Also sprach Zarathustra*, part 2 (1883). The Kaufmann title of the speech cited is “On Self-Overcoming” (part 2, the twelfth speech). In the public-domain Common translation the same speech is titled “Self-Surpassing” (speech XXXIV in Common's continuous numbering, second part). The German title is “Von der Selbst-Überwindung.” The work is cited by part and speech title; **no page number is given because no copy of the Kaufmann text was available** and publisher and year were taken from the standard bibliographic record of the Portable Nietzsche rather than from a copy in hand.

A public-domain translation, *Thus Spake Zarathustra: A Book for All and None*, translated by Thomas Common (Project Gutenberg eBook 1998), was read to check the content of the speech in lawful text.

## Essay use

Primary source for self-overcoming (*Selbstüberwindung*) as Nietzsche's name for what life, and the will to power, do: in this speech life says of itself that it must always overcome itself. The essay uses the concept as the possibility it then restates as an exact question; the question (how can I exceed what I have become if every encounter must first confirm that this is what I am) is the essay's own.

**Claim boundary:** the speech concerns life as will to power, and the will of the wise to make being thinkable; it does not state the essay's question.
