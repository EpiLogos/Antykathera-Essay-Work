## Essay use

### Source's relevant contribution

Gebser presents consciousness structures through their representative visual, linguistic, and intellectual forms. The mental-rational structure receives its historical expression in the Renaissance discovery and construction of perspective: Giotto's tentative externalisation of depth, Petrarch's discovery of landscape, Alberti's visual pyramid, and Leonardo's perfected optical and technical articulation. Perspective thereby makes space and ego-consciousness visible while fixing the observer and observed within a sector. Aperspectivity names liberation from that structure's exclusive validity through the co-presence and transparency of the structures, not their amalgamation.

For the zero–subject historical braid, this is a stadial account: integral consciousness is a higher mutation that makes the mental-rational structure's constituting conditions available without abolishing its powers. The braid's `180°→360°` QL articulation is authorial; Gebser supplies the historical problem and the aperspectival/integral direction.

His own presentation gives the Return of Zero a methodological precedent of unusual exactness: a historical mutation is read from changes in the means by which a world is rendered—painting, optics, language, thought, science. The essay extends this procedure into the Internet/AI field, where prompt, memory, retrieval, permissions, policy, provenance, feedback, and relation have become operational conditions of intelligence. That contextual mutation and its `0/1` formalisation are the essay's argument from Gebser's method, not claims attributed to Gebser.

### gebser-1985-ever-present-origin-s01-lock-p001 — Arational versus irrational (p. 147)

**Provenance:** Status:** locator-verified (page and wording read in the project-held PDF, 2026-10-04, by `s01-sourcelock`); the Books pool PDF is a locator witness, not a quotation-verification carrier.

**Locator:** pt. 1, chap. 3, section 4, "The Unique Character of the Structures (Additional Cross-sections)," printed p. 147, immediately after the table of attributions that ends on p. 146.

**Located operation:** Gebser says it is of fundamental importance to distinguish irrational from arational; that arationality is possible neither without irrationality nor without the pre-rational or the rational, all three of which form its basis; and that the arational attempts to go beyond the measurable "knowing and respecting it while striving to be free from it," where the irrational rejects and disregards measure by regressing into the immoderate and unfathomable. The essay's paraphrase in M05 (`s01-gebser-arational`) follows this page closely.

### gebser-1985-ever-present-origin-s01-lock-p002 — Aurobindo in the preface to the second edition (p. xxix)

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition detail: Authorized English translation of Ursprung und Gegenwart; illustrated revised edition; local copy recorded in this house; URL https://openlibrary.org/books/OL3160747M/The_ever-present_origin; accessed 2026-07-15.

**Locator:** "From the Preface to the Second Edition," printed p. xxix; see also p. 102 n. 4 (Aurobindo's priority on the transformation of consciousness and the German edition of *Der integrale Yoga* that first introduced Gebser to him).

**Located operation:** In the preface Gebser names Sri Aurobindo and Pierre Teilhard de Chardin as pre-eminent among the achievements that counterbalance the ominous events since the first edition; says both develop a newly emergent consciousness that Aurobindo designates the "supramental"; and says he defined it in turn as the "aperspectival (arational-integral)" consciousness. The year of the second edition does not appear in the English text of the preface (the preface mentions a 1962 and a 1969 work); the essay's "1966" is the date of the German second edition and is not read here.
