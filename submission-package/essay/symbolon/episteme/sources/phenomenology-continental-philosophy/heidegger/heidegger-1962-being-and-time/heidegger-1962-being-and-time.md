# Heidegger — *Being and Time* (Macquarrie and Robinson, 1962)

## Bibliographic identity

Martin Heidegger, *Being and Time*, translated by John Macquarrie and Edward Robinson (New York: Harper & Row, 1962), from *Sein und Zeit*, 7th German edition (Tübingen: Max Niemeyer, 1953; first published 1927). The English edition prints the German pagination of the later German editions in its outer margins as "H." numbers; the English page numbers and the H. numbers are both standard locators. ## Source scholarship

*Geworfenheit*, rendered "thrownness" by Macquarrie and Robinson, is introduced in §29, "Being-there as State-of-mind" (the translators' title), as the name for Dasein's being delivered over to its "there": the "that it is" of Dasein disclosed in mood, veiled in its "whence" and "whither." The translators' footnote states that the term is "introduced here" and "further discussed in Section 38" ("Falling and thrownness"). The essay uses the word for the condition of finding oneself already in a world one did not choose; Heidegger's own text develops it as facticity and as disclosed in mood, and the essay's extension to children, parents and agents is the author's.
