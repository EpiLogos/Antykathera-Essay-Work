### whitehead-1925-science-modern-world-q001 — Contradiction as the first step toward a victory (chap. 12, "Religion and Science," p. 267)

> In formal logic, a contradiction is the signal of a defeat: but in the evolution of real knowledge it marks the first step in progress towards a victory.

- **Locator:** chap. 12, "Religion and Science," Macmillan 1925, p. 267 (the sentence follows Whitehead's two illustrations, the argon and isotope discoveries, and precedes his remark on toleration, which runs onto p. 268). Cambridge University Press edition (1926), p. 231.
- **Status:** quotation-ready for the sentence. Page 267 is read from the page header of the Macmillan scan ("XII] RELIGION AND SCIENCE 267").
- **Verification:** read by `s01-sourcelock`, 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** Extracted. The essay's own sentence takes Whitehead's contradiction-as-victory as a description of the arational reading of a threshold, which is the author's use.

### whitehead-1925-science-modern-world-q002 — The fallacy of misplaced concreteness (chap. 3, "The Century of Genius," pp. 74–75)

> There is an error; but it is merely the accidental error of mistaking the abstract for the concrete. It is an example of what I will call the ‘Fallacy of Misplaced Concreteness.’

- **Locator:** chap. 3, "The Century of Genius," Macmillan 1925, pp. 74–75: the sentences “It is an ex-” end p. 74 and “ample of what I will call the ‘Fallacy of Misplaced Concreteness.’ This fallacy is the occasion of great confusion in philosophy” open p. 75 (running heads “74 SCIENCE AND THE MODERN WORLD” and “III] THE CENTURY OF GENIUS 75” in the Macmillan scan). The author's index entry reads “Fallacy of Misplaced Concreteness, 75, e.s.; 85”. The phrase recurs with substance and quality on p. 77 of the same chapter, closes the chapter's argument on p. 82 (“the ascription of misplaced concreteness to the scientific scheme of the seventeenth century”), and is restated at the head of chap. 4 on p. 85 (“the real error is an example of what I have termed: The Fallacy of Misplaced Concreteness”).
- **Status:** quotation-ready. Wording matched against Project Gutenberg eBook 68611 (transcribing the 1925 Macmillan text) and read in the Macmillan scan (`sciencemodernwor0000alfr_c3e9`); chap. 3 title and chapter and page structure read from the scan's table of contents (chap. 3 begins at p. 57).
- **Verification:** read by `s01-sourcelock` (G2), 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-whitehead-concreteness`.
- **Use boundary:** the passage is about the abstraction of simple location (instantaneous material configurations) and, on pp. 77 and 82–85, substance and quality and the mind–matter scheme; “the root error” of the whole picture is the essay's reading. The page-75 sentence is the naming.

### whitehead-1925-science-modern-world-q003 — A doctrine as the foundation of social order; disregarding half the evidence (chap. 12, pp. 266 and 268)

> Suppose that for some reason the chemical doctrine had been highly prized throughout some district as the foundation of its social order:—would it have been wise, would it have been candid, would it have been moral, to forbid the disclosure of the fact that the experiments produced discordant results?

> It is easy enough to find a theory, logically harmonious and with important applications in the region of fact, provided that you are content to disregard half your evidence.

- **Locator:** chap. 12, "Religion and Science," Macmillan 1925, p. 266 (first quotation, in the Rayleigh–Ramsay illustration; the page header “266 SCIENCE AND THE MODERN WORLD” stands above “thoughtfulness. The important question is, In what spirit are we going to face the issue?”) and p. 268 (second quotation; the passage runs on to “Such people are apt resolutely to ignore, or to explain away, all evidence which confuses their scheme with contradictory instances”).
- **Status:** locator-verified for both; the wording is read in the Gutenberg text and the Macmillan scan and is quotation-ready for the passages as printed above.
- **Verification:** read by `s01-sourcelock` (G2), 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** the essay paraphrases both passages.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-whitehead-smw` (second use, the sentence “Whitehead also saw why such an encounter is resisted …”). The first use of that marker (the contradiction quotation) is q001, p. 267.
- **Use boundary:** Whitehead's hypothetical is a district where a chemical doctrine is the foundation of social order; he asks whether it would be right to forbid disclosure of discordant results. The essay's “whether a doctrine … may justify suppressing the observations” keeps that conditional form.
