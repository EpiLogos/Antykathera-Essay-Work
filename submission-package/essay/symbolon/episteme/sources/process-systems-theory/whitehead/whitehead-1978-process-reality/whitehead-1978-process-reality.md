### q001 · Part I, chapter II, §II, printed 21–22 / PDF 48–49

Creativity, many and one form the Category of the Ultimate. The achieved singular entity unifies the received diversity and enters the diversity available for further becoming. Whitehead expressly distinguishes this sense of one from the integral number. Concrescence names the production of novel togetherness, not merely the rearrangement of a given aggregate.

### q002 · Part I, chapter II, §II, printed 23–26 / PDF 50–53

Becoming constitutes an entity's being. Prehension distinguishes subject, datum and subjective form; consciousness is not necessary to each prehension. Objectification names one actual entity's functioning in another's self-creation. Satisfaction is the fully determinate final integration. These metaphysical terms are not automatically measurements of an artificial agent.

### q005 · Spinoza and the philosophy of organism — Part I, chapter I, §II, printed 6–7 (Macmillan 1929, pp. 10–11)

**Locator:** Part I, chapter I, “The Speculative Scheme,” §II, corrected ed., pp. 6–7 (local PDF pp. 33–34). On p. 6 Whitehead takes Descartes's two substances as an instance of incoherence and says the attraction of Spinoza's philosophy lies in its modification of Descartes's position toward greater coherence, starting from one substance, *causa sui*, with attributes and individualised modes; on p. 7 he names the gap (“the arbitrary introduction of the ‘modes’”), says a multiplicity of modes is nevertheless a fixed requisite if the scheme is to bear on the many occasions of experience, and that the philosophy of organism is closely allied to Spinoza's scheme but differs by abandoning the subject-predicate form, so that Spinoza's modes become the sheer actualities. In the 1929 Macmillan text the passage is pp. 10–11 (page header “10 PROCESS AND REALITY” above “a one-substance world, only mental”; “The attraction of Spinoza's philosophy” stands on p. 10).

**Status:** paraphrase and locator verified; no quotation admitted.

**Verification:** read by `s01-sourcelock` (G2), 2026-10-04, in the local PDF (running heads 6 and 7) and the Macmillan 1929 archive.org scan (item `processrealityes0000alfr_z8u0`).

**Source relation:** paraphrased.

**Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-whitehead-pr` (second use, the sentence “Whitehead's question is how anything becomes a definite actuality at all …”).

**Use boundary:** Whitehead names a gap in Spinoza (the arbitrary introduction of the modes). The essay's formulation that he “asks how the many acquire actual existence within that unity” is a reading of that gap, not Whitehead's wording. Further Spinoza references (the 1978 index lists Spinoza's monism at 7 and 48, his modes at 7 and 81) have not been read.
