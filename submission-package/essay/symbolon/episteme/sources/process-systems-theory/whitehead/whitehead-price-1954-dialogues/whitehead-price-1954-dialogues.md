# Whitehead and Price — Dialogues of Alfred North Whitehead (1954)

## Bibliographic identity

Lucien Price, *Dialogues of Alfred North Whitehead* (Boston: Little, Brown, 1954), “as recorded by Lucien Price”; reprinted Boston: David R. Godine, 2001; also issued in paperback as a Mentor Book (New York: New American Library, 1956), with different pagination. The book is organised as a Prologue by Price, forty-three numbered dialogues, and an Epilogue. The wording of the dialogues is Price's reconstruction from memory, written down after each conversation. The Internet Archive holds lending copies of the Little, Brown printing (items `dialoguesofalfre0000whit`, `dialoguesofalfre00whit`), the Godine reprint (`dialoguesofalfre00whit_0`) and the Mentor edition (`dialoguesofalfre0000unse_i8q3`); all are access-restricted, so only search-result snippets were seen and no pagination could be read.

The sentence “There are no whole truths; all truths are half-truths. It is trying to treat them as whole truths that plays the devil,” which the essay puts in Whitehead's mouth.

### whitehead-price-1954-dialogues-q001 — half-truths (Prologue)

> There are no whole truths; all truths are half-truths. It is trying to treat them as whole truths that plays the devil.

- **Locator:** Prologue (not one of the numbered dialogues). In all four archive.org copies the sentence is introduced by Price in his own voice (“One-sided seeing is what Whitehead called ‘half-truth’ —”) and is followed by his parenthesis “(The arithmetical quips to which this lends itself were all made long ago.)”. The Mentor edition's Prologue runs pp. 9–22 (contents: Prologue 9; The Forty-three Dialogues 23), and its running head “All Truths Are Half-Truths” stands on p. 19, inside the Prologue. Printed page of the sentence: reported as p. 14 for the 2001 Godine reprint (Goodreads and an online quotation index), and as p. 16 in a United States court brief's citation of a 1954 printing (*Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals*, 509 U.S. 579, a footnote reading “Dialogues 16 (1954)” in the archive.org transcription). The two witnesses disagree, so the working note's “prologue, 14” is unconfirmed for the Little, Brown printing; the printed note gives “prologue” only. One web index also gives Dialogue 25 (1941), which the snippets contradict, since the sentence stands in Price's own voice.
- **Status:** wording verified in archive.org full-text snippets of the four copies (identical in all four apart from line-break hyphenation). Locator: Prologue confirmed; page unsettled.
- **Verification:** read by `s01-sourcelock` (G2), 2026-10-04, through archive.org full-text search snippets. The book is not in the public domain and no lawful full text was read, so the wording is **not** certified as a transcription.
- **Source relation:** extracted, as Price's record of Whitehead's remark.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-whitehead-halftruths`.
- **Use boundary:** Price's wording is his reconstruction. The sentence carries no date in the Prologue; the essay's “Late in life” is plausible (the book records Whitehead in his seventies and eighties) but undated here.
