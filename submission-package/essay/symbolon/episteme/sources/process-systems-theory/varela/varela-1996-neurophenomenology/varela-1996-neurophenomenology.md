## Passage card — `varela-1996-neurophenomenology-q001` — the working hypothesis of reciprocal constraints (reported)

**Paraphrase:** Varela’s “working hypothesis” of neurophenomenology is that phenomenologically precise first-person data provide strong constraints on the analysis and interpretation of the physiological processes relevant to consciousness, with third-person data in turn constraining first-person accounts (“reciprocal constraints”).

- **Locator:** Varela 1996, as reported in Lutz and Thompson 2003, 33 (page in Varela not located).
- **Status:** paraphrase-only.
- **Verification:** Known through Lutz and Thompson 2003, p. 33 (see [[lutz-thompson-2003-neurophenomenology]]); Varela’s text not read. agent G6b, 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased.
- **Evidential action:** supports.

- **Use boundary:** Warrants the prose’s “first-person descriptions and physiological findings constrain and refine one another.” The sentence about the neglected direction is checked against Lutz and Thompson, not against Varela.
