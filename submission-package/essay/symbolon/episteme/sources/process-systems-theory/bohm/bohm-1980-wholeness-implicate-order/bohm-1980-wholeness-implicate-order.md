## Passage card — `bohm-1980-wholeness-implicate-order-q001` — mind and body as relatively independent sub-totalities of a higher-dimensional common ground

> So we are led to propose further that the more comprehensive, deeper, and more inward actuality is neither mind nor body but rather a yet higher-dimensional actuality, which is their common ground and which is of a nature beyond both. Each of these is then only a relatively independent sub-totality and it is implied that this relative independence derives from the higher-dimensional ground in which mind and body are ultimately one (rather as we find that the relative independence of the manifest order derives from the ground of the implicate order).

- **Locator:** chap. 7, “The Enfolding-Unfolding Universe and Consciousness,” §8 “Matter, Consciousness and Their Common Ground,” p. 265 (Routledge Classics 2002).
- **Status:** locator-verified (wording seen only in unclear-provenance copies; not quotation-ready).
- **Verification:** OCR text of two archive.org uploads, which agree (end-of-line hyphenation in “higher-/dimensional” and “elem-/ents” resolved). https://archive.org/details/bohm-wholeness-and-the-implicate-order. Verifier: agent, 2026-10-05.
- **Source relation:** Extracted (paraphrase until collated).

- **Use boundary:** Bohm presents this as a proposal (“we are led to propose further”). The common ground is “neither mind nor body.” Do not report it as a result of physics, and do not identify the ground with mind or with matter.

## Passage card — `bohm-1980-wholeness-implicate-order-q002` — mind and body as related projections, not causal interactants

> So we do not say that mind and body causally affect each other, but rather that the movements of both are the outcome of related projections of a common higher-dimensional ground.

## Passage card — `bohm-1980-wholeness-implicate-order-q005` — the ground as a stage and the whole account as at most a proposal (qualifying)

> Is this ground the absolute end of everything? In our proposed views concerning the general nature of ‘the totality of all that is’ we regard even this ground as a mere stage, in the sense that there could in principle be an infinity of further development beyond it. At any particular moment in this development each such set of views that may arise will constitute at most a proposal. It is not to be taken as an assumption about what the final truth is supposed to be, and still less as a conclusion concerning the nature of such truth.

- **Locator:** chap. 7, §8, p. 270 (Routledge Classics 2002), near the chapter’s close (the chapter ends p. 271). The paragraph before it calls cosmos, matter, life and consciousness “projections of a common ground,” and says that “we have no detailed perception or knowledge of this ground.”
