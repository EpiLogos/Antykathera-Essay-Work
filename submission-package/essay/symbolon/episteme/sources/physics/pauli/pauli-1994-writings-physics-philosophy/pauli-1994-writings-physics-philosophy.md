### pauli-1994-writings-physics-philosophy-p001 — Observer's choice and the loss of complementary knowledge (lead, unverified)

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition status: selected; accessed 2026-10-04.

**Locator:** "The Philosophical Significance of the Idea of Complementarity," pp. 35–42 (Writings); *Experientia* 6 (1950): 72–75.

**Located operation:** A search snippet attributes to Pauli's 1950 essay the thought that every gain of knowledge of atomic objects by observation must be paid for by a loss of other knowledge, and that which knowledge is obtained and which irrevocably lost is left to the free choice of the experimenter among mutually exclusive arrangements. The sentence and its page are NOT verified.

**Status:** unverified lead

## Open acquisition and verification

Read the English essay in *Writings*, pp. 35–42, from a library copy, or the German text in Pauli, *Aufsätze und Vorträge über Physik und Erkenntnistheorie* (Vieweg, 1961), 10–17, and pin the sentence. Confirm that the "irrevocable loss" wording is Pauli's, not Bohr's or Heisenberg's.
