## Source scholarship

A collection of essays on the "extreme phenomena" of the contemporary: pornography and obscenity, transaesthetics, transpolitics, transsexuality, the end of the scene and the mirror, and the fractal stage of value. Its load-bearing concepts:

- **Obscenity.** "The most intimate becomes the most visible." The end of the scene and the mirror: nothing is hidden, everything is displayed, the obscene is the total visibility of the surface. The pornographic society extends beyond sexuality to information, politics, and aesthetics — everything becomes transparent, everything becomes a spectacle of its own visibility.
- **The fractal stage of value.** The fourth stage of value (after use, exchange, and sign value): total contamination — every value is convertible into every other, no hierarchy remains, value radiates in all directions without reference. (The earlier stages: natural value, commodity value, structural value; the fractal is the fourth, in which value has no referent at all.)
- **The perfect crime motif** (further developed in *The Perfect Crime*, 1995): the real has been assassinated and replaced by its double; the perfect crime leaves no corpse and no clue — no provenance, no residue.

Scholarly status: the late-period essay collection in which the "ecstasy of communication" (1987) and "transparency" themes converge. The obscenity/fractal material is the most direct carrier of the total-visibility diagnosis. Critiques already recorded for the corpus (Norris, Kellner, Wolin) apply; Poster's "failure to define key terms" gate applies to the essay form especially.

## Argument relations (candidate — for the harmonisation pass to weigh)

- **Obscenity ↔ transparency without trust.** The essay's §5 argument (advent-of-zero contribution): observability is not trust — a benchmark can make performance observable while the ownership and purpose of the system remain untrustworthy; "greater visibility for the apparatus can coincide with a more radical destruction of trust for the beings made visible to it" (the Palantir-type case). Baudrillard's obscenity — the most intimate becoming the most visible while nothing is thereby known or entered — is a candidate external witness for the same operation at cultural scale. *Candidate join, not decided.*
- **Total visibility ↔ prakāśa without vimarśa (candidate reading).** In Śaiva terms: light that displays without bending back to know itself as light — the inert mirror that shows everything and apprehends nothing. Baudrillard's transparent society is a candidate mythematic refraction of light without self-apprehension; the essay's lost aesthetic ("the presence of the invisible within the visible") is exactly what total visibility deletes. *Candidate reading to be weighed at harmonisation.*
- **Fractal value ↔ the code completed.** The fourth stage of value (total convertibility, no referent) is the endpoint of the sign-value thesis of *Symbolic Exchange and Death*: the gauge occupied everywhere, so that any value can be exchanged for any other without a ground. Candidate relation to the technical addendum's "regime of total equivalency" and to the gauge tower. *Candidate.*
- **The perfect crime ↔ the ledger posted as its own collateral.** The ground's murder with no corpse: no provenance, no residue, no return — the trust floor deleted and the deletion concealed. The candidate counter-move from the essay's own technical thread: the perfect crime is impossible because cohomology does not vanish — the sheaf obstruction is where the surety lives; the ungluable remainder is the clue the perfect crime would have to destroy. Whether this counter-move is argued against Baudrillard or developed alongside him is a harmonisation decision, not made here.
