# Tesla — My Inventions (Electrical Experimenter, 1919)

[Source index](../../../README.md)

## Bibliographic identity

“My Inventions” was serialized in six parts in the *Electrical Experimenter* in 1919: Part 1, “My Early Life” (February 1919, vol. 6, no. 10, whole no. 70), Part 2, “My First Efforts at Invention” (March), Part 3, “My Later Endeavors” (April), Part 4, “The Discovery of the Tesla Coil and Transformer” (May), Part 5, “The Magnifying Transmitter” (June), Part 6, “The Art of Telautomatics” (October). The visualisation passage the essay uses is in **Part 1**, not Part 2. Part 1 opens on p. 696 (contents list, p. 696: “My Inventions—No. 1 of a Series”), is continued “from page 697” on p. 743 and ends on p. 747. Checked against the page scan of the February 1919 issue (archive.org item n10electricalexperi06gern, running heads and page numbers) and the Wikisource transcription of the six parts, 2026-10-04 (agent G6b, 2026-10-04). Tesla died in 1943; the 1919 text is public domain.

## Source scholarship

Tesla writes in his own voice at sixty-three, addressing “our young men readers.” The passage sets his method against “the purely experimental”: he claims to have discovered, from about seventeen, that he could visualise machines with the greatest facility and so to build, alter and run them in the mind before making any apparatus.

## Passage card — `tesla-1919-my-inventions-q001` — building, altering and operating the device in the mind

> When I get an idea I start at once building it up in my imagination. I change the construction, make improvements and operate the device in my mind. It is absolutely immaterial to me whether I run my turbine in thought or test it in my shop. I even note if it is out of balance.

## Passage card — `tesla-1919-my-inventions-q002` — no fault seen; the device works as conceived

> When I have gone so far as to embody in the invention every possible improvement I can think of and see no fault anywhere, I put into concrete form this final product of my brain. Invariably my device works as I conceived that it should, and the experiment comes out exactly as I planned it. In twenty years there has not been a single exception.
