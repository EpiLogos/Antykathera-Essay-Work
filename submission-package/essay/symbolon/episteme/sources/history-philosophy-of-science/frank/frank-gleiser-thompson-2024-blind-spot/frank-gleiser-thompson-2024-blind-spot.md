## Essay use

Contemporary philosophy-of-science diagnosis of the substitution by which scientific abstractions are treated as more real than the experience that makes observation, measurement, and justification possible. It gives a field-facing formulation of the subject/experience problem and directly touches AI, mind, and planetary science.

## Passage card — `frank-gleiser-thompson-2024-blind-spot-q001` — what the authors call the Blind Spot

**Paraphrase:** The authors name the source of the meaning crisis in the scientific worldview the Blind Spot: at the heart of science lies something we do not see that makes science possible, as the visual blind spot (where the optic nerve sits) lies at the heart of the visual field; in the scientific blind spot sits direct experience, “a precondition of observation, investigation, exploration, measurement, and justification.”

- **Locator:** “An Introduction to the Blind Spot,” the section introducing the term; printed page not verified (PDF position 12 of a Z-Library scan; roughly the fifth printed page).

## Passage card — `frank-gleiser-thompson-2024-blind-spot-q002` — the reversal of the dependence of abstract on concrete

**Paraphrase:** In chap. 1, the authors describe the “surreptitious substitution” of abstractions for concrete realities (Husserl) as what Whitehead calls “the fallacy of misplaced concreteness,” and say that reducing the concrete to the abstract “nonsensically reverses the dependence relation (the abstract always presupposes the concrete).” They say elsewhere that the Blind Spot is a worldview that is an optional metaphysics attached to, but separable from, the practice of science.

- **Locator:** chap. 1, “The Surreptitious Substitution: Philosophical Origins of the Blind Spot,” the discussion of Whitehead’s fallacy of misplaced concreteness; printed pages not verified (PDF positions 40 and 22).
- **Status:** locator-verified.
- **Verification:** read in the project’s locator-only Z-Library scan; wording not certified. 2026-10-04 (agent G6b).
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased.
- **Evidential action:** qualifies.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-blindspot`.
- **Use boundary:** the “reversal” the authors describe is of the dependence of abstract on concrete (and of experience as source of science), not of the order of Descartes’s *Meditations*. The prose’s Cartesian order-of-inquiry reading is the essay’s own argument, and the printed note says so; whether the sentence should say the authors name “this reversal” the Blind Spot, or the worldview built on it, is reported to the owner as a prose issue.
