## Essay use

Primary source for the relation between saying/showing, the ladder at 6.54, and the silence of proposition 7. It places the limits of propositional articulation inside the work's own method.

**Passages needed:** propositions 4.12–4.1212, 6.4–6.522, 6.54, and 7 in the chosen translation.

**Claim boundary:** the source warrants claims about the limit of saying under the *Tractatus*'s own project. The QL reading concerns the structural pressure at that limit; any positive metaphysical development belongs to the essay's further argument.

## Passage card — `wittgenstein-1922-tractatus-q003` — the subject as limit of the world
^wittgenstein-1922-tractatus-q003

> The subject does not belong to the world but it is a limit of the world.

> Where in the world is a metaphysical subject to be noted? You say that this case is altogether like that of the eye and the field of sight. But you do not really see the eye. And from nothing in the field of sight can it be concluded that it is seen from an eye.

- **Locator:** proposition 5.632 (first quotation) and 5.633 (second); Ludwig Wittgenstein Project PDF p. 87. Proposition 5.6331 (the field of sight has no such form, followed by a figure of an eye in a visual field) follows at the head of the next page.
- **Status:** quotation-ready (Ramsey–Ogden 1922 wording).
- **Verification:** Agent G5b, 2026-10-04; text layer of the Ludwig Wittgenstein Project PDF read for 5.632 and 5.633; the Project states that its text reproduces the Project Gutenberg edition of the Ramsey–Ogden 1922 translation. The wording of 5.632 matches the essay's quotation exactly. Other translations differ (Pears–McGuinness 1961 reads “The subject does not belong to the world: rather, it is a limit of the world”), so the note must name the 1922 translation, which it does.
- **Source relation:** extracted (5.632); paraphrased (5.633, as the eye and the field of sight).
- **Evidential action:** supports.
- **Argument function:** the subject as a limit and not a content of the world; the eye is not in its field of sight.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, markers `s01-wittgenstein`, `s01-tractatus-subject`.
- **Use boundary:** licenses the Tractarian claim that the metaphysical subject is a limit of the world, not a thing in it. That the same pressure reaches the essay's crossed zero is the essay's own argument.

## Passage card — `wittgenstein-1922-tractatus-q004` — the ladder
^wittgenstein-1922-tractatus-q004

## Translator correction — 2026-10-04 (§0/1 M02)

The frontmatter and Chicago forms above credit the 1922 translation to F. P. Ramsey and the editing to C. K. Ogden. The Kegan Paul bilingual edition of 1922 is standardly credited as translated by C. K. Ogden, with substantial help from the nineteen-year-old F. P. Ramsey (encyclopedic record read 2026-10-04; to confirm against the title leaf of the Ludwig Wittgenstein Project facsimile). The printed M02 note now reads "trans. C. K. Ogden" with Ramsey acknowledged only in this house; the Chicago forms of this house should be revised to "trans. C. K. Ogden" and the `translator` / `editor` fields corrected once the title leaf is checked. Proposition numbers are unaffected.
