## Source scholarship

Locke’s *Essay* (first published 1689–90, dated 1690) is the work in which the modern English use of *consciousness* is fixed for philosophy; the chapter on identity makes consciousness the ground of personal identity. See Larry Jorgensen, “Seventeenth-Century Theories of Consciousness,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (house `jorgensen-2025-sep-seventeenth-century-consciousness`), §4.3, which quotes 2.27.9.

## Passage card — `locke-1975-essay-concerning-human-understanding-q001` — 2.1.19: consciousness is the perception of what passes in a man’s own mind

> Consciousness is the perception of what passes in a man’s own mind.

(The Gutenberg text; the Nidditch edition’s spelling and capitals differ and are not reproduced here.) The sentence stands in the argument against the claim that the soul always thinks; thinking is said to consist in being conscious that one thinks.

- **Locator:** bk. 2, chap. 1 (“Of Ideas in General, and Their Original”), §19, in the passage on whether the soul always thinks.
- **Status:** locator-verified

## Passage card — `locke-1975-essay-concerning-human-understanding-q002` — 2.27.9: person, consciousness and the self

**Paraphrase:** Locke defines a person as a thinking intelligent being with reason and reflection that can consider itself as itself, the same thinking thing at different times and places; it does so only by consciousness, which is inseparable from thinking and essential to it, since it is impossible to perceive without perceiving that one perceives. Consciousness accompanying thinking is what makes everyone what he calls *self*, and personal identity consists in its extent.

- **Locator:** bk. 2, chap. 27 (“Of Identity and Diversity”), §9 (Nidditch and scholarly numbering; §11 in the Project Gutenberg text).
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Passage found 2026-10-04 in Project Gutenberg ebook 10615 (as §11) and in Bennett’s *Early Modern Texts* Book II (as §9); SEP (Jorgensen, §4.3) quotes the “person” definition as *Essay* 2.27.9. Nidditch not consulted.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Confirms that 2.27.9 is the passage in which consciousness grounds personal identity; the essay’s own use of Locke in M03 is limited to the consciousness/conscience separation.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-conscientia
