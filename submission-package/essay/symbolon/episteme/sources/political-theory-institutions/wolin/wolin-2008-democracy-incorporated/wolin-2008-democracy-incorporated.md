## Source scholarship

Wolin argues that the United States has developed a political form, "inverted totalitarianism," in which corporate power and a managed electorate work through institutions that keep the forms of constitutional democracy, in contrast to the leader-centred regimes of classical totalitarianism. The term and its contrast are developed in chap. 3 ("Totalitarianism's Inversion, Democracy's Perversion") and chap. 11 ("Inverted Totalitarianism: Antecedents and Precedents"); "managed democracy" is the subject of chap. 8 ("The Politics of Superpower: Managed Democracy"). Chapter assignments follow the chapter titles; the argument inside the chapters was not read for this house.
