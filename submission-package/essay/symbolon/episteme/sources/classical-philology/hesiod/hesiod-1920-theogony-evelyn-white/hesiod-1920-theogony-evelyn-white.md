# Hesiod — Theogony (Evelyn-White, 1920)

## Chicago 18 forms

**Full note:** Hesiod, *Theogony*, trans. Hugh G. Evelyn-White, in *The Homeric Hymns and Homerica* (London: William Heinemann; New York: G. P. Putnam’s Sons, 1920), lines 195–206, 933–37, 975–78.

**Shortened note:** Hesiod, *Theogony*, lines 933–37.

**Bibliography:** Hesiod. *Theogony*. Translated by Hugh G. Evelyn-White. In *The Homeric Hymns and Homerica*, 79–155. London: William Heinemann; New York: G. P. Putnam’s Sons, 1920. https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Hesiod,_the_Homeric_Hymns_and_Homerica/The_Theogony.

## Essay use

Hesiod provides genealogical provenance for the Offered image in §2/#3: Cytherea is identified as Aphrodite, and the poem’s genealogy places Harmonia in relation to Aphrodite and Ares. The image’s logic of war, love, and concord remains Taylor’s offered reading.

**Claim boundary:** the text does not establish a doctrine of dialectic, reconciliation, surveillance, two logics, or computation. It does not narrate Hephaestus’s capture of Ares and Aphrodite.

## Passage card — `hesiod-1920-theogony-evelyn-white-q004` — the division at Mecone
^hesiod-1920-theogony-evelyn-white-q004

> “For when the gods and mortal men had a dispute at Mecone, even then Prometheus was forward to cut up a great ox and set portions before them, trying to befool the mind of Zeus. Before the rest he set flesh and inner parts thick with fat upon the hide, covering them with an ox paunch; but for Zeus he cut the white bones dressed up with cunning art and covered with shining fat. Then the father of men and of gods said to him: ‘Son of Iapetus, most glorious of all lords, good sir, how unfairly you have divided the portions!’”

- **Locator:** *Theogony*, lines 535–44 (Greek: 535 ὅτ᾽ ἐκρίνοντο θεοὶ θνητοί τ᾽ ἄνθρωποι; 537 δασσάμενος; 540 δολίῃ ἐπὶ τέχνῃ; 544 ὡς ἑτεροζήλως διεδάσσαο μοίρας).
- **Status:** quotation-ready.
- **Verification:** agent, 2026-10-05; transcribed from the Wikisource proofread text of the 1920 edition (Hesiod, The Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, printed pp. 119–21), and the Greek collated in the Perseus TEI text (tlg0020.tlg001.perseus-grc2). The Perseus English (perseus-eng2, from the 1914 printing) differs in small words (“deceive” for “befool”; “put” for “cut”); the 1920 wording above is the house edition’s.
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports.
- **Argument function:** a division performed as a choice and arranged as a deception; Evelyn-White renders ἐκρίνοντο “had a dispute” (κρίνω: separate, decide; see `lsj-1940-greek-english-lexicon-q420`).
- **Consumers:** §2 · #0 (reworked draft, 2026-10-05).
- **Use boundary:** reading the scene as a cut-and-choose turned into a con is the author’s.

## Passage card — `hesiod-1920-theogony-evelyn-white-q005` — Zeus’s choice, the bones on the altars, fire and its price
^hesiod-1920-theogony-evelyn-white-q005

> “But Zeus, whose wisdom is everlasting, saw and failed not to perceive the trick, and in his heart he thought mischief against mortal men which also was to be fulfilled. With both hands he took up the white fat and was angry at heart, and wrath came to his spirit when he saw the white ox-bones craftily tricked out: and because of this the tribes of men upon earth burn white bones to the deathless gods upon fragrant altars.” … “Forthwith he made an evil thing for men as the price of fire; for the very famous Limping God formed of earth the likeness of a shy maiden as the son of Cronos willed.”

- **Locator:** *Theogony*, lines 550–57 and 570–72 (Greek 571: γαίης γὰρ σύμπλασσε περικλυτὸς Ἀμφιγυήεις).

- **Verification:** agent, 2026-10-05; Wikisource proofread 1920 text, printed pp. 121–23; Greek collated in Perseus perseus-grc2.
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports.
- **Argument function:** the choice made knowingly; the ritual consequence; the craftsman god Hephaestus (the Limping God, Ἀμφιγυήεις) fashions the woman who is the price of fire.
