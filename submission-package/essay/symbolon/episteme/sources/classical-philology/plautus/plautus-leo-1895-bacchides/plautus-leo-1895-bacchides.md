## Source scholarship

*Bacchides* is a comedy of two courtesans of the same name, mistaken identity and two young men; Pistoclerus and Mnesilochus quarrel at III.vi over whether Pistoclerus has betrayed his friend, and Pistoclerus ends the exchange by threatening to carry him indoors if Mnesilochus persists in giving him little credit.

## Passage card — `plautus-leo-1895-bacchides-q001` — 3.6.41 (line 570): “parvam mihi fidem arbitrarier”

> Postremo, si pergis parvam mihi fidem arbitrarier,
> tollam ego ted in collum atque intro hinc auferam.

Speaker: Pistoclerus, to Mnesilochus. Sense: “finally, if you keep on judging my credit to be small,” i.e. giving me little credence; the threat that follows is to carry Mnesilochus indoors. This is the line Lewis and Short cite under *arbitror*, II.A, for the construction they print as *fidem alicui arbitrari*; the text has the passive infinitive *arbitrarier* and the dative *mihi*.
