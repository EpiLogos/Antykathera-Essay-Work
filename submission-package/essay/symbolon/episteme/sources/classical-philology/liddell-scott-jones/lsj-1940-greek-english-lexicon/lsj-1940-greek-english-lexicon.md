## Selected lexical witnesses

<a id="lsj-1940-greek-english-lexicon-q001"></a>
### q001 — Topos

**Paraphrase:** The entry distinguishes physical place, situated position, a passage in an author and a rhetorical topic. Aeschines 3.78 and Aristotle, Rhetoric 1358a14, are dictionary witnesses for distinct uses. These senses do not establish the authorial Arche-Topos compound or a mathematical theorem.

**Locator:** s.v. τόπος, place/position, textual passage and rhetorical topic.

**Access:** [selected LSJ transcription](https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/grc/τόπος).

**Verification:** named senses read 2026-09-08; no full-dictionary or independently collated ancient quotation claim.

<a id="lsj-1940-greek-english-lexicon-q002"></a>
### q002 — Logos

**Paraphrase:** The entry distinguishes reckoning, monetary accounts, account-giving, esteem, relation and mathematical ratio. Euclid V, definition 3, is cited for ratio. Their shared headword does not collapse their objects; the selected semantic range does not establish a complete philosophy of Logos.

**Locator:** s.v. λόγος, LSJ I.1–4 and II.1–2.

**Access:** [selected LSJ transcription](https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/grc/λόγος).

**Verification:** named senses read 2026-09-08; no full-dictionary or independently collated ancient quotation claim.

### q003 — Nomos

**Paraphrase:** The entry associates νόμος with νέμω and distinguishes customary usage from law or ordinance. The differently accented νομός has its own entry. This is lexical evidence, not proof of a compulsory pasture-to-state history or of the essay’s sixfold.

**Locator:** s.v. νόμος, introduction and habitual practice/custom/law senses.

**Access:** [selected LSJ transcription](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn:cite2:scaife-viewer:dictionaries.v1:lsj-n71005/).

### q004 — Krisis

**Paraphrase:** The entry derives the noun from κρίνω and distinguishes separating, judging, choosing and judicial proceedings. Its Aristotle Politics 1275a23 citation connects judgment and office within the cited civic context. The dictionary is the verified witness here; that Aristotelian passage has not been independently collated for this card.

**Locator:** s.v. κρίσις, LSJ introductory and decision/judgment senses.

**Access:** [selected LSJ transcription](https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/grc/κρίσις).

**Verification:** named senses read 2026-09-08; no full-dictionary or independently collated ancient quotation claim.

### q005 — Hybris

**Paraphrase:** The initial sense concerns overbearing violence and insolence; the Odyssey suitors supply several cited examples. The semantic witness is narrower than Taylor’s exact theorem of a local determination occupying its determining condition. That theorem retains authorial standing; the lexicon does not independently derive it.

**Locator:** s.v. ὕβρις, LSJ initial violence/insolence sense.

**Access:** [selected LSJ transcription](https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/grc/ὕβρις).

**Verification:** named senses read 2026-09-08; no full-dictionary or independently collated ancient quotation claim.

### q006 — Anamnesis

**Paraphrase:** The entry distinguishes calling to mind or recollection, reminders, a memorial sacrifice, and a separate lunar-return use. Its recollection citations include Plato, Phaedo 72e and 92d, Philebus 34c, and Aristotle, De memoria 451a21. These are dictionary witnesses; no complete Platonic argument is established by this card.

**Locator:** s.v. ἀνάμνησις, introductory recollection sense and senses 2–3.

**Access:** [selected LSJ transcription](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn:cite2:scaife-viewer:dictionaries.v1:lsj-n7019/).

**Verification:** entry read 2026-09-08; selected senses paraphrase-ready, ancient citation contexts and print edition not independently collated. **Consumer:** E2 recollection branch; the authorial return remains register 3.

<a id="lsj-1940-greek-english-lexicon-q007"></a>
### q007 — Homologia

**Paraphrase:** The entry distinguishes agreement and verbal consistency; assent, admission and concession; and compacts, including terms of peace or surrender. Plato, Symposium 187b and Theaetetus 164c, Aristotle, Topics 110a33, and the historical treaty citations are lexical witnesses here, not independently collated primary passages. The semantic range does not establish that every agreement is true, freely made or an instance of QL homology.

**Locator:** s.v. ὁμολογία, agreement, admission and compact senses, including the peace/truce/surrender sub-sense.

**Access:** [selected LSJ transcription](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn:cite2:scaife-viewer:dictionaries.v1:lsj-n73748/).

**Verification:** named senses read 2026-09-08; no print-edition collation or exact quotation claim. **Consumer:** E5 historical Greek branch and homological-agreement office; evidence register 2, with the authorial comparison at register 3.

### q413 — Ἁμαρτία and ἁμαρτάνω — failure, fault, sin; miss the mark

**Paraphrase:** The entry ἁμαρτία gives “a failure, fault,” (Aesch. *Ag.* 1197; “error of judgement,” Th. 1.32) and “in Philos. and Religion, guilt, sin” (Pl. *Lg.* 660c; Arist. *EN* 1148a3; LXX Gen. 18.20; John 8.46). ### q414 — Λῆμμα — anything received; in logic, a premiss; lemma

**Paraphrase:** The entry derives λῆμμα from λαμβάνω and gives “anything received” (opposed to δόμα), “receipt,” “gain, profit,” then “in Logic, statement taken as true, assumption; esp. premiss in a syllogism” (Arist. *Top.* 101a14; 156a21; Gell. 9.16), “prop. the major premiss” (Stoic), “matter, substance, or argument of a sentence,” “title or argument of an epigram, Lat. *lemma*,” and in the Septuagint “burden laid on one, commission received, esp. of prophecy.”

**Locator:** s.v. λῆμμα, first sense and the logic sense (second).

**Verification:** paraphrase-ready; short glosses quotation-ready

- **Status:** [CLD transcription of LSJ](https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/grc/λῆμμα), read 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Supports *lēmma* from *lambanein* and its logical sense of a statement taken as true, a premiss. The essay’s wording “a proposition a proof must take without deriving it” agrees with the ancient logical sense; it does not describe the modern mathematical lemma, which is usually a proved auxiliary proposition.

### q415 — Ὕβρις — wanton violence, insolence; named of an act; an overreach toward the gods

**Paraphrase:** The entry gives “wanton violence, arising from the pride of strength or from passion, insolence,” frequent in the *Odyssey* of the suitors (Od. 15.329; 17.565; 1.368); “θεοὶ … ἀνθρώπων ὕβριν τε καὶ εὐνομίην ἐφορῶντες” (Od. 17.487); “δυσσεβίας μὲν ὕβρις τέκος” (Aesch. *Eum.* 533); the word “predicated of actions,” among them “ὕβρις τάδ’ ἐστί, κρείσσω δαιμόνων εἶναι θέλειν” (Eur. *Hipp.* 474); then “lust, lewdness,” “of animals, violence,” “an outrage,” “an outrage on the person, esp. violation, rape,” in law “a term covering all the more serious injuries done to the person” (Isoc. 20.2; Dem. 21), “a loss by sea,” and “a violent, overbearing man.” This supplements q005, which records only the initial sense.

**Locator:** s.v. ὕβρις, the opening sense block, from “wanton violence … insolence” to the clause “predicated of actions.”

**Verification:** paraphrase-ready; short Greek citations quotation-ready (collated in the Euripides house for *Hipp.* 474–75)

- **Status:** [CLD transcription of LSJ](https://cld.bbaw.de/lemma/grc/ὕβρις), full entry read 2026-10-04. The *Hipp.* 474 citation is collated against Murray’s text in house euripides-murray-1902-hippolytus (lines 474–75).
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** LSJ’s headline sense is wanton violence and insolence from pride or passion, not an overreach into divine authority as such; the overreach of a mortal toward the gods is one attested use (*Hipp.* 474–75, spoken by the Nurse), as is the juxtaposition with *eunomia* in the *Odyssey*. The essay’s “the overreach of a mortal into an authority not its own” is therefore the author’s reading of an attested use, not the lexicon’s definition.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker s01-hybris

### q421 — Θεωρός — envoy sent to an oracle or a festival; spectator; one who travels to see
