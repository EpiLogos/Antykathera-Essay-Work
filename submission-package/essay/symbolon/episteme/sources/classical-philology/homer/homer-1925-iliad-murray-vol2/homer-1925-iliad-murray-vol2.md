## Source scholarship

Book 22 narrates the duel of Hector and Achilles before the walls of Troy. Before they fight, Hector proposes an oath that the victor will return the loser’s body, calling the gods witnesses and guardians of their *harmoniai*, covenants (22.255; LSJ s.v. ἁρμονία II). Achilles refuses any compact.

## Passage card — `homer-1925-iliad-murray-vol2-q002` — Achilles refuses
^homer-1925-iliad-murray-vol2-q002

> “Hector, talk not to me, thou madman, of covenants. As between lions and men there are no oaths of faith, nor do wolves and lambs have hearts of concord but are evil-minded continually one against the other, even so is it not possible for thee and me to be friends, neither shall there be oaths between us till one or the other shall have fallen, and glutted with his blood Ares, the warrior with tough shield of hide.”

- **Locator:** *Iliad*, 22.261–67 (Greek 261: Ἕκτορ μή μοι ἄλαστε συνημοσύνας ἀγόρευε).
- **Status:** quotation-ready.
- **Verification:** agent, 2026-10-05; Perseus TEI Murray translation (book 22, card 260) and Greek (perseus-grc2).
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Evidential action:** qualifies.
- **Argument function:** refusal of covenant ending in the image of Ares glutted with blood: war as the demand that the relation produce a winner.
- **Consumers:** §2 · #3 (reworked draft, 2026-10-05).
- **Use boundary:** Achilles’s word for covenants at 22.261 is συνημοσύνας, not ἁρμονίαι; the passage refuses the relation Hector named.
