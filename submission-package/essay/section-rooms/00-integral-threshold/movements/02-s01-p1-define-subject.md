# §0/1 · #1 — Define the Subject Without Making It an Object

## Claim
The subject is the non-objectifiable pole of a differentiating event: the opening through which a determinate world is given. Treating that condition as a hidden substance inside the world would turn it into another object and repeat the error under examination.

## Warrant
“I see x” already entails knower, means, and known; reflection can objectify any self-description but cannot exhaust the condition under which that description appears. Śaiva epistemology names the three moments *pramātṛ*, *pramāṇa*, and *prameya*. *Prameya* is the definitive pole of what can be made known; *pramātṛ* is the indefinitive pole present in knowing but never exhausted as another known; *pramāṇa* carries their asymmetric relation. The familiar subject/object pair is therefore already a compressed triad: it conceals the seeing, thinking, measuring, or valuing through which knower and known become available together. The compressed triad is the first clue that what appears as duality may have to be thought as Trika.

## Tension / limit
“Non-objectifiable” establishes a discipline of inquiry: relations and effects remain describable even though the pole cannot be captured as an occult object or exempted from scrutiny.
