## Claim
*Buddhi*, *ahaṃkāra* and *manas* are determinate internal functions: judgment, appropriation as "I/mine", and coordination of sensory and conceptual contents. Each is available to reflection as a content and an operation, each conditions what can appear, and together they compose the local world through which a subject acts. The subject remains the pole under which the functions and their products become available.

## Warrant
A remembered insult shows the three at work. Sensation, recollection and a possible alternative reading are coordinated (*manas*); a judgment settles what happened (*buddhi*); and appropriation gathers it under what was done to me (*ahaṃkāra*). Reopening the memory can change the presentations, the judgment, or the identity defended through it. The offices are distinguishable without forming a fixed temporal succession and without implying a little knower hidden among them, because each can be reflected on, conditioned and functionally decomposed. The Sāṃkhya source's ascertainment, egoity and mind keep their own doctrinal setting, and the Śaiva genealogy places the differentiated instrument within manifestation and recognition.

The same distinction stops us from identifying an artificial agent with its model, memory, policy or self-description. Each of these is a content or a means available inside the agent's operative interior, and none is the pole for which that interior is a world.

## Tension / limit
Technical comparison becomes exact at the level of office: determination, appropriation, coordination, memory and selection can be made inspectable in an artificial operative interior. The Indian apparatus gives those offices a philosophical anatomy, and the computational stack gives them another material and operational embodiment. The comparison is strongest where the differing grounds stay visible.
