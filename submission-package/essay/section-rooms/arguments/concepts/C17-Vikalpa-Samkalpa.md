# C17 — Vikalpa–Saṃkalpa


## #0 — A course arises within a circumstance

Vikalpa–saṃkalpa is the constructive movement through which a field of experience becomes a possible course. *Vikalpa* differentiates what is available into qualified alternatives, and *saṃkalpa* gathers selected differences into an intention, resolve or workable arrangement. They share the root `√kḷp`, to fit, to arrange, to make suitable, and each has its work within the other: an intention needs distinctions through which it can take form, and a distinction changes which intentions can be formed. Without them there is no practical cognition and no world to act in.

The circumstance is already active. Memory, language, concern and the available means give a possibility its present significance. Two people may bear the same name, and the situation lets a speaker address this person and not the other. [Name, instance and circumstance](../../../symbolon/episteme/sources/analytic-philosophy/kripke/kripke-1981-naming-and-necessity/kripke-1981-naming-and-necessity-NOTES.md) are held together before every condition of that address has been separately articulated, and constructive thought draws from this field and changes it by what it brings into focus.

## #1 — Differentiate what can be proposed

Differentiation and gathering are two directions of one formative power: a field is articulated into alternatives, and selected differences are composed into a course. Vikalpa makes a possibility determinate enough to compare, this rather than that, this under one description and not another, so that the alternatives receive their character through an act of qualification. The act specifies what would count as a different course, and a comparison made under another circumstance can alter the alternatives themselves as well as which of them is preferred.

The Buddhist logicians Dignāga and Dharmakīrti called the mind's construction of kinds vikalpa, the conceptual carving of a world into usable terms, and Bhartṛhari, the grammarian and an older contemporary of Dignāga, had argued that no cognition occurs without the accompaniment of the word: language permeates experience as experience forms and does not label an experience already finished. Centuries later the Śaiva teachers of Kashmir took up the analysis of vikalpa and gave it its widest reach. In the Spanda teaching, consciousness itself conceives the world-thought, *viśvavikalpa*, by turning from its pure I-ness (*ahantā*) toward thisness (*idantā*), and so produces the polarity of subject and object with every distinction by which a world can be described and handled.

The [inner instrument](C16-Antahkarana-Inner-Instrument.md) differentiates the work involved. Judgment determines, appropriation gathers a determination as mine or as belonging to this undertaking, and coordination holds presentations and alternatives available together, so that these functions give an intention material on which to work. [Apoha](C18-Apoha.md) makes one relation within that material exact, since a term's excluded contrast participates in what the term can mean. Forming a course uses such determinations and adds the question of what to do with them.

## #2 — Recollection becomes material for another possibility

A retained experience can be recalled without simply being repeated. In [Dyczkowski's account of reflection](../../../symbolon/episteme/sources/indian-philosophy/dyczkowski/dyczkowski-2000-doctrine-vibration/dyczkowski-2000-doctrine-vibration.md#page-71-continuation), an affection leaves a residual trace, remembrance recalls a prior affection, and imagination selects and recomposes retained material into a new one. Retention, recollection and composition are different capacities, and the difference between them is what allows the inherited field to become the material of a new formation.

Saṃkalpa gives selected possibilities a direction. Their gathering becomes an intention or commitment settled enough to guide an act, so that what was available as an alternative is now sustained as a course. The settlement changes the situation: something can be attempted, another possibility is left aside, and the attempt can meet a consequence that was not present in the first imagining.

The [constructive process](../../../symbolon/episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy.md) therefore includes both differentiation and recomposition. A course inherits conditions, selects among them and contributes another condition, and its return cannot be reduced to repeating the state from which it began.

## #3 — A formed course can conceal its formation

A dependable course permits continuity. It becomes [frozen conditioned will](C12-Script-Frozen-Conditioned-Will.md) when its alternatives and conditions can no longer affect how it is enacted, so that the achieved selection presents itself as necessity and what first organised a field begins to govern which evidence about that organisation is admissible. Spanda teaching names the bondage this creates: a fettered self is held by powers that arise with words, the *mātṛkā*, the "little mothers" of the alphabet that measure reality into forms and become the thoughts through which a person takes themselves to be this limited someone.

A generated account makes the arrest concrete. Its conclusion enters a later context, where it can be used as a conclusion drawn from a particular source, and if that relation disappears, the conclusion can take the source's place. A later account then confirms an earlier paraphrase while the original text recedes, and repetition increases the appearance of support without restoring the act of comparison that would establish it. Once the earlier paraphrase sets what the next account can admit, [the course repeats its own selection as necessity](../A07-Vikalpa-Samkalpa-Script-Frozen-Conditioned-Will.md) and loses the source's power to correct it.

The constructive pair remains the means of reopening it. Vikalpa differentiates the inherited account from its source and makes another reading available, and saṃkalpa gathers the recovered relation into a course that can now proceed differently. The next act can inherit a corrected source, answer or representation under a criterion that already fits the inquiry, and where the encounter exposes a failure in that criterion, the criterion itself becomes available for revision. The formation changes at the condition the comparison actually reaches, and the later judgment proceeds from that changed inheritance.

## #4 — Thought can turn upon its own determination

The [pure notion](../../../symbolon/episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-definition-god-draft3/taylor-2026-definition-god-draft3.md) carries the counter-operation within thought. Abhinavagupta's way of knowledge works through *śuddha vikalpa*, the purified thought-construct: a thought such as "I am Śiva", cultivated until the power of construction turns back toward its source and the impure constructs that bound the person loosen their hold. A śuddha-vikalpa is worked as a determinate formation that holds its own opposite and opens the conviction it would otherwise fix, and thought refines thought until the formed proposition can release its claim to enclose what it concerns. Its success lies in the recognition it makes possible, including recognition of its own limit. *Nirvikalpa* names awareness without constructed alternatives, and a thought that opens toward this limit still has its own work to perform. A mind whose constructs know themselves as constructs can relate to everything they construct without being owned by any of it.

A photograph gives the relation a visible form. A landscape appears under the conditions of an aperture, a frame and a sensitivity, and recognising those conditions changes what can be inferred from the image without taking away what the image shows. Devotion to the specific frame and openness to what exceeds it belong together, because the frame is a means of encounter whose particular disclosure matters.

The historical debate concerns the standing of such constructions as well as their operation. [MacCracken's discussion](../../../symbolon/episteme/sources/indian-philosophy/maccracken/maccracken-2017-recognizing-recognition/maccracken-2017-recognizing-recognition.md) **compares** Dharmakīrti's account of relations as epistemic with realist objections to a merely contrived relation, and an epistemic account and a realist account make different claims about what a relation constitutes, whether an epistemic relation or a real one. The disagreement stays active when the constructive operations are compared. [Prakāśa–vimarśa](C13-Prakasa-Vimarsa.md) gives appearing and reflexive self-articulation their particular Śaiva relation.

## #5→0 — The encounter changes what can be formed next

A [prompted action-space](C44-Prompt-Thrownness.md) inherits a task, a history and available means, and their arrangement conditions which alternatives can be proposed and carried forward. A returned source, refusal or consequence becomes effective through the condition it exposes: answer, source, task interpretation, representation, evaluator, permission or commission. Restoring a misquoted passage can change the interpretation and the next citation while retaining a fitting criterion of fidelity. If the rule admits only the earlier paraphrase, or a permission prevents the original from reaching judgment, return has to reach that rule or its responsible authority. The criterion changes when the encounter warrants that change, and reasoned retention can renew a course whose means now receive the source more faithfully. A correction that is recorded and never inherited by a later judgment leaves the return unperformed.

[Deferential intelligence](C47-Deferential-Intelligence.md) names the capacity for an encounter to reach the model or criterion as well as its answer. [Name-through-Count](../../../symbolon/episteme/etymologies/encounter-region-name-count/WHOLE-FIELD-encounter-region-name-count.md#name-through-count) restores the circumstance through which the selected account became addressable: another person can challenge the description under which they were received, and an original text can change the interpretation that has been made of it. The gathering becomes answerable to the relation it gathered.

A formed intention can then be retained, revised or relinquished for reasons the encounter supplies. Conditioning continues and its alternatives become available again, and the earlier act remains part of the history, which now supports a course it could not previously form.
