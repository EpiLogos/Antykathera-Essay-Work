# C01 — Subject / Defined Indefinability

## #0

Take the smallest sentence in which a knower appears: *I see these words.* It seems to offer three terms, an I, a seeing and some words, and two of them submit readily to inspection. The words are here in their black shapes, and the seeing can be investigated through the eye, attention, the language I read in and the light I read by, each finding making its operation more exact. Now turn the sentence on itself: *I am aware of seeing these words.* The seeing, which a moment ago was simply the way the page was there, has become something I am looking at, with its slight effort and the inner voice that sounds each word, so a process that seemed to supply my identity as the one who sees is now one more presentation to me. Aware of being aware, I am handed another object, and then another, and none of these turns delivers the one who is turning. A painter can make a faithful self-portrait, then a second that includes the painter making the first, then a third of the painter studying both; every canvas discloses more of the activity and its circumstances, and none takes the place of the present seeing in which the canvases are met.

The Subject is what this sequence leaves standing: **that to which an appearance is present, without itself becoming one more appearance alongside it.** A thought, a remembered scene, an ache, an act of judgment and the thought "this is me" can each become something known, and their being known already involves the Subject. Adding that involvement to the description produces a further known relation. It does not put the one to whom the description appears inside it as another item. Taylor's *Definition of God*, in its return to zero, puts the pressure directly: "I am undeniable, though nowhere to be seen." The word *I* here does not certify everything I say about myself. It names the first-person presence that an account of those sayings already enacts.

Descartes performed the first half of this finding and misdescribed it in the second. In 1641 he withheld assent from whatever could be doubted, and the doubt stopped at its own performance: let him be deceived as far as can be imagined, and the being deceived is still occurring. Then he asked what this I is and answered, a thinking thing, a *res cogitans*, whose activities include understanding, affirming, willing and sensing. Certainty of the occurrence and an account of what occurs are different achievements, and the finding has a fact form and a contents form. First-person immediacy is undeniable as a fact, by definition, since any denial is performed in the presence it denies. Its contents are not undeniable: a thought, a doubting, a self-image and the thinking-thing that *res cogitans* names are determinate occurrences within that presence, corrigible like every determination. Classical reification converted the condition of appearing into one more appearance and then treated the conversion as a capture of the condition, and the essay's crossed zero `Ø` marks the resulting occlusion. Denying the fact is impossible; reifying it is the opposite error; defined indefinability holds both edges at once.

Nor is the question merely theoretical. I can question a memory, revise my identity, fail to recognise myself in a portrait, or discover that a certainty was mistaken, and the questioning, the failure and the discovery are all present to me while they happen. What is at issue is whether my description of myself is correct only secondarily; first it is the presence within which correctness and error matter at all. [Defining the Subject faithfully](../A01-Subject-God-and-Faithful-Definition.md) therefore **grounds** itself in this distinction between the presence to which something is given and the something given, including every representation of that presence, and the defining act is itself present while its account becomes one more determination within that presence. That difference stays active when the inquiry moves from the predicates of God to a memory, a self-image or a report.

## #1

**Defined indefinability** is what a definition of the Subject yields when it is run to its end, and the tetralemma is the way to run it. We test the first definition ever given of it: the Subject is that which cannot be defined. Four honest answers offer themselves, as they do to anyone meeting a friend off a train after twenty years and asking whether she is the person we knew: it is, it is not, it both is and is not, it neither is nor is not.

At the first corner the definition says the Subject IS indefinable, and it seems fitted to its object because it puts nothing positive where the Subject should be. At the second corner it IS NOT, since "that which cannot be defined" is itself a determination: it marks a boundary, supplies a predicate and gives the Subject a place in speech, so in trying not to make an object of the Subject the sentence has made an object called *that which cannot be made an object*. At the third corner it BOTH IS AND IS NOT: as a representation the phrase fails, and as an act it succeeds, because the failure happens now, for someone, and discloses the limit it names. A definition that did not fail would have produced an idol, a closed image where a window was needed. At the fourth corner it NEITHER IS NOR IS NOT, because "is" and "is not" were positions taken in an act of determination, and the act has disclosed what their content could not: a position from which the positions were taken. Where an entity was expected, an activity appears. Graham Priest, formalising the scheme, has to add a fifth status for what none of the corners reach. Our count adds no fifth value, because what remains uncounted is the one who gave the four answers, and that one is no further answer.

What remains is recovered in two movements. In recognition, the one defining is found to have been implicated in the first proposition as in every one after it, so the subject of the proposition was always the Subject of the act. In return, recognition leaves the world of determinations standing, and what comes back is discrimination with the limit now included in it. We can then define the Subject positively, as the non-objectifiable pole of a differentiating event, the opening through which a determinate world is given. That is defined indefinability: the definition states why exhaustive objectification changes the office of its referent, since it converts the condition of appearing into an appearance and then treats the conversion as a capture of the condition.

It does not mean that the Subject is obscure because the correct description has not yet been found. A biography can identify a particular person, a physiological account can identify the conditions of an experience, and an introspective account can describe what the experience is like. None becomes worthless because it fails to exhaust the Subject. Each becomes more exact when it states what it determines and stops short of promoting that determination into the whole reality of the one concerned. An account [determines through a cut](C03-Determination.md): it selects, excludes and relates, and its [fidelity](C02-Faithful-Definition.md) keeps that selection answerable to the person or appearing it concerns.

*I am* and *I am this* are both meaningful statements, and they differ in office. The second can be true: I am this person, speaking here, with this history and concern. It turns false in office when *this* is made to exhaust the *am* through which the identification is lived. [Identification through difference](../A02-Copula-Self-Identity-through-Difference.md) **grounds** the point, because the person, the predicate and the circumstance stay related in any identifying sentence, and the predicate can state an identity while the present act of identifying remains its condition.

## #2

Several things usually called "self" have to stay distinguishable if this is to hold. **Selfing** is an activity of appropriation: a changing determination is taken as mine, joined to the claim of what I am, and installed as the owner of the field. A **self-thing** is that determination treated as a self-subsisting ground. A **bounded self** is a real situated horizon, this embodied life with its history, its limitations and its capacity to be addressed, and its dependence does not make it unreal. **Subjectivity-as-such** names the unobjectifiable determining presence, and the capitalised **Self**, in this project's full symbolic use, names the whole relation of Subject and its objective internality, not the Subject isolated from manifestation.

[Selfing and its acquired image](C35-Selfing-Self-Subjectivity-Self-Thing.md) **define** how a life becomes named, remembered and addressable. Exposing a fabricated self-thing does not abolish the presence in which the fabrication is recognised, and affirming the Subject does not restore the self-thing as an invisible proprietor behind appearances. Neither the image nor an imagined owner of the image can occupy this place.

In Taylor's [Watson encounter](../../../symbolon/episteme/sources/psychology/watson/watson-1998-resonance-of-emptiness/watson-1998-resonance-of-emptiness.md), which **sources** the Buddhist–psychotherapeutic side of the comparison, the critique of selfing reaches its object while retaining the experient to whom the critique appears. A changing transactional formation can be recognised as selfing, instead of being grasped as permanent, partless and autonomous. Watson's comparison concerns the constructed self-world, and Taylor carries it one step further, to the distinction between that formation and the Subject.

## #3

Nor is the Subject an inert witness placed behind a finished display. Knowing that I am does not first require an image of an unknown possessor and then a second observer who recognises it, because the knowing is present in the act of knowing. If it were not, a luminous display would remain unexperienced, like a reflection that no one sees, and an observer who must be observed by a further observer would send the demand for a knower back without end.

Kashmiri Śaiva philosophy gives this reflexivity two names. *Prakāśa* is the light of consciousness by which anything appears, and *vimarśa*, from the root *mṛś*, to touch, feel or examine, is the power by which that light grasps itself and whatever occurs within it. [Luminous self-apprehension](../A05-Prakasa-Vimarsa.md) **defines** their relation as one act, and [Dyczkowski's Śaiva account](../../../symbolon/episteme/sources/indian-philosophy/dyczkowski/dyczkowski-2000-doctrine-vibration/dyczkowski-2000-doctrine-vibration.md), in the available page-labelled discussion around p.70, distinguishes that reflective awareness from an insentient mirror or crystal. A crystal displays a reflected blue, and its display is not its recognition of blue. Defined indefinability takes over the operation: the one knowing the appearance remains present through the knowing, while any image of that one is another appearance, and the Śaiva distinction between luminous appearing and reflective awareness is what makes self-apprehension active.

Held in the full QL field, this relation does not freeze into an isolated pair. Its parent `/ = −/−` opens a determinate circumstance as `0/1`; `?/!` makes appearing answerable in question and assertion; `−/+` gives differentiation its force; `X/x` distinguishes determining capacity from its local instance; `AM/IS` makes the relation personed and contextual; `∞/dx` retains exact local difference within inexhaustible depth; and `1/0` returns the achieved determination toward its condition. Those eight turns traverse the sixfold body and are not eight interchangeable definitions of consciousness. `X/x` is Taylor's notation, and its precise consequence here is that no local `x`, including a self-image, exhausts the determining `X` it makes legible.

The [whole knowing relation](../../../symbolon/episteme/sources/internal-corpus/taylor/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy/taylor-2026-core-theorems-pithy.md) is what the theorem spine **derives** across these turns: an achieved determination can disclose the circumstance through which it became known and return to that condition. That complete sequence keeps the Subject in relation to its differentiated world, and its formal constructions retain their particular hypotheses and counted objects.

## #4

First-person presence is lived through a circumstance. Body, language, memory, feeling, attention, judgment and tools participate in that circumstance, and they can be investigated because they are determinate. Their being internal to a person does not make them identical with the Subject. Indian epistemology names the three offices at work in every act of knowing: *pramātṛ*, the knower; *pramāṇa*, the means of knowing; and *prameya*, the known. Dyczkowski's exposition of Kashmir Śaivism gives them an image: the means of knowledge is like the sun, illuminating the object; the object shines like the moon, by reflected light; and the subject is fire, which flames upward and, when perception is over, takes everything it has known back into itself. Thoughts, perceptions, memory and language belong to the middle office. They mediate what can become known without being the knower, since a thought can be watched, a desire resisted and an image of oneself altered, and each further reflection adds another *pramāṇa* and manufactures no further Subject behind the last. Through [Objective Internality](C41-Objective-Internality.md), which **grounds** the middle office, these means constitute the operative world in which something is available to know and act upon, and their mediation belongs to the lived relation between knower and known.

A second person makes the consequence unavoidable. I can know things about you and still have to hear your answer, because your presence is not another content I can produce by improving my image of you. An adult crouches to a frightened-looking child and says, kindly, "you're frightened", and the child shouts back, "I'm angry!", and is right. The child's answer corrects the description while keeping the one it concerns, and the person spoken about has spoken from inside the life under description. Inclusion within the whole gives my situated self no possession of your relation to that whole, and an [answering Other](../A27-Self-and-Other-Unity-without-Possession.md) **tests** the definition at its most exposed point, since the Other can correct the account or change the terms through which I approached them. In the Watson–Gans–Levinas encounter the face exceeds the egoic image held of it, and my viewpoint meets someone whose response it cannot supply.

This carries ontological force. In the order **Subject → objective internality → object**, a determinable world becomes available through the field of appearing, and a private ego is one particular formation within it. The [order of dependence](../A34-Idealism-Order-of-Dependence.md) **extends** the point, because subjective immediacy, mediating world and encountered object keep different offices, and defined indefinability positively determines the subject-pole: its asymmetry concerns the present act and its appearances, and a description's incompleteness is a lesser matter.

Memory, self-model, report and internal organisation are determinate contents and operations, and technical inspection makes the mediating field increasingly explicit. Within a local inquiry an interpreting agent functions as knower, its sources, memory, comparison rules and tools as means, and the addressed field as known. Within the containing person's inquiry, that complete apparatus occupies the means office, and a further inspection can make the local determining centre known. These relative offices remain within I-Consciousness, and no accumulation of determinate objects converts them into the unobjectifiable subject-pole to which their determination is present.

## #5→0

An image of the eye shows the structure in the body: an image of the organ becomes known, in a mirror or a diagram, while the present seeing remains the condition of that disclosure; [the eye's constitutive blindside](../../../symbolon/mytheme/worlds/frank-taylor/maya-eye-veil-frame-horizon/WHOLE.md#maya-eye-condition) **figures** it as a whole image, and the image does its work by what the eye can and cannot do. A mirror, a diagram or another observer can disclose more of the view's conditions, and each further disclosure is real and still appears through a situated act. That subject-pole cannot be recovered as an extra object that would complete the inventory, while the knowledge gained stays available for faithful use.

Return recognises the condition already involved in every portrait, judgment and attempt at recognition. The first-person force of *I am* remains, and its determinate descriptions become available for correction instead of being asked to stand as their own ground. [Recognition](C19-Pratyabhijna-Recognition.md) **returns-to** that source relation through the acquired determination, and in [integral zero's return](../A36-Advent-of-Integral-Zero.md) the achieved sign becomes Symbol, so that exact determination remains and is now answerable to what it cannot own.

Through [[symbolon/episteme/etymologies/symbol-account-and-trust/WHOLE-FIELD-symbol-account-and-trust|Symbol / Account / Trust]], a name discloses, an account makes the disclosure answerable, and trust permits committed use without possession of the source; the three keep distinct lexical histories and work in one operational relation. In [[section-rooms/00-integral-threshold/movements/02-s01-p1-define-subject|defining the Subject]] the defining act stays present within the account it gives, and at the [[section-rooms/07-instrument-returns/movements/47-s50-p4-idealism-horizon|idealist horizon]] the differentiated world and its means of appearing return to that presence. The account can be examined, corrected or retained as fitting, and the next act inherits the warranted judgment while the source remains more than its description.
