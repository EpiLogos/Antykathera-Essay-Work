Gebser - On the birth of perspective as the 3rd person essence of the mental-rational stage - the integral-intuitive stage is "4th" person, where perspective becomes **context**, where "seeing through" becomes "being with the text" or "being with the reading", including text and subtext in its scope. The achievement of context goes beyond perspective in that perspective, in trying to extricate seeing from what it sees (objectification), nullifies the mediating potential of seeing, which requires a passionate care for the outcome, the final cause or telos, of the situation observed. Context, by contextualising all seeing as inherently an engagement within the context of lived, embodied/ensouled experience, participatory experience, can actually achieve the equanimous objectivity that perspective fails to realise, in that context, "being with" what is observed or contended with, is *a priori* invested in the situation under observation (i.e it is situated within and in relation to it), yet all context are contextualised by the ultimate, transcendental "Rotundum", the en-compassing round of Unitary Being. That is to say, the ultimate context, as an *a-contextual contextualising*, is truly impartial, by definition not able to come from any limited perspective or perspectives. Perspective, despite its feints to the contrary, is always partial, only in a largely (and often wilfully) unconscious manner - a perspective falls for the joke of its own "objectivity" - and thus by and large objectification qua the mental-rational mode is in a deep sense capricious, and the caprice is so familiar and day to day; domination through aloofness. It is the classic "absent Father" principle, the Father whose abjection is a tyranny in its own right, a hauntingly conspicuous and present absence felt not only as an inherent lack (Lacanian desire and jouissance are born here), but as a marked *numbness to the lack*, a denial of the loss, a primordial, founding denial, such that a Son can go his own way, proclaim the name of the Father dead, for a faithless one may think "he might as well be". So the partiality of perspective is born of the caprice of the lack itself, which seeks in every object that which can fill the Father's empty shoes... even the image of the Father, the name of the Father, here are substitutes for his Presence. Each surrogate attempts to relocate that which, necessarily, is without situatedness, without implication in the fray, the absolute and transcendent, which to the chaos of life is the promise of law and order, mathematically the pattern and equation, which ensures us that intelligence guides the wild hand of natures. In the life of Man this appears as the ineluctable yet irreducible role of Truth in the life of mind and society, the aloof generator of all we take as real, the self-determination of what really is, and thus the de-termination of what truly counts. The lack that becomes of the Father is the very drive toward *the* standard, the tropic leaning toward the "be all and end all", towards the last word on what is. The Father is the power of truth and the truth of power, and for this very reason, being absent from the fray, is only ever imported as a scapegoat for the outcomes and conclusions we come to make in his name - this is our mistake, for the Father must be *seen-through* as a true per-spectus, or we end up locating him, either in ourselves or in our enemy. This is the prime failure of the mental-rational mode, to support the infrastructure of dissociation whilst in the same breath sullying the truly transcendent by failing to respect its ultimate inability to be objectified. Hence, the Subject as such is the quiet enemy of the mental-rational stage of Consciousness, where its own basis is occluded so definitively from its activity. This leads, as ever enantiodromic principles do, to a reversion of the original Father principle; it undergoes a shift from an implicit ordering and causing to a ruling that seeks to sit above its own law, which we can recognise as the structural form of injustice for which Jung's exegesis of Yahweh in Answer to Job serves as prime example. The hidden hand falls from a metaphysical station to a physical one; God orders no longer the life of soul, but of ego. The aloof order becomes, in the wake of a true relation to the transcendent, physically relative. Do we all not sense the hidden hand on the world stage, the hand of the salacious patriarchal spirit that never really comes home, down to the everyday level, to check in with the family? Seeking the Father, any idol close enough to the mould of our lack will, for a time, suffice.
