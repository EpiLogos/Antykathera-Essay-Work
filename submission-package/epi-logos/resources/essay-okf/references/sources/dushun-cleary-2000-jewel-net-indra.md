# Dushun (attributed) — The Jewel Net of Indra (Cleary, 2000)

## Bibliographic identity

The selected English carrier is Thomas Cleary's translation of “The Jewel Net of Indra,” printed under Tu-shun's name in *Dharma Rain*, pages 58–60. Kaza and Kraft drew the excerpt from Cleary's *Entry into the Inconceivable: An Introduction to Hua-yen Buddhism* (University of Hawai‘i Press, 1983), 66–68; that earlier container will receive its own source house if the essay cites material beyond this excerpt. The underlying Chinese passage belongs to the *Huayan wujiao zhiguan* (華嚴五教止觀, *Calming and Contemplation in the Five Teachings of Huayan*), conventionally attributed to Dushun (557–640), at Taishō canon T45, no. 1867, 513a28–513b21. Modern scholarship disputes that attribution, so the record preserves it as conventional provenance rather than settled authorship.

## Essay use

### Source's relevant contribution

The passage makes each jewel of Indra's net a site in which the remaining jewels appear, then follows the reflections recursively so that the whole net is implicated within every local jewel. This is the primary-text pathway for the essay's image of a field whose members disclose one another through their relations.

### Licensed essay use

- Establish the jewel-net as a Huayan image of reciprocal reflection and recursive implication.
- Give the myth history a textual route from the Indian figure of Indra into Chinese Huayan philosophical elaboration.
- Place the image beside Hephaestus's bonds as the source-bearing half of the essay's Offered double-net reading.

### Claim boundary

This passage does not derive apoha, Nāgārjuna's account of dependent arising, Kashmir Śaiva *saṃkalpa*, QL's `0/1`, or a theory of digital networks and commons governance. Those relations must be argued across their own sources. The Dushun attribution remains conventional and contested, and Cleary's printed pages must be collated before any English wording is promoted as quotation-ready.
