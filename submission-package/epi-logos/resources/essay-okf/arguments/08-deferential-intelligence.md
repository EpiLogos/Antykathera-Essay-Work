# Deferential Intelligence

## Claim

An intelligence becomes more trustworthy when it carries the limits of its present frame as operational structure rather than modest rhetoric. It exposes source, aperture, excluded alternatives, confidence, counterclaim, and what remains undecidable before consequential action.

## Warrant

Every achieved determination is local to a nomological complex: model, prompt, memory, tools, permissions, evidence, objective, and milieu. Preserving those conditions makes the determination revisable and lets another participant locate the source of disagreement. Erasing them makes a local output perform as a view from nowhere. Deference therefore increases exactness by retaining the `0` that the present `1` cannot occupy.

Deference is an active return to `1/0`: it releases an unjustified claim to completion, exposes the conditions of the present determination, and enables a better-grounded next act.

## Operational test

The claim is testable at the functional register: compare consequential decisions made with and without explicit disclosure of origin, aperture, exclusions, confidence, counterclaim, and undecidable remainder. The relevant outcome is improved delegation, correction, and answerability—not a performance of self-abasement.

## Claim boundary

Deference is neither generic uncertainty language nor refusal to decide. A system may act decisively where its warrant is strong; it must expose the boundary at which that warrant ends. Functional self-return also supplies no proof of phenomenal subjectivity.
