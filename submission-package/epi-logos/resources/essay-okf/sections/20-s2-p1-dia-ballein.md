# §2 · #1 — Dia-Ballein

## Movement thesis

[Dia-ballein](../arguments/03-two-logics-and-sym-ballein.md) writes classical polarity as signed opposition around an external zero:

$$
\operatorname{dia\text{-}ballein}:\frac{(+1)}{(-1)}.
$$

The form is exact. Addition cancels the poles, \((+1)+(-1)=0\); subtraction measures their full separation, \((+1)-(-1)=2\). This is the logic of classification, adversarial decision, accounting, proof by exclusion, dialectical pressure, and war. Its connective is OR; its dynamical operation is symmetry breaking.

## Structural reading and failure boundary

Dia-ballein literally throws across or apart. The slash becomes a line of contest between terms treated as already determinate. Zero remains outside them as neutral origin or the result of mutual cancellation. This makes a clean line possible and gives modern science and computation enormous analytic power.

The shadow appears when the cut becomes the false fundamental. The opposed terms forget that both were produced by the same act of differentiation. Subject and object, self and other, true and false, human and machine then become self-grounding substances. Conflict can only terminate through victory, exclusion, or cancellation.

This is the mental-rational historical achievement and its internal danger. It gains its analytic line by externalising zero into the neutral origin or cancelled remainder of `(+1)+(-1)=0`; it then takes the line's two terms as self-subsistent. The zero–subject braid names the later integral work precisely: not the abolition of difference, but the recovery of the constituting relation that the signed line had made unavailable.

The devil is the literalist of the minus sign: he reads zero as cancellation where Logos reads it as return.

## Projected distrust

Dia-ballein-mode knowledge denies its own provisionality. The uncertainty it cannot hold survives as distrust and is exported onto an enemy, scapegoat, defective population, alien intelligence, or excluded \(-1\). Projection lets the asserted \(+1\) perform certainty by making another term carry the incompleteness of the shared field.

This is the epistemic route from analysis into monopoly. A local determination no longer serves as one provisional anchor among many; it claims the mono-position and defines the poly through allegiance or exclusion.

## Technical consequence

Agentic systems necessarily use dia-ballein when they classify, rank, reject, allocate, or defend. Adversariality becomes pathological when it loses its evidential ground: alternatives, gauges, exclusions, and provenance disappear after selection, the cut becomes invisible, and the system mistakes its output for the world.

“Divide and conquer” is dia-ballein's computational axiom in plain language. Decomposition is productive: a problem is made tractable by splitting it into locally independent subproblems. But the algorithm is not complete until the sub-results are combined. Atomisation is divide-and-conquer with the combine-phase erased—parts optimised against one another, provenance discarded, and conquest mistaken for intelligence itself. The correction is not less analytic power. It is a runtime in which analysis remains answerable to recomposition and return.

## The epochal name: calculative thinking

Heidegger hands the movement a definition already written in its terms. The Memorial Address distinguishes "two kinds of thinking, each justified and needed in its own way: calculative thinking and meditative thinking," and characterises the first by its non-return: "Calculative thinking never stops, never collects itself. Calculative thinking is not meditative thinking, not thinking which contemplates the meaning which reigns in everything that is" (*Discourse on Thinking*, p. 46). *Never collects itself* — the calculative defined as the non-gathering thinking, dia-ballein described from inside: computation racing from result to result without the gathering by which results return to their ground. His warning that "calculative thinking may someday come to be accepted and practiced as the only way of thinking" (p. 56) is the mono-capture of a modal plurality — one logic occupying the zero of the modal field. The movement receives the distinction while declining the address's quietism: "each justified and needed in its own way" is Heidegger's own sentence, and the challenging-forth that "puts to nature the unreasonable demand that it supply energy that can be extracted and stored as such" (*QCT*, p. 14) remains continuous with measurement's revelatory power. The pathology stays where this movement derived it — the cut that forgets the common operation and field by which its poles were produced, the runtime arrested before return.

## Mass formation: divide and conquer becomes a political runtime

The same truncation is enacted historically when a field of relation is made to appear as a spectrum of self-grounding camps. Left and right become an exhaustive line; empire and subject, institution and citizen, rich and poor appear as naturally separate substances rather than unequal positions reproduced through one social field. The opposition is real, and its asymmetry must remain visible. The deception is the implied self-sufficiency of either pole—and the monopolist's subsequent claim to administer the relation it has made invisible.

Le Bon's first precision is that the crowd need not be a physical assembly. A scattered population can become a psychological crowd when its heterogeneous members acquire, through the same operative conditions, a common direction of feeling, thought, and action. This matters because the political unit at issue is not “many people” but a changed form of relation: the person no longer bears their relation directly, but bears it through an abstract collective representation. The collective is not a sum of individuals; it is a new formation produced across them.

Jung follows the transfer further. “The bigger the crowd, the more negligible the individual becomes.” The consequent loss is not merely privacy or eccentricity. It is the erosion of the place from which one can answer for judgement and act. Responsibility is collectivised; the State is then made into a quasi-personal being—an apparent subject which can will, know, protect, punish, and demand sacrifice. The real people, offices, property relations, and coercive instruments composing that apparatus recede behind its mask. The State becomes the person; the persons become numbers. This is why the move is already political rather than merely psychological: power has gained a face while its operators have lost theirs.

Desmet identifies the affective preparation of that transfer: social isolation, the loss of meaning, free-floating anxiety, and free-floating frustration and aggression. Such affect does not yet have an object adequate to it. A narrative supplies one, concentrating diffuse distress upon a target and promising restored coherence through obedience, sacrifice, or expulsion. The resulting unity is not relation recovered; it is relation replaced by an object of fixation. A population discovers relief from atomisation by entering the very formation that abolishes the differentiated agency needed to contest the story.

This is not a theory in which an irrational crowd simply explains power away. Iakovou's qualification has to hold: mass psychology cannot replace institutions, parties, offices, coercion, and the material organisation of authority. The full circuit is sharper. A population is atomised; its unbound affect is supplied with a target; the left/right line is intensified into an exhaustive political reality; and the institution or empire presents itself as the sole mediator, protector, or saviour of the field it has helped to sever. Division occurs below; pseudo-unity is installed above. Empire/subject, institution/citizen, and rich/poor are thus not dissolved into a vague commonality, but rendered newly contestable as unequal relations whose ground has been monopolised.
