## Source scholarship

The Court held that the city's taking of petitioners' property under an integrated economic development plan qualified as a "public use" within the Takings Clause (syllabus, pp. 477–490; Part IV, 483–90, applies the doctrine to the plan and states the holding at 484; "Promoting economic development is a traditional and long-accepted function of government," 484). The facts: New London had been designated a "distressed municipality" (1990); the Naval Undersea Warfare Center in Fort Trumbull closed in 1996; the New London Development Corporation (NLDC), "a private nonprofit entity" reactivated to plan revitalization, produced a plan for 90 acres, approved by the city council in January 2000, which designated the NLDC its development agent and authorised it to purchase property or acquire it by eminent domain in the City's name (473–75). Pfizer announced in 1998 a $300 million research facility adjacent to Fort Trumbull (473). The plan's seven parcels included a waterfront conference hotel (parcel 1), about 80 new residences (parcel 2), and at least 90,000 square feet of research and development office space immediately north of the Pfizer facility (parcel 3) (474). Petitioners were nine owners of 15 properties; ten of the parcels were occupied by owner or family, five held as investments; none was alleged to be blighted (475).

- **Use boundary:** the Court held that this city's plan, carefully considered and comprehensive, satisfied the public-use requirement; it did not hold that any transfer to a private owner qualifies (Part III, at 477, states that a taking for the sole purpose of transferring property from A to B, or for conferring a private benefit on a particular private party, would be forbidden).
