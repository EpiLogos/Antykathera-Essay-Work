## Source scholarship

A general English dictionary with etymologies traced to Proto-Indo-European roots. Its brackets are compact and summarise; they are lexicographic claims, not independent philological demonstrations.

## Passage card — `ahd-2022-english-language-fifth-edition-q001` — science and scission: “see skei-”

**Paraphrase:** The etymology bracket for *science* derives it from Latin *scientia*, from the present participle of *scīre*, “to know,” and refers the reader to *skei-* in the Indo-European roots appendix; that for *scission* derives it from Late Latin *scissiō* and Latin *scissus*, past participle of *scindere*, “to cut, split,” likewise referring to *skei-*.

- **Locator:** s.vv. *science*, *scission*, etymology brackets (https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=science; https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=scission)
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Entries read 2026-10-04; no quotation promoted.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Public corroboration that the standard English reference ties *scīre* and *scindere* to one root, *skei-*.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-scire

## Passage card — `ahd-2022-english-language-fifth-edition-q002` — con: short for confidence

**Paraphrase:** The entry *con* (slang) defines the verb “to swindle (a victim) by first winning the victim’s confidence” and the noun “a swindle,” with the adjective use in *con artist* and *con job*, and gives the etymology as short for *confidence*.

- **Locator:** s.v. *con*, the verb/noun/adjective entry closing “[Short for CONFIDENCE.]” (https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=con)
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Entry read 2026-10-04; no quotation promoted. The entry gives no date; dating rests on the New-York Herald report and Etymonline.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Confirms the clipping *con* < *confidence*; does not date it.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-con

## Passage card — `ahd-2022-english-language-fifth-edition-q003` — digit: from Latin digitus

**Paraphrase:** *digit* is derived through Middle English from Latin *digitus*, “finger, toe,” referring to the root *deik-* in the Indo-European appendix.

- **Locator:** s.v. *digit*, etymology bracket (https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=digit)
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Entry read 2026-10-04; no quotation promoted. The entry’s etymology does not itself state the numeral sense; see Etymonline and Lewis and Short s.v. *digitus* (computare digitis).
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Confirms the Latin source of *digit*.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-digit

## Passage card — `ahd-2022-english-language-fifth-edition-q004` — manipulation: from French, Spanish and Latin manipulus

**Paraphrase:** *manipulation* is derived from French, which the entry takes from Spanish *manipulación* (originally of implements and substances in alchemical procedures), from Latin *manipulus*, “sheaf, handful”; the entry refers to *maniple*.

- **Locator:** s.v. *manipulation*, etymology bracket (https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=manipulation)
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Entry read 2026-10-04; no quotation promoted. Etymonline instead gives French *manipulation* from *manipule*, “handful” (a pharmacists’ measure), a different intermediate route to the same Latin word.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Both reference works derive *manipulation* through French from Latin *manipulus*; the intermediate stages differ.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-digit

## Passage card — `ahd-2022-english-language-fifth-edition-q005` — savour: Old French savour from Latin sapor

**Paraphrase:** The English noun *savour* comes from Middle English *savour*, from Old French, from Latin *sapor*, from *sapere*, “to taste,” with a reference to the root *sep-* in the Indo-European appendix.

- **Locator:** s.v. *savor* (also spelled *savour*), etymology bracket (https://ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=savor)
- **Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only
- **Verification:** Entry read 2026-10-04; no quotation promoted.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Supports “*savour* comes through Old French *savour* from *sapor*.”
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker s01-savoir
