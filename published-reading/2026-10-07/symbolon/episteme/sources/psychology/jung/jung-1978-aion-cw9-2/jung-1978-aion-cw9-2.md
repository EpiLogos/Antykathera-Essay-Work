## Essay use

### Source's relevant contribution

- A primary Jung work for the Self, shadow, anima/animus, symbolic totality, and the historically situated Christ/Antichrist polarity.

Within the zero–subject historical braid, *Aion* supports the Jungian psychic register and its symbolic totality, not the authorial `X/x` matheme. ### jung-1978-aion-cw9-2-p023 — The two fishes of Pisces and the aeon

### jung-1978-aion-cw9-2-p028 — The four quaternios and Jung's own names

**Locator:** ¶402, printed pp. 256, 259 (PDF 268, 271; plate II intervenes); ¶403, pp. 259–260; ¶404, p. 260; ¶406, p. 261; ¶410, pp. 262–264 (PDF 274–276).

**Located operation:** Looking back, Jung names the "Positive Moses" or Anthropos Quaternio and the Paradise Quaternio as the two Gnostic quaternities, and says he felt impelled to insert a Shadow Quaternio between Man and Serpent, a quaternity not mentioned in the texts, because a psyche oriented only upwards is no longer conceivable; he calls this a specifically modern state of affairs and, in Gnostic terms, an anachronism (¶402). The shadow is both regrettable weakness and healthy instinctivity, prerequisite for higher consciousness (¶402). The Paradise Quaternio with the lapis leads to the beginnings of natural science (¶404); the development runs from the Shadow to the Lapis Quaternio (¶406). In the formula, the Anthropos A descends through his Shadow B into Physis C (= serpent) and rises through the lapis D; the four sets are named "kingless race," Shadow Quaternio, "Paradise," and the world of matter (¶410).

**Naming finding:** Jung's own name for the third quaternio is the **Paradise Quaternio** (¶¶402, 404; "Paradise" again in ¶410). "Physis" is the letter C of the formula (Physis = serpent, ¶410), not Jung's name for the quaternio. Jung's footnote to ¶402 specifies the earthly Garden of Eden, not heaven. Anthropos, Shadow, Paradise, Lapis is therefore the source-faithful naming; "Physis" may be used only as the formula's term for the third state.

**Status:** locator-verified; paraphrase-only.

**Verification:** as p023.

**Source relation:** Paraphrased; the Shadow Quaternio is Jung's own reconstruction by his explicit statement.

### jung-1978-aion-cw9-2-p030 — The shadow's positive contents; scrutiny of oneself

**Locator:** ¶423, printed pp. 266–267 (PDF 282–283); ¶424, pp. 267–268 (PDF 283–284).

**Located operation:** The shadow proves not to consist only of morally reprehensible tendencies but also of good qualities, such as normal instincts, appropriate reactions, realistic insights and creative impulses; all archetypes have favourable and unfavourable effects; the self is a *complexio oppositorum*; good and evil receive their moral accent only within human endeavour (¶423). It is more to the point to scrutinise one's own moods than another's errors, since only knowing where oneself goes wrong lets one do something about it (¶424).
