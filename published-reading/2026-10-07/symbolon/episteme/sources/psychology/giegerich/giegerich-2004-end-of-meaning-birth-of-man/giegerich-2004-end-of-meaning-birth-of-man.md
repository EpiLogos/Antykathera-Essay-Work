### giegerich-2004-end-of-meaning-birth-of-man-p001 — The birth of man (section 7)

**Provenance:** Locator:** §7, "The birth of man" (author's PDF about pp.

**Locator:** §7, "The birth of man" (author's PDF about pp. 18–20); the unborn side is §6, "Man the unborn." Journal pages unverified.

**Located operation:** The abstract says that man, though biologically born, remained psychologically unborn, with language, myth and metaphysics serving as a secondary psychological uterus, and that the changes since about 1800 can be seen as the eventual birth of man. The essay's sections are: 1 the self-contradiction in the search for meaning; 2 in-ness as the reality of the pre-modern ages; 3 the end of in-ness; 4 two lines of reaction; 5 Jung's idea of the death of symbols; 6 man the unborn; 7 the birth of man; 8 the fate of God(s); 9 critique of the feeling of loss and need; 10 the logic and genesis of Jung's psychology.
