# Keynes — Newton, the Man (1947)

[Source index](../../../README.md)

## Bibliographic identity

Keynes wrote the essay for the Royal Society’s Newton tercentenary celebrations, planned for 1942 and held on 15–19 July 1946; Keynes died in April 1946 and his brother Geoffrey Keynes read it. It was printed in *Newton Tercentenary Celebrations, 15–19 July 1946* (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press for the Royal Society, 1947), pp. 27–34 (the range is the standard citation; the 1947 volume was not seen). The same essay is reprinted as ch. 35 of *The Collected Writings of John Maynard Keynes*, vol. 10, *Essays in Biography*, ed. Elizabeth Johnson and Donald Moggridge (Royal Economic Society), pp. 363–74 (Cambridge Core record checked; the Cambridge Core page gives 1978 for that online edition). Wording below was checked against the MacTutor (St Andrews) transcription of the essay, a secondary host; Keynes’s text is out of copyright (author died 1946). agent G6b, 2026-10-04.

## Source scholarship

Keynes had examined the Newton papers sold at Sotheby’s in 1936. The essay’s thesis is that Newton was not the first of the age of reason but “the last of the magicians”; its claim about the *Principia* is that the geometrical form of the exposition does not reflect the mental processes of discovery.

## Passage card — `keynes-1947-newton-the-man-q003` — the universe as a riddle; clues in nature and in ancient tradition

> Why do I call him a magician? Because he looked on the whole universe and all that is in it as a riddle, as a secret which could be read by applying pure thought to certain evidence, certain mystic clues which God had laid about the world to allow a sort of philosopher’s treasure hunt to the esoteric brotherhood. He believed that these clues were to be found partly in the evidence of the heavens and in the constitution of elements (and that is what gives the false suggestion of his being an experimental natural philosopher), but also partly in certain papers and traditions handed down by the brethren in an unbroken chain back to the original cryptic revelation in Babylonia.
