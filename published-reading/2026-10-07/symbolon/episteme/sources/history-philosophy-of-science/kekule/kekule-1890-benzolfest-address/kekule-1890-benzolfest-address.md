# Kekulé — Benzolfest Address (1890)

[Source index](../../../README.md)

## Bibliographic identity

Kekulé’s address at the Benzolfest, the celebration held by the Deutsche Chemische Gesellschaft in Berlin on 11 March 1890 to mark twenty-five years since his 1865 paper on the structure of benzene. It is printed within Gustav Schultz’s report, “Bericht über die Feier der Deutschen Chemischen Gesellschaft zu Ehren August Kekulé’s,” *Berichte der Deutschen Chemischen Gesellschaft* 23 (1890): 1264–1312 (the volume index lists the report at 1265), with the address at 1302–11. The report’s span and the index entry were checked in the subject index of the archive.org scan of the Berichte volume 23 (Referate part: “Feier der Deutschen chemischen Gesellschaft zu Ehren August Kekulé’s, G. Schultz 1265”) and in note 2 of an article in the GDCh *Mitteilungen* Fachgruppe Geschichte der Chemie 3 (1989) (which cites the report as 1264–1312); the address’s page range 1302–11 is the standard citation (Wikipedia gives the same) and was not confirmed against a page image (agent G6b, 2026-10-04). English translation: O. Theodor Benfey, “August Kekulé and the Birth of the Structural Theory of Organic Chemistry in 1858,” *Journal of Chemical Education* 35, no. 1 (1958): 21–23, https://doi.org/10.1021/ed035p21 (DOI and pages checked in Crossref; the translation’s contents not read).

## Source scholarship

The snake episode is told as an after-dinner retrospect, twenty-five years after the structure paper. Historians of chemistry treat the account with caution: Wotiz doubts that the text printed in the Berichte is wholly authentic (as reported in GDCh *Mitteilungen* 3 (1989)), and Alan J. Rocke, *Image and Reality: Kekulé, Kopp, and the Scientific Imagination* (University of Chicago Press, 2010), studies the story’s reliability. Neither reading changes what the prose says, which reports what Kekulé said in 1890 and not that the vision occurred as told; both are recorded here as qualifications and are not cited in the printed note.

## Passage card — `kekule-1890-benzolfest-address-q001` — the snake seizing its own tail

**Paraphrase:** Kekulé tells the Benzolfest audience that, half asleep before his fire, he saw atoms in motion, “schlangenartig sich windend und drehend”; one of the snakes seized its own tail and whirled before him; he woke as if by lightning and spent the rest of the night working out the consequences of the hypothesis.

- **Locator:** Address, Berichte 23 (1890): 1302–11; the snake passage falls in the later part of the address (commonly cited as p. 1306; page not confirmed here).
- **Status:** paraphrase-only.
- **Verification:** The German wording was read as quoted in GDCh *Mitteilungen* Fachgruppe Geschichte der Chemie 3 (1989), 37–38 (the quotation there is attributed to the Berichte address); not read in a page image of the Berichte. agent G6b, 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased.

- **Use boundary:** Warrants the prose’s description of what Kekulé said in 1890: a dozing vision of snake-like forms, one seizing its own tail, then a night spent working out the hypothesis. It does not establish that the event occurred as narrated.
