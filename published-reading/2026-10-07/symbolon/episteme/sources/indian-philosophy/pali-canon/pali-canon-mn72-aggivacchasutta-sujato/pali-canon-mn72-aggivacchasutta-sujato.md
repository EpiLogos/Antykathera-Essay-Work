## Source scholarship

The wanderer Vacchagotta asks the Buddha whether the cosmos is eternal, whether it is finite, whether the soul is the same as the body, and whether a realized one (Tathāgata) exists after death, in ten questions. For the last question he puts all four forms in turn: that a realized one still exists after death, no longer exists, both still exists and no longer exists, and neither still exists nor no longer exists. To each the Buddha answers, "That's not my view, Vaccha." The Buddha later calls each of these convictions a "thicket of views" and says he avoids all of them (13–14). The sutta then moves to the simile of a fire gone out.

## Passage card — `pali-canon-mn72-aggivacchasutta-sujato-q001` — the four forms put and declined

> "Then is this your view: 'A realized one still exists after death. This is the only truth, anything else is futile'?" ... "That's not my view, Vaccha."

(The same question follows for "no longer exists," "both still exists and no longer exists," and "neither still exists nor no longer exists," each answered "That's not my view, Vaccha.")
