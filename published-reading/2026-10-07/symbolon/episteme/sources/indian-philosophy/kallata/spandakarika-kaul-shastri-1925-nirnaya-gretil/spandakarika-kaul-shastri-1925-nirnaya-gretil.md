## Source scholarship

Kārikā 1.4 answers two rival accounts of the self, which Kṣemarāja names in his introduction to the verse (`VSpkC_1.3:19`): the Buddhists (*saugatāḥ*), for whom reality is a stream of cognitions (*jñānasaṃtāna*), and the Mīmāṃsakas, for whom the self known in I-cognition is always obscured by pleasure and the like. The verse places the cognitions "I am happy", "I am miserable", "I am attached" in "another" (*anyatra*) in which the states of pleasure and the rest are strung through (*anusyūte*). Kṣemarāja's first reading identifies that other as the abider through the states (*avasthātṛ*), the principle of the self (*ātmatattva*), in which the cognitions rest turned inward; without it, momentary cognitions could not be connected (*anusaṃdhāna*). His second reading, offered to answer the Mīmāṃsakas, applies "strung through" to the knower in the form of the subtle body (*puryaṣṭaka-pramātṛ*), woven warp and weft (*otaprota*) by those states, and not to the Self of consciousness and bliss (*cidānandaghana*), which is never obscured by them.

### spandakarika-kaul-shastri-1925-nirnaya-gretil-p002 — Nirṇaya 1.4: the abider through the states (supporting)

> ya evāhaṃ sukhī sa eva duḥkhī … ityādayaḥ saṃvido jñānāni tā anyatreti avasthātary ātmatattve vartante tatraivāntarmukhe viśrāmyanti sphuṭaṃ svasākṣikaṃ kṛtvā

- **Locator:** Kṣemarāja, *Spandanirṇaya* on 1.4 (GRETIL `VSpkC_1.4:1`; ellipsis marks an omitted clause on attachment and aversion). The following sentence (`VSpkC_1.4:2`) adds that otherwise, since momentary cognitions are exhausted in themselves, their connection (*anusaṃdhāna*) would be impossible.
- **Status:** quotation-ready (Sanskrit, GRETIL); rendering for paraphrase: the very one who is happy is the one who is miserable; these cognitions abide "in another", namely in the abider through the states, the principle of the self, and rest there turned inward, evident as self-witnessed.

### spandakarika-kaul-shastri-1925-nirnaya-gretil-p003 — Nirṇaya 1.4: the second reading, the subtle-body knower woven through (qualifying)

> ahaṃ sukhītyādisaṃvido yās tā anyatreti puryaṣṭakasvarūpe pramātari sukhādyavasthābhir anusyūte otaprotarūpe sphuṭaṃ lokapratītisākṣikaṃ vartante tiṣṭhanti na tv asmadabhyupagate 'smiṃś cidānandaghane śaṃkarātmani svasvabhāve

- **Locator:** *Spandanirṇaya* on 1.4 (GRETIL `VSpkC_1.4:9`), introduced at `VSpkC_1.4:8` as the explanation to be given in answer to the Mīmāṃsakas.
- **Status:** quotation-ready (Sanskrit, GRETIL); rendering for paraphrase: the cognitions "I am happy" and the like abide in the knower in the form of the subtle body (*puryaṣṭaka*), strung through and woven warp and weft by the states of pleasure and the rest, as common experience attests, and not in our Self, dense with consciousness and bliss, Śiva, one's own nature.
- **Verification:** agent, 2026-10-05; GRETIL TEI file read in place.
- **Source relation:** Extracted; qualifies.
- **Consumer:** §4 · #3 (reworked draft, 2026-10-05).
- **Use boundary:** the commentary gives "strung through" two placements: in the first, the threaded series rests in the abiding self; in the second, the subtle-body knower is what is threaded by the states. The essay's image of the thread running through every cognition draws on the first reading and must keep the second in view.
