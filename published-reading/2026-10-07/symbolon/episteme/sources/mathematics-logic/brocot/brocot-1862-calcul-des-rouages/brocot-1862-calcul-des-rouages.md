## Source scholarship

Brocot, a Paris clockmaker ("horloger" on the title page), addresses a workshop problem. A required ratio of speeds between two arbors often cannot be realised with practical tooth counts. The task is then to find a nearby ratio whose numerator and denominator factor conveniently into wheel and pinion counts. He judges continued fractions, the usual tool, to be beyond most practitioners. His method rests on what happens when two fractions are added "terme à terme" (p. 4), that is, on the mediant `(a + c)/(b + d)`.

Part One proves the propositions the method needs. Proposition 3 (pp. 5–6) states the determinant property. If `a/b` exceeds `c/d` by `1/bd`, then `ad − bc = 1`. Proposition 5 (p. 6) shows that the term-by-term sum of two unequal fractions falls strictly between them. Proposition 7 (p. 8) shows that if two fractions differ by one over the product of their denominators, their mediant differs from each in the same way. Proposition 6 (pp. 7–8) shows that the mediant has larger terms than either fraction. The practical part works an example. The ratio `23/17` is bracketed between `1/1` and `2/1`, mediants are inserted step by step, and an error column is kept alongside (pp. 12–14).

**What Brocot does not do.** In the pages read (pp. 3–14) Brocot starts from two neighbouring approximations of a given ratio, `1/1` and `2/1` in his example. He does not start from `0/1` and `1/0`. His object is a finite table of approximations to one target ratio, not the infinite tree of all positive rationals. The tree rooted at `0/1` and `1/0` and the theorem that every positive fraction appears once in lowest terms belong to the later synthesis (see Graham, Knuth and Patashnik, *Concrete Mathematics* (1994)). The integer additive scheme and the coprimality theorems are Stern's (see [[stern-1858-zahlentheoretische-funktion]]). Pages 15–97 were not read.

## Passage card — `brocot-1862-calcul-des-rouages-q004` — neighbours differ by one over the product of denominators: ad − bc = 1
^brocot-1862-calcul-des-rouages-q004

> *Si une fraction a/b surpasse une autre fraction c/d de l'unité divisée par le produit des deux dénominateurs, ou de 1/bd, le produit a d des termes extrêmes surpasse d'une unité le produit b c des moyens.*

> […] donc a d − b c = 1.

> Si deux fractions a/b, c/d diffèrent entre elles de l'unité divisée par le produit de leurs dénominateurs, la fraction (a + c)/(b + d) formée en les ajoutant terme à terme diffère aussi de l'une quelconque des proposées de l'unité divisée par le produit des deux dénominateurs.

## Essay uses

§1 · #5→0 (reworked draft, 2026-10-05): Brocot is the second half of the Stern–Brocot attribution. He can be cited for the clockmaker's problem, for adding fractions term by term as the basis of the method, for the mediant lying between its parents, and for `ad − bc = 1` between neighbours. He cannot be cited for the tree rooted at `0/1` and `1/0`.
