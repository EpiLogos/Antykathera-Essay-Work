## Source scholarship

The essay opens with the question how equality can be informative: a = a and a = b are statements of different cognitive value (p. 25). Frege's first worked example of two designations of one object is the point of intersection of the lines a and b and of b and c (p. 26); he then distinguishes the *Bedeutung* of a sign from its *Sinn*, which contains the mode of presentation (*Art des Gegebenseins*), and gives "Abendstern" and "Morgenstern" as having the same *Bedeutung* but not the same *Sinn* (p. 27). The same pair returns on p. 32 (the thought of "the morning star is a body illuminated by the sun" against that of "the evening star ..." and the person who does not know that the evening star is the morning star) and on pp. 37–38, where Frege says the *Bedeutung* of "Morgenstern" is the planet Venus except in indirect speech.

## Passage card — `frege-1892-ueber-sinn-und-bedeutung-q001` — equality informative across a difference of presentation

Paraphrase: a = a and a = b differ in cognitive value; the difference lies in the sign's differing way of giving the same object (*Art des Gegebenseins*); the point of intersection of two pairs of lines illustrates it; the morning star and the evening star have the same *Bedeutung* and different *Sinn*.
