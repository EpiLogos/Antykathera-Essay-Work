# Frege — Grundgesetze der Arithmetik (1893, 1903)

## Bibliographic identity

Gottlob Frege, *Grundgesetze der Arithmetik, begriffsschriftlich abgeleitet*, 2 vols. (Jena: Hermann Pohle, 1893 and 1903). Vol. 1 appeared in 1893, vol. 2 in 1903. The consulted object is the Internet Archive's Google Books scan, item `bub_gb_LZ5tAAAAMAAJ` (title page “Grundgesetze der Arithmetik … Von Dr. G. Frege … I. Band. Jena, Verlag von Hermann Pohle, 1893”); the one scan holds both volumes in sequence (vol. 1, then vol. 2 with its own pagination), and its page heads give the printed page numbers. Public domain. A second scan of vol. 1 is `11505810`.

## Essay use

Primary source for the principle (Basic Law V) from which Russell derived the paradox and for Frege's own response to the letter of June 1902. The essay paraphrases Basic Law V as “any condition we can state clearly gathers a collection”; strictly, Law V states that the value-range of one function coincides with that of another exactly when the two functions agree at every argument, and the existence of a value-range for every concept is built into Frege's notation (§§3, 9). The paraphrase is the usual popular form of the principle, and the note says so.

**Claim boundary:** the source warrants Frege's statement of Law V, his concession of the contradiction and his proposed repair. It does not warrant the essay's account of what Frege did afterwards; that rests on secondary scholarship.

### frege-1893-1903-grundgesetze-q001 — Basic Law V (vol. 1, §20, p. 36)

> Wir sahen (§ 3, § 9), dass eine Werthverlaufsgleichheit immer in eine Allgemeinheit einer Gleichheit umsetzbar ist und umgekehrt: [formula (V)]

### frege-1893-1903-grundgesetze-q002 — The afterword to vol. 2 (pp. 253–265)

> Einem wissenschaftlichen Schriftsteller kann kaum etwas Unerwünschteres begegnen, als dass ihm nach Vollendung einer Arbeit eine der Grundlagen seines Baues erschüttert wird.

- **Locator:** vol. 2 (1903), “Nachwort” (afterword), pp. 253–65, dated at its end “Jena, im Oktober 1902”; the sentence opens the piece on p. 253. The Russell classes are set out on pp. 253–54; the proposed replacement of Law V by a weakened law (V′) runs to p. 265. English editions call the piece “Appendix” (Furth, Ebert and Rossberg); the 1903 German heading is “Nachwort”, and the Wörterverzeichnis follows it. The essay and the note call it an appendix to follow the English-language usage.
- **Status:** quotation-ready for the German sentence.
- **Verification:** read by `s01-sourcelock` (G2), 2026-10-04, in the Google scan's OCR text (page heads 252, 254–264 read; p. 253 and p. 265 identified by position). Russell's own note at the end of Appendix A of *The Principles of Mathematics* (1903) independently names “pp. 253—265” of vol. 2 of the *Grundgesetze* as Frege's discussion of the contradiction.
- **Source relation:** extracted (the German sentence); the essay paraphrases.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M04, marker `s01-frege` (the sentence “Frege replied within the week …”).
- **Use boundary:** Frege there also says he had pointed out Law V's lack of obviousness in the preface to vol. 1 (p. VII) and that he sees no way to ground arithmetic scientifically unless one may, at least conditionally, pass from a concept to its extension. He proposes a repair (V′) that the afterword itself leaves open (“Immerhin wird eine Durchprüfung aller bisher gefundenen Sätze nöthig sein”). That the repair fails is not stated here and rests on later work (see the Zalta entry in the Source scholarship below).

## Source scholarship

- Edward N. Zalta, “Gottlob Frege,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/frege/ (accessed 2026-10-04): says Frege's attempts to salvage the system by restricting Basic Law V failed, and that he published six papers between 1903 and 1917 and three more (1918–1923) after retiring. Supports “found no repair he could trust”; “published little” is arguable (see the prose-issue return).
- Andrew David Irvine and Harry Deutsch, “Russell's Paradox,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russell-paradox/ (accessed 2026-10-04): dates Russell's letter to 16 June 1902 and cites it with Frege's reply in van Heijenoort 1967; gives the Law V citation “sect. 20, p. 36”.
