## q003 — Neural correlates do not close the gap (§4) and the placement of §6–§7

**Paraphrase:** In §4 (“Some case-studies”) Chalmers argues that even a successful account of neural oscillations (Crick and Koch’s 40-Hz proposal) explains binding and storage functions but leaves open why the oscillations give rise to experience. §6 (“Nonreductive explanation”) argues that experience may need to be taken as fundamental, and §7 (“Outline of a theory of consciousness”) sets out the structural-coherence, organizational-invariance and double-aspect-information principles.

- **Locator:** §4 (neural correlates, the “explanatory question remains: why do the oscillations give rise to experience?”); §6; §7.
- **Status:** paraphrase-only.
- **Verification:** section headings and passages read in the author-hosted HTML on 2026-10-04 (agent G6b): the headings are 1 Introduction; 2 The easy problems and the hard problem; 3 Functional explanation; 4 Some case-studies; 5 The extra ingredient; 6 Nonreductive explanation; 7 Outline of a theory of consciousness; 8 Conclusion. Publication note on the page: *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 2(3):200–19, 1995.
- **Relation:** Paraphrased.
- **Consumer:** current M04, `s01-chalmers`, the sentence on neural correlates and the Subject appearing among them.
- **Use:** supports the prose’s statement that neural correlates bring real relations within reach without explaining why the functions are accompanied by experience; the Subject-pole step (“expecting the Subject to appear among them”) is the essay’s own.
