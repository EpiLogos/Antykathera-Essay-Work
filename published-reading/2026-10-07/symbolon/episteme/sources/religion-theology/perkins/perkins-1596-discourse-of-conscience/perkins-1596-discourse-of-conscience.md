## Source scholarship

Chapter 1 (“What conscience is”) defines conscience as “a part of the vnderstanding in all reasonable creatures, determining of their particular actions either with them or against them”; the *conscire*/*scire* derivation comes at the close of the chapter (p. 5), after Perkins has said that conscience's “manner of determination … is to set downe his iudgement either with the creature or against it” and that conscience is “a thing placed by God in the middest betweene him and man, as an arbitratour”. Chapter 2 then distinguishes its two offices, testimony and judgement.

### Passage card — `perkins-1596-discourse-of-conscience-q001` — *conscire* as two knowing one secret

> Scire, to know, is of one man alone by himselfe: and conscire is, when two at the least know some one secret thing; either of them knowing it togither with the other. Therefore the name Conscientia conscience, is that thing that combines two togither, and makes them partners in the knowledge of one and the same secret. Now man and man, or man and Angel can not be combined; because they can not know the secret of any man vnlesse it be reuealed to them: it remaines therefore that this combination is onely betweene man and God. God knowes perfectly all the doings of man, though they be neuer so hid and concealed: and man by a gift giuen him of God, knowes togither with God, the same things of himselfe: and this gift is named Conscience.

### Passage card — `perkins-1596-discourse-of-conscience-q002` — conscience speaks with or against the person

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition status: selected; URL https://ota.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12024/A09400; accessed 2026-10-04.

> The manner of consciences determination, is to set downe his iudgement either with the creature or against it: I adde this clause, because conscience is of a diuine nature, and is a thing placed by God in the middest betweene him and man, as an arbitratour to giue sentence and to pronounce either with man or against man vnto God. For otherwhiles, it consents and speakes with God against the man in whome it is placed: other whiles againe it consents with him and speakes for him before the Lord.

- **Locator:** chap. 1, pp. 4–5 (the sentence runs across the page break; “consents and speakes with God against the man” is on p. 5).
- **Status:** quotation-ready on the same terms as q001.
- **Source relation:** extracted.
- **Evidential action:** supports (and qualifies the prose's “participant in God's knowledge” only in that Perkins grounds the combination in a “gift” given by God).
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03 marker `s01-perkins`.

## Essay uses

M03 (conscientia / consciousness), marker `s01-perkins`: the prose's sentence that Perkins “explained *conscire* as two parties knowing a secret together: another person can know what I reveal, but God knows even what I conceal, and conscience makes me a participant in God's knowledge of my own life, able to speak against me or on my behalf” is supported by q001 and q002 (revealed secrets reach other persons; God knows what is “hid and concealed”; conscience “knowes togither with God, the same things of himselfe”; it speaks “against” or “for” the person).
