## Source scholarship

The book restates Girard’s mimetic theory through a reading of the Gospels. Desire is imitative; imitation of another’s desire turns models into rivals; rivalry spreads by imitation until a community is caught in a crisis in which its members mirror one another’s hostility. The crisis resolves when the hostility converges, all against one, on a single victim, whose expulsion or killing restores peace; the community believes in the victim’s guilt, and the mechanism works only so long as its participants do not see it as a mechanism. Girard reads the name Satan, “the accuser,” as a name for this process of contagious accusation that unites a crowd against a victim, and the Gospel accounts of the Passion as the narrative that takes the victim’s side and so exposes the mechanism. The essay uses the theory for the operation of accusation in the collapsed form of dia-ballein; it does not adopt Girard’s Christian apologetics or his claims about the origin of culture as its own.
