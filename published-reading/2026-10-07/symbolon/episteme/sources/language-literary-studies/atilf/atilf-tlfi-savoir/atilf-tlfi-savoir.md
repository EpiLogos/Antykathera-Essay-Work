## Passage card — `atilf-tlfi-savoir-q001` — savoir: from popular Latin *sapēre*, altered from classical sapere

**Paraphrase:** The entry derives French *savoir* from popular Latin *\*sapēre*, an alteration of classical *sapĕre*, which meant, absolutely, “to have flavour, taste, scent” (of things) and “to have taste, discernment, be wise” (of persons); used transitively it came to mean “to be versed in, understand, know,” displacing *scire*, which is little represented in the Romance languages. The original Latin sense survived in Old French, where *savoir* used absolutely meant “to feel” and “to please” from the twelfth to the fourteenth century. Earliest attestations are tenth-century (infinitive “to have the possibility, the capacity to”; “to know something through having been informed,” second half of the tenth century, *Saint Léger*).

- **Locator:** s.v. *savoir* (verbe), Étymologie et historique, closing etymological paragraph after the dated attestations.

- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
