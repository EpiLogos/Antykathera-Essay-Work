# Lewis and Short — A Latin Dictionary

## Work and access

Lewis, Charlton T., and Charles Short. *A Latin Dictionary*. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1879. The selected witnesses are the Scaife ATLAS and Alatius transcriptions linked below, consulted on 8 September 2026. Cite the headword, sense and actual online witness. This record does not claim collation of the complete printed dictionary or independent reading of every ancient passage cited by its editors. Its nineteenth-century comparative reconstructions are not adopted as present specialist consensus.

## Use and return

The dictionary **sources** Latin semantic distinctions in [E2 Arbitration](../../../../etymologies/arbitration-hybris-regard-anamnesis/WHOLE-FIELD-arbitration-hybris-regard-anamnesis.md) and [E3 Fides–Credere](../../../../etymologies/trust-place-logos-nomos-natio-credere/WHOLE-FIELD-trust-place-logos-nomos-natio-credere.md), and the *signum* / *significatio* family in [Symbol / Account / Trust](../../../../etymologies/symbol-account-and-trust/WHOLE-FIELD-symbol-account-and-trust.md#signification-and-significance). Their generated relations remain authorial register 3. The lexical evidence supplies particular attested senses and a recoverable citation chain; it does not derive the QL field or establish an institutional chronology.

### q001 — Fides: reliance and credibility

**Paraphrase:** the entry connects *fides* with *fido* and distinguishes reliance upon someone or something, credibility and fidelity. Its testimony example cites Cicero, *Pro Sulla* 3.10; the relation of competence and justice to confidence cites *De officiis* 2.9.33.

**Locator:** s.v. *fides*, initial literal senses and transferred quality

**Verification:** s.v. *fides*, initial literal senses and transferred quality; [Scaife entry n18127](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn:cite2:scaife-viewer:dictionary-entries.atlas_v1:lat.ls.perseus-eng2-n18127/), selected entry text read 2026-09-08. The stringed-instrument homograph is separate. No exact Ciceronian quotation is promoted.

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q002"></a>
### q002 — Credo: lending, protection and confidence

**Paraphrase:** the entry distinguishes lending, committing something to another's protection, confidence and belief. It gives *creditum* as a loan and cites Cato, *De agri cultura* 5.4 in its discussion of lending under an owner's authority.

**Locator:** s.v. *credo*, business use and transferred uses

**Verification:** s.v. *credo*, business use and transferred uses; [Scaife entry n11525](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn:cite2:scaife-viewer:dictionary-entries.atlas_v1:lat.ls.perseus-eng2-n11525/), selected senses read 2026-09-08. Cato's primary passage and the proposed deeper reconstruction retain independent collation tasks. E3's before/after-arbitration relation is not the dictionary's chronology.

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q003"></a>
### q003 — Natio: birth, kinds and peoples

**Paraphrase:** the entry associates *natio* with *nascor*. Its uses range from birth through group or kind to peoples; examples also concern animals and things. Its political vocabulary does not define the modern nation-state.

**Locator:** s.v. *natio*, introductory morphology and senses I–II/B

**Verification:** s.v. *natio*, introductory morphology and senses I–II/B; [Alatius transcription](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=natio), read 2026-09-08. Primary contexts and the later history of national institutions are separate evidence tasks.

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q004"></a>
### q004 — Arbitrari: observation, weighing, judgment and credited testimony

**Paraphrase:** *arbitror* carries observation into mental examination and weighing, then distinguishes judicial decision, testimony and opinion. The dictionary's judicial subsection records the exact lexical construction **“fidem alicui arbitrari”**, citing Plautus, *Bacchides* 3.6.41, for credit accorded to someone.

**Locator:** s.v. *arbitror*, I, II.A and II.B

**Verification:** s.v. *arbitror*, I, II.A and II.B; [Alatius transcription](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=arbitror), full entry read 2026-09-08. The quoted four-word construction is verified as dictionary wording. It is not presented as a continuous quotation from an independently collated Plautus edition. E2's witness→weighing→decision distinction and the E3 Fides bridge now have this exact lexical witness; the ancient dramatic context remains to be recovered independently.

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q005"></a>
### q005 — Arbiter: witness and deciding office

**Paraphrase:** the entry distinguishes a person present to see or hear from an appointed deciding office. Judicial uses include inquiry and settlement, followed by general judging and governing uses. Its selected examples concern boundary disputes and the distribution of an estate; these keep the object of judgment identifiable.

**Locator:** s.v. *arbiter*, introduction, I and II.A–C

**Verification:** s.v. *arbiter*, introduction, I and II.A–C; [Alatius transcription](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?l=arbiter), full entry read 2026-09-08. The dictionary's proposed going/coming derivation and historical account of Roman procedural change remain the editors' claims, requiring independent specialist collation before stronger use. The semantic distinction supports E2 without making witnessing and judging identical offices.

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q006"></a>
### q006 — Ratio — reckoning and rendering an account

**Paraphrase:** The entry connects ratio with reor/ratus and distinguishes reckoning, account, calculation and computation. Its examples include receipts and expenditure and rendering an account. This attests the selected Latin semantic field; it does not verify a common ancestry with Greek arithmos or harmony.

**Locator:** s.v. ratio, introduction and literal accounting senses

**Verification:** s.v. ratio, introduction and literal accounting senses; https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn:cite2:scaife-viewer:dictionary-entries.atlas_v1:lat.ls.perseus-eng2-n40449/ ; selected senses read 2026-09-08, raw witness retained in T21-E6-private-development-packet.json. Paraphrase-ready to the online entry, with no independently verified ancient quotation or print collation. E6-ratio consumes this range while retaining A15’s native derivation and separate comparative-root debt.

### q410 — Scio — to know; “prop. to distinguish, discern”; compared with Greek “to split”

**Paraphrase:** The entry gives *scio* as “to know, in the widest signif. of the word; to understand; perceive; to have knowledge of or skill in any thing,” and in its etymological bracket gives the root as *sci-* and compares Greek κείω (for σκείω) and κεάζω, “to split, divide,” and Latin *scisco*, *plebiscitum*, with the gloss “prop. to distinguish, discern.”

**Locator:** headword *scio*, etymological bracket and I.A.

**Verification:** quotation-ready for the short lexical gloss only

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q412"></a>
### q412 — Conscientia and conscio — joint knowledge, consciousness, conscience

**Paraphrase:** The entry for *conscientia* begins “a knowing of a thing together with another person, joint knowledge, consciousness,” and distinguishes I. joint knowledge of something (being privy to, a knowing along with others), II.A consciousness, knowledge, feeling, sense in general (with examples from Cicero, Livy, Quintilian and Tacitus; self-consciousness at Cic. *Fin.* 2.22.71), and II.B “a consciousness of right or wrong, the moral sense, conscience” (Cic. *Mil.* 23.61; Sen. *Ep.* 43.5; Quint. 5.11.41, “conscientia mille testes”). The short entry *conscio* gives only “to be conscious of wrong” (Hor. *Ep.* 1.1.61) and, in late Latin, “to know well.”

**Locator:** headword *conscientia*, introduction and I–II.B; headword *conscio*, I–II.

**Verification:** quotation-ready for the short lexical glosses only

- **Status:** [Alatius transcription](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=conscientia) and [*conscio*](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=conscio), full entries read 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Shows the three Latin senses the essay joins: shared knowledge, inward awareness, moral testimony.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-conscientia

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q413"></a>
### q413 — Confidentia and confido — firm trust, then boldness

**Paraphrase:** The entry *confidentia* gives “a firm trust in a thing, confidence (thus very rare)” (Plaut. *As.* 3.2.3) and, as a quality or habit, “self-confidence, boldness; in a good and bad sense,” with the bad sense “audacity, impudence” the more frequent (Cic. *Phil.* 2.40.104). The entry *confido* (*con-fido*) gives “to trust confidently in something, confide in, rely firmly upon, to believe, be assured of,” as an intensive of *sperare*.

**Locator:** headwords *confidentia* I–II.B and *confido* I.

**Verification:** quotation-ready for the short lexical glosses only

- **Status:** [Alatius transcription](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=confidentia) and [*confido*](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=confido), opening of each entry read 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Supports *confidence* from *confidentia* and *confidere* (*con-* + *fidere*), and shows that the Latin noun more often meant self-confidence or audacity than trust given to another.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, marker s01-con

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q414"></a>
### q414 — Fido — to trust; compared with πείθω; the source of fides

**Paraphrase:** The entry *fido* gives “to trust, confide, put confidence in, rely upon a person or thing,” with a bracket setting out roots proposed for it: Sanskrit *bandh*, Greek πείθω and πεῖσμα, Latin *fidus*, *foedus*, with Fick’s alternative from *bhidh*; its participle *fidens* is “confident, courageous, bold” (Cic. *Tusc.* 3.7.14). The entry *fides* opens “[fido], trust in a person or thing, faith, confidence, reliance, credence, belief (syn.: *fidelitas*, *fiducia*, *confidentia*),” which confirms the existing card q001.

**Locator:** headword *fido*, bracket and I; headword *fides*, opening line.

**Verification:** quotation-ready for the short lexical glosses only

- **Status:** [Alatius transcription](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=fido) and [*fides*](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=fides), openings read 2026-10-04.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Confirms that Lewis and Short connect *fides* with *fido* and compare Greek πείθω; the comparative derivation is the editors’ nineteenth-century proposal.
- **Consumers:** essay §0/1, M03, markers s01-con and s01-fides

### q415 — Digitus and manipulus — finger, counting, handful

**Paraphrase:** The entry *digitus* gives “a finger” (with Greek δάκτυλος), and among its special connections “computare digitis, to count on the fingers, to reckon up” (Plaut. *Mil.* 2.2.51) and “venire ad digitos, to be reckoned.” The entry *manipulus* (bracket “manus-pleo, plenus”) gives “a handful, a bundle,” with transferred senses including the military “maniple,” a company of soldiers belonging to one standard.

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q416"></a>
### q416 — Sapio and sapor — taste, flavour; discernment, wisdom

**Paraphrase:** The entry *sapio* opens “to taste, savor; to taste, smack, or savor of,” with a bracket of kindred words including Greek σαφής and σοφός; its literal senses are of things eaten or drunk; the transferred sense “to have good taste, i.e. to have sense or discernment; to be sensible, discreet, prudent, wise” is “the predominant signif. in prose and poetry.” The entry *sapor* (from *sapio*) gives “a taste, relish, flavor, savor,” distinguished as the objective taste inherent in a thing from *gustatus*, the taste experienced, with a transferred sense of style and elegance.

**Locator:** headword *sapio*, opening, I and II.2; headword *sapor*, opening, I and II.

**Verification:** quotation-ready for the short lexical glosses only

- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Supports *sapere* joining tasting, discerning and being wise, and *sapor* as flavour.

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q418"></a>
### q418 — Signum — mark, token, sign; Cicero’s definition of the sign

**Paraphrase:** The entry gives *signum* first, in general, as “a mark, token, sign, indication,” with uses for recognition tokens, footprints, scars, a signal given with the eyes, and signs of feeling (*signa doloris*, *timoris*); particular senses follow for military standards and further technical uses. Its definitional witness is Cicero, *De inventione* 1.30.47, which the entry quotes: “Signum est, quod sub sensum aliquem cadit et quiddam significat, quod ex ipso profectum videtur” — a sign is what falls under some sense and signifies something that appears to have proceeded from it. The etymological bracket offers only “perh. Sanscr. *sag-*, to cling to, adhere; cf. *sigilla*.”

**Locator:** headword *signum*, etymological bracket and I (general sense), with the Cicero citation.

**Verification:** quotation-ready for the short lexical gloss and for the Ciceronian sentence **as dictionary wording only**

- **Status:** [Alatius transcription](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=signum), headword block and sense I read 2026-10-07. The *De inventione* sentence has not been collated against an independent edition of Cicero.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Attests the Latin sign as a sensed mark that points to what it came from; the bracketed derivation is the editors’ hedged guess and is not adopted.
- **Consumers:** [Symbol / Account / Trust historical branch — signum and significatio](../../../../etymologies/symbol-account-and-trust/HISTORICAL-BRANCHES-symbol-account-and-trust.md#signum-significatio-and-the-double-sense-of-meaning).

### q419 — Significo — to make a sign; to betoken; to mean

**Paraphrase:** The entry derives *significo* from *signum* and *facio* (“[signum-facio]”) and gives, in general, “to show by signs; to show, point out, express, publish, make known, indicate; to intimate, notify, signify”; in particular, “to betoken, prognosticate, foreshow, portend, mean” (II.A), “to call, name” (II.B) and, “of words,” “to mean, import, signify” (II.C), citing Cicero, *Tusculan Disputations* 1.36.88 and Varro, *De lingua Latina* 5.3. Its participle *significans* is, in rhetorical use, “full of meaning, expressive, significant” (Quintilian 9.2.44).

**Locator:** headword *significo*, etymological bracket, I, II.A–C; participle *significans*.

**Verification:** quotation-ready for the short lexical glosses only

- **Status:** [Alatius transcription](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=significo), full entry read 2026-10-07.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Supplies philological descent (register 1) for the verb: signifying is sign-making, *signum* + *facere*.
- **Consumers:** as q418.

<a id="lewis-short-1879-latin-dictionary-q420"></a>
### q420 — Significatio — pointing out; approbation; emphasis; meaning of a word

**Paraphrase:** The entry gives *significatio* (from *significo*) as I. “a pointing out, indicating, denoting, signifying; an expression, indication, mark, sign, token,” glossed with *indicium*, *signum* and Greek ἐπισημασία. In particular: II.A, “like the Gr. ἐπισημασία, a sign or token of assent, an expression of approbation, applause” (Cicero, *Pro Sestio* 49.105); II.B, a weather-sign (Pliny); II.C, “in rhet. lang., significance, emphasis,” with the definition from the *Rhetorica ad Herennium* 4.53.67, “significatio est, quae plus in suspicione relinquit, quam positum est in oratione” — the figure that leaves more to be surmised than is set down in the speech — noted as “a transl. of the Gr. ἔμφασις”; II.D, “in gram., meaning, sense, import, signification of a word or phrase” (Varro, Cicero, Quintilian), with the titles *De verborum significatione* of Aelius Gallus, Verrius Flaccus and Festus; II.E, “meaning, intent.”

**Locator:** headword *significatio*, I and II.A–E.

**Verification:** quotation-ready for the short lexical glosses and for the *Ad Herennium* definition **as dictionary wording only**

- **Status:** [Alatius transcription](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=significatio), full entry read 2026-10-07. The *Ad Herennium* and Quintilian passages have not been collated against independent editions.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Attests (register 2) that one Latin noun holds the determining sense of meaning (II.D), the surplus over what is said (II.C) and the token of assent (II.A). The Greek equivalents ἐπισημασία and ἔμφασις are the lexicon’s; neither is σύμβολον, and no shared descent with Greek *symbolon* or *logos* follows.
- **Consumers:** as q418; essay §0/1, M02.

### q421 — Significantia — force or energy of words; later, meaning

**Paraphrase:** The entry gives *significantia* (from *significo*, post-Augustan) as I. “force, energy, significancy of words,” citing Quintilian 10.1.121, and II. “in gen., meaning, import, signification” (Tertullian, Arnobius, Lactantius). The related *significatus* (post-Augustan for *significatio* II.B and D) gives a prognostic sign and “meaning, import, signification of a word.”

**Locator:** headwords *significantia* and *significatus*.

**Verification:** quotation-ready for the short lexical glosses only

- **Status:** Alatius transcriptions of [*significantia*](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=significantia) and [*significatus*](https://www.alatius.com/ls/index.php?met=up&ord=significatus), full entries read 2026-10-07.
- **Source relation:** Paraphrased
- **Evidential action:** Shows the Latin pair from which English *significance* and *signification* are formed already present, with *significantia* first naming the force of words. The English route through Old French and the dates of the English words are not witnessed here.
- **Consumers:** as q418.
