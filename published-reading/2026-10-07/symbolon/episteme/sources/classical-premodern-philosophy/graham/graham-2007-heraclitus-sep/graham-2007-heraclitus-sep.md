# Graham — “Heraclitus” (SEP): the river fragments B12, B49a, B91

## Bibliographic identity

Daniel W. Graham, “Heraclitus,” in *The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, ed. Edward N. Zalta and Uri Nodelman, first published 8 February 2007, substantive revision 8 December 2023, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/ (read 2026-10-04, g5a). Graham's bibliography names the fragment edition: Hermann Diels and Walther Kranz, *Die Fragmente der Vorsokratiker*, 6th ed. (Weidmann, 1951–52; the entry gives 1951). ## Passages and excerpts

<a id="graham-2007-heraclitus-sep-q001"></a>
### Passage card — `graham-2007-heraclitus-sep-q001` — B12

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition status: selected; URL https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/; accessed 2026-10-04.

> On those stepping into rivers staying the same other and other waters flow.

- **Locator:** §3.1 “Flux”; Graham's B12 (DK 22 B12; source: Cleanthes, via Arius Didymus, via Eusebius). Graham treats it as the one river fragment with “the linguistic density characteristic of Heraclitus' words” and, after Kirk and Marcovich, as the only actual quotation.
- **Status:** quotation-ready (open-access SEP text).
- **Source relation:** extracted. **Evidential action:** supports.

<a id="graham-2007-heraclitus-sep-q002"></a>
### Passage card — `graham-2007-heraclitus-sep-q002` — B91

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition status: selected; URL https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/; accessed 2026-10-04.

> It is not possible to step twice into the same river according to Heraclitus, or to come into contact twice with a mortal being in the same state.

- **Locator:** §3.1; Graham's B91[a] (DK 22 B91; Plutarch). Graham calls it “patently a paraphrase by an author famous for quoting from memory rather than from books” and notes that, like Plato's *Cratylus* line, it denies that one can step in twice, against B12.
- **Status:** quotation-ready. **Source relation:** extracted. **Evidential action:** qualifies.

<a id="graham-2007-heraclitus-sep-q003"></a>
### Passage card — `graham-2007-heraclitus-sep-q003` — B49a

**Provenance:** as recorded for this source: edition status: selected; URL https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/; accessed 2026-10-04.

> Into the same rivers we step and do not step, we are and are not.

- **Locator:** §3.1; Graham's B49a (Heraclitus Homericus). Not cited by the essay; recorded as the third “river fragment”.
- **Status:** quotation-ready. **Source relation:** extracted.

## Essay uses

M03, marker `s01-heraclitus`: the essay says “I am present to a river that can never be crossed twice”; the note cites B12 and B91, compares *Cratylus* 402a (DK 22 A6; see `plato-fowler-cratylus`), and states that where Heraclitus speaks of stepping into the same river the essay's sentence says “crossed”. Graham's discussion supports the note's honesty about the sources: B12 is the best-attested wording, B91 and the Platonic line are later paraphrases of it.
