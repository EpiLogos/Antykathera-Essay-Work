# *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers* (*Liber viginti quattuor philosophorum*)

## Bibliographic identity

An anonymous medieval Latin collection of twenty-four short definitions of God, each ascribed to a "philosopher." The standard modern critical edition is *Liber viginti quattuor philosophorum*, ed. Françoise Hudry, Corpus Christianorum, Continuatio Mediaevalis 143A (Turnhout: Brepols, 1997). An earlier edition is Clemens Baeumker, "Das pseudo-hermetische 'Buch der vierundzwanzig Meister' (Liber XXIV philosophorum): Ein Beitrag zur Geschichte des Neupythagoreismus und Neuplatonismus im Mittelalter," in *Beiträge zur Geschichte der Philosophie des Mittelalters* 25, nos. 1–2 (Münster: Aschendorff, 1927), with the text at pp. 194–214; Jasper Hopkins's notes to his translation of Cusa give this Baeumker location for the second definition (p. 208). Neither edition was opened. The Hudry identity rests on the series and year as listed in a reference article (Wikipedia, read 2026-10-04, a lead only) and must be confirmed from the publisher or a library record. Dating and authorship are disputed: the earliest witness is a twelfth-century manuscript, and some scholars (Hudry among them) have argued for a late-antique authorship.

## Source scholarship

The second definition reads, in the Latin tradition, that God is an infinite sphere whose centre is everywhere and whose circumference is nowhere (*Deus est sphaera infinita cuius centrum est ubique, circumferentia nusquam*). Nicholas of Cusa knew and reworked the image (see `cusa-on-learned-ignorance`, *De docta ignorantia* II.12 n. 162); Hopkins's notes to that passage cite the work as the source and point to Karsten Harries, "The Infinite Sphere: Comments on the History of a Metaphor," *Journal of the History of Philosophy* 13 (January 1975): 5–15.
