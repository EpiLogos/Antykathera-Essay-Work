# C34 — Individuation

## #0

Passing from childhood to adulthood involves more than replacing the parent's commands with one's own. It includes discovering that the authority to act carries obligations that neither the parent nor the individual simply invented. Jung's concept of individuation describes a differentiation of the person that deepens their collective relationships: he opposed any levelling of a life to collective standards, which averages out its uniqueness, and held that individuation must lead to more intense and broader collective relationships and not to isolation. In his sense the person becomes an *in-dividual*, an indivisible unity or whole.

Individuation, in this record's sense, is **differentiated indivisibility**: a local life becomes more capable of bearing the relations through which it is itself without dissolving into them or installing one part as the whole. The positive movement is toward a more fully lived particularity whose dependence is conscious.

## #1

The distinction from mere aggregation is decisive. A collection of processes can stabilise into an apparently self-standing unit, and Frank's [Watson encounter](../../../symbolon/episteme/sources/psychology/watson/watson-1998-resonance-of-emptiness/watson-1998-resonance-of-emptiness.md) contrasts that use of individuation with the return by which the self-thing recognises its constituting plurality. Becoming more isolated or self-sufficient is no criterion, and the local person remains this person as the relations through which the person lives become less disowned.

## #2

Complexes stabilise channels of association and response. They have real competence and partial autonomy, and none thereby becomes the Self. [Symbolon Dynamics](<../../../../../working/sources-texts-references/Epi Paper Write-ups/Symbolon Dynamics — Archetype, Attractor, and Objective Internality.md>) makes transformation a relation among these agencies: a symbol can expose a basin boundary or carry a life into a changed regime. Restoring every disturbance to the old settlement would sometimes preserve the very organisation that needs to change.

## #3

The source encounter follows known→unknown→known. The unknown unsettles the categories by which the life had understood itself, and return makes the changed capacity inhabitable in a particular life. Frank's native `x→X→x` inscription gives this expansion and return a formal and mythemic reading: determining capacity is encountered through a particular that returns with greater freedom to vary. `X/x` is Taylor's notation, and its Jungian refraction does not transfer authorship of the notation to Jung.

## #4

Enough integrity must remain for the person to persist and enough openness for the organisation to change. Integration, reconciliation and recognition mark related but different operations: agencies can be coordinated, a conflict resituated, and provenance recognised without being fused or erased. The Self is neither the largest complex nor an executive that abolishes the autonomy of the others. When opposing agencies cannot settle their relation within the existing frame, [their containing field](C36-Complexio-Oppositorum.md) must become available, and bearing that opposition can change what the particular life is capable of doing while preserving the differentiated agencies through which it acts.

## #5→0

Individuation returns to [resolution in reconciliation](../../../symbolon/episteme/etymologies/arbitration-hybris-regard-anamnesis/WHOLE-FIELD-arbitration-hybris-regard-anamnesis.md#resolution-in-reconciliation) where reconciliation preserves differentiated participation and installs no ego as its author. Recognition, integration and reconciliation have different offices within this movement: reconciliation gives differentiated agencies an inhabitable relation, and recognition lets the achieved form acknowledge how it came to be. The Greek recollection branch, Sanskrit recognition and Jung's psychological transformation keep distinct historical warrants within Taylor's native known–unknown–known operation.

Recognition returns the achieved differentiation to its sources and history. Greek anamnesis, Śaiva pratyabhijñā and Jungian individuation remain distinct traditions, and their operational relation is recollective transformation and no shared philological origin. The [cultural extension](C59-Cultural-Individuation-Epi-Logos-as-Culture.md) makes inherited images, measures and institutions answerable to the persons and histories through which they live, and [sensitivity to those origins](../A35-Compassion-Sensitivity-to-Origins-Epi-Logos-as-Vocation.md) admits dissent as part of that return. A culture's transformed forms and a particular person's psychic transformation have different objects, so gathering the former gives no collective ego possession of the latter. The returned life can judge and act with more of its conditions acknowledged.
