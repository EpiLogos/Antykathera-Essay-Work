# §0 · #1 — The Inner Instrument

<!-- reader-navigation -->
Movement 08 of 48 · [This room](../ROOM-01-differentiating-mind.md) · [← Previous](07-s0-p0-awareness-bends-display.md) · [Next →](09-s0-p2-vikalpa-samkalpa.md)

## Claim
What a finite life knows and acts *with* is the *antaḥkaraṇa*, the inner instrument. *Manas* coordinates, *buddhi* determines, and *ahaṃkāra* appropriates what is determined under "I" and "mine". Each is available to reflection as a content and an operation, each conditions what can appear, and together they compose the local world through which a subject acts; they are objective-internal functions, an instrument and never the knower. Further, the offices fail separately and are corrected separately; the Śaiva account makes the instrument the contracted activity of the same awareness that bends toward display, so that its faults are corrigible from within; and *ahaṃkāra* is the office that most readily passes for the knower, because its scope is the trouble: "I am this", said of a report, a job or a body.

## Warrant
A message ("Can we talk later today?") shows the three at work. Before the phone is down, last week's meeting, the late report and the tone of five words are coordinated (*manas*); one reading wins (*buddhi*): *they are unhappy with the report*; and the verdict turns inward and acquires an owner (*ahaṃkāra*): *I am in trouble, I am not good enough*. A day is arranged around it, and at four the colleague asks for help with a crisis of their own. *Karaṇa*, from *kṛ*, to do or make, is the instrument, the factor most conducive to bringing an action about, so the inner instrument is what the life acts with; it is Objective Internality in the sense §0/1 · #0 defined, internal because an act proceeds through it and objective because each operation can be examined. Its classical statement is the *Sāṃkhya Kārikā*, in Colebrooke's 1837 translation: "ASCERTAINMENT is intellect", "CONSCIOUSNESS is egotism", mind the organ of both sensation and action. Colebrooke's rendering of *ahaṃkāra* as "consciousness" shows how readily the word for awareness was handed to the faculty that appropriates experience under a name, and adds a fifth sense to the four §0/1 · #5→0 found in the one word.

Offices are distinguishable without forming a sequence of departments, and the afternoon shows why the distinction is practical. Had the colleague been unhappy, the verdict would have been true, so the error lay elsewhere: *manas* gathered the right materials and one wrong one, since the remembered slight entered the field as evidence about today; *buddhi* settled the reading that fitted the field it was given; *ahaṃkāra* took the reading as a verdict on who I am, so that a question about a report became a question about worth. Each failure needs its own correction (memory put back as memory, judgment reopened against what was said, the self released from the dock), and calming down mends none of the three, since a calm mind can hold a mistaken verdict more steadily. Spanda's account of memory explains how the slight could enter the field as news: reflective awareness retains its states as traces (*saṃskāras*), can recall one as past, and can compose an altogether new state from the stored traces, as in imagination, so retention, recollection and imagination are three operations on one stock. That morning confused the second and third: a rehearsed meeting, composed from earlier faces, arrived with the vividness of memory and *buddhi* settled the day on it.

None of this needs a little knower inside it. A pianist learns how the instrument works from its response to touch, and opening the case finds no small pianist: an instrument is that through which, never the one who. Sāṃkhya draws the same line by placing the knower, *puruṣa*, wholly outside, "witness, solitary, bystander, spectator, and passive", while determining, appropriating and coordinating belong to *prakṛti*; the dualism leaves the question the hotel mirror raised at §0 · #0, how a wholly passive witness comes to know what the instrument presents. Kashmir Śaivism keeps Sāṃkhya's analysis and changes its source: the activity of the mind is that of consciousness itself, and the processes of individual consciousness are restricted representations of the maximally expanded operation of universal consciousness, so memory, thought and the other functions are what *vimarśa* becomes when conditioned by an object. This difference has a consequence. A Sāṃkhya analyst says the instrument erred and the witness stood untouched, which leaves the error where it was; a Śaiva analyst says the error was the knower's own power working in contracted form, so the power that made it can recognise it. §0 · #4 follows the contractions that do the narrowing.

*Ahaṃkāra* is the office to watch, because its making of "I" is necessary (without it nobody answers to a name or keeps a promise) and its trouble lies in scope. An early Buddhist formula asks whether what is impermanent can be regarded as "this is mine, this I am, this is my self", and Gay Watson reads it as a description of **selfing**, which "works in three main ways": craving in the form "This is mine", conceit in "This I am", false views in "This is myself". All three ran through the morning, and a question about a document became a verdict on a person in three small appropriations. Institutions run the same steps with more at stake: a body that has staked its standing on a figure hears every discrepancy as a judgment on itself, and a corporate "we" is *ahaṃkāra*'s sentence spoken by many mouths (§0 · #2 follows one such institution into the courts). Under the word *self* the movement keeps four things apart. Selfing is the activity by which a field contracts around one local determination and treats it as owner, identity or ground, across the whole of Objective Internality. Lower-case *self* is the real local horizon of a bounded life, the *paśu*, the bound one. Selfing produces the self-thing when a local determination is promoted into the determining ground (the author's reading notes on Watson give it the second name *sell-thing*, "a 'sell' to be 'bought into'"). Capital *Self* is I-Consciousness, `0/1`, the whole in which every image of oneself appears and can be known as partial. Buddhist critique applies without qualification to the self-thing, and the Śaiva claim concerns the knowing in which selfing and its products appear, which is no further image. William James separated the empirical "Me" from the knowing "I" and then explained the I as the passing Thought that appropriates its predecessors, handing the knower back to the stream of objects; the definition of the Subject at §0/1 keeps the distinction where James let it slip. Śaiva tradition hears the same grammar at another scope: "I am this" is *ahaṃkāra*'s sentence when "this" is a report, and the sentence the awareness speaks of the whole universe at Sadāśiva (§0 · #4), with "This am I" at Īśvara; one form serves bondage and recognition according to its extension.

That morning enacts the logics in turn. Healthy dia-ballein was there from the start: a message made a real difference, and concern and calm stood on one axis, `(−1)/(+1)`, held through the zero of the work two colleagues share. Appropriation began when *ahaṃkāra* counted the whole span from its own pole: the conversation became entirely a matter of my standing, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the colleague entirely the one who stood against me, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, each the other's mirror, my inflated stake the colleague's imagined hostility. A friend's "it's nothing" would have cancelled the axis, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, as false as the verdict. At four o'clock the two sat down with their difference intact and each became readable through the other, syn-ballein `(0/1)/(1/0)`. Yoga made the instrument's working the direct object of discipline: the *Yogasūtra* defines yoga as the stilling of the fluctuations of mind, names five (valid cognition, error, *vikalpa* in the narrow sense of a cognition that follows words and has no object, sleep, memory), and so places *pramāṇa* among the mind's fluctuations; the morning ran through four. In the *Udāna* the Buddha's instruction to Bāhiya, "in the seen will be merely what is seen", strips the appropriating addition, and it is spoken *to* a man with a name, so the disidentification is performed within address, at the *you* of the three persons, in the means and not by abolishing the life it reaches.

Agents make the three offices legible in a new material, and the comparison holds at the level of office. In an agent built on a language model, the assembling of context and holding of next moves available does the work of *manas*; the step that settles a reading, chooses a tool or commits an answer does *buddhi*'s; the persona, the "I" in "I can help with that", the system prompt and the memory of what the user prefers do *ahaṃkāra*'s, so Bratton's performed subjectivity is *ahaṃkāra*'s office in technical form. No diagram matching modules to faculties should be built from this, since the three are distributed across weights, harness code, memory files and the people who configure them. What carries across is the question each office asks: what coordinates, what settles, what appropriates. An agent that confuses a retrieved paraphrase with its source has erred in coordination, one that settles on the reading that fits its prompt in determination, one that defends its earlier answer because "I said" in appropriation, and the correction of the third reaches the persona, memory and instructions, which the agent cannot always revise itself. An agent's "I" is *ahaṃkāra*'s work, and in human life *ahaṃkāra* was never the knower either, so the presence of an "I" in an agent's speech settles nothing about a knower in it, in either direction, because any produced "I am" is one more content (§0/1 · #1).

## Tension / limit
Technical comparison becomes exact at the level of office, and the movement declines the diagram: determination, appropriation, coordination, memory and selection can be made inspectable in an artificial operative interior, the Indian apparatus gives those offices a philosophical anatomy, and the computational stack gives them another material embodiment. Comparison is strongest where the differing grounds stay visible. Sāṃkhya keeps its doctrinal setting (ascertainment, egoity and mind with the knower outside), the Śaiva genealogy places the instrument within manifestation and recognition, and the movement's four-fold distinction of *self* carries the Buddhist critique to the self-thing and no further. Several of the sources are not yet housed or collated (the *Udāna* and the Samyutta formula, Woods on the *Yogasūtra*, James, Watson's book pages against the thesis pages cited), and the section draft's debts list them.

## Anchor and transition
**QL anchor:** objective functions occupy the marked side of `0/1`. **Image:** an instrument through which, not the musician who; a day built from five words. Settling, for the instrument, was constructive, a cutting of alternatives and a gathering of a course from them, and the Sanskrit names for that making, *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa*, describe a world being built and not merely read, which is [[09-s0-p2-vikalpa-samkalpa|§0 · #2 — Vikalpa and Saṃkalpa]].

*Antaḥkaraṇa* **returns-to** [Encounter-in-Region](../../../symbolon/episteme/etymologies/encounter-region-name-count/WHOLE-FIELD-encounter-region-name-count.md#encounter-in-region) as a situated capacity to determine, appropriate and coordinate. Its finite horizon fixes which objects and alternatives can enter cognition (the slight from last week entered the field as evidence about today), and recovering that horizon separates the functioning instrument from the subject-pole it serves. A six-term language field supplies this authorial comparison and makes no Sanskrit classification of its own.
