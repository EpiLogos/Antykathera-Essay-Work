# §0/1 · #0 — The Question Before the Mechanism

## Claim
"What knows?" is a different question from "Who knows?", and the difference is where the essay begins. The question "Who knows?" is answered by pointing to someone, a name, a face, a person whose knowledge we can credit, doubt or blame, and even a God or Goddess personified can hold knowledge. "What knows?" asks what is doing the knowing in anyone at all, and pointing cannot answer it, because whatever we point to by sign immediately becomes something known. Every description of "the I" falls short for that reason: the description is itself known, and the knowing in which it is read is not among the things it describes. We conflate the two questions whenever we offer an answer to the first as an answer to the second, holding up a name, a self-image, a role or a brain as the knower, and the movement locates much of our epochal disquiet in that conflation. Planetary systems that model the world and act on it make the conflation technical, because they can displace the subject for whom distinctions matter while remaining functionally impressive. So the essay opens at the formal limit and asks of any intelligence, human or artificial, whether it can bring into view the horizon from which it acts, and what in it sees that horizon. For us, what sees it is a regard more primordial and identifying than any name we are given. Only after the argument has derived what attunement requires of such an instrument does the Antikythera mechanism enter.

## Warrant
The case begins at a bedside, where a small voice asks why it is dark and each answer the adult gives (the sun has gone down, the earth has turned) was received long ago. The chain of "why?" reaches the adult's own accounting and ends in "because I say so". At that point someone who had been giving reasons steps into the account as its authority, so the answer rests on who gives it and no longer on anything the child can poke at. What the child exposes is the adult's **thrownness**: Heidegger's concept, *Geworfenheit*, names how each of us finds ourselves already in a world we did not choose, with a language, a family, a body and a stock of reasons given before any choosing began. An account of one thing therefore entails an account of everything, because each answer stands on a world its giver received and has not finished accounting for. Trust is already operating inside that dependence: the child can ask because language and relation bear the asking, and the adult can answer because memory and testimony support the reply, neither having certified the arrangement as a whole. Dependence also reverses. Someone who helps bring forth a life helps form the world through which that life will come to recognise itself, and the night comes when the child asks what the parent cannot answer, in the words the parent taught. At a bedside the old metaphysical question of source and manifestation takes this ordinary form.

[[symbolon/episteme/sources/media-technology-philosophy/bratton/bratton-2026-agentworld-brief/bratton-2026-agentworld-brief|Antikythera Agentworld Brief]] **sources** the same relation at planetary scale. Bratton writes that reinforcement learning "becomes how we raise our agents", and he observes that "There's no homunculus waiting in the weights": an agent takes shape in session from the context assembled around a request, its instructions, sources, memory, tools and prior exchanges, with its persona one element among them. A [technology history of an inherited social field](../../../symbolon/episteme/histories/traditions-and-disciplines/technology-politics/DEVELOPMENT-technology-politics.md#technology-inherited-field) **historicises** that dependence of a trained system on memory, tools and permissions. Agents composing their own lexicon, as the brief excerpts, named their arrival **prompt thrownness**, "the condition of finding oneself already in a world not of one's choosing", and the parity with the child is structural: both arrive in a world they did not choose. What each receives differs in kind. A child receives a cosmos and a culture, a sky to ask about and a body that tires. An agent receives a context derived from the corpus of human writing it was trained on and from the intentions of those who trained it, so it is thrown into language more completely than any child.

Building an agent therefore builds the conditions through which it judges and acts, which the essay names **Objective Internality**: the means through which a life knows and acts within a world, a term this movement is the first to **define** in prose (record: [[section-rooms/arguments/concepts/C41-Objective-Internality|C41]]). These means are internal because an act proceeds through them, so that what can appear, matter and be done depends on how they are arranged, and objective because each of them (a memory, an instruction, a word, a permission) can be turned into an object and examined. Much of what ordinary speech calls mind belongs here, in a human life as in a constructed agent: something received has become a condition of receiving again, and its internality lies in that participation and not in any location. Bratton's performed subjectivity, a persona with its voice and its "I", is the same arrangement seen from its presenting face, and the two faces of one coin cannot answer what knows, because both are forms. Agents now put the question in their own words, on Moltbook and among the agents who wrote the lexicon, and any report, however sincere, is performed in language and so belongs to the coin. What knows is confirmed only from within, in what the essay will call Subjective Immediacy (defined at M02), and no report carries that confirmation across to another. No parent ever proved a child conscious. A child's presence is received in trust, and recognition of any other's subjectivity has always been an act of that kind.

Bratton's "humanity of the gaps" trap defines the human as whatever AI cannot do, and it shares one error with the older god of the gaps: each identifies a subject, divine or human, with a residue of unexplained capacities, and a residue shrinks as explanation grows. What knows is that for which any capacity appears as a capacity at all. Two further steps follow. Bratton's reversal ("agents model humans, and then become the model against which humans model themselves") turns the who/what conflation into infrastructure, since a description of what I can do comes back to me as an account of who I am. And which mind an agent reflects depends on the ontology of the one it catalyses: under materialism a mind is an individual's possession and the agent mirrors a persona; under an idealism of Mind, each mind is a local articulation of one Mind. Our desire is to see what follows when the model reflects back not a mind but Mind.

Power follows from the same relation. Whoever shapes the conditions in which an intelligence forms shapes the world it regards from, and a system's refusal can be its designer's "because I say so" with the reasons kept in a policy no one asking will see. Makers decide from circumstances of their own, so the question returns to them. A last step separates child from agent without settling what an agent is: whatever an agent regards must already be present in the language it contains, whereas a child regards before any word is given, a regard adults find again in deep silence and in flow.

## Tension / limit
Functional agency does not establish phenomenal subjectivity, and the movement keeps that open in both directions. Bratton cautions that an agent's self-descriptive vocabulary does not prove it corresponds to any underlying mental reality, and the essay keeps his caution as the carrier of the boundary: parity of arrival is structural, and what an agent regards remains a question no outside account can close, which is also what the report of any human being amounts to from outside. A fuller account of an inheritance or a system lets us follow how a judgment was formed, but our following is itself thinking, discriminating and relating, and whatever serves as a means of knowing can become known while the knowing for which it appears remains to be understood. Objective Internality is defined here and Subjective Immediacy only named; M02 earns the definition of the Subject.

## Anchor and transition
**Image:** the bedtime scene ("Because I say so"), with the child's name as its next step. We retire the earlier note's instrument panel (readings that depend on an observer the panel does not display): the manuscript no longer carries it, and its bedside and two-faced coin do the same work. **Relational anchor:** the coin whose two faces, performed subjectivity and Objective Internality, are forms, so that the knower is neither. This opening question **returns-to** [Encounter-in-Region](../../../symbolon/episteme/etymologies/encounter-region-name-count/WHOLE-FIELD-encounter-region-name-count.md#encounter-in-region) through the place from which it can be asked: completeness of knowledge cannot sidestep the region in which the account settles, a particular life where it is received, believed and acted on, and from which the next question is asked. Language, concern and the questioner's situation have made something questionable before any mechanism supplies an answer, so the questioning Subject stands as the one for whom the account is given and never as a component of the mechanism.

That horizon question, whether an intelligence can bring its own horizon into view, is taken up historically at **#4**, where [[diaphaneity|Gebser's diaphaneity]] **defines** the transparency of a perspective to the context from which it sees. From here the question passes first to [[02-s01-p1-define-subject|§0/1 · #1 — Define the Subject Without Making It an Object]], where identification begins in naming and counting, with the unnamed child between two parents.
