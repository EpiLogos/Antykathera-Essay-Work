# Prose Standard — The Return of Zero

This is the one standard for manuscript prose in *The Return of Zero*: what fails, what succeeds, how a passage is repaired, and when a section can be stamped done. It replaces the writing laws, rubric, calibration files and negation gate that lived under `writing-guidance-tools/`. That stack is retired because it worked against the essay in practice. A model asked to audit its own prose against a list of gates passes its own habits, and the generic anti-slop treatments added a new template — a short sentence in every paragraph, a sensory detail in every paragraph, banned-word lists — on top of the old one.

Faults are named and numbered here as *fault 1* to *fault 13*. Do not abbreviate them to letter codes; `S`, `P`, `L`, `A`, `C` and `F` already belong to the essay's canon.

## Why model prose fails in this manuscript

Four failures recur in every model that has drafted for the essay, including Claude.

1. **It will not make certain claims affirmatively.** Given a proposition the essay has derived or argued, the model states a weaker neighbour, surrounds it with "can", "may", "need not", "does not by itself", or defers it to a later section. This is the first thing to correct, and fault 1 governs it.
2. **It sandwiches the author.** Frank's wording comes through in many places. The prose fails around it: a generic opener before an authorial claim, a padded middle between two authorial sentences, or a recap closer after one. Repair starts by finding the authorial sentences and asking which surrounding sentence lets them down.
3. **It writes efficiently.** Model prose is evenly paced, evenly sized and free of rough edges, and that evenness is what makes it read as machine-made. Style needs edges: a sentence that runs on because the thought is still moving, a digression taken for the pleasure of it, a joke, a word chosen for its sound. Length is not the fault. Cutting is for dead sentences, not for living ones.
4. **It games checklists.** A list of forbidden constructions produces synonyms for them. This standard is therefore applied by reading the passage in its section, never by pattern-matching. Searches can locate candidates; they never establish a fault.

## The faults

### Fault 1 — Withheld affirmation

The prose says less than the argument has earned.

- *Looks like:* hedges on derived or argued claims; "it is enough to notice"; "we need not answer that here"; a claim stated only as a question; "this does not mean that…" where nobody had said it did.
- *Instead:* **the essay is allowed to claim what it argues.** `Derived` and `Argued` are positive warrants. A source boundary limits attribution; a proof boundary limits formal consequence; an implementation boundary limits observed technical achievement. None of these licenses weakening a proposition whose argument has been made. Order a claim as **positive proposition → warrant or derivation → the one necessary boundary → consequence**.
- A reviewer flags overclaim only by naming the exact proposition and the warrant it exceeds, and must equally flag underclaim. "This sounds strong", disciplinary caution, and an imagined sceptical reader are not grounds. If an objection does work in the passage, name its actual carrier: a source, a documented rival view, the venue brief, a mathematical convention, an implementation state.

### Fault 2 — Claims without their because

Assertions arrive in a row and a "therefore" follows. The warrant is a feeling of continuity.

- *Looks like:* "Making an intelligence places the maker inside a relation whose consequences return." Nothing shows how.
- *Instead:* every claim that carries weight gets its mechanism in the same or the next sentence: *by* what operation, *because* of what dependence, through which named case. "Hold the because" means keeping the dependence, not inserting the word.

### Fault 3 — One cadence, no edges

- *Looks like:* paragraphs of 80–110 words, six to eight mid-length sentences, a topic noun at the start, and a closing sentence that restates the paragraph one notch more abstractly.
- *Instead:* let the work set the size. A decisive claim can stand as one sentence. A derivation, a case or a historical scene runs as long as it needs. Do not close paragraphs on a recap. Rhythm comes from the thought: a long sentence that holds several dependencies at once, then a short one that commits.

### Fault 4 — Riddles in place of propositions

- *Looks like:* chiasmus and self-referential loops that state nothing checkable. "The impossibility is not outside the logic. It is what makes the next logic necessary." "The walker belongs to the walk."
- *Instead:* say the proposition. Where the content is a paradox, name both terms and what holding them together lets the reader do.

### Fault 5 — Nouning

- *Looks like:* abstract nouns as grammatical subjects — "the means", "the act", "the relation", "the account", "the condition", "the activity" — and the template **"gives X its Y"**: "gives this distinction a sensory life", "gives the con its staying power".
- *Instead:* put the actor in the subject and the action in the verb. A "gives X its Y" sentence usually hides "X shows Y because…"; write the because.
- Weak predicates belong here too: "the distinction matters", "X plays a role", "Y is key". Say what the distinction does.

### Fault 6 — Disembodied prose

- *Looks like:* things "become" with nobody making them; a timeless present; no reader placed in the argument. "What was tacitly held becomes articulable through its own differentiation."
- *Instead:* tense that tracks the argument (what we derived, what we are doing now, what remains open), a *we* or an *I* doing it, and a reader who has just read the previous paragraph and is carrying a particular question.

### Fault 7 — Fake-poetic lines

- *Looks like:* balanced one-liners that perform no operation. "Presence is intimate without becoming private property."
- *Instead:* write as if working with actual truths. An image has to do the reasoning, and the prose says which part of the image does it.

### Fault 8 — Strings of terms

- *Looks like:* runs of terms held between dashes (`— memory, perception, language, habit —`) or chained with semicolons (`x; y; z`), and runs of names or sources followed by a loose point that does not tell the reader what they share.
- *Instead:* many voices may and often should land on one point. Write the common denominator first, or let it surface as each voice speaks to it, so the reader sees each source making the insight in its own register. A list is admissible where the items are given before they are listed, or where enumeration is itself the point (a count, a sequence, an inventory the argument uses).

### Fault 9 — The hackneyed meta-point

- *Looks like:* the same general sentence about return, self-implication or conditions being stated where a facet deserves its own nuance. "Our method follows its consequences through a particular field, returning from each exact discovery to the condition of further discovery."
- *This is not a fault:* reading a new case through `0/1` and the related mathemes. The notation is the lens through which different dynamics are spoken of together while each keeps its own character; using it on a new case displays the meta-point by working it. Repeated use of the notation is method.
- *Instead:* when a facet returns to the meta-point, say what only that facet adds. Gödel's version is truth separating from provability relative to a stated theory; the VOC's is a company chartered with the powers of a state; Wolin's is power that survives the replacement of its face.

### Fault 10 — Points posing as aphorisms

- *Looks like:* a bolded "X is Y" line the paragraph has not earned. "Simulation is the technological face of an ancient metaphysics." "The invariant is ratio, not recipe."
- *Instead:* do not mint aphorisms. Recover the point and state it as a real sentence: give the operation that makes X like Y, or make the comparison an explicit simile or metaphor with its working shown. Frank's ratified formulations and defined terms at first definition are the only bold in body prose.

### Fault 11 — Narrating the essay

- *Looks like:* "This is the point at which our own notation has to be born." "Count the predicament now." "§3 supplies the fuller formal language." A paragraph saying what the movement has just done.
- *Instead:* do the thing. A forward or backward reference is a clause or a link carrying a particular question the reader now has.
- The worst case is narrating at a key term: "This is where Epi-Logos receives its necessity…" launches into a claim *about* a term before the reader has the term. Define a load-bearing term first, from its etymology, history and canonical sentence, and only then say what follows from it.

### Fault 12 — Straw negation

- *Looks like:* "A matheme is not a replacement for argument. It is…"; "This does not take us away from the rational."
- *Instead:* lead with the positive claim. Keep a negation where it answers a named position, or where the negation is itself the content: `1/0`, apoha, the tetralemma, the unobjectifiable Subject.

### Fault 13 — The logics left implicit

- *Looks like:* a passage enacting cancellation, appropriation or retained relation without naming which logic is at work, or pointing vaguely at `0/1` where the case has a precise operation.
- *Instead:* when the material enacts one of the two logics, write it in situ. **Dia-ballein `(−1)/(+1)`** holds a polar axis through zero; its collapsed operations are `(−1)+(+1)=0` (cancellation) and appropriation, which is always given in **both chiralities**, `(+1)−(−1)=+2` and `(−1)−(+1)=−2` (Frank's ruling, 2026-10-04). `±2` is the image of imbalance against the healthy polarity, the duality hardened into opposition instead of oscillating cleanly; in a case, name the mirror (the public's `+2` is the scapegoat's `−2`). **Syn-ballein `(0/1)/(1/0)`** retains the relation, so either orientation can be read through the other. Name the operation and say what it does to the people or terms involved.

## What succeeds: the authorial hand

These are taken from Frank's own sentences in the developed §0/1, and they are the standard the rest of the prose is held to.

- **First-person commitment.** The author stands behind the claim: "I pinpoint the issue at the general tendency of thought to reify what is quite viscerally and immediately a processual reality."
- **Puns that argue.** con / *con-scire*; Mind as the one "on the take"; banked accounts and bank accounts; law as sporting where it should be supporting. The pun carries a real connection between registers, so it stays.
- **The actual case.** Carlin's "big club", the Epstein redactions, Snowden and Assange, the VOC charter and the Dutch student societies, MKUltra and Paperclip, context rot in a model's window.
- **Derivation inside the material.** The triangle's 180° doubled into the square's 360°, the square as two triangles in co-opposition, context therefore needing at least two chiral perspectives, and the excluded triangle as the shadow.
- **Registers crossed in one move.** Śiva and Śakti read as Mono and Poly, and the same dynamics deciding "whether or not I get to eat tomorrow".
- **The present of the writing.** "the disquiet I sense and see in the collective".
- **Long sentences that hold several dependencies**, with connectives that carry logic: *because*, *by*, *precisely because*, *thus*, *insofar as*.

Authorial sentences keep their wording. Their faults are copyediting faults — typos, unclosed run-ons, cases piled faster than the sentence can carry — and are repaired as such, never by rewriting them into house style.

## How a passage is repaired

1. Read the whole movement in its section, and recover the canonical claim from the plan, the movement note and the argument records it routes to.
2. Mark the authorial sentences and the canonical formulations. They are the load-bearing wording. A reviser oversteps by replacing them with an equivalent that reads more smoothly; the chosen word (*ratio*, *under-writing*, *world-containing and world-contained*) usually carries a connection the paraphrase drops. Keep the wording and make the sentence around it legible.
3. For each paragraph, find what lets the authorial wording down: the opener, the sandwiched middle, or the closer. Rebuild the paragraph so its real claim leads and its warrant follows. Model-drafted material is a quarry for facts, sources and connective steps; keep what is accurate and needed.
4. Restore the facet's own nuance wherever the paragraph had fallen back on the meta-point (fault 9), and write the logics in situ wherever the material enacts them (fault 13).
5. Recover buried points: arguments the passage gestures at, or that its ideas, sources, etymologies and canonical records already carry, and that the prose never states.
6. Only then normalise mechanics (below).
7. Reread the whole movement aloud-in-the-head as one piece. If it now reads at one pace, it has been over-edited.
8. Run the verification pass and report it. Search the finished prose for each tell, reread every hit in place, reread every new paragraph as prose, and check that every note marker resolves. Record what was fixed and what was kept, and why.

### Lessons carried from M06

- **Show a system working; don't catalogue it.** When the essay draws on the MEF or another wider system, present the few lenses in use as compact tables labelled with their coordinates (`L2`, `L1`, `L1′`, `L0`), then read across a row to show one position refracting differently and recognisably through each lens.
- **Check a series in canon before labelling it.** Two series can look alike and be different things: the Logos cycle (A-logos → An-a-logos, homed at M5-5) is not the L5′ lens (Arche → Epi-Logos). When an instruction and the canon disagree, set out the disagreement for Frank; never resolve it silently.
- **Place `0/1` by context.** It is a symbol, and its terms are offices within a relation. A power can stand toward the `1` in one reading and at the slash in another (Māyā beside prakāśa and vimarśa, against Māyā Śakti between Parā Śakti and Nara). State which relation is being read.
- **Check history and chronology.** A claim of influence has to fit the dates (Dignāga precedes the Spanda Kārikās). Write the route the history supports, and flag the difference.
- **Find the source's own words.** Look for a passage in the source houses first, then in the author's local library under `/Users/admin/Documents/Books`, citing *Collected Works* by paragraph. Copies labelled Z-Library, or of unclear provenance, are locators only. Located passages go into the section ledger as passage cards to create.
- **Give recaps their result only.** When an earlier derivation returns (the torus, the dice), state its result in a clause and spend the sentence on the new step.
- **Define a term by its own sources first.** Build a load-bearing term's definition from the author's own carriers (Draft 3 for Epi-Logos, the theorem file for the six positions) before saying anything else about it.
- **Watch the opening rate.** If more than about one sentence in ten opens with "The …", the passage is nouning its way forward. Most such openings can lead with an actor, a demonstrative of what was just shown, or the thing itself.
- **Collect updates in the section ledger.** Canon, room, argument and source updates go into `working/s01-hardening-2026-10-01/SECTION-UPDATE-LEDGER.md` (or the equivalent for each section) and are done together when the section is accepted.

### Lessons carried from M05

- **No announcing sentences.** A short sentence that sets up the next one around a name or a topic ("Jung gave the reason.", "Gebser recognised the correspondence.", "Our notation names the position this event installs.", "The myth keeps the price of both gifts.") is a trailer for a claim. Fold it into the sentence that carries the claim ("…and Jung explained why they do: …"). A short sentence that commits to a claim stays ("We face a post-symbolic exposure."). In verification, list every sentence under about ten words and ask of each whether it states something or announces something.
- **A sequence is not a cast.** When a series of stages or structures leads to a position, do not parade them with numbers as titles. Say the operation that carries one to the next (the integral voids the earlier claims by contextualising them at **#4**), and let a table hold the assignments.
- **Recover the root before adding a sense.** When a term needs a connotation the argument relies on, look first for it in the word's history (*legein*, to gather, inside *Logos*), so the move reads as recovery and not as invention.
- **Qualify change.** Where prose says something "changed" or "shifted", say what kind of change it was.
- **A fold-in is not a rebuild.** Merging an announcing sentence into its neighbour leaves the paragraph's model-written opener, padded middle and repeated argument in place. Reread this standard in full before every pass. Then rebuild each paragraph from its real claim. Cut the sentence that restates an argument already made (the GPT habit of returning to the same point in every paragraph). Use each defined phrase ("plays the `0`") once, where it does its work.
- **Use every logic the case enacts, not only occlusion.** A case of power usually shows several operations at once: cancellation in public, appropriation in private, a polar axis drawn across a population, `Ø` written into a person, `(0/1)/(1/0)` as the proper return. Write each where it acts.
- **Place a passage where its concept is in place.** Material that depends on a concept (context, after the **#4** row of the table) comes after the passage that establishes it. An established concept (Objective Internality) is used, not introduced again.

### Lessons carried from M04

- **Check every back-pointer against the earlier movements.** "Whose return we have already encountered" and "in the language of the earlier movement" must point at a passage that exists. If the term has not appeared, define it where it is used and drop the pointer.
- **A formal result licenses only what its hypotheses carry.** When a theorem is read through the notation (Gödel's second theorem as `1/0`), state the reading as the essay's, with the boundary in the same paragraph, and reread for slips like "Gödel proved that…" attached to the essay's own conclusion.
- **Thread a canonical spine through its cases.** When a movement carries a canonical relation (trust and faith under the formal limit), let each case advance it in its own terms (Russell's request, the *con*, Whitehead's two trusts, Gödel's stated hypothesis, the aphorism, the future-physics promise, Jung's knowing), rather than stating it once and leaving the cases to imply it.
- **Fold every announcing sentence, and watch the "The…" rate after folding.** Rebuilding drafts often push the "The…" opening rate above one in five. Count it before handing over, and change openings by leading with the actor, the case or the relation.
- **Counts check a rebuild; they never drive one.** Steering a draft toward a target rate produces nouning in disguise ("Their theory of types writes…", "Its subscript names…"). Rebuild from what the reader needs, then count.
- **Make the case plain before reading it.** A paradox, theorem or episode gets its actual case (who, when, what broke), an everyday version the reader can feel, and only then the formal statement. The logics come after that, in one reading of the whole episode. Name every referent: "the permission" must become *which* principle.
- **Say why, at every turn.** When figures go different ways, say what the encounter forced them to decide and how each choice answers it (Russell placing the fault in the expressions, Whitehead in the picture of reality).
- **Introduce others' ideas as concepts with their operation.** Write "Heidegger's concept of *Bestand*, standing-reserve, names…", "Whitehead's concept for that becoming is concrescence", "In Jung's concept, an archetypal image…". Don't write "Heidegger's Bestand…" as if it were an object.
- **No evaluative adverbs about the work's quality.** "Unusually exact" and "remarkably articulate" voice the reviser's surprise; the essay backs its own thinking. Descriptive intensity in the author's voice ("especially palpable", "real gifts") is fine.
- **When a source is "expounded", say what is expounded and why.** For example: "Dyczkowski, whose *Doctrine of Vibration* reconstructs the school's teaching from the Stanzas and their commentators…".

### Lessons carried from M01–M03

- **Speak through the human example.** Every reader has been the child at the bedside, has been named and misdescribed, and has said "I know myself" and been wrong; some are parents. Put the reader inside the scene, in the present of their own experience, with its particulars ("Why is it dark? Because the sun has gone down…"). Commit to one mode per scene: speak the scene out (narrated, in its own tense) or speak to it, never a muddle of both. Place the reader by recognition ("All of us have been the child in that bed, and some of us have since stood at its door"), not by casting them in a role with "you". The second person is brittle (not every reader is a parent) and wears thin with use; reserve it for an exercise the reader actually performs. Tenses must track the scene: no deictic "this evening" inside a generic present. "A child may…", "the parent could…" and "take a child's relation to a parent" hold the scene at arm's length and read as robotic.
- **Repair is not only subtraction.** A pass that only cuts and corrects is privative. Use the freedom to enrich: a fuller example, a clearer because, a transition that carries the reader's question, more body where the argument wants it.
- **Fake-poetic lines hide in the formal passages too.** "'A is A' makes a minimal journey", "Even a portrait of the viewer is a portrait for a viewer" and "The cycle completes through what lets it begin again" perform no operation. Replace each with the proposition or the actual case (Frege's morning star and evening star).
- **Tics of construction.** Watch for "x was there before y" and the reactive "x is y, without a and b". Each usually hides the nuance the facet deserves; say what this moment adds instead.
- **No list-like, stochastic prose.** A run of items or a string of single-move paragraphs is a symptom. Replace it with a sentence of reasoning, or merge the moves into one paragraph that thinks continuously.

## Mechanics and normalisation

The formats are already locked; this section points to them.

- **Notation** (`submission-package/essay/quilt/ql-expression-grammar.md` §I): display math in `$$…$$`; mathemes named in prose in backticks with Unicode (`0/1`, `1/0`, `4+2`, `5→0`, `(0/1)/(1/0)`); inline `\(…\)` and `\[…\]` are retired; rare inline maths in `$…$`. The two logics appear in their full expressions on first use in a section. Reader-facing naming is *syn-ballein* (Frank's ruling, 2026-10-03; never "sym-ballein").
- **Notes**: Chicago 18 notes defined at the section's close as `[^<section>-<slug>]`. Every marker in the body has a definition, every definition is referenced, and every quotation and named external fact has a note. Note text states only what the source house supports.
- **Source houses** (`submission-package/essay/symbolon/episteme/sources/README.md`): a source the prose relies on gets a house if it lacks one, and a passage the prose consumes gets a passage card in its house. Quotation readiness stays separate from citation readiness.
- **Movement notes and rooms**: when a movement's argument has evolved in the manuscript, its note in `section-rooms/<room>/movements/` is updated to the claim the manuscript now makes, and the room is rebuilt with the builder.
- **Structure**: in §0/1, movements are marked by their `<!-- movement:Mnn -->` comment and anchor and carry no heading. Other sections keep their current movement headings until Frank decides otherwise.
- **QL positions** keep their `#` in prose and tables (**#4** as `0`, never "4 is 0", which is false as arithmetic). The positions carry their canonical names: Ground, Definition, Dynamis, Pattern, Context, Quintessence. `5→0` is the **Möbius return**.
- **Bold**: defined terms at first definition and Frank's ratified formulations only.
- **Residue**: no tool or paste artefacts (`:chatgpt-content-reference{…}`, site names left from web citations, "Pasted text"), no merged paragraphs, no missing spaces at sentence seams.

## Length

The ceiling for §0/1 is **50,000 words**. Most of the work cuts weak prose and replaces it with more exact expression; many places also need more words, not fewer. Length is judged by whether the argument is carried, never by a target pace.

## Stamping a section done

A section is done when all of these hold:

1. Every movement has been repaired by the method above, and the whole section has been reread as one piece.
2. No fault in this standard remains unless a stated reason keeps it.
3. The section's buried arguments have been recovered or explicitly deferred to a named later movement.
4. Notes, source houses and movement notes are normalised and every note resolves.
5. Its admitted figures and plates are in place with captions wired to their sources of record.
6. Frank has read the section and accepted it.
