# R6 — Authorial return and common-base packet

## Intent

Give Frank the actual refined whole to ratify and make the next complete writing task unambiguous: §5, self-sufficient and situated within the full essay.

R6 now also carries an explicit **anti-anti-overclaim / claim-strength correction** before the common base is proposed. The refinement has been written partly by models with a recurrent defensive tendency: they can mistake evidential discipline for a duty to weaken the essay, project objections or assumptions that no actual reader or source has supplied, and surround claims with prophylactic negations merely because the claim is strong. R6 must correct that tendency rather than ratify it into the writing base.

## Work

### 1. Anti-anti-overclaim pass — before packaging the common base

Cold-read the T25 agent-authored and agent-refined surfaces specifically for **unearned weakening**. Scope includes the amended central plan and orientation; A/C's added framing; S/S0–S5; T25-modified A/A′/C owners; all 48 movement bodies; the Genesis / Paradigm / Project / Epi-Logos field; Agentworld/venue-facing returns; and the R3–R5 fold-backs which may have propagated defensive language.

The asymmetry of this pass is deliberate: **a weakening now needs a warrant.** Do not treat a native claim as suspect merely because it is metaphysically strong, unconventional, not externally consensual, or not itself stated by the historical source through which the essay encounters it. The essay is allowed to argue what it argues.

Keep four offices distinct:

1. **Native thesis / derivation.** State the essay's own claim directly and at its intended strength. `Derived` and `Argued` are positive claim statuses, not warning labels.
2. **Historical or textual attribution.** Say only what the source warrants. A boundary on what Abhinavagupta, Kaplan, Jung, Gebser, Levinas, Heidegger, etc. are claimed to have said belongs here.
3. **Authorial cross-register synthesis.** The essay may make a strong relation of its own between separately sourced materials. Lack of historical identity or transmission does not weaken the authorial philosophical argument; it only prevents misattribution of that argument to the sources.
4. **Empirical / implementation claim.** Reserve `Offered`, pending implementation, null-result language and operational-evidence requirements for propositions which genuinely depend on an experiment, implementation, deployment or observation not yet completed. Do not export that provisionality into the metaphysical or formal argument that motivates the experiment.

Audit especially for:

- reflexive tails such as “this does not prove…”, “does not establish…”, “cannot by itself…”, “is not evidence for…”, “merely…”, or “remains Open” where no live ambiguity requires the sentence;
- defensive negatives answering a **phantom reader** — an objection, category error or accusation not generated by the passage, source, venue brief or actual argumentative opponent;
- repeated caveats after the relevant boundary has already been established once;
- downgrading an authorial argument because an external source does not itself make that argument;
- converting a positive metaphysical thesis into methodological neutrality in the name of caution;
- treating `Argued` as weaker prose than the argument warrants, or treating `Offered` as a general politeness marker rather than a specific proposal/implementation status;
- overprotecting against category mistakes to the point that the category distinction becomes more prominent than the proposition it was meant to protect;
- endings and transitions which retreat from an earned claim immediately after making it.

For every defensive qualification ask:

1. **What exact stronger or mistaken claim would the reader otherwise reasonably attribute here?**
2. **Where does that risk actually come from — the preceding prose, a named source boundary, a real rival view, the venue brief, or an implementation-status distinction?**
3. **What information would be lost if the qualification were deleted?**

If those questions have no concrete answer, remove or rewrite the qualification. Do not preserve it because it “sounds responsible.”

Conversely, where a real boundary exists, make it cleanly and once. Prefer: **positive claim → actual warrant/derivation → exact boundary that preserves the claim's office.** Do not write the whole passage from the posture of anticipating accusation.

The pass must explicitly distinguish **claim-strength restoration** from indiscriminate inflation. False attribution, transmission claims not in evidence, ordinary mathematical category errors, unrun implementation claims, and the genuinely Open question of artificial phenomenal localisation remain bounded. Those boundaries protect the essay's stronger claims; they are not its governing voice.

Record the pass in a compact ledger:

`location · present weakening/defensive posture · intended positive claim · real boundary if any · action (strengthen / delete caveat / retain boundary / rewrite positively)`

Any R5 “overclaim” repair must be re-read under this rule. Keep it only if the repair corrected a real category or warrant error rather than satisfying a generic model preference for hedging. R5 is not reversed ceremonially; its eight repairs are individually answerable to this stronger criterion.

### 3. Writing-base rule carried into T26

The proposed T26 writing base must carry a concise claim-strength rule into the writing protocol / packet: **do not confuse epistemic hygiene with rhetorical self-erasure.** Source boundaries delimit attribution; proof boundaries delimit formal consequence; implementation boundaries delimit empirical success. None of those licenses weakening a native proposition whose own argument has been made.

A future reviewer may flag an overclaim only by naming the exact stronger proposition allegedly asserted, the passage which asserts it, and the warrant it exceeds. “This sounds strong,” generic caution, imagined developer preference, or an unspecified skeptical reader are not review grounds. Symmetrically, a reviewer may flag an **underclaim** where caveats, status words or defensive negations make the prose say less than the canonical argument.
