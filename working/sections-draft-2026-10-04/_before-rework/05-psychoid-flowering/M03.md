### #2 · Complexio, Quaternity and Senarius

In a long letter of May 1953, Pauli tried to draw his own position as a figure. He had dreamed for years of a controversy between quantum mechanics and what he called official physics, and the dreams had shown him the controversy as a one-dimensional section through a more meaningful two-dimensional world whose second dimension could only be the unconscious and the archetypes. He now believed, he wrote, that the same archetype could show itself both in an observer's choice of an experimental arrangement and in the result of the measurement, and that Einstein had played the role of the shadow in his life, though the dream had shown him that the Self was also contained in the shadow. Against his own strong conscious resistance, the unconscious had built up for him a correspondence between physics, with mathematics, and psychology, and it had done so by sacrificing the traditional requirement of objectivity, which it found disturbing, and choosing completeness instead. So he could represent his relation to physics and psychology through a quaternio, with Einstein opposite Bohr and Jung opposite Pauli, four people standing for four mental attitudes, Jung standing, of course, for analytical psychology. Physics, he went on, works with statistical laws and is fated to strive for completeness, and in striving it must come up against the psychology of the unconscious, because what it lacks is precisely the unconscious and the psyche of the observer; and just as physics seeks completeness, analytical psychology seeks a home, since psychology lives like an illegitimate child of the spirit on the fringe of the academic world. That was how the archetype of the *coniunctio* was constellated, and whether the union would ever be realised he did not know.[^s4-060]

Jung's reply came in October, and it contained a footnote that turned Pauli's figure back on itself. In placing Jung in the quaternio as the representative of the psychology of the unconscious, the fourth to three physicists, Pauli was repeating the dilemma of Plato, whose demiurge had to force the fourth element into the mixture of the cosmic opposites because it was so made that it did not easily blend. The transition from three to four is often awkward, Jung observed, because in the historical symbolism either the three deny the fourth or the fourth neutralises the three into a whole, as in the axiom of Maria the Prophetess, in which out of the third comes the one as the fourth.[^s4-061] He closed the letter with the same difficulty in a different tone: it meant much to him to see their points of view converging, he felt as isolated as Pauli did in this work, and oddly enough the problem was still the same two-thousand-year-old one, how one gets from three to four.[^s4-062] A few months earlier he had drawn a quaternio of his own for Pauli, setting transcendental reality as the whole above three aspects, the material, the spiritual and the psychic, and saying that this was as usual three plus one, with the fourth determining the unity and the whole.[^s4-063]

The figure had a history in Pauli's own work. His Kepler essay, which §4 · #0 met at the end of its writing, had set Kepler's trinitarian image of the cosmos, the sphere whose centre, surface and intervening space stand for Father, Son and Spirit, against Fludd's insistence on a fourfold that included the dark, material and feeling side Kepler's mathematics left out, and Pauli had found in Fludd's fourfold an attitude that modern science still needed beside Kepler's achievement.[^s4-064] Kepler's three were exact and complete in their own terms; Fludd's four admitted the observer and the obscure, and could not be checked. In 1953 Pauli drew his quaternio with himself and Jung as the two corners that classical physics had left out, so that the old quarrel came back with Pauli holding the pen, and Jung's footnote answered it in the old quarrel's own terms.

Both figures say more than either man said about them. Pauli's quaternio places four people at four corners, and its centre is empty; its coherence comes from the opposition of Einstein to Bohr and of Jung to Pauli, two axes crossing, a pair and its reflection, which is exactly how his egg dream produced its four. Jung's footnote names the two ways the figure can fail. If the three deny the fourth, the psychology of the unconscious stays outside the circle, the illegitimate child is refused a home, and the figure is a triangle pretending to be complete. If the fourth neutralises the three into a whole, the psyche absorbs the physicists, and the quaternity becomes the King Midas wholeness Pauli had complained of, in which everything turns psychic. In our arithmetic the first failure is an exclusion that forgets its own cut, and the second is an appropriation, the fourth counting the whole span of the figure from its own corner, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, while the three, reckoned from their side, appear as so much `−2`, a deficit the fourth was needed to make up. Jung's own quaternio shows the third possibility. Its fourth, transcendental reality, does not sit beside the three as one more aspect; it is the unity of the three, the whole within which material, spiritual and psychic are distinguished. In that figure the fourth determines the unity without becoming one of its parts.

Jung's quaternio came with an argument that shows how he meant the fourth. Pauli had sent him three readings of one of his dreams, a physical, a theological-metaphysical and a psychological one, and had said that even the third was not yet the ultimate truth. Jung agreed: the psychological reading contains only what can be conceived and represented in psychic terms, and compared with the whole truth the psychic picture is as incomplete as the ego is compared with the Self, though it is the conception of truth we have. We are, he wrote, virtually sealed off in the psyche, even though we can extend our prison to the wide world outside, and the thought had given Leibniz his windowless monads; but Jung did not accept the windowlessness, and believed that the psyche has windows through which ever broader realities can be seen. For that reason he held the psychic aspect of reality to be the most important one, and drew the quaternio in which transcendental reality is the whole of which material, spiritual and psychic are the three.[^s4-065] The passage holds both of Jung's commitments at once, and they are the commitments of the *complexio*. Psyche is the medium through which everything is known, so its picture is the only one we have; and the psyche is not the whole, so its picture is incomplete in exactly the way the ego is incomplete before the Self. Here the fourth determines the unity because it is the one term that no picture contains.

§0/1 has already followed Jung's quaternity some way. It quoted his sentence that the quaternity "forms the logical basis for any whole judgment", set his four functions beside Aristotle's four causes and the four corners of the tetralemma, read the connected quaternios at the close of *Aion* as passages in which a whole enters, four determinations are articulated and a whole returns, and placed the alchemists' "One born of the Four" at **#5** as wholeness held as unity.[^s4-066] It also answered Jung's **3+1** problem with an exact sequence: at **#4** the three perspectives are voided as independently complete accounts, at **#5** their relation is recognised as one appearing-act, and at **#0** that achieved unity returns through its originating condition, so that `3:3` is also `3:1`. What §0/1 could not yet do was say what holds the quaternity together while it moves, and why its two framing places, which the quaternal systems of the tradition leave implicit, are not further members of the four. That requires the concept Jung used for the Self itself.

**Complexio oppositorum**, the binding together of opposites, names the indivisible relation within which opposed determinations can become distinct and consequential. Jung took the phrase from the theological tradition and made it his name for the Self's whole: a totality that holds conscious and unconscious, good and evil, light and dark in one relation without cancelling their energy.[^s4-067] The concept has two moments, and our notation distinguishes them. Original *complexio* is the relation `0/1` before any opposition has been lived: One and All, source-unity and manifest-unity, are different aspects of one whole, and their inseparability precedes every later work of reconciling divided terms. Achieved *complexio* is an encountered opposition brought back into a containing relation that has changed by receiving it. One is the ground of differentiation; the other is what individuation accomplishes, repeatedly, through a life. Between them lie the two ways of losing the relation that our account has followed from the start: severance, which eliminates the relation and leaves two terms facing each other across nothing, and fusion, which eliminates the terms and leaves a relation that relates nothing. A *complexio* refuses both, and the refusal is what lets opposites keep their force.

Jung's typology shows the *complexio* at work inside one person, and it shows something about the count. In the introduction to *Psychologische Typen* of 1921 he distinguished two attitudes, introversion and extraversion, the directions of interest toward the subject's own psychic processes and toward the object, from four basic functions, thinking, feeling, sensation and intuition. He insisted that both attitudes occur in every person, a type naming only which predominates, and that each function can be either introverted or extraverted, and he retracted, in so many words, an earlier classification of his own that had identified thinking with introversion and feeling with extraversion.[^s4-068] The correction is the decisive move for us, because it makes the attitudes orientations of the functions and not two further functions. Four functions oriented in two directions are not six functions, and QL's senarius is not a discovery of six Jungian faculties. It is the `4+2` that §0/1 derived from a distinction's relation to itself, `2+2²`, two implicate offices and four explicate operations, and the comparison with Jung's typology has content because the relation holds, not because the numbers match. A function cannot operate without an orientation, and an orientation cannot do what a function does; the two attitudes are the directions through which the whole field of functions opens and returns, and that is exactly the office of the two implicate positions. §0/1 set introversion at **#0** and extraversion at **#5** in its phenomenal lens. Introversion is the inward turn from which a function draws its material before any of it has become explicit, and extraversion is the outward completion in which the differentiated functions return into a shared world. Four functions are the explicit body of a psyche's relation to its world; the two attitudes are the poles that let that body breathe.

Jung found the quaternity's own drama in the fourth function. Commenting on Schiller's letters on aesthetic education, he described how a culture that prizes one differentiated function above the others drives the rest into an inferior position, so that the privileged function serves society while the person is reduced to it, and modern man, as he put it, is represented by one function and identifies with it while denying the relevance of the others.[^s4-069] The **inferior function**, in his typology, is the one least developed and most unconscious, the fourth that arrives last because it was neglected first, and Jung argued with Schiller that what unites the opposites on the path of nature is not the redemption of the inferior function by the superior one but an acknowledgement of it, a coming to terms with it.[^s4-070] Here the arithmetic of the two logics is plain. A culture that privileges one function counts the whole span of the psyche from that function's pole, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the inferior function, read from the same span, becomes a deficit, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, a failure to measure up; the one who identifies with the superior function and the one who is despised for the inferior one are the two ends of one appropriation. Acknowledging the fourth is the syn-ballein move. It does not reverse the hierarchy, which would only shift the appropriation to the other pole; it lets the fourth determine the unity of the four, as transcendental reality determined the unity in Jung's own quaternio, and so changes what the first three were all along. In our notation the fourth arrives at **#4**, the position of Context, where each of the three explicit perspectives is voided of its claim to stand alone and seen within the situation that includes it. The inferior function is the context the superior function excluded in order to be superior.

Alchemy had a formula for the passage from three to four, and Jung made it a leitmotiv. It is the axiom attributed to Maria the Prophetess: one becomes two, two becomes three, and out of the third comes the one as the fourth.[^s4-071] Jung read it as running beneath Christian dogma like a dream beneath consciousness, interpolating the even numbers, which the tradition had associated with the feminine, the earth and what lies under it, between the odd numbers of the Trinity, and he noticed that alchemy never settled between three and four: three as well as four procedures, three as well as four colours, four elements of which three are often grouped with the fourth set apart. A psychologist, he said, finds the same wavering in the unconscious, where the least differentiated function is so contaminated with the collective unconscious that, when it becomes conscious, it brings up with it the archetype of the Self, the one as the fourth.[^s4-072] The inferior function is therefore more than a neglected skill. It is the door through which the whole enters, precisely because it is the part least able to pretend to be the whole. A person who has always thought her way through life meets the Self, in Jung's account, through feeling she cannot command; a person who has always felt her way meets it through a thought she cannot avoid.

Jung was still asking for an inventory in 1953, and the letter of May in which he called the psychoid an approach to neutral language also says what he expected the inventory to find: "Inasmuch as the number is an archetype, it can be safely assumed that it: (1) has substance, (2) has an individual form, (3) has meaning, and (4) has relationship connections to other archetypes."[^s4-073] He had begun the work a year before and abandoned it, and he asked Pauli whether there was really no systematic compilation of the mathematical properties of the numbers from one to nine. §0/1 claimed that QL is that inventory, begun where Jung's letter could not begin, at zero. His four properties already lay out its shape. Substance, individual form, meaning and relations are four explicit properties of a number, the first two closer to its outward face and the last two to its inward one, and they are held between a ground no property exhausts and a statement the number makes of its own accord; two years later Jung would write that number, thanks to its dynamis, is capable of making mythical statements. Read as a relation, his list is a quaternity of number's properties held between number's zero and number's speech, a `4+2` of number itself. This reading is the essay's, and it stays a reading; Jung counted from one, and the place he could not count from is the place this essay began.

What the two framing places add can now be stated exactly. Jung's quaternity gives differentiated wholeness an explicit form: four functions, four elements, four quarters of a mandala, four connected quaternios. QL's `4+2` adds the implicate poles, the ground from which the four open and the recognition through which they return, and with them it adds motion. A quaternity drawn as a square around a centre is a still figure, and Jung knew it; that is why his *Aion* diagram is a ring of quaternios that turn, each passing into the next, and why the circulation goes, in his account, from an unconscious totality to a conscious one.[^s4-074] The two places that make the ring turn are the places a static quaternity leaves implicit: a whole entering and a whole returned. The **senarius**, then, is a dynamic quaternity, four held between two, and it derives its force from that exact relation, not from any sacred or numerological associations that may gather around the number six. Perfection, harmonic ratio and the other mathematical offices of six belong to §3; here six is simply what a two amounts to once its distinction includes its own relating, written at the scale of a psyche that has to keep changing while remaining whole.

Six positions have an internal grammar of their own, which the author's theorem field sets out and which keeps the senarius from being read as a ladder of six rungs or a flat list of six items. Read in adjacent pairs, the positions form three harmonics. Being joins Ground and Definition, **#0** and **#1**: a field is present and can be called to account. Becoming joins Dynamis and Pattern, **#2** and **#3**: force becomes intelligible as recurrence, and recurrence stays alive only as force capable of a further instance. Knowing, which is also unknowing, joins Context and Quintessence, **#4** and **#5**: a situated claim becomes honest only when its horizon exceeds it. Read in complementary pairs, positions whose numbers sum to five, the same six form three folds. Essence joins **#0** and **#5**, the conscious circumstance and the inexhaustible depth; Constitution joins **#1** and **#4**, the answerable definition and the world in which someone asks and asserts; Text and Texture join **#2** and **#3**, the felt weave of force and its readable pattern.[^s4-075] Only one pair belongs to both readings, **#2** with **#3**, which is at once adjacent and complementary, so that Becoming is also Text and Texture: the hinge on which the whole figure turns. Jung's quaternity, set inside this grammar, is the four explicit positions, Definition, Dynamis, Pattern and Context; the senarius holds them by Essence, the fold of the two implicate poles, and lets them move through their harmonics. A *complexio oppositorum* in this sense is not one tension held still but three kinds of pairing working at once, and the Self that Jung called a *complexio* par excellence is the whole that keeps all of them in play.

Pauli's letter had already found the shadow in the quaternio, Einstein as the figure who opposed him and in whom the Self was nonetheless contained, and Jung's *Aion* makes the shadow part of the turning ring. In its closing chapter he reconstructed four quaternios out of Gnostic and alchemical material, the Anthropos, the Shadow, the Physis and the Lapis, in the names §0/1 gave them, and he was explicit that the Shadow Quaternio was his own modern insertion into the sequence, introduced because an exclusively upward-turning religious orientation could not contain the psychic situation of his own time.[^s4-076] The circulation therefore passes downward as well as upward, through the dark material as well as the bright, and Jung's conclusion keeps two consequences together. A shadow contains capacities and possibilities as well as what consciousness morally condemns, so incorporating it is not endorsing evil; and a more inclusive symbolic totality leaves the differentiated person fully responsible for judgment, since a *complexio oppositorum* holds opposites whose force must still be met and answered.[^s4-077] If completeness made every act equally right, the movement would destroy the very responsibility through which unconscious determination becomes conscious participation. Wholeness in Jung's sense is harder than goodness, because it has to keep the difference between good and evil consequential while refusing to put evil outside the whole.

At that point the quaternity meets the problem §0/1 called paradox, and our concept for it makes the stakes precise. **Paradox**, in the sense the essay develops, is the pressure through which a containing relation becomes available to transformation. Two determinations with genuine standing cannot be held together by the relation under which they acquired their standing, and another answer of the same kind will not settle the difficulty, because the means of answering has entered the problem.[^s4-078] Russell's paradox was such a pressure on the principle of forming collections; Gödel's sentence placed it on the relation between truth and proof; Pauli's dream of a two-dimensional world whose second dimension was the unconscious placed it on physics. In a life it arrives when the person can neither go on being what she has been nor simply become its opposite: the daughter of the last movement can neither keep repeating her father's sentence nor become a mother who never says no. Choosing one existing pole leaves the relation unresolved. Resolution, when it comes, changes the slash, the containing relation through which the poles were produced and understood, and a quaternity is the form in which that change can be seen. Two opposites become two corners of a square whose other two corners are their relation read from each side, and the square sits between a ground and a return that neither corner owns. A third position appears that is no compromise between the first two, because it is the relation itself become visible as a position.

Greek myth has a figure for this, and §2 developed it at length. When Ares and Aphrodite, War and Love, lay together, the child they bore was Harmonia, and her name says what she is: a fitting together, a joint, the concord of things that remain different.[^s4-079] Harmonia does not cancel her parents; she carries both powers in a relation neither could produce alone, and §2 followed the difference between that birth and the apparatus-marriage of Hephaestus's net, which held the same two lovers in a perfect enclosure and bore nothing. Heraclitus gave the relation its instrument: the harmony of the bow and the lyre, in which the string's tension against the frame is the condition of the note.[^s4-080] Recalled here, beside Jung's quaternity, the myth adds one thing the diagram cannot show. Opposition bears concord only when the relation between the opposed survives the opposition, and the child of that survival is a new term, with its own life, in the next generation.

A second myth carries the senarius as a whole story, and the essay gives it a plate of its own rather than a second narrative here. In Apuleius's telling, Psyche, separated from Eros by her own forbidden look, must perform four labours set by Venus: sorting the seeds, gathering the golden fleece, fetching water from the source of the Styx, and descending to the underworld for a box of Proserpina's beauty. The four labours are the explicit middle of her story; her descent and her waking, from the deathlike sleep that the opened box pours over her, are its implicate thresholds; and the marriage that follows is a union in which the two remain two, crowned by the birth of their daughter Voluptas, delight, the fruit of a traversed tension.[^s4-081] Read through QL, the tale is a `4+2` lived from the inside, and the linked plate follows its helpers, its reserved return and its ending through its own sources. In the prose it needs only one sentence: wholeness that can move is earned through four differentiated works held between a descent and an awakening.

Jung's quaternity has become a form in which opposites can be held, a relation that can turn without losing its terms, and a figure that now includes its own shadow and its own ground. A form of this kind has to be transmitted if it is to do anyone any good, from Jung to Pauli, from a dream to the dreamer, from one generation to the next, from this essay to its reader; and every transmission risks closing what it carries, turning the living relation into a figure that stands where the relation stood. Lacan made that risk his life's problem, and he wrote a notation to meet it. Our next movement asks what his notation does, what ours does, and how a stroke that first hides zero can become the bar through which its hiding can be seen.
