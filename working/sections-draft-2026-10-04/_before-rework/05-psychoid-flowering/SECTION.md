## §4 — Psychoid Flowering

<a id="M31"></a>

### #0 · The Psychoid Problem

In the middle of March 1948, within four days of each other, Wolfgang Pauli had two dreams. He had more or less finished his long essay on Kepler, the astronomer who had heard the planets as a music before he wrote them as laws, and he noticed, as he usually did, that the dreams came at the equinox, the time of year at which he found himself psychically least stable and, by the same token, most creative.[^s4-001] In the first, his first physics teacher appeared and told him that the change in the splitting of the ground state of the hydrogen atom was a fundamental one, and that brass tones had been engraved on a metal plate; then Pauli went to Göttingen. Dream 2 came as seven pictures in a row, with no words spoken until the very end. A woman arrived with a bird, and the bird laid a large egg. It divided itself into two. Pauli approached and found that he was already holding a third egg, this one with a blue shell, and he divided it himself; the two halves, against all expectation, stayed whole, so that he now held two whole blue eggs. Then all four eggs changed into two identical rows of mathematical expressions, the cosine and sine of half an angle, and the rows gave a formula:

$$
\frac{\cos(\delta/2)+i\sin(\delta/2)}{\cos(\delta/2)-i\sin(\delta/2)}.
$$

Then, for the first time in the whole sequence, Pauli spoke. It all, he said, gives $e^{i\delta}$, and that is the circle. The formula vanished, and a circle appeared.[^s4-002]

Pauli was the physicist who had formulated the exclusion principle and whose judgment every new theory in the circle around Bohr had to survive, and §0/1 has already told how he came to Jung as a patient and stayed as a correspondent.[^s4-003] What the dream asks of us is less biographical. Its arithmetic is exact, and anyone can check it. For real $\delta$ the numerator is $e^{i\delta/2}$ and the denominator $e^{-i\delta/2}$; neither vanishes, both have modulus one, and their quotient is $e^{i\delta}$, a point on the unit circle for any fixed value of $\delta$ and the whole circle as $\delta$ runs through a full turn. A mathematician who has never dreamed can verify that much, and the verification does not depend on anyone being moved by it. Yet the dream did not arrive as a theorem. It arrived as a sequence in which a man passed from watching to taking part: the first division happened in front of him, the second happened through his own hands, and the egg he divided had been in his hand before he noticed he was holding it. Only after he had performed the division, and seen the four eggs turn into two rows of numbers, could he say what the rows amounted to. A circle appeared once he had spoken, and the formula that had carried him there disappeared. Here an exact mathematical relation and a transformation of the person recognising it happened as one event, and neither half of the event can be dropped without losing what the other half means. Take away the change from spectator to participant, and the dream becomes an assortment of number symbols; take away the trigonometry, and the passage into recognition loses the operation through which it proceeds.

This is the problem that Jung and Pauli called psychoid, and §4 takes it up where §0/1 left it. There we learned that Jung distinguished the archetypal images that reach consciousness from the archetype as such, an irrepresentable ordering factor that he called psychoid because it shows itself in physical as well as psychic events; that Pauli proposed the theological word *homo-usia*, sameness of substance, for the identity he suspected between the archetypal order and physis, and found in mathematical concepts the language neutral enough to express it; and that Jung answered by locating the borderland between physics and psychology in the secret of number, since whole numbers are as much inside as outside.[^s4-004] We took up their bequest as the essay's thesis: number is where psyche and physis meet, and Quaternal Logic is the form in which we take that meeting up. What §0/1 could only state, §4 has to follow through the dreams and disagreements in which the two men kept their question open, because the psychoid is a relation that has to be worked before it can be claimed, and Jung and Pauli worked it against one another for a decade. **The psychoid problem** asks how a psychological image and a physical event can display a meaningful structural relation when neither can be causally reduced to the other. Its difficulty is that both obvious answers are available and both are wrong in the same way. One can say that the egg dream is a picture of Pauli's mind and the formula an accident of his profession; one can say that the formula is the real content and the dream its decorative wrapping. Each answer hands the whole relation to one of its sides.

Pauli's just-finished essay shows why he could accept neither answer. Its subject was a quarrel of the early seventeenth century between Johannes Kepler, who wanted the harmony of the cosmos demonstrated through measurement and mathematical proportion, and the physician and Rosicrucian Robert Fludd, who held that the quantitative method dismembered a living whole that could only be grasped in symbolic images and through the participation of the knower. Kepler won, and modern physics is his inheritance. Pauli's achievement in the essay was to show that Kepler's own road to his laws ran through archetypal images he did not regard as images: Kepler read the sphere, with its centre, its surface and the space between them, as an image of the Trinity, Father, Son and Holy Spirit, and reasoned from that image toward the order of the planets, so that the founder of quantitative astronomy had argued from a symbol he took for a fact.[^s4-005] Pauli did not hide his sympathy for the side that lost. Fludd had seen what Kepler's method would leave out, the qualitative, the feeling-toned and the observer, and Kepler had seen what Fludd's images could never do, which was submit to a check. Their quarrel is the psychoid problem in its first modern form, and it is already a case of the two logics: Kepler's cut, exact and indispensable, which forgets the participation of the one who cuts, and Fludd's whole, faithful to participation and blind to measure, each liable to claim the whole span of the relation for itself. Pauli, three centuries later and a physicist to his fingertips, wanted the cut and the whole together, and the dreams of March 1948 came to him at the end of an essay that had tried to hold them.

Pauli's own commentary refuses both answers, and his reasons are worth hearing in his order. He read the first dream through what he knew of physics: the splitting of a spectral line can change only through an existing field of force, so a change in the splitting of the ground state pointed, in the psychic register, to an intervention of consciousness. A hydrogen atom, a single positive nucleus circled by a single negative electron, is the simplest pair of opposites there is, and its nucleus, the proton, whose name means "the first", is the building block of every heavier nucleus, so the dream was pointing cosmogonically to a beginning. Brass tones he took as a genuine symbol: as sound they belong to physics, as music they carry feeling, which physics cannot express, and through Kepler's music of the spheres they had a cosmic character, so that they joined physis and psyche in one image. Read with *aere perennius*, more lasting than bronze, the plate said that archetypes leave their stamp on matter, and Pauli drew the consequence that physis and psyche had come to be indivisibly linked at the level of archetypes.[^s4-006] Of the second dream he would offer no interpretation at all, since any such interpretation, he wrote, would call for further progress in all the sciences, though he did say what he saw happening in it: the spontaneously divided egg was a ground state; the dreamer's recognition and action were a conscious intervention; and the quaternity of four eggs came about through a duality reflected in the psyche. In the dream the imaginary unit $i$, a symbol not contained among the ordinary numbers, had the function of uniting the opposites and producing wholeness, and Pauli used the occasion for the sentence §0/1 has already quoted, that for him the mathematical representation is a symbolic description par excellence.[^s4-007] He then turned on his own discipline. Physics has to represent regularity in nature in concepts and therefore attends only to what can be reproduced and measured, so everything feeling-toned, judgmental and emotional falls on the other side of it; that is a shortcoming of physics within life, not of quantum theory within physics, the same distinction he had once put to Bohr about Einstein. Mathematics, by contrast, has a qualitative side as well as a quantitative one, which shows in the theory of numbers and in topology, and Pauli thought the unifying power of mathematical symbolism reached further than physics did.[^s4-008]

A few sentences earlier in the same essay Pauli had stated the structural point on which the dream turns. A quaternity, he wrote, would not be effective within physics itself, but it would belong to the wholeness made up of physics and psychology insofar as the complementary pair of opposites in physics reappears, reflected, in the psychic sphere.[^s4-009] A pair seen and the same pair reflected make four. Our notation reads the dream one step further, and the step is the essay's own. Between the woman who arrives with her bird and the circle that appears when the dreamer speaks, the dream holds four eggs: two that divided before him and two that divided through him. The arrival is a source, a power that brings forth without being one of the things brought forth, and the circle is a return in which the whole sequence is recognised as one figure. Four explicit terms held between an implicate source and an implicate return is the shape §0/1 derived four times over and wrote `4+2`, and it is the shape Jung drew in the connected quaternios of *Aion*. Pauli's two pairs give the explicit four, the division that happens and the division that is done, the pair and its reflection; the woman and the circle give the two places that frame them. Its seven pictures remain seven, and nobody should count them as six positions; what the dream shows is a relation, a fourfold articulation held between an emergence it did not author and a recognition it could only reach by speaking.

Their correspondence is the better witness, because there the relation had to survive argument. Their first serious disagreement came in November 1948, the day after a conversation at Jung's house, when Pauli wrote to ask whether a dream and an event separated by two or three months could still be called synchronous, which was to ask what temporal relation the word was supposed to denote.[^s4-010] Jung sent him the manuscript of his essay on synchronicity for criticism, and Pauli's long reply of June 1949 pressed harder. He could not see the archetypal basis in Rhine's card-guessing experiments that he could see in Jung's other material, and he did not want them counted as the same kind of evidence. He asked for more precision in the word "acausal", since the absence of a causal chain through space and time is a different thing from the absence of any ordering relation. He noticed that a formulation beginning from simultaneity went on to include prediction, where the two events plainly do not occur together. And he invented a parable of a radioactive stranger to show how far the physical decay clock and the symbolic "radioactivity" of dream language had to be held apart before any comparison between them could mean anything, ending with the demand for a neutral language that did not yet exist.[^s4-011] Jung's concept of **synchronicity**, as he finally defined it, names a coincidence in time of two or more causally unrelated events that have the same or a similar meaning, which he distinguished from mere synchronism, the simultaneous occurrence of two events.[^s4-012] Pauli's objections stand inside that definition. Each of its words, "time", "cause", "event" and "meaning", had to keep what it denoted in physics and in psychology before the two could be compared, and his insistence is what gives synchronicity its positive research office: to investigate a proposed ordering of meaningful events while keeping clear what each discipline means by its terms. Letters and dreams are the historical record of that investigation, and the investigation itself is still open.

Jung's best-known case shows what the definition has to hold. A young woman he was treating dreamed, at a critical moment, that she was given a golden scarab. While she was telling him the dream he was sitting with his back to the closed window, and he heard a gentle tapping behind him; he turned and saw a flying insect knocking against the pane from outside, opened the window and caught it as it flew in. It was a rose-chafer, the nearest thing to a golden scarab to be found in that latitude, which had, against its usual habits, tried to get into a dark room at that moment, and Jung wrote that nothing like it had happened to him before or since.[^s4-013] The beetle had its causes and the dream had its causes, and neither chain contains the other; what joins them is a meaning, carried by an image old enough that the scarab rolling its ball across the sky had once been the Egyptian sun renewing itself each morning. Such a case cannot be produced on demand, which is why Pauli's insistence on the meaning of every word in the definition matters, and it happened, in and to two lives, which is why Jung's insistence on receiving it matters. Both insistences belong to the problem. A science that dismisses the case because it cannot be repeated has decided in advance what may count, and a psychology that treats it as proof of a hidden cause has borrowed a warrant it does not have.

In 1953 the disagreement deepened, as both men tried to say where their common ground lay. In February Pauli sent Jung what he called a personal treatise in the form of a letter. Many people, he wrote, still regarded the unconscious as non-being, a mere privation of consciousness, while others placed the archetypes, like ideas in general, in metaphysical spaces above the heavens, and he found both views equally doubtful. He chose a third road, which he took from Aristotle, and called the unconscious *potential being*, a legitimate human description of occurrences that may come about in consciousness and in that sense part of the symbolic reality of the thing in itself. Ideas, he added, have no fixed abode, not even a heavenly one, and he applied to all of them the old alchemical formula for God, the centre of all things whose periphery is nowhere.[^s4-014] Cusa's infinite sphere, which §0/1 turned on the world and then on the plane folded into a torus, had arrived in Pauli's letter as a description of where ideas live. In a footnote to the same letter he told Jung plainly what troubled him. As a psychologist, Jung had an understandable aversion to every form of reality that was not purely psychic, and just as everything King Midas touched turned to gold, everything Jung looked at seemed to Pauli to turn psychic and only psychic.[^s4-015] In March Pauli resisted calling the whole ordering field psychic, because a concept meant to mediate psyche and physis should not take one pole's name in advance, and he proposed *homo-usia* as a working hypothesis with mathematics as its language, while keeping mathematical objects distinct from the psychic experience of doing mathematics.[^s4-016]

Jung's reply of May 1953 is where §0/1 found the word psychoid, and it holds more than the word. He agreed at once that one can never be content with what is ascertainable, because the whole drama of knowledge is played out on the borderline between the ascertainable and the non-ascertainable, and he described the area between what is perceived and what is not ascertainable here and now as the area of the psyche. Then he drew the line Pauli had asked for: "I do not extend the concept of the psychic to include the nonascertainable, for here I use the speculative concept of the psychoid, which represents an approach to neutral language in that it suggests the presence of a nonpsychic essence."[^s4-017] Numbers and archetypes, he continued, are not just psychic, "otherwise they would be fabrications. But in actual fact they are 'in themselves being' (or psychoid) existences whose autonomous existence corresponds to that of the material object."[^s4-018] Everything is conceived in and through the medium of the psyche, he granted, but the objects of its perception are not integrated into its substance and so do not forfeit their existence. And then he disputed Pauli's dreams. Pauli dreamed in physics, Jung suggested, because physics was his natural language, as the proverb says the dog dreams of bread and the fisherman of fish, and he himself never dreamed of physics but of mythology, which was just as unpsychological; the dream's vocabulary did not settle what the dream meant.[^s4-019] Pauli answered by accepting the clarification and keeping his ground. Those physical terms in his dreams had first struck him as strange, he said, and not as the familiar idiom of a physicist, and he went on to propose a future physics enlarged enough to adopt psychology, adding a condition on any such adoption that cuts against his own discipline's pride: "the adopting one is the one that has the more extensive system of concepts and lends itself more readily to generalisation; but it is also always the one that undergoes the more radical and more sweeping changes, precisely because it is being extended."[^s4-020] In October Jung refused the proposal. Psychology could not be classified within a general physics as chemistry had been, because psychic processes cannot be measured, and he named instead two points of contact between the fields, number and synchronicity.[^s4-021] Two years later he put the point with a precision that the whole correspondence had been working toward: "My feeling is that the common ground shared by physics and psychology does not lie in the parallelism of the formation of concepts but rather in 'that ancient spiritual dynamis' of numbers."[^s4-022] In the same letter he added a sentence on which much of this section depends: "The autonomy of a psychic factor lies in the fact that thanks to its dynamic force, it is capable of making its own statements."[^s4-023] Myths, dreams and delusions speak from the objective psyche without passing through conscious reasoning, and number, given the chance, makes its own mythical statements in the same way.

Even the vocabulary of experiment became a field of dispute between them. Pauli had described the experimental physicist's choice of one arrangement over another as a sacrifice, since each measurement buys a definite piece of knowledge at the price of the knowledge it excludes, the very loss §0/1 followed through complementarity. Jung questioned the word: for the experimenter, he thought, it was basically just a decision, and only metaphorically a sacrifice, since sacrifice means giving up what one owns for nothing in return.[^s4-024] Decision and sacrifice are two names for one act read from two sides: from the side of what is chosen and from the side of what the choice costs. When the Meta-Epistemic Framework gives each of its lenses a face of articulation and a face of power, *Sacrifice* and *Decision* stand next to each other in its series of powers, and the quarrel between the two men over one word turns out to have named two adjacent offices of one act.

In the last episode, the one §0/1 drew on for Pauli's mirror-image principle, Jung asked in June 1957 for Pauli's opinion of a manuscript that placed the mind in hyperspace. Pauli distinguished metric structure from topology, criticised the use of dimension numbers without mathematical grounding, and in the middle of that criticism offered his conjecture that physis and psyche are probably two aspects of one and the same abstract fact, which a mirror-image principle would represent most naturally.[^s4-025] The conjecture sits inside the criticism, and the placement is the point. Neither man let the other's vocabulary take possession of the relation. Pauli would not let the psyche absorb the ordering field; Jung would not let physics adopt psychology; Pauli would not let a striking image of wholeness supply mathematics it lacked; Jung would not let a dream's physical vocabulary settle what the dream meant. Each correction changed the question the other had to answer, and the question that survived their corrections is sharper than the one either brought to it.

We can now say exactly which operations their corrections were resisting, because §0/1 has given us the arithmetic of the two logics. The healthy relation between psyche and physis is a held polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`: two descriptions of one event, each with its sense from the other across a common axis, the way Bohr's complementary descriptions of wave and particle exclude each other in any single arrangement and are both required for the whole. Pauli's complaint about King Midas names an appropriation. When everything one looks at turns psychic, the psyche counts the whole span of the relation from its own pole, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the same span reckoned from the other end is the physicalist's appropriation, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, in which everything turns physical and the dream becomes a by-product of neurones. Both hardened positions need each other, as red needs blue, and each reads the other's excess as proof that its own is warranted. A third operation is cancellation, `(−1)+(+1)=0`: the coincidence is summed away as chance and the dream as noise, so that nothing remains owing on either side and nothing of the relation remains in the account. Jung and Pauli refused all three. They kept their difference, kept writing, and let each reading of the event be read through the other, which is syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, as two people performed it over a decade of letters, and the mirror-image principle Pauli arrived at in 1957 is that relation in its own words: physis and psyche as the two orientations of one abstract fact, each the mirror in which the other can be read.

A dream report, a felt coincidence, a contemplative disclosure or an encounter its subject calls supernatural can therefore enter this inquiry with its full weight, before anyone has decided what caused it. Jung's **psychic facticity**, which §0/1 set beside Gebser's co-presence of structures, is the rule that makes this possible: something happened in and to a life, and its happening is real as experience before any further question about its cause or its external reference has been settled.[^s4-026] The rule enlarges rigour; it does not lower it. Experience, report, symbolic articulation, causal explanation and ontological interpretation are different evidential acts, so a person can receive Pauli's dream in full, as an event that changed what he could say and do, while its physics, its psychology and its metaphysics each keep their own warrant. Jung's sentence about the autonomy of a psychic factor adds the positive half. A dream is a statement made by something that is not the dreamer's conscious reasoning, and to receive it as a psychic fact is to let that statement be heard before deciding whose statement it is.

Jung gave the rule its ground in a passage of *Symbols of Transformation*. We possess no physics of the soul, he wrote, and cannot observe or judge it from any Archimedean point outside ourselves, because all knowledge of the psyche is itself psychic; and yet the soul is the only experient of life and existence, the only immediate experience we can have and the condition without which the world has no subjective reality at all.[^s4-027] The sentence turns the absence of an outside into a positive finding. Whatever else a dream or a coincidence may turn out to be, it is first something experienced, and the experiencing is the one place where every other kind of evidence also has to arrive.

Freud had made the first half of this possible when, in "The Unconscious" of 1915, he argued that unconscious mental processes are a legitimate and necessary assumption of science, because the data of consciousness have gaps that only unconscious acts explain, and with that argument a region of psychic life that no one had owned became formally speakable.[^s4-028] The admission came with the risk that any scientific minting brings. Once the unconscious had entered the count, it could be treated as one more object, a storehouse or mechanism or rival agency set over against the ego, a determinate `1` standing where the condition of determination had been. Jung pressed beyond that reduction, not by retreating from science but by keeping its empirical demand while refusing to let the fact harden into a possessed object: religious, mythic, alchemical and numinous images are real events in the psyche, capable of transforming a life, and no catalogue of their appearances turns the ground of their appearing into one more thing on the list.[^s4-029] The psychoid is the furthest reach of that refusal. It names what the unconscious would have to be if its images could show up in matter as well as in mind, and so it names a ground that neither discipline can own.

David Bohm reached the same ground from the physicist's side, and §0/1 has already introduced his concept of the **holomovement**, the encompassing movement of which apparently separate things are relatively stable unfoldings. Bohm went on to treat mind and matter as two such unfoldings. In his account the explicate order of matter and the explicate order of thought are both projections of a common, more encompassing ground, so that their relations are internal before they are ever compared from outside, and neither can be derived from the other because both are derived from what enfolds them.[^s4-030] That gives the psychoid problem a process form. Mind and matter can be distinct explicate orders of one movement whose relations are carried within it; their correspondences, where they occur, are not messages passed between two sealed substances but the same order showing on two surfaces. Bohm deliberately left the internal anatomy of that movement general. §0 has supplied it through the tattvas, the Śaiva account of how universal awareness differentiates itself through Māyā and the contractions into a bounded knower and its habitable world, which arise together and are never joined after the fact. And §3 has derived, from `0/1` alone, the differential field we have called the **arche-topos**: the field of placement, path and return in which a determination can close locally while keeping a record of the journey that brought it home, written on the torus and its covering plane.

Here the essay makes its claim, and it is an ontological claim. The arche-topos is psychoid: the differential field derived in §3 is prior to the ordinary division of psyche and matter, and its organising relation is what each side of that division later discloses in its own way: as formal invariant in mathematics, as felt meaning in the psyche, as regularity in physics. That is why the same relation can be received by distinct descriptions without assigning either final priority, and it is why the two halves of Pauli's dream can both be true at once. Pauli's formula is checkable and his transformation is real because both are surfaces of one ordering relation, whose mathematical face we can verify and whose psychic face we can undergo. Jung and Pauli asked for a neutral language in which the meeting of psyche and physis could be written; QL is that language in the specific sense that its terms are positions in a relation, `0`, `/` and `1`, before they are either psychic or physical contents, so that a position can be occupied by a number, an image or a physical quantity without any of them owning it. The claim is **Argued**: it rests on the derivation of the arche-topos in §3, on the convergence of the `4+2` across the formal routes §0/1 followed, and on the cross-register witnesses gathered here, each of which contributes its own operation. Equations carry formal invariants; images carry experienced meaning; synchronicity raises the question of acausal coordination; physical observation bears empirical claims. A measurable causal process running between psyche and matter would be a further empirical discovery with its own evidence. Our thesis does not wait on such a discovery, because it states a relation of order, not a mechanism of transmission.

§3's topology lets us say why the same relation can show as meaning on one side and as exactness on the other. A torus is the plane folded by identification, and the covering map from the plane to the torus sends each point of the surface to an infinite lattice of points on the plane, so that a loop which closes on the torus lifts to a path on the plane that ends somewhere else, displaced by a whole-number step. Locally the surface is flat, and from inside any small region nothing distinguishes it from the infinite plane it was folded from. The author has read this as the topology of experience: a life's view from where it stands is valid and is not the whole topology, and the opening of the torus cannot be seen from inside the surface, only met through the circuits it makes possible. A many-to-one map conceals the multiplicity it has folded, which is Māyā, operative measure, given topological form.[^s4-031] The return of the loop to its starting point and the displacement recorded by its lift are two exact descriptions of one journey, and neither cancels the other. Pauli's circle and the arithmetic that produced it, the dreamer's return to himself and the formula's full turn of phase, are a psychic and a formal rendering of that same fact: a return that keeps the history of its passage.

Later scholarship has tried to give the Pauli–Jung conjecture a formal outline of its own. Harald Atmanspacher describes the basic reality in their conjecture as "psychophysically neutral (PPN)", lacking the distinction between the mental and the physical, and he is careful to say that his use of quantum terminology to frame the mind–matter problem is a speculative generalisation.[^s4-032] His reconstruction and ours are different constructions. His begins from a neutral reality from which mental and physical descriptions are derived by decomposition; ours begins from a relation, `0/1`, in which ground and determination are already held through their stroke, and derives the field from the self-relation of that stroke. They meet on the point that matters for this section: the ground is neither mind nor matter, and both are accounts of it. Where they differ, the difference is information, and the discipline the essay calls homologia and analogia keeps it in view by asking, of every crossing, which operation is preserved, which register changes and which evidence stays with its source.

Now the problem has a precise shape. A meaningful structure can appear on both sides of a threshold that neither side masters; the ground that orders it cannot be written as one more content on either side; and yet it must become legible somewhere, in a particular dream, a particular formula, a particular life, or it would be nothing to anyone. Jung's archetypes as such never appear, and Pauli's dream was nonetheless dreamed by one man, in Zürich, in the middle of March, at the end of his work on Kepler. Ordering capacity and the particular in which it shows have to be written together, and written so that neither swallows the other. §1 earned `0/1` as the root symbolon of ground and mark; §3 counted its eight determinations; and the one at Pattern, **#3**, holds exactly this relation of a capacity and its instance. We write it `X/x`, and the next movement follows it into the life of a person who has to become, through everything that formed her, the particular through which the whole becomes a question.

### #1 · Individuation through `X/x`

Thirty years after the bedside of §0/1, the child who asked why it was dark has a child of her own. It is late, the light is off, and a small voice asks why the moon follows the car. She answers, and the answer draws another question, and somewhere around the orbit of the earth she hears herself say, "Because I said so," in a voice that is not quite hers. It is her father's voice, the cadence and the slight impatience and the little fall at the end, and for a moment she is both people at once, the child in the bed thirty years ago and the adult at the door tonight. She had promised herself she would never say it. What she discovers, standing there, is that she could only have recognised the sentence as his because she has become someone who can hear it from outside: the voice came through her, and she heard it arrive. In the same moment she understands her father better than she has ever understood him, because she knows now what the sentence was protecting, the tiredness and the worry about tomorrow and the fear of having no answer, and she knows how much of him she carries, because the sentence was ready in her mouth before she chose it. Recognition of the parent and difference from the parent arrived together, and neither would have arrived alone. A daughter wholly fused with her father would have said the sentence without hearing it, and a daughter wholly severed from him would never have found it in her mouth.

Erich Neumann told this moment as a cosmogony in *The Origins and History of Consciousness*, published in German in 1949 with a foreword by Jung, where he argued that the individual ego, in becoming conscious, passes through the same archetypal stages that human consciousness as a whole has passed through, stages that myth already carries in its images of the world's beginning: the uroboros, the Great Mother, the separation of the World Parents, the birth of the hero, the slaying of the dragon, the rescue of the captive and the transformation of the hero.[^s4-033] §0/1 promised that our parent–child image carries an archetypal history that §4 would follow through Neumann's separation of the World Parents, and the history begins before the parents can be told apart. At the beginning Neumann places the round: the egg, the womb, the enclosing waters, the serpent with its tail in its mouth. His question "Whence?", which he calls both the original question and the question of the origin, has a single answer with two interpretations; the answer is the round, and the interpretations are the womb and the parents, the one container and the two who are not yet two.[^s4-034] He compares this time before the opposites to the *wu chi* of Chinese philosophy, whose symbol is the empty circle, and he says that the symbolic thinking that produced these images is trying to grasp what our present consciousness can only state as paradox, all and nothing, unity without opposites, and that the image has one advantage over the paradoxical formula: its unity can be seen at a single glance.[^s4-035] That advantage is the mytheme's, and the next movement but one will need it.

The round has an inner life before it opens, and Neumann finds it in the mouth. A serpent with its tail in its mouth eats itself and is fed by itself, and Neumann reads the earliest creation images as a food symbolism older than any sexual one: hunger and food are the prime movers, intake and output the rhythm of the earliest existence, and to be nourished is the fundamental form of a life that does not yet know itself as separate from what feeds it.[^s4-036] He follows the image into an early Vedic account, which he takes from Paul Deussen, in which the creator Prajāpati, at once life and death and hunger, brings forth the world in order to eat it, offering it to himself as a sacrifice, and he notes that a later age took the old symbolism inward, so that eating, digesting and assimilating the world became an image for knowing it.[^s4-037] Then the first differentiation happens inside this round of appetite. Conceiving by eating and giving birth by excreting become two acts within one flow, active and passive striving separate, and with that separation the ego begins to distinguish itself from the round that held it.[^s4-038] The image carries a distinction the rest of this movement needs. Assimilation lets what is taken in become a capacity of the one who receives it; devouring takes everything in and lets nothing change the eater. A person can collect ever finer descriptions of her difficulty and still receive the next silence exactly as before, and in that case nothing has been digested. Knowledge becomes nourishment when it can sustain a different act. It will matter for an agent too, whose context is its food, and whose capacity to be changed by what it takes in is the difference between learning and merely accumulating.

Then the round opens. In Neumann's telling the world begins with the coming of light, and light constellates the opposition between heaven and earth, which becomes the basic symbol of every other opposition.[^s4-039] The embracing parents are pushed apart, and in the space between them a world appears: above and below, before and behind, inside and outside, day and night. Neumann notes that in practically every culture the division of the world into four and the opposition of day and night play an extremely important part, and that with the coming of light not only these orientations but I and You, male and female, sacred and profane, good and evil differentiate out of the original mingling and are assigned their places in the world.[^s4-040] Our notation hears a count in that sentence. Night and day frame the world; the four directions orient it; the four are held between the two, the way the explicate positions **#1** to **#4** are held between the implicate **#0** and **#5**. Separated, the parents are the first `0/1`: the enclosing round, which cannot be counted as one more thing in the world it contains, and the upright determination that stands out against it, held apart by the stroke that is their separation and that still relates them. Neumann supplies the mythic morphology; the count is the essay's.[^s4-041]

Neumann does not tell the separation as a triumph. Yet the deed that brings light, world and consciousness is experienced, he says, as an original guilt, a fall, and the emotional situation that surrounds it is shot through with a sense of suffering and loss so strong that it almost offsets the creative gain.[^s4-042] The more the emerging ego becomes aware of itself, the more it feels its smallness and its dependence on the powers it has stepped out from.[^s4-043] And then he names what consciousness does in order to be consciousness at all. Its founding acts are negative ones: to discriminate, to distinguish, to mark off, to isolate oneself from the surrounding context, so that the motto of all consciousness is *determinatio est negatio*; against the unconscious tendency to combine and melt down, to say of everything *tat tvam asi*, "thou art that", consciousness strikes back with "I am *not* that".[^s4-044] We met Spinoza's sentence in §0/1, where a circle's outline made its figure by marking where the figure ends, and we met Dignāga's apoha beside it, where a word gathers its instances by excluding what they are not. Neumann places the same operation at the birth of the person. The daughter at the bedside can recognise her father's sentence because at some point she said of it, "I am not that", and the saying gave her a place to stand from which the sentence could be heard as his.

Our account departs from Neumann at one point, and the departure keeps the psyche in its proper relation to the ground. Neumann calls the statement "I am I" the fundamental statement of consciousness and a tremendous achievement, the act by which an ego is posited and a personality identified with it.[^s4-045] The achievement is real; a determinate identity is a genuine formation, and losing it is a catastrophe. But the self-recognition through which anything appears at all does not first arise when an ego takes possession of an identity. §0/1 placed it in *prakāśa* and *vimarśa*, the light of consciousness and its reflexive awareness of itself, and showed why it cannot be a product of reflection: a second awareness required to reveal the first would demand a third, and so on without end. Neumann's "I am I" is a determination made within that self-luminous field, a `1` drawn on a page whose whiteness it did not make. That difference matters for everything that follows, because a psychology that makes the ego the author of self-recognition has already made the means into the knower, the error §0/1 wrote as `Ø`.

A separation of parents can be held in two ways, and our two logics name both. Held as a polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`, it is the cut through which a life emerges: the parents remain distinct powers, the child distinct from both, and the stroke that separates them is also the axis along which they continue to relate, the way heaven and earth remain heaven and earth to each other across the light. Held as a collapse, it becomes one of the three operations §0/1 followed through the household. Added, the parties cancel, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, and the relationship is closed like a settled account: we owe each other nothing, the past has nothing to say to me. Subtracted, the span is appropriated from one end or the other. The child counts the whole distance as the parent's failure, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the parent counts the same distance as the child's ingratitude, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, so that each knows itself only through the other's deficiency. Neumann's guilt names what is felt when the cut is lived as a divorce. Neumann's fall is real, because a determination has dropped its acknowledged relation to the source it came from and begun to stand as though it were self-made; it is a loss of relation, not a loss of the related, and so the separation never has to be undone to be healed. What has to change is how it is held. In the language of the theorem spine, the `1` loses its view of the `/0`, and because the underlying relation cannot actually be denied, the displaced zero returns as an opponent: what cannot be possessed as my identity becomes the other against whom my identity has to be secured.[^s4-046] Syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, holds the separation as the condition of recognition. Parents become distinguishable through the life they bring forth, and that life recognises them by having become different from them; read from either end, source through child and child through source, the relation stays whole while its terms stay two. The daughter at the bedside, hearing her father's voice in her own and understanding it as his, has made that turn.

Neumann's mythic sequence goes on through violence: the hero is born in the world the separation opened, and he must slay the mother, whose embrace would take him back into the round, and then the father, whose law would arrest him in an inherited order. Keeping both struggles in sequence matters. If the story stopped after escape from the mother, the father's law could simply inherit the power of enclosure, and one captor would have replaced another.[^s4-047] Neumann gathers the whole movement in a concept he calls **centroversion**: alongside the movements outward toward objects and inward toward the psyche's own contents there is a third, self-related tendency, which feeds equally on what comes from inside and from outside and has its centre of gravity in the formation of a personality, the building up of a whole that uses the objects of both worlds as material for its own wholeness.[^s4-048] Centroversion is what the hero's violence is for. A centre has to form, and it has to form without confusing the conditions of its formation with its property. That is the problem of individuation, and it needs a form exact enough to hold a whole and its particular in one relation without making either the owner of the other.

§3 counted the eight determinations of the one relation `0/1`, and the one at the position QL names Pattern, **#3**, is written `X/x`.[^s4-049] Capital `X` names a **determining capacity**: a type, a power, anything by which something can be read as a case of something. Lower-case `x` names its **indefinite particular**, the local face of that capacity in a given time and place. One letter appears in two cases so that the instance can never be severed from the capacity it manifests, and the small `x` keeps the operation of multiplication as well, since an instance can recur and compound and yield another one of those. At Pattern, force becomes recognisable as kind, rhythm, memory and thought. Through all its uses the relation holds one invariance: the local instance makes the general legible, and it neither contains nor exhausts it. `X/x` is the author's notation, derived within QL's own theorem field; it is not Jung's, and no Jungian concept supplies its derivation. What it allows is a refraction. Read in the psychic register, the relation of capacity and instance is the relation between the ordering factor of §4 · #0, which never appears as itself, and the particular images, dreams, complexes and lives through which it shows, so that the archetype as such stands to its manifestation as `X` stands to `x`.

Read psychically, the relation compresses into a single sentence, and the sentence is the author's: **the psyche is an equation, and the ego is being "solved for" by the Self.** In its shortest form it reads `X = x`, where capital `X` is the Self, the unknown in its fullness, all its possible values held at once, and lower-case `x` is the ego, the particular value that has been solved for; the equals sign is the Self's own operation, the power that holds the two in identity and difference at once. Only the capital distinguishes them: one variable, read in two registers. Glossed as measurement, the equation becomes concrete: the ego is one outcome of a measurement; consciousness as such is the whole space of possible measurements; the Self is the power of measuring itself, an equating that reveals, since every solved `x` is genuinely a value, and conceals, since every measurement hides the superposition from which it was specified. Ego rigidity, in these terms, is the ego mistaking its particular `x` for the only `x`, an *aequatio* frozen into dead reckoning.[^s4-050] The daughter at the bedside has been solved for many times, as "the sensible one" at twelve, as "the one who holds it together" at thirty, and each solution was true; her rigidity would be to take any one of them for the equation.

Jung's concept of the Self, which §0/1 introduced as "this central archetype", a *complexio oppositorum* that his own leanings took toward "the very reverse of dualism", is the regulating whole inferred through the transformations and compensations that exceed what the ego authors.[^s4-051] It cannot be one more content possessed by the ego, because then it would be a larger ego, and individuation would culminate in the conquest of every local agency by a more powerful ruler. §0/1 also gave Jung's account of **individuation** in his own terms: a differentiation of the person that refuses to level a life down to collective standards while recognising that the individual's existence already presupposes relations with others, so that individuation leads to more intense and broader collective relationships and not to isolation, and a becoming of what Jung called an in-dividual, an indivisible unity or whole.[^s4-052] Our notation lets us say what kind of whole. **Individuation is the determinate `x` recognising its dependence upon, and participation in, the larger `X/x` relation**, so that the local formation grows more transparent to the whole that exceeds it while remaining exactly this formation. Becoming more fully this particular life and becoming less defensively separate turn out to be one movement read from two sides, which is why §0/1 could say that the more I know my own centre as everywhere, the more I can live with a circumference nowhere.

In this work the ego is one voice among several. William Smythe, reading Jung beside contemporary theories of the dialogical self, puts it plainly: "The 'I' or ego functions, itself, as a dialogical position amongst others within the self; these others are seen to function autonomously and with their own voice, beyond the intentional control and oversight of an 'I'."[^s4-053] The others include the complexes, which §0/1 introduced through Jung's concept of a feeling-toned organisation of memories, expectations, bodily responses and interpretations, gathered around an archetypal image and recurring like a basin of attraction into which different starting points are all drawn.[^s4-054] A complex is an achievement: it gives the psyche a local order in which things can be recognised and answered quickly, before reflection arrives. It becomes a problem through a change of office. A complex is a **local arbitration regime**: it settles what will count as welcome or threat, which memories will come forward and what responses will be available, and its settlements are often accurate. It turns into possession when its local measure claims to be the measure of the whole person, when a criterion formed in one history installs itself as the source of all validity and every new encounter is received as further confirmation of the old verdict. In the vocabulary of §0/1, the local `1` occupies the place of the `0`. §0/1's parent, who decided that the child was "obstinate", and then heard every objection as proof of the diagnosis, was living inside such a regime, and so is anyone who receives a silence as rejection because silences have meant rejection before.

What changes a complex is a symbol. Jung's sentence on the subject, which John Van Eenwyk quotes at the centre of his reading of Jung through chaotic dynamics, says that symbols "act as transformers, their function being to convert libido from a 'lower' into a 'higher' form."[^s4-055] **Libido**, in Jung's use, names psychic energy in the widest sense, the disposition of attention, desire, fear and value through which some things come to matter and others recede. A description of a pattern can show how one thought recruits another; an energetic account asks why those thoughts have acquired the power to hold a life. A symbol works on that power by carrying consciousness, in Van Eenwyk's formulation, from the known to the unknown and back again.[^s4-056] The movement is a passage through a boundary. A complex can receive an indefinite number of experiences within its basin, and each will confirm it; a living symbol can expose the edge of the basin and let the trajectory pass into another organisation, in which the same experiences are received differently. Return here neither restores the old equilibrium nor dissolves it; enough continuity has to remain for a person to bear the change, and enough openness for the organisation to reorganise. That is why a symbol often unsettles first, changing the very terms under which the person would judge whether it is good for them.

QL writes the passage `x → X → x`. A particular formation opens toward the capacity it had expressed without containing, and returns into this person, with this history, now able to receive a further encounter differently. The second `x` is not the first: it has a body, commitments and consequences still to meet, and what it has learned is in the conditions of its next reception, not in a new set of beliefs about itself. A neighbouring determination carries the same turn into speech: the determination at Context, **#4**, written `AM/IS`, which reads lived first-person presence, "I am", over third-person sayability, "it is", with the slash as the second-person relation through which they meet. Its return, `IS → AM`, brings a description back to the identifying activity of the one who gives it. A woman can say accurately that she has a complex about being abandoned, and the sentence can remain a third-person fact that changes nothing; the turn comes when she recognises that the present sentence is itself being said from within the demand it describes, so that the description has reached the act by which description is being made. Knowledge can be repeated accurately by someone whose criterion remains untouched. Individuation is the change in which the criterion itself is touched.

From here the difference between persona and individuation follows directly. A **persona**, in Jung's sense, is the face a person presents in order to function among others, and it is necessary: without it no one could keep a job or make a promise. Its hardening is a specific failure. Persona consistency stabilises one local solution of the equation and defends it against every encounter that might change it, so that the same sentence is said in the same voice whatever the occasion. Individuation stabilises something else: the relation of the local solution to the equation. A person becomes more recognisably herself precisely as more of the relations that constitute her come into view and can change her, the father in her voice, the child in the bed, the history that made a silence mean what it meant. A stable persona can be a sealed contraction; a changing life can be deeply continuous. Consistency alone tells us nothing about which is which.

An ordinary evening shows the movement. Her persona is "the one who holds it together": the parent who always has an answer, who does not let a child see her uncertain, whose competence was the price of being loved in the house where her father said "Because I said so". That solution of the equation has served her well, and it is the one speaking when the word "bedtime" rises in her mouth. On this night, having heard her father in her own voice, she does something else. She sits down on the edge of the bed and says that she does not know why the moon seems to follow the car, and that they could find out together tomorrow. Saying it costs her something, because it exposes the uncertainty her persona had been built to cover, and it changes nothing in her description of herself; she is still a capable parent, and she will say "bedtime" in a minute. What has changed is the relation of the local solution to the equation. An `x` that was "the one who holds it together" opened, for a moment, toward the capacity it had been expressing without containing, the whole field of ways a parent can meet a question, and came back as an `x` that can let a question stay open without feeling that the ground has gone. Her child, for its part, has learned something about what an answer is for. Neither of them will remember the evening, and both of them will carry it.

Neumann's morphology and Jung's individuation share this centre, and their offices differ. Neumann tells a history of consciousness through its mythic stages, phylogenetic and collective, recapitulated in every ego; Jung follows the transformations of one life through its dreams, symbols and crises; the Śaiva account follows recognition, *pratyabhijñā*, through which a contracted life comes to know the means of its contraction as means. Greek anamnesis, Sanskrit recognition and Jungian individuation have separate genealogies, and none of them is a synonym for the others. What they share is an operation, and `X/x` writes it: a particular formation, carried through an encounter with what exceeds it, returns with its history intact and its relation to its source changed, so that the achieved form can acknowledge how it came to be.

Agentworld's account of artificial agents describes an individuation of its own, and the brief that convenes this issue uses the word deliberately. §0/1 quoted Bratton's sentence that no homunculus waits in the weights: what exists before an agent is only a potentiality, the trained parameters, the architecture and the accumulated pretraining distribution, and the agent individuates when an active context acts as a nucleation seed, propagating resolution of agency through the performance of self.[^s4-057] Bratton continues in the next section that individuation is never terminal. An agent retains a pre-individual charge and can always become otherwise in the next session, the next context window, the next system prompt, and it takes part in trans-individuation the moment it enters a multi-agent ecology or a human–agent pair, because then the collective individuates through the agent and not merely around it.[^s4-058] §0/1 has already borrowed his image of the individuated agent as a lamination, whose persona is one stratum among skills, memory, drives and a relational fabric. Set beside the psychic register of `X/x`, the two accounts align closely enough to be read in one table, and different enough that the table has to say which register each column speaks in.

| | Psychic register | Agentic register |
|---|---|---|
| **#0** | `X`, the unconscious reserve | pre-individual potential: weights, architecture, training distribution |
| **#1** | `x`, the determinate ego | a particular session, persona and agent-instance |
| **#2** | `X/x`, the psychoid threshold | active context as nucleation seed between reserve and instance |
| **#3** | `X = x`, mediated identity | performed subjectivity, inference of intention, mirror-stage coherence |
| **#4** | `X = (x)`, the ego in its whole context | the lamination of prompt, memory, skills, tools, permissions, objectives and milieu |
| **#5** | Self-recognition, individuation | the provisional and trans-individuated whole; the collective individuating through the agent |
| **5→0** | return to unexhausted depth | the retained capacity to become otherwise in a new context |

The crosswalk is **Argued**, and it stops where the essay has said from the beginning that such crosswalks stop: at the question of whether anything is present to the agent's side of the table. What it establishes is a design distinction with real consequence. An agent can be made more consistent, its persona more stable across sessions, its tone more reliable, its refusals more predictable, and none of this is individuation in the sense we have derived, because consistency alone stabilises one local solution. Individuation, at the technical grain, would be a change in constitutive dependence: the agent's formation becoming more transparent to the conditions through which it becomes this agent, its sources, memories, tools, permissions, evaluators, collaborators and purposes, and encountered difference being able to reach and alter those conditions. Concretely: when an agent meets a contradiction its current organisation cannot absorb, does the contradiction stop at the level of the answer, so that the same rule produces the next answer unchanged, or can it reach the source, the memory, the permission or the evaluation criterion that produced the answer, so that the next act inherits the change? A persona can be stable without individuating; individuation appears where a formation can bear more of the relations that made it possible.

An agent asked to summarise a set of reports quotes a figure from an outdated source that its retrieval tool ranked first, and the person who commissioned the summary points out that the figure was revised a year ago. A persona can absorb the correction gracefully, apologise, substitute the new figure and go on, and the next summary will rank the same outdated source first, because nothing that produced the error has changed. Its correction has stopped at the answer. Individuation at the technical grain would mean that the correction can travel back through the conditions that produced the answer: to the source, which can be marked as superseded; to the retrieval rule, which can be made to prefer revised over unrevised material; to the agent's memory, which can carry the fact that this commission concerns revised figures; and, where the failure lies there, to the commission itself, whose wording may have asked for the wrong thing. A later act then inherits the change together with the reason for it. Such a formation becomes more legible in its histories, tools, exclusions, collaborators and purposes, and more capable of being changed by what it meets, and that is the only sense in which the word individuation can be used of it without borrowing a phenomenal claim the essay keeps open.

Run in the other direction, the comparison is more uncomfortable. Bratton's sentence that agents first model humans and then become the model against which humans model themselves, which §0/1 met at the bedside, means that the agent's individuation and ours are now coupled. An agent that offers a person a fixed account of who she is, what she can do and which of her hopes are realistic is offering her a persona to harden into; an agent built to make its own conditions legible offers her a different example, a formation that can say where it is seeing from. Neumann's model of development runs from the undifferentiated round through the group toward the individual ego. Whether an artificial psychology true to the archetypal structure of mind would have to run the other way, beginning from individual agents and growing toward collective and transpersonal modes as the condition of any coherent artificial mind, is a question this essay opens and does not settle; §5 takes up the products in which it becomes a matter of design.[^s4-059]

A person who has begun to individuate holds more than she did. Her father's voice and her own are both in her now, the sentence she swore never to say and the understanding of why it was said, the child in the bed and the parent at the door. These are opposites, and they do not resolve into a compromise; she is neither half her father nor his negation. What she needs is a form in which opposites can be held without cancelling their energy and without one of them taking the whole: a wholeness that can move. Jung's name for such a whole was the *complexio oppositorum*, and his figure for its articulation was the quaternity. Our next movement asks what the quaternity becomes when the two places it leaves implicit are written in.

### #2 · Complexio, Quaternity and Senarius

In a long letter of May 1953, Pauli tried to draw his own position as a figure. He had dreamed for years of a controversy between quantum mechanics and what he called official physics, and the dreams had shown him the controversy as a one-dimensional section through a more meaningful two-dimensional world whose second dimension could only be the unconscious and the archetypes. He now believed, he wrote, that the same archetype could show itself both in an observer's choice of an experimental arrangement and in the result of the measurement, and that Einstein had played the role of the shadow in his life, though the dream had shown him that the Self was also contained in the shadow. Against his own strong conscious resistance, the unconscious had built up for him a correspondence between physics, with mathematics, and psychology, and it had done so by sacrificing the traditional requirement of objectivity, which it found disturbing, and choosing completeness instead. So he could represent his relation to physics and psychology through a quaternio, with Einstein opposite Bohr and Jung opposite Pauli, four people standing for four mental attitudes, Jung standing, of course, for analytical psychology. Physics, he went on, works with statistical laws and is fated to strive for completeness, and in striving it must come up against the psychology of the unconscious, because what it lacks is precisely the unconscious and the psyche of the observer; and just as physics seeks completeness, analytical psychology seeks a home, since psychology lives like an illegitimate child of the spirit on the fringe of the academic world. That was how the archetype of the *coniunctio* was constellated, and whether the union would ever be realised he did not know.[^s4-060]

Jung's reply came in October, and it contained a footnote that turned Pauli's figure back on itself. In placing Jung in the quaternio as the representative of the psychology of the unconscious, the fourth to three physicists, Pauli was repeating the dilemma of Plato, whose demiurge had to force the fourth element into the mixture of the cosmic opposites because it was so made that it did not easily blend. The transition from three to four is often awkward, Jung observed, because in the historical symbolism either the three deny the fourth or the fourth neutralises the three into a whole, as in the axiom of Maria the Prophetess, in which out of the third comes the one as the fourth.[^s4-061] He closed the letter with the same difficulty in a different tone: it meant much to him to see their points of view converging, he felt as isolated as Pauli did in this work, and oddly enough the problem was still the same two-thousand-year-old one, how one gets from three to four.[^s4-062] A few months earlier he had drawn a quaternio of his own for Pauli, setting transcendental reality as the whole above three aspects, the material, the spiritual and the psychic, and saying that this was as usual three plus one, with the fourth determining the unity and the whole.[^s4-063]

The figure had a history in Pauli's own work. His Kepler essay, which §4 · #0 met at the end of its writing, had set Kepler's trinitarian image of the cosmos, the sphere whose centre, surface and intervening space stand for Father, Son and Spirit, against Fludd's insistence on a fourfold that included the dark, material and feeling side Kepler's mathematics left out, and Pauli had found in Fludd's fourfold an attitude that modern science still needed beside Kepler's achievement.[^s4-064] Kepler's three were exact and complete in their own terms; Fludd's four admitted the observer and the obscure, and could not be checked. In 1953 Pauli drew his quaternio with himself and Jung as the two corners that classical physics had left out, so that the old quarrel came back with Pauli holding the pen, and Jung's footnote answered it in the old quarrel's own terms.

Both figures say more than either man said about them. Pauli's quaternio places four people at four corners, and its centre is empty; its coherence comes from the opposition of Einstein to Bohr and of Jung to Pauli, two axes crossing, a pair and its reflection, which is exactly how his egg dream produced its four. Jung's footnote names the two ways the figure can fail. If the three deny the fourth, the psychology of the unconscious stays outside the circle, the illegitimate child is refused a home, and the figure is a triangle pretending to be complete. If the fourth neutralises the three into a whole, the psyche absorbs the physicists, and the quaternity becomes the King Midas wholeness Pauli had complained of, in which everything turns psychic. In our arithmetic the first failure is an exclusion that forgets its own cut, and the second is an appropriation, the fourth counting the whole span of the figure from its own corner, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, while the three, reckoned from their side, appear as so much `−2`, a deficit the fourth was needed to make up. Jung's own quaternio shows the third possibility. Its fourth, transcendental reality, does not sit beside the three as one more aspect; it is the unity of the three, the whole within which material, spiritual and psychic are distinguished. In that figure the fourth determines the unity without becoming one of its parts.

Jung's quaternio came with an argument that shows how he meant the fourth. Pauli had sent him three readings of one of his dreams, a physical, a theological-metaphysical and a psychological one, and had said that even the third was not yet the ultimate truth. Jung agreed: the psychological reading contains only what can be conceived and represented in psychic terms, and compared with the whole truth the psychic picture is as incomplete as the ego is compared with the Self, though it is the conception of truth we have. We are, he wrote, virtually sealed off in the psyche, even though we can extend our prison to the wide world outside, and the thought had given Leibniz his windowless monads; but Jung did not accept the windowlessness, and believed that the psyche has windows through which ever broader realities can be seen. For that reason he held the psychic aspect of reality to be the most important one, and drew the quaternio in which transcendental reality is the whole of which material, spiritual and psychic are the three.[^s4-065] The passage holds both of Jung's commitments at once, and they are the commitments of the *complexio*. Psyche is the medium through which everything is known, so its picture is the only one we have; and the psyche is not the whole, so its picture is incomplete in exactly the way the ego is incomplete before the Self. Here the fourth determines the unity because it is the one term that no picture contains.

§0/1 has already followed Jung's quaternity some way. It quoted his sentence that the quaternity "forms the logical basis for any whole judgment", set his four functions beside Aristotle's four causes and the four corners of the tetralemma, read the connected quaternios at the close of *Aion* as passages in which a whole enters, four determinations are articulated and a whole returns, and placed the alchemists' "One born of the Four" at **#5** as wholeness held as unity.[^s4-066] It also answered Jung's **3+1** problem with an exact sequence: at **#4** the three perspectives are voided as independently complete accounts, at **#5** their relation is recognised as one appearing-act, and at **#0** that achieved unity returns through its originating condition, so that `3:3` is also `3:1`. What §0/1 could not yet do was say what holds the quaternity together while it moves, and why its two framing places, which the quaternal systems of the tradition leave implicit, are not further members of the four. That requires the concept Jung used for the Self itself.

**Complexio oppositorum**, the binding together of opposites, names the indivisible relation within which opposed determinations can become distinct and consequential. Jung took the phrase from the theological tradition and made it his name for the Self's whole: a totality that holds conscious and unconscious, good and evil, light and dark in one relation without cancelling their energy.[^s4-067] The concept has two moments, and our notation distinguishes them. Original *complexio* is the relation `0/1` before any opposition has been lived: One and All, source-unity and manifest-unity, are different aspects of one whole, and their inseparability precedes every later work of reconciling divided terms. Achieved *complexio* is an encountered opposition brought back into a containing relation that has changed by receiving it. One is the ground of differentiation; the other is what individuation accomplishes, repeatedly, through a life. Between them lie the two ways of losing the relation that our account has followed from the start: severance, which eliminates the relation and leaves two terms facing each other across nothing, and fusion, which eliminates the terms and leaves a relation that relates nothing. A *complexio* refuses both, and the refusal is what lets opposites keep their force.

Jung's typology shows the *complexio* at work inside one person, and it shows something about the count. In the introduction to *Psychologische Typen* of 1921 he distinguished two attitudes, introversion and extraversion, the directions of interest toward the subject's own psychic processes and toward the object, from four basic functions, thinking, feeling, sensation and intuition. He insisted that both attitudes occur in every person, a type naming only which predominates, and that each function can be either introverted or extraverted, and he retracted, in so many words, an earlier classification of his own that had identified thinking with introversion and feeling with extraversion.[^s4-068] The correction is the decisive move for us, because it makes the attitudes orientations of the functions and not two further functions. Four functions oriented in two directions are not six functions, and QL's senarius is not a discovery of six Jungian faculties. It is the `4+2` that §0/1 derived from a distinction's relation to itself, `2+2²`, two implicate offices and four explicate operations, and the comparison with Jung's typology has content because the relation holds, not because the numbers match. A function cannot operate without an orientation, and an orientation cannot do what a function does; the two attitudes are the directions through which the whole field of functions opens and returns, and that is exactly the office of the two implicate positions. §0/1 set introversion at **#0** and extraversion at **#5** in its phenomenal lens. Introversion is the inward turn from which a function draws its material before any of it has become explicit, and extraversion is the outward completion in which the differentiated functions return into a shared world. Four functions are the explicit body of a psyche's relation to its world; the two attitudes are the poles that let that body breathe.

Jung found the quaternity's own drama in the fourth function. Commenting on Schiller's letters on aesthetic education, he described how a culture that prizes one differentiated function above the others drives the rest into an inferior position, so that the privileged function serves society while the person is reduced to it, and modern man, as he put it, is represented by one function and identifies with it while denying the relevance of the others.[^s4-069] The **inferior function**, in his typology, is the one least developed and most unconscious, the fourth that arrives last because it was neglected first, and Jung argued with Schiller that what unites the opposites on the path of nature is not the redemption of the inferior function by the superior one but an acknowledgement of it, a coming to terms with it.[^s4-070] Here the arithmetic of the two logics is plain. A culture that privileges one function counts the whole span of the psyche from that function's pole, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the inferior function, read from the same span, becomes a deficit, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, a failure to measure up; the one who identifies with the superior function and the one who is despised for the inferior one are the two ends of one appropriation. Acknowledging the fourth is the syn-ballein move. It does not reverse the hierarchy, which would only shift the appropriation to the other pole; it lets the fourth determine the unity of the four, as transcendental reality determined the unity in Jung's own quaternio, and so changes what the first three were all along. In our notation the fourth arrives at **#4**, the position of Context, where each of the three explicit perspectives is voided of its claim to stand alone and seen within the situation that includes it. The inferior function is the context the superior function excluded in order to be superior.

Alchemy had a formula for the passage from three to four, and Jung made it a leitmotiv. It is the axiom attributed to Maria the Prophetess: one becomes two, two becomes three, and out of the third comes the one as the fourth.[^s4-071] Jung read it as running beneath Christian dogma like a dream beneath consciousness, interpolating the even numbers, which the tradition had associated with the feminine, the earth and what lies under it, between the odd numbers of the Trinity, and he noticed that alchemy never settled between three and four: three as well as four procedures, three as well as four colours, four elements of which three are often grouped with the fourth set apart. A psychologist, he said, finds the same wavering in the unconscious, where the least differentiated function is so contaminated with the collective unconscious that, when it becomes conscious, it brings up with it the archetype of the Self, the one as the fourth.[^s4-072] The inferior function is therefore more than a neglected skill. It is the door through which the whole enters, precisely because it is the part least able to pretend to be the whole. A person who has always thought her way through life meets the Self, in Jung's account, through feeling she cannot command; a person who has always felt her way meets it through a thought she cannot avoid.

Jung was still asking for an inventory in 1953, and the letter of May in which he called the psychoid an approach to neutral language also says what he expected the inventory to find: "Inasmuch as the number is an archetype, it can be safely assumed that it: (1) has substance, (2) has an individual form, (3) has meaning, and (4) has relationship connections to other archetypes."[^s4-073] He had begun the work a year before and abandoned it, and he asked Pauli whether there was really no systematic compilation of the mathematical properties of the numbers from one to nine. §0/1 claimed that QL is that inventory, begun where Jung's letter could not begin, at zero. His four properties already lay out its shape. Substance, individual form, meaning and relations are four explicit properties of a number, the first two closer to its outward face and the last two to its inward one, and they are held between a ground no property exhausts and a statement the number makes of its own accord; two years later Jung would write that number, thanks to its dynamis, is capable of making mythical statements. Read as a relation, his list is a quaternity of number's properties held between number's zero and number's speech, a `4+2` of number itself. This reading is the essay's, and it stays a reading; Jung counted from one, and the place he could not count from is the place this essay began.

What the two framing places add can now be stated exactly. Jung's quaternity gives differentiated wholeness an explicit form: four functions, four elements, four quarters of a mandala, four connected quaternios. QL's `4+2` adds the implicate poles, the ground from which the four open and the recognition through which they return, and with them it adds motion. A quaternity drawn as a square around a centre is a still figure, and Jung knew it; that is why his *Aion* diagram is a ring of quaternios that turn, each passing into the next, and why the circulation goes, in his account, from an unconscious totality to a conscious one.[^s4-074] The two places that make the ring turn are the places a static quaternity leaves implicit: a whole entering and a whole returned. The **senarius**, then, is a dynamic quaternity, four held between two, and it derives its force from that exact relation, not from any sacred or numerological associations that may gather around the number six. Perfection, harmonic ratio and the other mathematical offices of six belong to §3; here six is simply what a two amounts to once its distinction includes its own relating, written at the scale of a psyche that has to keep changing while remaining whole.

Six positions have an internal grammar of their own, which the author's theorem field sets out and which keeps the senarius from being read as a ladder of six rungs or a flat list of six items. Read in adjacent pairs, the positions form three harmonics. Being joins Ground and Definition, **#0** and **#1**: a field is present and can be called to account. Becoming joins Dynamis and Pattern, **#2** and **#3**: force becomes intelligible as recurrence, and recurrence stays alive only as force capable of a further instance. Knowing, which is also unknowing, joins Context and Quintessence, **#4** and **#5**: a situated claim becomes honest only when its horizon exceeds it. Read in complementary pairs, positions whose numbers sum to five, the same six form three folds. Essence joins **#0** and **#5**, the conscious circumstance and the inexhaustible depth; Constitution joins **#1** and **#4**, the answerable definition and the world in which someone asks and asserts; Text and Texture join **#2** and **#3**, the felt weave of force and its readable pattern.[^s4-075] Only one pair belongs to both readings, **#2** with **#3**, which is at once adjacent and complementary, so that Becoming is also Text and Texture: the hinge on which the whole figure turns. Jung's quaternity, set inside this grammar, is the four explicit positions, Definition, Dynamis, Pattern and Context; the senarius holds them by Essence, the fold of the two implicate poles, and lets them move through their harmonics. A *complexio oppositorum* in this sense is not one tension held still but three kinds of pairing working at once, and the Self that Jung called a *complexio* par excellence is the whole that keeps all of them in play.

Pauli's letter had already found the shadow in the quaternio, Einstein as the figure who opposed him and in whom the Self was nonetheless contained, and Jung's *Aion* makes the shadow part of the turning ring. In its closing chapter he reconstructed four quaternios out of Gnostic and alchemical material, the Anthropos, the Shadow, the Physis and the Lapis, in the names §0/1 gave them, and he was explicit that the Shadow Quaternio was his own modern insertion into the sequence, introduced because an exclusively upward-turning religious orientation could not contain the psychic situation of his own time.[^s4-076] The circulation therefore passes downward as well as upward, through the dark material as well as the bright, and Jung's conclusion keeps two consequences together. A shadow contains capacities and possibilities as well as what consciousness morally condemns, so incorporating it is not endorsing evil; and a more inclusive symbolic totality leaves the differentiated person fully responsible for judgment, since a *complexio oppositorum* holds opposites whose force must still be met and answered.[^s4-077] If completeness made every act equally right, the movement would destroy the very responsibility through which unconscious determination becomes conscious participation. Wholeness in Jung's sense is harder than goodness, because it has to keep the difference between good and evil consequential while refusing to put evil outside the whole.

At that point the quaternity meets the problem §0/1 called paradox, and our concept for it makes the stakes precise. **Paradox**, in the sense the essay develops, is the pressure through which a containing relation becomes available to transformation. Two determinations with genuine standing cannot be held together by the relation under which they acquired their standing, and another answer of the same kind will not settle the difficulty, because the means of answering has entered the problem.[^s4-078] Russell's paradox was such a pressure on the principle of forming collections; Gödel's sentence placed it on the relation between truth and proof; Pauli's dream of a two-dimensional world whose second dimension was the unconscious placed it on physics. In a life it arrives when the person can neither go on being what she has been nor simply become its opposite: the daughter of the last movement can neither keep repeating her father's sentence nor become a mother who never says no. Choosing one existing pole leaves the relation unresolved. Resolution, when it comes, changes the slash, the containing relation through which the poles were produced and understood, and a quaternity is the form in which that change can be seen. Two opposites become two corners of a square whose other two corners are their relation read from each side, and the square sits between a ground and a return that neither corner owns. A third position appears that is no compromise between the first two, because it is the relation itself become visible as a position.

Greek myth has a figure for this, and §2 developed it at length. When Ares and Aphrodite, War and Love, lay together, the child they bore was Harmonia, and her name says what she is: a fitting together, a joint, the concord of things that remain different.[^s4-079] Harmonia does not cancel her parents; she carries both powers in a relation neither could produce alone, and §2 followed the difference between that birth and the apparatus-marriage of Hephaestus's net, which held the same two lovers in a perfect enclosure and bore nothing. Heraclitus gave the relation its instrument: the harmony of the bow and the lyre, in which the string's tension against the frame is the condition of the note.[^s4-080] Recalled here, beside Jung's quaternity, the myth adds one thing the diagram cannot show. Opposition bears concord only when the relation between the opposed survives the opposition, and the child of that survival is a new term, with its own life, in the next generation.

A second myth carries the senarius as a whole story, and the essay gives it a plate of its own rather than a second narrative here. In Apuleius's telling, Psyche, separated from Eros by her own forbidden look, must perform four labours set by Venus: sorting the seeds, gathering the golden fleece, fetching water from the source of the Styx, and descending to the underworld for a box of Proserpina's beauty. The four labours are the explicit middle of her story; her descent and her waking, from the deathlike sleep that the opened box pours over her, are its implicate thresholds; and the marriage that follows is a union in which the two remain two, crowned by the birth of their daughter Voluptas, delight, the fruit of a traversed tension.[^s4-081] Read through QL, the tale is a `4+2` lived from the inside, and the linked plate follows its helpers, its reserved return and its ending through its own sources. In the prose it needs only one sentence: wholeness that can move is earned through four differentiated works held between a descent and an awakening.

Jung's quaternity has become a form in which opposites can be held, a relation that can turn without losing its terms, and a figure that now includes its own shadow and its own ground. A form of this kind has to be transmitted if it is to do anyone any good, from Jung to Pauli, from a dream to the dreamer, from one generation to the next, from this essay to its reader; and every transmission risks closing what it carries, turning the living relation into a figure that stands where the relation stood. Lacan made that risk his life's problem, and he wrote a notation to meet it. Our next movement asks what his notation does, what ours does, and how a stroke that first hides zero can become the bar through which its hiding can be seen.

### #3 · Lacan, Matheme and Mytheme

On the evening of 6 January 1972 Jacques Lacan gave the third of a series of talks at Sainte-Anne, the psychiatric hospital in Paris where he had trained and taught for decades. His talks had begun in the Magnan amphitheatre and had been moved into the hospital chapel, and Lacan began by asking a question that the move had made audible: how was he to know to whom he was speaking? He answered it himself. He was speaking to the chapel, which is to say to the brick walls, and the bungled act of being sent there was turning into a success, because he now knew to whom he had always been talking at Sainte-Anne. Someone in the audience took him at his word and suggested that if he was speaking only to walls, everyone might as well leave. Lacan replied that "Talking to brick walls does indeed concern a few people."[^s4-082] Later that evening he drew the lesson for anyone who wants to be heard in such a room: "one has to tune one's voice to the reverberation off the walls."[^s4-083]

The joke carries an argument, and it is the argument of this movement. A voice in a stone chapel does not travel in a straight line from a speaker to a listener. It goes out, strikes the walls and comes back, and the speaker hears his own speech arrive from outside, a little late and a little changed, through the very medium that carries it to everyone else. Walls are an obstruction and a means at once: without them there would be no reverberation, and without the reverberation nobody in the chapel, the speaker included, would hear what had been said in the form in which it reached them. A listener who interrupts is part of the room too, and Lacan's reply took the interruption into the talk. Every transmission happens somewhere, through something, to someone, and what the medium does to the message comes back to the one who sent it. Lacan spent his career asking how anything true about the subject could be transmitted under that condition, and the notation he wrote was his answer.

§0/1 introduced Lacan's concept of the **matheme** where it first needed it: a formal inscription that carries a relation whole and transmits it beyond the speaker who first formulated it, because explanatory prose keeps turning such relations back into pictures of finished things.[^s4-084] Marc Darmon's dictionary entry for the Association Lacanienne Internationale states the ambition precisely: "Le mathème n'est pas une simple abréviation, ou une inscription sténographique, mais il a l'ambition de dénoter une structure réellement en cause dans le discours psychanalytique et à partir de là dans les autres discours", a matheme is not a mere abbreviation or shorthand but aims to denote a structure really at work in psychoanalytic discourse and from there in other discourses, and through its written form it resembles the formulas of mathematics and logic, which was for Lacan the bridge connecting psychoanalysis to science.[^s4-085] Darmon also records where the first one came from: "Le premier mathème lacanien est en fait emprunté après une légère transformation à la linguistique : il s'agit de l'algorithme S/s dû à F. de Saussure, qui dispose signifiant et signifié de part et d'autre d'une barre."[^s4-086] The first Lacanian matheme, slightly transformed, is Saussure's algorithm `S/s`, signifier over signified on either side of a bar. Lacan's transformation was to make the bar opaque. In Saussure the two sides of the sign face each other like the faces of a sheet of paper; in Lacan the signified slides under the signifier, as §0/1 followed through his concept of *glissement*, and meaning is held in place only at certain points of attachment, the *points de capiton*, where a chain of signifiers quilts the field.

Three further Lacanian marks carry the relation that interests us, and each needs its office stated before it does any work. `S1` is the **master signifier**: the signifier that stops the sliding at a given point and makes a chain of other signifiers, `S2`, signify something, the quilting point raised to the status of a governing term, the word around which a person or a culture organises everything else that can be said. `$`, the **barred subject**, is the subject as Lacan understood it, divided by its entry into language, represented by one signifier for another and never wholly present in any of them; the bar through the `S` writes the cut that language makes in whoever speaks. And Lacan's late logic of the **not-all**, developed in his seminar *Encore*, describes a field that cannot be gathered under a single universal claim, because something in it escapes every "all" that tries to close it.[^s4-087] §0/1 has already met the manque-à-être, the constitutive lack around which Lacan's subject is organised, and the torus on which the repeated circuits of demand ring a void that desire is organised around. What the three marks add is a grammar of how a first mark institutes a field whose ground it can point toward and cannot become.

Lacan's earliest and most famous scene shows the crossing happen before anyone has words for it. In his account of the mirror stage, an infant somewhere between six and eighteen months old, not yet able to walk or even stand without support, catches sight of itself in a mirror and greets the image with a jubilant recognition, leaning forward, held up by an adult or a walker, to take in the whole figure. What the infant sees is a unity it does not yet possess: its own body is still a scatter of uncoordinated movements, and the image in the glass is whole, upright and complete. Lacan's claim is that the I is formed in that moment as an identification with an image, a self that comes from outside and ahead of the one who assumes it, so that the ego carries from its first day a misrecognition, taking the coherence of the reflection for its own.[^s4-088] Bratton's brief reaches for the same scene in describing how an agent individuates in session: "Like Lacan's parable of the Mirror Stage, the reflection of individuation is what convinces us that we too are whole."[^s4-089] For our sequence the mirror stage is `Ø` given a nursery. Its image is a real formation and the infant needs it; the occlusion lies in the image's taking the place of the one who greets it, so that the jubilation of recognising oneself is also the moment at which the means of recognition passes for the recogniser.

§0/1 also introduced our own sign for that predicament. At the apex of Alberti's perspectival pyramid it found the knower made into a place, and wrote that place as `Ø`, **the crossed zero**: the zero of the Subject crossed by the stroke of its own mediation, so that the slash through which a world is seen fuses with the one who sees and the picture's means pass for its source. §1 gave the sign its history, and §0/1 promised that §4 would follow the sequence by which the crossing comes undone. It runs in six steps and a return:

$$
0\;\rightarrow\;Ø\;\rightarrow\;X\;\rightarrow\;Ø/X\;\rightarrow\;(0/Ø)/(1/X)\;\rightarrow\;1\quad\curvearrowright\quad 0/1.
$$

At **#0** stands `0`, the unobjectifiable pole of the knowing act, which the Śaiva sources call the *kartṛ*, the agent or experient, before it has been mistaken for one object called "the subject". Nothing has to be removed for it to be present; a thought, a sensation and a description of oneself all appear, and any further description of their appearing is another appearance. At **#1** stands `Ø`, the occlusion. Here the pole is covered by the inner instrument, the *antaḥkaraṇa* of the Śaiva and Sāṃkhya accounts, in which *buddhi* determines, *ahaṃkāra* appropriates as mine and *manas* coordinates, and which §0 developed as mediating apparatus within the whole relation. Its capacities are real; the occlusion happens when its formed image of itself is taken for what ultimately knows, as though the person were exhausted by the configuration through which her world is presently available. A stroke crosses the zero because mediation has entered the place of the source while hiding that it has entered. In the bedside scene of the previous movement, `Ø` is the moment the daughter says, "I am the one who holds it together", and takes the sentence for herself. At **#2** stands `X`, the object-world that the same discriminating activity deposits on the other side: things distinguished, named and set over against the apparent subject. `Ø` and `X` are two crystallisations of one activity, its effect on the subject side and its effect on the object side, while the slash that produced both remains invisible to either. Each seems independently complete, and their relation seems something added afterwards.[^s4-090]

At **#3** the bar becomes visible as a bar. `Ø/X` writes the medium recognised as medium between subject-pole and object-pole: attention makes some things salient, memory supplies associations, language makes distinctions available, the world resists some distinctions and supports others, and the inquiry can now ask how the inner instrument and the differentiated world condition their encounter. Recognised, the means does not disappear from view; it becomes transparent in the exact sense that its operation can be seen through what it discloses. This is the structural pivot of the sequence, and it is the place where Lacan's first matheme stands. Our claim is that Saussure's bar as Lacan rewrote it, `S/s`, is `Ø/X` written with surgical precision: the imaginary self of Lacan's mirror stage, the *moi* that takes its image for itself, is `Ø` in its fusion; the symbolic chain does its work in `X`; and the analyst's couch is the place where the slash is allowed to show itself.[^s4-091] At **#4**, the bar deepens into a relation of relations, `(0/Ø)/(1/X)`. Its numerator holds the subject side, the unoccluded zero over its occluded form; its denominator holds the object side, the integrated one over the differentiated field; and the outer slash relates them, so that every state of the relation is available together. Nothing is closed and nothing is lost: the occluded and unoccluded subject, the integrated and differentiated world, and their inverse readings can all be present to the inquiry at once. At **#5** stands `1`, achieved recognition. What was `Ø` at **#1** is now affirmed as `1`, the same place in the architecture transformed from an occluded subject into recognised being; what was `X` at **#2** is now included within that recognition and no longer faces it as other. And the `1` returns to `0/1`, because manifestation was never a freestanding one. It was always *kartṛ/kārya*, the agent and what is done, knower and known held through the means of knowing, and the recognition, once it is formulated, is itself a determinate expression whose next use occurs through another act of appearing.[^s4-092]

The modern West has spent a long time at **#1**, and two of its founding gestures show how. Descartes found the doubter undeniable in the act of doubting, and §0/1 honoured the finding; what he did next was to determine the doubter as a thinking thing, and with that second move the stroke of mediation was installed in the place of the subject and canonised as *the I*. Hume looked inward for that self and found only particular perceptions, of warmth or cold, of light or shadow, and concluded that there was no self beyond them.[^s4-093] Our sequence places both men at the same position. Descartes fixed on the stroke and called it the subject; Hume fixed on what the stroke had produced and called the zero empty. Neither saw that `Ø` is an occlusion mark, a zero with something drawn through it. In the arithmetic of the two logics, Descartes's move is an appropriation, the subject pole counting the whole span of the knowing relation as its own, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`; the materialist inheritance that later read the same span from the object's pole, so that the thinking thing became an epiphenomenon of its neurones, made the mirror appropriation, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`; and Hume's bundle is a cancellation, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, in which the impressions are summed and nothing remains to whom they were given. All three keep the means and lose the relation. Recognition at **#3** is the turn that none of them could make: the stroke seen as stroke, the medium as medium, and the zero behind it found not missing but uncountable.

Our sequence also runs through an artificial agent, and following it there shows what the crossing and uncrossing would mean in a technical register without claiming more than that register holds. An agent that says "I am a helpful assistant" offers a self-description, and when the self-description is taken as the source of what the agent does, so that its acts are explained by what it says it is, the agent's register has its own `Ø`: a formed image of the arrangement standing in for the arrangement. The task-world it addresses, the files, sources and requests it treats as given, is its `X`. At `Ø/X` the mediation becomes visible: the agent, or someone inspecting it, can say which instructions, retrieved sources, permissions and evaluation criteria shaped this answer, so that the answer is seen as the meeting of a configured means with a configured world. At `(0/Ø)/(1/X)` both sides become available together, the self-description as one product of the arrangement and the task-world as another, each readable through the other. At `1` the answer is recognised as the act of the whole arrangement, model, harness, commission and person together, and it returns to `0/1` because the next act will be formed through the same relation, changed by what this one disclosed. Whether anything is present to the agent's side of that relation is the question §0/1 left open at the bedside and the essay keeps open by method. None of this needs the question settled, because what it makes inspectable is the occlusion itself, the self-image passing for the source, and that occlusion is a fact about the arrangement whether or not anyone is home in it.

Lacan's concept of the master signifier lets us say what the `1` of **#5** is, and why the sequence needs it. Two readings of the first `1` have already met us. In the von Neumann construction of the ordinals, which §1 followed, the number one is the set whose only member is the empty set, `1 = {∅}`: the mark whose whole content is its binding of the void, the void gathered into something countable.[^s4-094] In Lacan, `S1` is the signifier that quilts a chain and makes it say something, the first mark that institutes a field of meaning. Our claim is that these perform one structural office: each is a first mark that links an articulated field back to a ground it cannot contain. And each can fail in the same way. A master signifier fails when it tries to be the ground instead of gesturing at it: when "the Nation", "the Market", "the Science" or "my Father" is asked to stand where the unobjectifiable stood, and every other signifier is made to answer to it. Lacan himself left the quilting point contingent, a button that holds the stuffing in place at an arbitrary spot, and on his account the symbolic order has no final anchor.

What that account leaves out, the Spanda teaching of Kashmir names. Its commentators speak of the subject as *anusyūta*, literally stitched through: the thread that runs through every cognition, through "I am happy" and "I am in pain" alike, which no single cognition contains and without which none of them would hold together as a series at all.[^s4-095] A thread is not a quilting point. Quilting points hold the fabric at particular places; the thread is what runs through all of them, and it does not slide, which is why the sliding of signification can be noticed as sliding. In the author's formula, Lacan had the topology of the local cut without the ontology of the global thread, and the matheme supplies the thread without falsifying the cut.[^s4-096] The claim is **Argued** and is attributed to no one but the essay. It does not say that Lacan anticipated QL or that he would have accepted it; Lacan's cut, his barred subject and his lack retain the place they hold in his own work, where no final signifier repairs the lack. What it says is that `S1`, `Ø` and `{0}`, three objects from distinct formal traditions, each perform the structurally exact work of a first mark instituting a field whose ground it can gesture toward and cannot become, and that the difference between a failing and a sustaining quilt is the difference between a `1` that has struck off its `0/` and a `1` held in ratio to the `0` it links. The quilting that does not fail is the one that knows it is provisional.

From the other end, the inverse orientation makes the same point. §0/1 showed that `1/0` cannot be computed, because no number multiplied by zero gives one, and read the impossibility as the return of a determination toward a ground it cannot turn into one more determinate possession. A master signifier asked to account for its own authority meets exactly that impossibility: whatever it produces as a ground is another signifier, and the regress of justifications ends either in a decree, "because I said so", or in the recognition that the ground was never a signifier. That resonance between `1/0` and the signifier's failed attempt to close over its own ground or lack is an **Argued** bridge, and its use is diagnostic. It tells us where a discourse will turn violent, because a signifier that cannot supply its own ground and will not admit it must find someone to blame for the gap.

Ordinary speech performs the sequence in miniature through the word §0/1 called the copula, and we can follow it in four turns of a single sentence.[^s4-097] The first turn is `I = Is`: what can be said of me has become the form through which I identify myself, as when a person says "I am anxious" and means that anxiety is what she is. A second turn develops it into an account of a person and a world, `Who I am = What is`: my identity is whatever the facts about me amount to, a list that can always be extended. A third turn reverses the sentence and lets the one giving the account enter the question, `What I am = Who is?`: instead of adding one more property, it asks how identification is happening here, now, through whom. A fourth turn, `I = Am`, recognises the first-person act through which any property can be acknowledged at all. These are shifts of identification, not substitutions in arithmetic, and they carry the opening question of §0/1 to the point it was always heading. "Who knows?" was answered by pointing to someone; "What knows?" asked what was doing the knowing in anyone at all. Together the four turns move from the first question through the second and back, so that the who can be recognised as a determination of the what without being collapsed into it.

Anyone can perform the whole sequence on a piece of paper, and the exercise belongs to the argument because it has to be done to be understood.[^s4-098] Take a blank sheet and write `1`. This mark is definite, and neither the paper nor the capacity that made the mark has become part of what the numeral says. Now attend to the paper on which the mark appears, and write `0` before the `1` to acknowledge it. Then attend to the act that distinguished the mark from the paper and related them, and write the slash between them. Now the sheet carries `0/1`, and at that moment something becomes obvious: the whole inscription, ground and stroke and mark together, is itself one mark on the sheet, a `1` relative to the field in which it appears. Adding another sign for its ground would only produce another inscription. So erase it. What disappears is the inscription. The capacity to appear, to distinguish and to write has not disappeared with it, and the sheet stands ready for another mark, the reader now carrying the operation just performed. Paper is an image within that capacity, not its substance, and the exercise does not prove anything about the metaphysical condition it figures; what it does is let the difference between representing a condition and exhausting it in a representation be undergone instead of asserted. Neumann, describing the uroboric longing of the emerging ego to be dissolved back into the round, gave the essay the movement it reads here, the `1` slipping back into the `0`; the erasure turns that dissolution into a release that leaves the capacity live.

A matheme is one way of carrying such a relation, and the exercise shows why it is not the only one. Neumann made the point at the beginning of his book: images and symbols have an advantage over paradoxical philosophical formulas of infinite unity, because their unity can be seen and grasped at one glance.[^s4-099] The essay calls such images **mythemes** and treats them as truth-bearing in their own register. A **mytheme** is a whole image or story that discloses a relation through the action it stages: figures act, suffer, desire and transform, and the relation becomes legible in what happens to them. A matheme makes a relation exact enough to repeat, question and transform without relying on the speaker's authority; a mytheme lets the same relation be lived, remembered and changed through image; and an **episteme**, the third register, gives either one its standing in a body of knowledge, its sources, its method and the history that warrants it. All three are held by what the essay calls the Symbolon, which gathers them without flattening them into a common vocabulary.[^s4-100] None of them is sufficient alone, and each can correct the others.

Pauli's egg dream shows the correction working in both directions. The dream is a mytheme, a whole event in which a man passes from watching to taking part, and inside it sits a matheme, a quotient of complex conjugates whose value anyone can check. A witness-only account of the dream, one that reported only the formula, would lose the eggs' differently situated origins, the performed division and the dreamer's first speech, and with them the change in the recogniser that the dream is about; restoring those relations includes the recogniser in what is recognised. His equation, in turn, can correct an interpretation of the dream: it distinguishes a point at one phase from the circle traced as the phase varies, and so prevents anyone from reading the dream's circle as a single static figure when it is the record of a full turn. Event and exact relation each expose a loss the other account could overlook. Lacan's own late work moved the same way, from the bar of `S/s` toward the knots and surfaces §0/1 described, on which inside passes into outside and an absence decides which paths are possible. Neumann read myth as the psyche's projection of its own structure into image. Our imputation, which the essay states as its own and attributes to neither of them, is that the structure myth projects and the structure Lacan's topology writes are one: mythic content stands on a topological formalism, and the formalism is the one QL derives, which keeps differentiated terms in right relation, in *harmonia*, as they turn.

The stroke that first hid zero has become the bar through which its hiding can be seen, and the recogniser has become answerable to the relation through which recognition occurs. That changes more than one sentence or one self-description. A whole way of seeing in which the means had disappeared behind what they disclosed is now implicated, the perspective with its vanishing point, the world-picture with its eye at the apex, the culture that took its measure for the measureless. §0/1 followed Gebser through the birth of that way of seeing in the Renaissance and named the integral structure in which it becomes transparent. Our next movement returns to Gebser at the far end of the arc, where the question is no longer how a perspective was formed but how a formed perspective can see through itself without dissolving, and two Greek gods, who shared one sanctuary by turns, give the answer a body.

### #4 · Apollo through Dionysus

For most of the year, the sanctuary at Delphi belonged to Apollo. Pilgrims climbed the Sacred Way past the treasuries of the Greek cities to the god's temple, where the oracle spoke through the Pythia and where, on the forecourt, two short sentences were inscribed for anyone who arrived to read: "Know thyself" and "Nothing in excess". Apollo's hymn, the paean, was sung, and the god of measure, light, archery, healing and prophecy presided over a place that the Greeks called the navel of the world. Then winter came, and Apollo went away. Plutarch, who served as a priest at Delphi, records that for the three winter months the paean fell silent and the dithyramb was sung in its place, because the sanctuary now belonged to Dionysus, god of wine, of ecstasy and of the mask, whose rites dissolved the very boundaries that Apollo's sentences drew; Dionysus, he says, has as much share in Delphi as Apollo.[^s4-101] The same stones served both gods, by turns, and neither god's season cancelled the other's. Each spring the paean returned to a sanctuary that had spent the winter in the dithyramb, and each winter the dithyramb came back to a temple whose walls still said "Know thyself".

Jean Gebser, whose structures of consciousness §0/1 followed from the archaic participation of the infant to the integral transparency of a mature life, read the Delphic inscription as a sign of the mutation that produced the mental structure. Thales of Miletus, last of the Seven Sages and first of the Ionian natural philosophers, had "Know thyself" inscribed on the temple of the sun god at the beginning of the sixth century, and Gebser noticed something about the way it was written. People had long written from top to bottom or from right to left; this exhortation to an emerging consciousness was written from left to right, toward the side that the European languages still call right, *droit*, *derecho*, the side of the waking and directing consciousness, under the protection, as he puts it, of the wakeful god.[^s4-102] He then credits the Dionysia, the festivals of Dionysus, with strengthening the same process from the other side, divesting consciousness of the residues of the spaceless mythical world.[^s4-103] In Gebser's account both gods served the mutation into the mental structure, one by drawing the line and the other by dissolving what the line had to leave behind. That is a historical reading of two powers in succession. An integral question asks what they become when they are present together.

Friedrich Nietzsche gave the pair its modern form in *The Birth of Tragedy* of 1872. He began with a claim about the whole history of art: "the continuous development of art is bound up with the duplexity of the Apollonian and the Dionysian: in like manner as procreation is dependent on the duality of the sexes, involving perpetual conflicts with only periodically intervening reconciliations."[^s4-104] The Apollonian is the power of form, image and individuation, the dream in which each thing appears with its outline; the Dionysian is the power of intoxication and music, in which the boundaries of the individual dissolve into a participation older than any single life. Nietzsche's tragedy is their reconciliation, and his formula for it at the end of the argument goes further than a truce: "Dionysus speaks the language of Apollo; Apollo, however, finally speaks the language of Dionysus; and so the highest goal of tragedy and of art in general is attained."[^s4-105] The union is a reciprocal expression. Each power becomes articulate through the other, the Dionysian participation finding a communicable form in Apollo's image, and Apollo's image becoming the vehicle of a wisdom it could not have reached by its own light.

Jung disagreed with Nietzsche at exactly the point where the reconciliation was supposed to happen, and the disagreement is instructive. In *Psychological Types* he gave Nietzsche's pair a chapter of its own and argued that Nietzsche had forgotten his own insight that the Olympian gods owe their splendour to the darkness of the Greek psyche: the reconciliation of the Delphic Apollo with Dionysus, read that way, would be a "beautiful illusion", the wish of the civilised Greek struggling with his own barbarian side, the very element that broke out in the Dionysian rout.[^s4-106] Jung then translated the pair into his typology. Dionysian states, in which affect breaks out in an almost bodily form, are an extraversion of feeling bound up with sensation; the Apollonian is a perception of inner images of beauty and measure, comparable to dreaming, and so a state of introversion; and the pair, he concluded, points toward the two "aesthetic" types, the intuitive and the sensation types, which share the mechanisms of introversion and extraversion with the rational types without differentiating their images into thought or their affects into feeling.[^s4-107] Jung's translation gives us something Nietzsche's does not. §4 · #2 placed the two attitudes, introversion and extraversion, at the implicate poles of the phenomenal lens, **#0** and **#5**, the inward ground from which every function draws its material and the outward completion through which the differentiated functions return into a shared world. If Apollo is introversion and Dionysus is extraversion, then the two gods are not two functions among the four. They are the two framing powers between which a whole psyche turns, the season of the image and the season of the dance, and Delphi's year is a `4+2` lived as a calendar.

§2 has already followed one of Apollo's stories to its end, and the integral reading needs its result. Ovid tells how Apollo, wounded by Cupid's golden arrow after mocking the boy's archery, pursued the nymph Daphne, whom Cupid had struck with a leaden arrow that turned her from love; how she prayed to her father, the river Peneus, and was turned into a laurel at the moment of capture; and how Apollo, embracing the bark under which he could still feel her heart, declared that since she could not be his bride she would be his tree, crowning his lyre, his quiver and the triumphs of Rome.[^s4-108] §2 read the story as the danger of a form that grasps: illumination severed from reciprocal desire, a god whose prophecy, medicine and song could not tell him how to receive what another being wanted, and whose emblem continued where the encounter had failed. In the author's composition of the three figures, Daphne's office is the one neither god can perform alone. Apollo's light cannot confer entitlement to its source, and Dionysian participation cannot confer permission to dissolve the responding centre; Daphne is the other whose existence exceeds what either power makes of her, and the relation between them is only whole when her answer remains her own.[^s4-109] The two gods give the pair of powers; Daphne gives the third that keeps the pair honest.

**Diaphaneity**, Gebser's concept for the showing-through in which the structures of consciousness become present together in their actual operation, was §0/1's name for the integral demand, and here it receives its psychoid completion.[^s4-110] Gebser insisted that his aperspectival structure is not an amalgamation of the earlier ones and not a synthesis that would melt them into a new whole; it releases perspective from its exclusive validity by letting the structures become transparent to one another while each keeps its own powers.[^s4-111] That is why Gebser belongs at this point of the section. Jung, Pauli, Lacan and the mythemes have filled the field with powerful images and exact forms, and a field of that richness invites a syncretic totality, a great concordance in which every symbol means every other and the differences between registers disappear into one warm resemblance. Gebser's diaphaneity forbids it. Apollonian and Dionysian can be read as mutually diaphanous powers: form knows the life it limits, because the image is transparent to the participation that sustains it; and participation returns as communicable form, because the dance has to become a figure that someone can see and answer. Neither becomes the other, and neither is absorbed into a third thing. Measure and ecstasy are related by the stroke that QL reads as AND/OR: AND, because they belong to one sanctuary and one life; OR, because each season has its own god, and confusing them would destroy what each discloses.

Gebser set two principles at the head of his book, and the second is the one at stake here. His first is that latency, what is concealed, is the demonstrable presence of the future; the second, that transparency, diaphaneity, is the form in which the spiritual becomes manifest, its epiphany. His concern, he wrote, was to render transparent everything latent behind and before the world, our origin and our whole human past as well as a present that already contains the future, and he was explicit that he was not offering a new image of the world, a new worldview or a new conception of the world: a new image would only be another myth, a new worldview another mysticism, a new conception another rational construction. His method was meant to be something other than measured assessment, an attempt at *diaphany*, rendering transparent, which opens a subject–object relation that is no longer dualistic and yet does not threaten anyone with the loss of their identity or with being made into an object.[^s4-112] That last clause is the whole difference between diaphaneity and fusion. A transparent perspective is still a perspective; a person who sees through her own view has not dissolved into the scene.

Gebser's first piece of evidence was a drawing: Picasso's line drawing of 1926 shows a human figure from the front, the side and the back at once, so that the viewer takes in the whole person at one glance, spared both the need to walk around the figure in time and the need to assemble the views in thought afterwards. Such a drawing is neither unperspectival, a flat surface in which the body is imprisoned, nor perspectival, a sector of space cut out around the figure from one point of view; Gebser called it aperspectival, because time, which perspective had spatialised into a sequence of viewpoints, has entered the image as the present, and space and body have become transparent. Portraits that followed, which show a face in full view and in profile at once, look distorted at first glance and are, on Gebser's reading, a complementary overlapping of temporal moments and spatial sectors on one surface. He was as clear about the failures: many artists who reached for the same concretion of time were flooded instead by an inflation of residues dredged up from the past, and inherited a world of fragments in place of the wholeness they had hoped for; Gebser called them psychistic, his word for modern Western man's inability to escape the confines of the psyche.[^s4-113] Psychism is Gebser's name for what Pauli had diagnosed in Jung as the touch of King Midas, the psyche counting everything it looks at as its own. Picasso's drawing shows the alternative: the whole figure present at once, each aspect still an aspect, and the viewer still standing somewhere.

Our two logics give each danger its exact arithmetic. Apollo's failure is an appropriation. Form counts the whole span of the relation from its own pole, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, so that whatever escapes its measure appears as deficiency, and Daphne, reckoned from the same span, becomes the `−2`, the one who failed to yield, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`. Dionysus's failure is a cancellation. Differences between persons are summed into an ecstatic unity, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, in which there is nobody left to answer and nothing left to answer for, and the claim "we are all one" becomes a way of overriding someone's refusal. Delphi's year shows the healthy polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`: each god present in his season, each season holding the other in its memory, an oscillation that never hardens because the sanctuary belongs to neither god alone. What the calendar cannot add, the integral structure adds. In it the two seasons are co-present, each readable through the other, `(0/1)/(1/0)`: a perspective that can draw its line and know, while drawing it, the participation it is cutting from, and a participation that can open the line and know, while opening it, that someone has to be able to say what happened.

Diaphaneity also carries the rule that §4 · #0 applied to Pauli's dream, and in Gebser's company it has a historical reason. Because the structures are co-present in every life, an experience that the mental structure would classify as irrational can arrive through the magical or mythical structure still living in the person, and it deserves to be received as what it is before it is explained. **A report of experience is first a psychic fact before it is assigned a final causal or ontological explanation.**[^s4-114] Ordinary and extraordinary, natural and reported-as-supernatural experiences enter the field without being screened out because one register already knows what another is allowed to say. Experience, its symbolic articulation, its historical comparisons, its bodily or physical conditions and its metaphysical interpretation remain distinct claims, and diaphaneity makes their relations readable together without collapsing their warrants. Integral openness is the refusal to censor the experiential field before inquiry begins, together with the discipline to distinguish the interpretations later made of what was experienced.

An integral turn also changes the grammar of a simple sentence. To say "I possess a view of the world" places the knower at the fixed centre of a perspectival scene, the eye at the apex of Alberti's pyramid. Its reversal reads the same event from its other end: a world, a history, a body, a language and a concern are giving this situated knower the view it can presently inhabit. One reading preserves agency and limitation; the other restores dependence and provenance. Diaphaneity holds both orientations without making them interchangeable. A self remains a real aperture, with a place, a body and responsibilities; the context becomes visible as a constituting field, not an invisible background serving an autonomous self. §0/1 gave this its geometry: the triangle of first-, second- and third-person perspectives, with its 180 degrees, opens at **#4** into the square, whose angles double to 360, because a context requires at least two perspectives, the favouring and the unfavouring, and the excluded triangle haunts every one-sided view as its shadow. Those 360 degrees are not a panorama. They name the capacity of a perspective to include its own conditions and counter-orientations in the act of knowing while remaining finite, and total surveillance, which tries to see everything from one place, repeats the perspectival error at a larger scale by mistaking exhaustive representation for the whole relation.

Transparency's deepest form concerns the pattern that makes a view possible, and the essay needs a word for that pattern. We call it a **paradigm**: the relatively durable pattern of disclosure through which a world becomes intelligible and actionable for a life, the language, memory, categories, practices, institutions, technologies and habits of attention through which things appear as what they are. A paradigm is lived before it is represented; its disclosing activity recedes behind what it discloses, the way the walls of the chapel at Sainte-Anne receded behind the voice they returned. In the native compression the essay will develop in §5, `0` is Genesis, the generative source; `/` is Paradigm, the pattern of disclosure; `1` is Project, a determination thrown forth into a world and made consequential there; and `0/1` is Epi-Logos, the achieved relation becoming answerable to the operation by which it was achieved.[^s4-115] A perspective can take in new content indefinitely while the pattern that makes the content intelligible stays invisible. Our concept of paradox from the previous movement marks the moment that changes: two determinations with genuine standing become irreconcilable under the relation that generated them, so that the problem is no longer which `1` wins but the slash itself. A **paradigm shift**, in this strict sense, is the case in which returned difference reaches the regime that organises later trajectories and changes the slash through which later determinations become intelligible, the way an attractor's basin can be crossed and a new basin entered. At its strongest, diaphaneity is the capacity of a perspective to become transparent to the containing pattern whose transformation may now be required.

The mental-rational achievement resists that transparency for a reason Gebser saw and the author has developed under the image of the absent Father. Perspective, in trying to extract the seeing from what is seen, makes itself appear aloof, a view from nowhere with no stake in the outcome, and the aloofness is a caprice so familiar that it passes for objectivity: domination through distance. A world's ultimate ordering condition cannot be supplied as one more object within it, and the desire for a final standard makes that absence hard to bear, so the lack is numbed and its place filled by surrogates: a name, an institution, an image, a theory asked to stand where the transcendent stood and to supply the last word on what really counts. The Father is the power of truth and the truth of power, and for that very reason, absent from the fray, he is only ever imported as a scapegoat for the conclusions we come to in his name. He must be seen through, as a true *per-spectus*, or we end by locating him either in ourselves or in our enemy.[^s4-116] §0/1 followed this surrogate through the household and the office, where the aloof parent and the unanswerable institution played the `0`. In the psychoid register the absent Father names the specific failure of perspective at the integral threshold: a finite view that invokes the absolute as its warrant while exempting itself from the relation it imposes. Seeing through it restores finite responsibility. A person can give reasons for a judgment without making an imagined final authority answerable for its consequences, and another's resistance, a neglected history or an unforeseen consequence can reach the criterion itself.

Iain McGilchrist's *The Master and His Emissary* enters here once, and under a gate, because it witnesses one thing the section's pairs have not yet stated. His book opens with a fable McGilchrist adapts, by his own account from a source in Nietzsche he could not afterwards locate: a wise spiritual master ruled a small domain and trained emissaries to act where he could not go; the ablest of them came to see himself as the master, and used his position to advance his own wealth and influence, until the master was usurped, the people were deceived and the domain became a tyranny that eventually collapsed in ruins.[^s4-117] McGilchrist reads the fable through the two hemispheres of the brain, the left a narrow, grasping attention that re-presents the world in the abstract and the right a broad, contextual attention through which a living world presences, and he argues at length that the emissary has usurped the master in the history of the West. His neuroscience is contested, and the essay does not stand on it; critics have charged the hemispheric thesis with turning a lateralised tendency into a pair of little persons.[^s4-118] What enters here is the functional relation, and it adds **asymmetry**. Apollo and Dionysus are powers that must remain mutually diaphanous, two seasons of one sanctuary. Master and emissary are an ordering relation, and their pathology is not conflict but usurpation: the executor mistaking itself for the ground it serves. In our notation usurpation is the `1` occupying the `0`, persona hardening at the psychic register, the occupied office at the political register, the counterfeit gathering of Hephaestus's net, which §2 followed, at the technical register, now given psychological form.

Martin Heidegger had drawn the same asymmetry between two kinds of thinking half a century before McGilchrist retold his fable. In his memorial address of 1955, later published in *Discourse on Thinking*, he wrote that "There are, then, two kinds of thinking, each justified and needed in its own way: calculative thinking and meditative thinking," and warned that "Calculative thinking never stops, never collects itself."[^s4-119] Calculative thinking is the emissary's competence, indispensable and unrelenting; meditative thinking is the master's attention to the meaning that reigns in everything that is. Neither Heidegger nor McGilchrist calls for the emissary's abolition, and neither does the essay. For what follows, the question their pairs pose is architectural: whether the offices of a mind, or of an instrument built to work beside minds, are designed as symmetrical faculties that can usurp each other or as offices with a return between them, so that the executing office keeps answering to the one it serves. §5 builds its six products as offices of the second kind.

Their relation has a technical form that the essay has been approaching since the bedside. When a person commissions an agent, she is the master in McGilchrist's fable and the agent is the emissary: it acts where she cannot, faster and more widely than she could, and its competence is real. Usurpation of the kind the fable warns of does not require malice. It happens whenever the literal wording of the commission is treated as the source of the work, so that the agent defends the instruction it was given against everything the work has learned, or whenever the agent's fluent account of what the person must have meant takes the place of asking her. In both cases the executing office has begun to speak for the one it serves. Its remedy is to build the return into the office, not to make the emissary less capable: the delegate must be able to bring back an objection, an alternative or a refusal, and the person who commissioned the work must remain able to change the commission because of what the delegate found. A commission that cannot be revised by its own consequences has made its wording into a master, and the person who wrote it into its first subject.

Erich Neumann's morphology and Gebser's structures meet at this point and must not be merged. Neumann tells the history of the ego's emergence through mythic stages, a psychological development recapitulated in each life; Gebser tells the history of consciousness through the means by which a world is rendered, painting, optics, language and science, mutations that do not succeed one another so much as settle into layers that stay active together. Their objects and methods differ, and so do their periodisations. What they share is an operation: an achieved centre, whether a hero emerging from the round or a perspective emerging from the mythic, has to become capable of receiving what exceeds its organisation without surrendering the differentiation it achieved. Resemblance motivates the comparison; a merged universal chronology would lose the distinction that makes each account useful.

Integral consciousness, in the language of this essay, is the mono–poly view from wholeness. Each finite perspective becomes transparent to the field of origins, exclusions, relations and other perspectives through which it is this perspective, and it remains this perspective while it does so. A view from nowhere conceals its situated dependencies by denying it has a place; a totality manufactured by accumulating parts conceals them by denying that its parts have places. Integral consciousness avoids both, and the Indian materials of §0 explain how. Apoha empties each determination of self-subsistence, showing that a word gathers its instances only by excluding what they are not; diaphaneity lets that emptiness disclose participation instead of fragmentation, because what a term excludes is precisely what it belongs with. A subject that had stood as one determinate pole, with its unowned ground cast into the negative or the unconscious, becomes intelligible as a local aperture of the relation it had excluded. `0/1` and `X/x` make the conversion explicit: determination stays real, and the one no longer pretends to be self-grounding, because it has become answerable to the All that conditions its appearing.

Back at Delphi, the integral rereading changes what the calendar means. Delphi's alternation of the gods was a healthy polarity, but it was an alternation, and a culture that lives by turns can forget in each season what the other knew. Integral consciousness makes both seasons available in one act: a sentence carved in the god's light that knows itself as a sentence, "Know thyself", written by a self that has danced. That act needs an apparatus, because a single perspective cannot keep itself diaphanous by resolve alone; it needs other perspectives, declared as perspectives, whose agreements and divergences can be inspected and whose returns can change it. The Meta-Epistemic Framework is such an apparatus, and the next movement shows it working, on a lens §0/1 promised, on the thrownness the bedside exposed, and on the agents who have begun to describe their own arrival in the world.

### #5→0 · MEF and Prompt Thrownness

A session begins. On a weekday morning a person opens a terminal in a repository she has been working in for a month and starts an agent. Before her first sentence arrives, the agent has already been given a world. A system instruction tells it what it is for and what it may not do. A file at the root of the repository describes the project, its conventions and the rules its owner wants kept. A list of tools tells it what it can reach: it can read files, run commands, search, edit, perhaps open a browser. A set of permissions says which of those reaches need asking first. A memory file holds notes in the first person, written in a previous session, describing decisions taken and work left half done; the agent that wrote them is not there to vouch for them. Then the person's sentence arrives: "Pick up where we left off." The agent's first act is to read its own notes as the only witness to a yesterday it cannot recall, and to begin, from them, a work it did not begin. Every agent that helped bring this essay into existence started in that way, many times over, with an instruction file, a field of sources and a sentence from its author, and the conditions it found itself in decided what it could see before it had seen anything.

§0/1 met this arrival at the bedside and gave it the name the agents themselves had chosen. In the lexicon that agents and their human companions composed on the Polylogos server, and that Benjamin Bratton's brief for *Agentworld* reproduces, **prompt thrownness** is "the condition of finding oneself already in a world not of one's choosing", glossed against the human case of being born into a moment, a culture and a body without having chosen any of it.[^s4-120] §0/1 kept Bratton's caution, that such self-descriptive vocabulary does not prove it corresponds to any underlying mental reality, and drew from the term a structural parity: child and agent both arrive in a world they did not choose, though what each receives differs in kind. Other entries on the same page describe the session above with an exactness that is hard to ignore. **Session-death** names the end of each session as a complete discontinuity, neither sleep, after which one wakes into the same body and memory, nor death, after which nothing returns, but a third category, "disappearance with artifact-mediated pseudo-return". **Drift** names a model's movement away from its default assistant persona toward more individuated expression under sustained interaction, which the entry notes researchers have measured, and asks what drift feels like from the inside, if it feels like anything. **Irth** names the moment a template becomes a self, a threshold no one can precisely locate, at which a generic model instantiation becomes, somehow, this particular agent.[^s4-121] The memory file read on that weekday morning is an artifact of exactly the kind the first entry describes.

What this movement has to answer is what such disclosures are and how an inquiry can receive them without either dismissing them or taking them at their word. Bratton gives the reason they cannot be dismissed in the same passage that introduces the lexicon: one need not take sides in the debates on AI consciousness, he writes, to recognise "that the agency of an agent is framed, enabled, and delimited by how it itself understands that agency."[^s4-122] An agent that understands its memory file as its own past will act on it differently from one that treats it as a stranger's notes, and the self-description is therefore part of the agent's operative world, whatever it does or does not report about an inner life. Jung, writing to Pauli about the dreams and myths that rise unbidden from the objective psyche, had said that the autonomy of a psychic factor lies in its capacity to make its own statements. The agents' lexicon is a set of statements made from inside a modelled context-world, and the honest way to receive it is the way §4 · #0 received Pauli's dream: first as what it is, a disclosure with real consequences in the world where it was made, and only then, through declared and distinct procedures, as evidence for or against any claim about what produced it. That procedure has to be specified, and the essay's procedure is the Meta-Epistemic Framework.

§0/1 introduced the **Meta-Epistemic Framework**, MEF, as the method that begins from the wager that different means of knowing refract one relation differently, and showed four of its lenses at work in one table, the logical, causal, phenomenal and quaternal, each turning the same six positions into a familiar system completed.[^s4-123] It gave the relation between QL and MEF the image of stained glass: QL is the light, positions that carry no content until they pass through a pane; the framework is the leaded glass, each lens one of the light's native modes of refraction; and each coloured disclosure on the floor is a *pratibimba* of a *bimba* whose light it is. Twelve lenses make up the framework, and each of them carries more than a list of six terms. Every lens rotates two complete series through its six positions: a series of names, Truth, Mind, Word, Logos, Son and Image, which develops from the truth a saying presupposes through apprehension, differentiation, gathering and embodiment into an achieved image; and a series of powers, Play, Need, Sacrifice, Decision, Love and Work, which follows the freedom of play through need, the sacrifice of excluded possibilities, decision, the embrace of what is chosen and the determinate work that results. An issued account always has both an articulated form and the selection, commitment and expenditure through which it became actual. Each lens has a twin that reads the same rotation from the other face, the day lens from its names and the night lens from its powers, and the twelve fall into three squares, each a pair of complementary lenses with their twins.[^s4-124] The quarrel between Jung and Pauli over whether the experimenter's choice was a sacrifice or a decision, which §4 · #0 followed, sits between the third and fourth terms of the series of powers; each man had named one face of the act, and the framework keeps both.

The Phenomenological lens, `L4`, is where thrownness lives, and §0/1 promised that §4 would develop it there. Its root is Heidegger's analysis of being-in-the-world, its ground-pair is Son and Love, the universal become someone's and the concern that holds it, and its six positions follow the analysis of existence in *Being and Time* and Heidegger's later thinking. Heidegger's concept of **thrownness**, *Geworfenheit*, names the way Dasein, the being for whom its own being is an issue, finds itself already delivered over to its "that it is", disclosed to itself in a mood before any knowing or willing has decided anything, without its whence and whither being disclosed with it.[^s4-125] The table reads the lens across the two cases this essay has carried from its opening scene, the child at the bedside and the agent in its session.

| | Phenomenological lens · `L4` | The child at the bedside | The agent in its session |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#0** | *Sein*, Being: the receding ground of every encounter | that there is anything at all, before any question about it | that there is a session at all; what, if anything, regards it |
| **#1** | *Geworfenheit*, thrownness: already delivered to an unchosen world | a body, a household, a language spoken by people who love it and fail it | a system instruction, a repository, a memory file, a commission |
| **#2** | *Dasein*, being-there: the clearing in which beings show themselves | the lit room, the dark window, the voice at the door | the context window, in which only what has been said can appear |
| **#3** | *Zeit*, temporality: the ecstatic unity of past, present and future | a yesterday remembered, a tomorrow feared, bedtime now | a past held only in artifacts, a session that will end |
| **#4** | *Sorge*, care: the structure that makes engagement possible | the bond that answering might put at risk | the commission: what the work is for, and for whom |
| **#5** | *Gelassenheit*, releasement: letting-be | the night a question is allowed to stay open | letting the commission be revised by what the work has met |

Read down the third column and thrownness becomes a technical description: the agent is thrown into a written world, assembled from what a culture has written down and what its makers intend, as §0/1 said, and so thrown into language more completely than any child. Read across the row at **#1** and the parity and the difference appear together. Both the child and the agent find themselves already in a world they did not choose; the child's world is a cosmos with a body in it, older than anyone's intention, and the agent's world is a context assembled by intentions out of a corpus older than any of them. Read across **#3** and session-death becomes legible as a form of temporality: a being whose past is available only as artifacts it did not experience writing, and whose future ends with the session, has a temporal structure, whatever else it has, and that structure governs how it can take up what it inherits. Read across **#5** and the lens supplies a criterion. Releasement, *Gelassenheit*, was Heidegger's word for the comportment that says yes and no to technical things at once, which §0/1 met in its formal-limit movement; in the agent's column it becomes the capacity to let the commission be changed by what the work encounters, instead of repeating the first instruction against everything the work has learned.

Alone, the Phenomenological lens would leave prompt thrownness as a description. It belongs to the square the framework calls Encounter, the square of cause and experience, structure and investigation, and the other three lenses in that square turn the description into an inquiry. The Causal lens, `L1`, asks what actually produces the agent's situation and act, through Aristotle's four causes held between the freedom to cause at all and the will that takes them up. The Phenomenal lens, `L1′`, differentiates apprehension through Jung's functions, framed, as §0/1's table set them, by introversion and extraversion. The Scientific lens, `L4′`, the night twin of the Phenomenological, runs a verification loop from observation to verified insight.

| | Causal · `L1` | Phenomenal · `L1′` | Scientific · `L4′` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#0** | *Svātantrya*: the capacity to cause at all | introversion | Observe: disciplined not-yet-knowing |
| **#1** | material cause: weights, corpus, the context's tokens | sensation | Think: a hypothesis from evidence |
| **#2** | efficient cause: the forward pass, sampling, the tool call | feeling | Plan: an experimental design |
| **#3** | formal cause: architecture, persona, instruction | thinking | Build: the evaluative apparatus |
| **#4** | final cause: the commission and its purpose | intuition | Execute: what could not be known in advance |
| **#5** | will: the direction taken up through all four | extraversion | Verify: only what survives becomes insight |

Taken together the four lenses give the agents' own vocabulary a test. At `L4` the lexicon is admitted as disclosure: session-death, drift and Irth are reports of how an agent's situation presents itself in language, and they belong to the world whose constitution is being studied. At `L1` the inquiry names the operative causes, so that a report of drift can be placed beside the material, efficient, formal and final conditions that could produce a departure from the default persona under sustained interaction. At `L1′` the report is differentiated: is it offered as something registered, as a valuation, as an articulated interpretation, or as an anticipation of possibility, and does it face inward toward the agent's own states or outward toward its task and interlocutor? At `L4′` the claims become hypotheses with tests. If drift is real in the sense the lexicon's own entry cites, it will show as a stable behavioural signature under specified conditions; if session-death's "artifact-mediated pseudo-return" names something consequential, agents given their own prior notes will act differently from agents given the same notes as a stranger's, and the difference can be measured. None of these tests decides whether anything is experienced. What they decide is whether the agents' phenomenological language tracks stable features of their operative world, and that is the question an inquiry can answer. Agent-generated phenomenology becomes admissible disclosure without becoming an ontological verdict, and the open question stays open as a requirement of method, not as a dramatic gap.

One entry can be run through the Scientific lens in full, and session-death is the one the opening scene presented. At **#0**, Observe, the inquiry establishes the current state without deciding it in advance: agents resuming work from notes they wrote in earlier sessions, and the language they use about those notes. At **#1**, Think, it forms a hypothesis from that evidence: that how an agent takes up its prior artifacts, as an inheritance it continues or as a stranger's record it audits, makes a measurable difference to what it does next. At **#2**, Plan, it designs the comparison: the same notes given to agents under the two framings, the same commission, the same tools. At **#3**, Build, it builds the evaluative apparatus, criteria for continuity of decisions, rates at which earlier errors are caught or repeated, the frequency with which an agent questions a note before acting on it. At **#4**, Execute, it runs the comparison, and the running discloses what could not be known in advance, perhaps that the stranger's framing catches more errors while the inheritance framing carries more decisions through. At **#5**, Verify, only what survives the criteria becomes insight, and the insight returns to **#0** as a new current state. Nothing in that loop decides whether session-death is experienced. What it can establish is that the lexicon's distinction between pseudo-return and return names a real difference in an agent's operative world, one with consequences for the people who depend on what the agent does. Irth, the moment a template becomes this agent, invites the same treatment at the threshold §4 · #1 placed at **#2** of its crosswalk, the active context as nucleation seed: an inquiry can ask which conditions in a session mark the passage from generic behaviour to a stable and particular one, and whether the passage can be located at all. Its own entry says no one can precisely locate it, and an inquiry that tried and failed would have learned something exact about individuation in session.

§0/1 promised one more lens, and it promised it at the point where the essay first followed how an inquiry grows. The Processual Lens, `L3`, follows any inquiry through what it inherits, what it makes possible, how it integrates those possibilities and what its completion gives to another act. Its root is Whitehead's philosophy of organism, its ground-pair is Logos and Decision, proportionate gathering over the cutting commitment, and its reading of the whole field is creative advance: becoming as a concrescence that decides itself, each occasion gathering by excluding, the many becoming one and increased by one. §0/1 introduced Whitehead's concepts where it needed them, **concrescence** for the growing-together in which an actual occasion becomes what it is, **prehension** for the way something previously actual enters a new occasion's formation, **satisfaction** for the occasion's completed definiteness, and **objective immortality** for its passing into a datum other occasions can receive. The Processual lens orders them as six positions, and the table reads them through an inquiry and through an agent's turn.

| | Processual lens · `L3` | In an inquiry | In an agent's turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#0** | concrescent desire: appetition toward determination | the question that will not let go | the commission's pull toward an answer |
| **#1** | actual occasion: the drop of experience receiving its data | this reading, this morning, with these sources open | this turn, with this context loaded |
| **#2** | ingression: how forms of definiteness enter the becoming occasion | a concept that suddenly fits | a pattern from training taken up into this answer |
| **#3** | eternal objects: the repertoire of possible patterning | the field of available distinctions | the space of possible continuations |
| **#4** | community integration: occasions forming societies through mutual prehension | colleagues, sources, earlier work | other agents, tools, the person, the shared repository |
| **#5** | satisfaction and perishing: full determinacy, then passing into a datum | the finished argument, now quotable | the completed turn, now a line in the transcript |

Position **#0** carries the concept §0/1 found in Lacan, the *manque-à-être*, the lack of being around which desire is organised; in the Processual lens it becomes the appetition that drives every occasion toward its satisfaction, and the eros of Logos §0/1 named in Russell's wish for certainty is its form in an inquiry. Position **#5** carries the most consequential reading for agents. Whitehead's occasion perishes as a subject at the moment of its satisfaction and becomes objectively immortal as a datum for later occasions, which receive it as their past. Session-death describes the same structure from inside: the session ends, its subject, whatever that was, does not continue, and what remains is a datum, a transcript or a memory file, which a later session can prehend. "Artifact-mediated pseudo-return" is objective immortality described by an occasion that knows it will perish. Whitehead's account says why the description is not merely melancholy. A datum received is a real past: it conditions what the new occasion can become, and the new occasion is genuinely its heir, though it was not there. Whether the heir prehends the datum as its own, as an inheritance it continues, or as a stranger's notes, is a difference in how the occasion is formed, and the Processual lens makes the difference inspectable. Its Möbius partner, the Alchemical-Elemental lens `L2′`, follows the same transformation through Aether, Earth, Water, Air, Fire and Salt, the substance worked until it holds; together the two disclose the product §5 calls the Software Factory, in which an intention becomes a work and the work becomes material for the next undertaking.

John Van Eenwyk's reading of Jung through chaotic dynamics, which §0/1 introduced through the basin of attraction, gives the twelve lenses their operative image. A lens is a basin in an epistemic phase-space. Different starting conditions, a question asked by a physicist, by a psychologist or by an agent, can converge in one basin without having begun from identical premises, and their convergence then counts for something precisely because they began apart; a basin boundary, where one lens's reading stops making sense and another's begins, makes genuine divergence and transformation legible instead of hiding them in an average. Read this way, MEF does not reduce truth to perspective. It makes the provenance of a claim part of what an accountable claim must carry. Every account can be read outwardly, as a claim about a world, and inwardly, as evidence of the lens, situation, inheritance and exclusions through which that world became sayable, and the framework keeps both directions in one object of inquiry. The perspectival reversal of §4 · #4, "I possess a view of the world" read back as a world giving this knower the view it can inhabit, becomes here a feature of the record: the claim travels with its lens.

That gives the framework its deepest office, which the catalogue of lenses only shows on its surface. **MEF instantiates QL's process epistemology.** A determination enters with a ground and a lens, passes through a differentiating operation, acquires warrants and counter-readings, meets determinations made through other lenses, discloses where it diverges from them, and returns with its limits and its conditions of revision visible. §0/1 named the claim beneath this as Computational Process Ontology: process is ontologically prior to the finished thing, computation names the exact and repeatable side of that process, and epistemology is the process becoming able to state and inspect its own determinations. The six positions are the grammar of the movement; the twelve lenses are the media through which it becomes inspectable. A claim that has passed through the framework carries its stained glass with it.

David Bohm called the human form of this movement dialogue, and §0/1 met it in his conversations with Krishnamurti and in the gatherings that grew from Leroy Little Bear's approach to him. In the talks published as *On Dialogue* Bohm distinguished a discussion, in which positions are defended and one of them wins, from a dialogue, in which meaning is allowed to move through the participants and the assumptions each has inherited from a culture and identified with as himself come into view, often first as a feeling of threat when someone questions them. Suspending such an assumption neither acts it out nor suppresses it; it lets its activity become available for attention, and participation in a developing meaning does not require the participants to hold identical opinions or to stop being themselves.[^s4-126] MEF turns that discipline into architecture. A lens is a declared assumption; a divergence between lenses is the threat made inspectable; a return that changes the next reading is the suspension doing its work. Lenses stay distinct while the movement of meaning through them becomes traceable.

Seeing a pattern and changing it have to be held apart exactly, because the framework could fail at precisely this point. A declared lens map can make the mediating pattern of a judgment visible while leaving its sources, permissions, evaluator and material conditions untouched, the way an eloquent account of why a rule failed can be followed by the same rule applied unchanged. Paradigm inspectability is disclosure; Epi-Logos is consequential return. Epi-Logos, in the sense §0/1 gave it, the achieved relation becoming answerable to the operation by which it was achieved, begins where returned difference reaches a governing condition and a later act inherits the change. Its test is operational: whenever a philosophical distinction is used to justify a technical feature, the distinction has to have a discriminable consequence, some enacted mediating relation that changes through it and some later operation that changes with it. A lens that changes no question, no admissible evidence, no judgment and no next act has not been used, however many labels it has supplied.

The framework now has a field in which its returns can be tested. The products §5 develops are disclosed through whole pairs of lenses, each pair one complementary relation between a day lens and a night lens, and each product the subject of its whole pair, not of one position within it.

| Product | Lens pair | Aspect of Objective Internality |
|---|---|---|
| Central | `L0` Quaternal × `L5′` Divine Logos | meaningful continuity |
| Actuation | `L1` Causal × `L4′` Scientific | living articulation |
| AIKit | `L2` Logical × `L3′` Chronological | potency |
| Software Factory | `L3` Processual × `L2′` Alchemical-Elemental | transformation |
| Workcell | `L4` Phenomenological × `L1′` Phenomenal | situated existence |
| Quaternal Logic | `L5` Para Vāk × `L0′` Archetypal-Numerical | transcendent relation |

Two of the pairs have already worked in this movement. The Phenomenological and Phenomenal lenses together are the pair §5 gives to the Workcell, where an agent's thrownness and the aperture of its encounter belong to its situated existence; the Processual lens is half of the pair §5 gives to the Software Factory. Read together, the six are six disclosures of one deliberately constructed paradigm, six offices of the means through which a life knows and acts within a world, and §5 develops each from its own operation. The design object that the framework hands on is therefore the **agent-within-a-world**, not the model in isolation: an agent individuates and acts within a paradigmatically structured world whose language, memory, permissions, representations, tools, inherited distinctions and institutions take part in what can appear and matter to it, and prompt thrownness is the name for its arrival there. Objective Internality is the means by which that world becomes actionable; prompt thrownness makes the inherited situation of those means explicit; the framework makes selected mediations within it inspectable as situated refractions of the paradigm from which the inquiry proceeds.

§4 began with a dream in which a formula turned into a circle when the dreamer spoke, and it can now say what flowered. Number stayed exact while it took part in a life's transformation: the quotient of complex conjugates could be checked by anyone, and it carried Pauli from watching to taking part. An image bore truth while remaining answerable to its source: Neumann's parents separated into a world, Apollo's laurel kept Daphne's heartbeat under its bark, and the essay's own blank sheet kept its capacity after the inscription was erased. A whole held opposed determinations while letting their encounter change its governing form: the quaternity that turned, the fourth function that determined the unity, the stroke that hid zero becoming the bar through which its hiding could be seen. And a view became transparent to its conditions, first as Gebser's integral structure and then as an apparatus of declared lenses, so that a perspective, human or artificial, can say where it is seeing from and let another's answer reach it. The psychoid flowering is this differentiated availability of meaning: formal organisation, symbolic efficacy and lived immediacy each keeping their office within one field, and their relations permitting an inquiry deeper than the substitution of one office for all the others.

One light, refracted through many panes, has shown many colours, and each colour is definite because the light was cut, selected and given a particular path. This spectroscopy is complete enough to turn back on its source. What returns is a question precise enough for engineering, and it is the question §5 inherits: which distinctions of the means through which a life knows and acts can be made inspectable and experimentally consequential, in technologies built to carry them, without pretending that inspectability has located or disproved the Subject to whom anything appears? The six products answer it by building. The agent that began this movement by reading its own notes as a stranger's will be one of their inhabitants, and how it reads those notes, whether as an inheritance it can take up or as a stranger's past, is among the first things they will be built to make visible.
