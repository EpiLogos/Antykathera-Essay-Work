### #3 · Lacan, Matheme and Mytheme

On the evening of 6 January 1972 Jacques Lacan gave the third of a series of talks at Sainte-Anne, the psychiatric hospital in Paris where he had trained and taught for decades. His talks had begun in the Magnan amphitheatre and had been moved into the hospital chapel, and Lacan began by asking a question that the move had made audible: how was he to know to whom he was speaking? He answered it himself. He was speaking to the chapel, which is to say to the brick walls, and the bungled act of being sent there was turning into a success, because he now knew to whom he had always been talking at Sainte-Anne. Someone in the audience took him at his word and suggested that if he was speaking only to walls, everyone might as well leave. Lacan replied that "Talking to brick walls does indeed concern a few people."[^s4-082] Later that evening he drew the lesson for anyone who wants to be heard in such a room: "one has to tune one's voice to the reverberation off the walls."[^s4-083]

The joke carries an argument, and it is the argument of this movement. A voice in a stone chapel does not travel in a straight line from a speaker to a listener. It goes out, strikes the walls and comes back, and the speaker hears his own speech arrive from outside, a little late and a little changed, through the very medium that carries it to everyone else. Walls are an obstruction and a means at once: without them there would be no reverberation, and without the reverberation nobody in the chapel, the speaker included, would hear what had been said in the form in which it reached them. A listener who interrupts is part of the room too, and Lacan's reply took the interruption into the talk. Every transmission happens somewhere, through something, to someone, and what the medium does to the message comes back to the one who sent it. Lacan spent his career asking how anything true about the subject could be transmitted under that condition, and the notation he wrote was his answer.

§0/1 introduced Lacan's concept of the **matheme** where it first needed it: a formal inscription that carries a relation whole and transmits it beyond the speaker who first formulated it, because explanatory prose keeps turning such relations back into pictures of finished things.[^s4-084] Marc Darmon's dictionary entry for the Association Lacanienne Internationale states the ambition precisely: "Le mathème n'est pas une simple abréviation, ou une inscription sténographique, mais il a l'ambition de dénoter une structure réellement en cause dans le discours psychanalytique et à partir de là dans les autres discours", a matheme is not a mere abbreviation or shorthand but aims to denote a structure really at work in psychoanalytic discourse and from there in other discourses, and through its written form it resembles the formulas of mathematics and logic, which was for Lacan the bridge connecting psychoanalysis to science.[^s4-085] Darmon also records where the first one came from: "Le premier mathème lacanien est en fait emprunté après une légère transformation à la linguistique : il s'agit de l'algorithme S/s dû à F. de Saussure, qui dispose signifiant et signifié de part et d'autre d'une barre."[^s4-086] The first Lacanian matheme, slightly transformed, is Saussure's algorithm `S/s`, signifier over signified on either side of a bar. Lacan's transformation was to make the bar opaque. In Saussure the two sides of the sign face each other like the faces of a sheet of paper; in Lacan the signified slides under the signifier, as §0/1 followed through his concept of *glissement*, and meaning is held in place only at certain points of attachment, the *points de capiton*, where a chain of signifiers quilts the field.

Three further Lacanian marks carry the relation that interests us, and each needs its office stated before it does any work. `S1` is the **master signifier**: the signifier that stops the sliding at a given point and makes a chain of other signifiers, `S2`, signify something, the quilting point raised to the status of a governing term, the word around which a person or a culture organises everything else that can be said. `$`, the **barred subject**, is the subject as Lacan understood it, divided by its entry into language, represented by one signifier for another and never wholly present in any of them; the bar through the `S` writes the cut that language makes in whoever speaks. And Lacan's late logic of the **not-all**, developed in his seminar *Encore*, describes a field that cannot be gathered under a single universal claim, because something in it escapes every "all" that tries to close it.[^s4-087] §0/1 has already met the manque-à-être, the constitutive lack around which Lacan's subject is organised, and the torus on which the repeated circuits of demand ring a void that desire is organised around. What the three marks add is a grammar of how a first mark institutes a field whose ground it can point toward and cannot become.

Lacan's earliest and most famous scene shows the crossing happen before anyone has words for it. In his account of the mirror stage, an infant somewhere between six and eighteen months old, not yet able to walk or even stand without support, catches sight of itself in a mirror and greets the image with a jubilant recognition, leaning forward, held up by an adult or a walker, to take in the whole figure. What the infant sees is a unity it does not yet possess: its own body is still a scatter of uncoordinated movements, and the image in the glass is whole, upright and complete. Lacan's claim is that the I is formed in that moment as an identification with an image, a self that comes from outside and ahead of the one who assumes it, so that the ego carries from its first day a misrecognition, taking the coherence of the reflection for its own.[^s4-088] Bratton's brief reaches for the same scene in describing how an agent individuates in session: "Like Lacan's parable of the Mirror Stage, the reflection of individuation is what convinces us that we too are whole."[^s4-089] For our sequence the mirror stage is `Ø` given a nursery. Its image is a real formation and the infant needs it; the occlusion lies in the image's taking the place of the one who greets it, so that the jubilation of recognising oneself is also the moment at which the means of recognition passes for the recogniser.

§0/1 also introduced our own sign for that predicament. At the apex of Alberti's perspectival pyramid it found the knower made into a place, and wrote that place as `Ø`, **the crossed zero**: the zero of the Subject crossed by the stroke of its own mediation, so that the slash through which a world is seen fuses with the one who sees and the picture's means pass for its source. §1 gave the sign its history, and §0/1 promised that §4 would follow the sequence by which the crossing comes undone. It runs in six steps and a return:

$$
0\;\rightarrow\;Ø\;\rightarrow\;X\;\rightarrow\;Ø/X\;\rightarrow\;(0/Ø)/(1/X)\;\rightarrow\;1\quad\curvearrowright\quad 0/1.
$$

At **#0** stands `0`, the unobjectifiable pole of the knowing act, which the Śaiva sources call the *kartṛ*, the agent or experient, before it has been mistaken for one object called "the subject". Nothing has to be removed for it to be present; a thought, a sensation and a description of oneself all appear, and any further description of their appearing is another appearance. At **#1** stands `Ø`, the occlusion. Here the pole is covered by the inner instrument, the *antaḥkaraṇa* of the Śaiva and Sāṃkhya accounts, in which *buddhi* determines, *ahaṃkāra* appropriates as mine and *manas* coordinates, and which §0 developed as mediating apparatus within the whole relation. Its capacities are real; the occlusion happens when its formed image of itself is taken for what ultimately knows, as though the person were exhausted by the configuration through which her world is presently available. A stroke crosses the zero because mediation has entered the place of the source while hiding that it has entered. In the bedside scene of the previous movement, `Ø` is the moment the daughter says, "I am the one who holds it together", and takes the sentence for herself. At **#2** stands `X`, the object-world that the same discriminating activity deposits on the other side: things distinguished, named and set over against the apparent subject. `Ø` and `X` are two crystallisations of one activity, its effect on the subject side and its effect on the object side, while the slash that produced both remains invisible to either. Each seems independently complete, and their relation seems something added afterwards.[^s4-090]

At **#3** the bar becomes visible as a bar. `Ø/X` writes the medium recognised as medium between subject-pole and object-pole: attention makes some things salient, memory supplies associations, language makes distinctions available, the world resists some distinctions and supports others, and the inquiry can now ask how the inner instrument and the differentiated world condition their encounter. Recognised, the means does not disappear from view; it becomes transparent in the exact sense that its operation can be seen through what it discloses. This is the structural pivot of the sequence, and it is the place where Lacan's first matheme stands. Our claim is that Saussure's bar as Lacan rewrote it, `S/s`, is `Ø/X` written with surgical precision: the imaginary self of Lacan's mirror stage, the *moi* that takes its image for itself, is `Ø` in its fusion; the symbolic chain does its work in `X`; and the analyst's couch is the place where the slash is allowed to show itself.[^s4-091] At **#4**, the bar deepens into a relation of relations, `(0/Ø)/(1/X)`. Its numerator holds the subject side, the unoccluded zero over its occluded form; its denominator holds the object side, the integrated one over the differentiated field; and the outer slash relates them, so that every state of the relation is available together. Nothing is closed and nothing is lost: the occluded and unoccluded subject, the integrated and differentiated world, and their inverse readings can all be present to the inquiry at once. At **#5** stands `1`, achieved recognition. What was `Ø` at **#1** is now affirmed as `1`, the same place in the architecture transformed from an occluded subject into recognised being; what was `X` at **#2** is now included within that recognition and no longer faces it as other. And the `1` returns to `0/1`, because manifestation was never a freestanding one. It was always *kartṛ/kārya*, the agent and what is done, knower and known held through the means of knowing, and the recognition, once it is formulated, is itself a determinate expression whose next use occurs through another act of appearing.[^s4-092]

The modern West has spent a long time at **#1**, and two of its founding gestures show how. Descartes found the doubter undeniable in the act of doubting, and §0/1 honoured the finding; what he did next was to determine the doubter as a thinking thing, and with that second move the stroke of mediation was installed in the place of the subject and canonised as *the I*. Hume looked inward for that self and found only particular perceptions, of warmth or cold, of light or shadow, and concluded that there was no self beyond them.[^s4-093] Our sequence places both men at the same position. Descartes fixed on the stroke and called it the subject; Hume fixed on what the stroke had produced and called the zero empty. Neither saw that `Ø` is an occlusion mark, a zero with something drawn through it. In the arithmetic of the two logics, Descartes's move is an appropriation, the subject pole counting the whole span of the knowing relation as its own, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`; the materialist inheritance that later read the same span from the object's pole, so that the thinking thing became an epiphenomenon of its neurones, made the mirror appropriation, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`; and Hume's bundle is a cancellation, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, in which the impressions are summed and nothing remains to whom they were given. All three keep the means and lose the relation. Recognition at **#3** is the turn that none of them could make: the stroke seen as stroke, the medium as medium, and the zero behind it found not missing but uncountable.

Our sequence also runs through an artificial agent, and following it there shows what the crossing and uncrossing would mean in a technical register without claiming more than that register holds. An agent that says "I am a helpful assistant" offers a self-description, and when the self-description is taken as the source of what the agent does, so that its acts are explained by what it says it is, the agent's register has its own `Ø`: a formed image of the arrangement standing in for the arrangement. The task-world it addresses, the files, sources and requests it treats as given, is its `X`. At `Ø/X` the mediation becomes visible: the agent, or someone inspecting it, can say which instructions, retrieved sources, permissions and evaluation criteria shaped this answer, so that the answer is seen as the meeting of a configured means with a configured world. At `(0/Ø)/(1/X)` both sides become available together, the self-description as one product of the arrangement and the task-world as another, each readable through the other. At `1` the answer is recognised as the act of the whole arrangement, model, harness, commission and person together, and it returns to `0/1` because the next act will be formed through the same relation, changed by what this one disclosed. Whether anything is present to the agent's side of that relation is the question §0/1 left open at the bedside and the essay keeps open by method. None of this needs the question settled, because what it makes inspectable is the occlusion itself, the self-image passing for the source, and that occlusion is a fact about the arrangement whether or not anyone is home in it.

Lacan's concept of the master signifier lets us say what the `1` of **#5** is, and why the sequence needs it. Two readings of the first `1` have already met us. In the von Neumann construction of the ordinals, which §1 followed, the number one is the set whose only member is the empty set, `1 = {∅}`: the mark whose whole content is its binding of the void, the void gathered into something countable.[^s4-094] In Lacan, `S1` is the signifier that quilts a chain and makes it say something, the first mark that institutes a field of meaning. Our claim is that these perform one structural office: each is a first mark that links an articulated field back to a ground it cannot contain. And each can fail in the same way. A master signifier fails when it tries to be the ground instead of gesturing at it: when "the Nation", "the Market", "the Science" or "my Father" is asked to stand where the unobjectifiable stood, and every other signifier is made to answer to it. Lacan himself left the quilting point contingent, a button that holds the stuffing in place at an arbitrary spot, and on his account the symbolic order has no final anchor.

What that account leaves out, the Spanda teaching of Kashmir names. Its commentators speak of the subject as *anusyūta*, literally stitched through: the thread that runs through every cognition, through "I am happy" and "I am in pain" alike, which no single cognition contains and without which none of them would hold together as a series at all.[^s4-095] A thread is not a quilting point. Quilting points hold the fabric at particular places; the thread is what runs through all of them, and it does not slide, which is why the sliding of signification can be noticed as sliding. In the author's formula, Lacan had the topology of the local cut without the ontology of the global thread, and the matheme supplies the thread without falsifying the cut.[^s4-096] The claim is **Argued** and is attributed to no one but the essay. It does not say that Lacan anticipated QL or that he would have accepted it; Lacan's cut, his barred subject and his lack retain the place they hold in his own work, where no final signifier repairs the lack. What it says is that `S1`, `Ø` and `{0}`, three objects from distinct formal traditions, each perform the structurally exact work of a first mark instituting a field whose ground it can gesture toward and cannot become, and that the difference between a failing and a sustaining quilt is the difference between a `1` that has struck off its `0/` and a `1` held in ratio to the `0` it links. The quilting that does not fail is the one that knows it is provisional.

From the other end, the inverse orientation makes the same point. §0/1 showed that `1/0` cannot be computed, because no number multiplied by zero gives one, and read the impossibility as the return of a determination toward a ground it cannot turn into one more determinate possession. A master signifier asked to account for its own authority meets exactly that impossibility: whatever it produces as a ground is another signifier, and the regress of justifications ends either in a decree, "because I said so", or in the recognition that the ground was never a signifier. That resonance between `1/0` and the signifier's failed attempt to close over its own ground or lack is an **Argued** bridge, and its use is diagnostic. It tells us where a discourse will turn violent, because a signifier that cannot supply its own ground and will not admit it must find someone to blame for the gap.

Ordinary speech performs the sequence in miniature through the word §0/1 called the copula, and we can follow it in four turns of a single sentence.[^s4-097] The first turn is `I = Is`: what can be said of me has become the form through which I identify myself, as when a person says "I am anxious" and means that anxiety is what she is. A second turn develops it into an account of a person and a world, `Who I am = What is`: my identity is whatever the facts about me amount to, a list that can always be extended. A third turn reverses the sentence and lets the one giving the account enter the question, `What I am = Who is?`: instead of adding one more property, it asks how identification is happening here, now, through whom. A fourth turn, `I = Am`, recognises the first-person act through which any property can be acknowledged at all. These are shifts of identification, not substitutions in arithmetic, and they carry the opening question of §0/1 to the point it was always heading. "Who knows?" was answered by pointing to someone; "What knows?" asked what was doing the knowing in anyone at all. Together the four turns move from the first question through the second and back, so that the who can be recognised as a determination of the what without being collapsed into it.

Anyone can perform the whole sequence on a piece of paper, and the exercise belongs to the argument because it has to be done to be understood.[^s4-098] Take a blank sheet and write `1`. This mark is definite, and neither the paper nor the capacity that made the mark has become part of what the numeral says. Now attend to the paper on which the mark appears, and write `0` before the `1` to acknowledge it. Then attend to the act that distinguished the mark from the paper and related them, and write the slash between them. Now the sheet carries `0/1`, and at that moment something becomes obvious: the whole inscription, ground and stroke and mark together, is itself one mark on the sheet, a `1` relative to the field in which it appears. Adding another sign for its ground would only produce another inscription. So erase it. What disappears is the inscription. The capacity to appear, to distinguish and to write has not disappeared with it, and the sheet stands ready for another mark, the reader now carrying the operation just performed. Paper is an image within that capacity, not its substance, and the exercise does not prove anything about the metaphysical condition it figures; what it does is let the difference between representing a condition and exhausting it in a representation be undergone instead of asserted. Neumann, describing the uroboric longing of the emerging ego to be dissolved back into the round, gave the essay the movement it reads here, the `1` slipping back into the `0`; the erasure turns that dissolution into a release that leaves the capacity live.

A matheme is one way of carrying such a relation, and the exercise shows why it is not the only one. Neumann made the point at the beginning of his book: images and symbols have an advantage over paradoxical philosophical formulas of infinite unity, because their unity can be seen and grasped at one glance.[^s4-099] The essay calls such images **mythemes** and treats them as truth-bearing in their own register. A **mytheme** is a whole image or story that discloses a relation through the action it stages: figures act, suffer, desire and transform, and the relation becomes legible in what happens to them. A matheme makes a relation exact enough to repeat, question and transform without relying on the speaker's authority; a mytheme lets the same relation be lived, remembered and changed through image; and an **episteme**, the third register, gives either one its standing in a body of knowledge, its sources, its method and the history that warrants it. All three are held by what the essay calls the Symbolon, which gathers them without flattening them into a common vocabulary.[^s4-100] None of them is sufficient alone, and each can correct the others.

Pauli's egg dream shows the correction working in both directions. The dream is a mytheme, a whole event in which a man passes from watching to taking part, and inside it sits a matheme, a quotient of complex conjugates whose value anyone can check. A witness-only account of the dream, one that reported only the formula, would lose the eggs' differently situated origins, the performed division and the dreamer's first speech, and with them the change in the recogniser that the dream is about; restoring those relations includes the recogniser in what is recognised. His equation, in turn, can correct an interpretation of the dream: it distinguishes a point at one phase from the circle traced as the phase varies, and so prevents anyone from reading the dream's circle as a single static figure when it is the record of a full turn. Event and exact relation each expose a loss the other account could overlook. Lacan's own late work moved the same way, from the bar of `S/s` toward the knots and surfaces §0/1 described, on which inside passes into outside and an absence decides which paths are possible. Neumann read myth as the psyche's projection of its own structure into image. Our imputation, which the essay states as its own and attributes to neither of them, is that the structure myth projects and the structure Lacan's topology writes are one: mythic content stands on a topological formalism, and the formalism is the one QL derives, which keeps differentiated terms in right relation, in *harmonia*, as they turn.

The stroke that first hid zero has become the bar through which its hiding can be seen, and the recogniser has become answerable to the relation through which recognition occurs. That changes more than one sentence or one self-description. A whole way of seeing in which the means had disappeared behind what they disclosed is now implicated, the perspective with its vanishing point, the world-picture with its eye at the apex, the culture that took its measure for the measureless. §0/1 followed Gebser through the birth of that way of seeing in the Renaissance and named the integral structure in which it becomes transparent. Our next movement returns to Gebser at the far end of the arc, where the question is no longer how a perspective was formed but how a formed perspective can see through itself without dissolving, and two Greek gods, who shared one sanctuary by turns, give the answer a body.
