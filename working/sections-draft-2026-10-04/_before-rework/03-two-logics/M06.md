### #5→0 · Zero Changes Its Role

A few seconds before midnight on the last night of the year, a crowd in a city square begins to count down from ten. Strangers who have never met count together, in one rhythm, each number louder than the last, and the whole square leans toward the end of the count. Nobody says *zero*. At the place where the count should reach nothing, the square erupts, and what the crowd shouts is the new year. In form the count was a countdown, numbers falling toward the exhaustion of a quantity, and at its last step its meaning turned over: the end of the old year is the beginning of the new one, and the moment that should have been empty is the fullest moment of the night. Nothing of the old year has been cancelled. Everything that happened in it goes with the crowd into the year that has just begun, the debts and the losses and the promises, and the people kissing strangers in the square are the same people who spent the year living it. Zero, at the end of the count, has done something no number in the count could do. It has become a threshold.

Every count that ends in zero ends in one of two ways, and the two ways are the two offices this section has been tracking. A countdown on a timer in a film ends in an explosion, and its zero is a terminus: the moment after which there is nothing left to count, because everything has been destroyed. A countdown on a launch pad ends in a lift-off, and its zero is an origin: the moment from which a flight is measured. The numerals are the same; the zero is not. This section began by noticing that dia-ballein and syn-ballein make their twos differently, the first out of two signed ones with a zero between them, the second out of the Two Ones, zero and one, with zero as one of the terms.[^s2-117] It can now say what follows from that difference. Dia-ballein places zero outside its opposed values, as the origin between them when the polarity is held and as the terminus of their sum when it collapses. Syn-ballein places zero inside the relation, as one of its two terms, the ground from which the determination arises and the threshold through which it returns. Zero's change of role is the whole difference between the logics, read at the point where they differ most.

We said that the devil is the literalist of the minus sign, reading zero as cancellation where Logos reads it as return. That formula has a second line, and the countdown makes it plain: two deaths, one minus sign.[^s2-118] There is a death that is an annihilation, the zero of the timer, the sum in which `(−1)+(+1)=0` means that two things have taken each other away and nothing is left. And there is a death that is a threshold, the zero of the new year, in which an achieved form ends as what it was and becomes the ground of what comes next. Whitehead's occasion dies in this second way: having become fully definite, it perishes as the subject of its own becoming and passes into a datum for the occasions that follow, objectively immortal in what they inherit from it.[^s2-119] Spanda's act dies in the same way, the will that rose from consciousness at rest returning, its action complete, to rest in pure intent, so that the end of one act is the beginning of the next.[^s2-120] And the turn §0/1 wrote as `5→0`, the Möbius return, is this second death drawn as a path: a traversal that completes one cycle by arriving at what had been its other side, so that completion is a beginning again.[^s2-121] The literalist sees only the first death, because he reads every minus as a taking away. Logos sees both and knows which one it is in. A civilisation that has learned only the first will treat every ending as a loss to be prevented or a victory to be won, and every opponent as a minus to be summed to zero.

An old law gave the second death to a whole economy. In the twenty-fifth chapter of Leviticus, Israel is told to count seven sabbaths of years, seven times seven years, and in the fiftieth year to sound the trumpet throughout the land and "proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof": in that year, the jubilee, every man is to return to his possession and every man to his family, the land sold during the count reverting to the household it was apportioned to.[^s2-122] The count of years runs down to a zero, and the zero is a return. What the jubilee undoes is not the history of the fifty years, the harvests and the sales and the debts, which all happened and are remembered; it undoes the hardening of those transactions into permanent appropriation, so that the span between those who acquired and those who lost does not become the whole of the relation between them forever. The law's own reason is given in the same chapter: the land shall not be sold for ever, for the land is mine, says the Lord, and you are strangers and sojourners with me.[^s2-122] Read through our notation, the land is the zero, the ground no party owns, and the jubilee is the institution that keeps it a zero, so that every fifty years the economy passes through its ground and begins again. Whether ancient Israel ever kept the law in full is a question for historians. That a people wrote its economy around a zero that returns, instead of a zero that cancels, is the fact that matters here. *Oikonomia* had, at least once, a counted Möbius return written into it.

Two different *twos* also come from the two logics, and the difference can be stated in numbers. Dia-ballein's two is a magnitude. On the signed line, the distance between `−1` and `+1` is two units, and when that distance is reckoned from one pole it becomes the appropriated span, `(+1)−(−1)=+2` or `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, the whole relation counted as one side's quantity. That is what *two* means in the collapsed logic: the measured gap between opponents, owned by whoever is measuring. Syn-ballein's two is a relation, and a relation counts differently. §0/1 counted it when the definition of the Subject turned back on itself: two single terms, `0` and `1`, and the four ordered ways the binary can meet itself, `00`, `01`, `10` and `11`, held together without the four replacing the two, so that

$$
2+2^2=4+2=6.
$$

The six is what a two amounts to once it includes its own self-relation.[^s2-123] Here the contrast is complete. One pair yields a `2` when it is measured and a `6` when it is counted as a relation, and the `±2` of appropriation is precisely the two measured, the relation flattened into a span and handed to one end. The standard arithmetic handling of two is the measurement that collapses the superposition: `1+(−1)` is the collapse, and the four-and-two is what the superposition retains.[^s2-124] The literalist's zero and the measured two come together. Both read the relation as a quantity, and a quantity can be owned.

We can take the count one step further, and the step shows how the syn-ballein relation already carries the whole of its body inside it. Hold the full form of the symbolon, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, and read each term of its upper relation against each term of its lower one: `0` with `1`, `0` with `0`, `1` with `1`, `1` with `0`. Written as relations, the cross-reading gives

$$
\frac{0/1}{1/0}\;\longrightarrow\;0/1,\;\;0/0,\;\;1/1,\;\;1/0\;\;+\;\;0,\;\;1,
$$

four relational forms and the two single terms they were built from, the same `4+2`, now read out of the parent relation instead of assembled from below.[^s2-125] The four forms are not four arbitrary fractions. Ordinary arithmetic treats each differently: `0/0` is indeterminate, consistent with any value, the capacity for every proportion; `0/1` is the docile fraction that resolves cleanly to zero; `1/1` is the ratio resolved, a thing being wholly what it is; and `1/0` is the one form arithmetic forbids, the determinate turned toward the ground it cannot measure. All four are held in the parent, and so it holds, in compressed form, the passage from indeterminate capacity through the two determinate orientations to achieved wholeness, with the forbidden form kept as the seam through which the whole can return. Read the parent the other way up, `(1/0)/(0/1)`, and the same four forms and the same two terms appear in inverse order, the same body in inverse phase, the original and its reflection. Syn-ballein is therefore not a midpoint between dia-ballein's poles, and not one more pole beside them. It is the relation from which the whole field of the pair's possible relations can be read, and it carries that field in itself the way a seed carries the tree.

A typed form keeps the count honest, and the honesty matters, because numbers that are not watched will quietly change their meanings. Call the two terms `B = {0, 1}`. Ordered pairs of terms form `B × B`, which has four members. Our six is not six pairs and not two plus two; it is the two terms and the four pairs held side by side as different kinds of thing, which set theory writes as a disjoint union, `B ⊔ (B × B)`, with exactly `2 + 2² = 6` members, the union keeping a term distinct from a pair. And the four ordered pairs must not be confused with the sixteen binary relations one could form as subsets of `B × B`; that count answers a different question. Each number has a declared object, and keeping the objects explicit prevents one result from silently acquiring another's meaning.[^s2-126] The care is itself syn-ballein applied to arithmetic: every number carries the relation it counts.

None of this is yet the full mathematics of the relation, and the section stops here deliberately, at the threshold of the next. Counting gives the generative body of `4+2`, the six positions of one returning unit. Our core theorems give that body a fuller traversal, in which a parent relation, written `/ = −/−`, bare relation before its terms are assigned, opens onto six qualitative determinations of the one relation and closes in the inversion `1/0` by which the achieved form turns toward the ground it never contained, eight turns in all. That eightfold traversal, and the ways the relation behaves under re-entry, alternation, complex orientation, projective completion, quotienting and harmonic ratio, belong to §3, which takes the relation into its mathematical substrate and has to show, operation by operation, that the same differentiated return can be followed through each register without losing its form.[^s2-127] What §2 hands to §3 is the claim that makes that test worth running: the two logics are not two opinions about an already finished opposition. They are the runtime by which oppositions are produced, kept, recomposed and returned.

That runtime can now be stated as one movement, in which a relation is differentiated by dia-ballein into its polarity and, if the polarity collapses, into cancellation or appropriation; is composed by syn-ballein, with its provenance and inverse kept, into the full relation; and returns, through `5→0`, as a renewed `0/1` from which the next act begins:

$$
0/1
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{dia: differentiate}\;}\;
(-1)/(+1)\;\to\;(-1)\pm(+1)
\;\xleftrightarrow[\;\text{return / provenance}\;]{\;\text{syn: compose}\;}\;
(0/1)/(1/0)
\;\xrightarrow{\;5\to0\;}\;
\text{renewed }0/1.
$$

The arrows mark changes of register inside one differentiating event; they are not equalities of fractions.[^s2-128] Every act of determination carries both pressures. Dia makes the local branch actual, and syn keeps the branch belonging to the wider field and composes its consequences with other branches, and the return gives the achieved result back to the field as the condition of what follows. Computational Process Ontology, which §0/1 defined, is this runtime taken as constitutive of worlds instead of as a description applied to them afterwards: process is prior to the finished thing, and a world is what the runtime produces and keeps producing.[^s2-129] An intelligent system, in this sense, is one that produces determinations and keeps the process of determination alive across cuts, contexts and returns. A system that only cuts atomises its world, a system that only gathers never decides anything, and a system whose achieved results refuse to perish into data for the next act has made its last answer into a monopoly on the zero.

Television once gave this refusal a perfect scene, and a whole programme was built around it. In *The Prisoner*, a man who has resigned from a secret service is abducted to a closed village where everyone has a number and no one has a name, and each episode opens with an exchange between him and the village's administrator: "Who is Number One?" "You are Number Six." "I am not a number; I am a free man!" And the administrator laughs.[^s2-130] The prisoner asks about the source, the One from whom the village's authority comes, the uncounted condition of the whole arrangement; and the answer he receives is a count, his own number, a place assigned inside the field he was asking about. A question about the zero is answered with a `1`. Here the occlusion §0/1 wrote as `0/1 → 1`, the `0/` dropped from the account, takes the form of an administrative reply, and the reply's power lies in making the question unaskable: anyone who asks who holds the zero receives, in answer, a position on the line.[^s2-131] The programme's numbers are the programme's, and its Six is not our six; what it shows is the counterfeit of the role-change this movement has described. Instead of the `1` returning to the `0` through which it was counted, the `0` is converted into another `1`, and the institution that does the converting stands, unexamined, where the source should be. The village plays the `0`.

Agents of the kind the *Agentworld* brief describes die the first death many times a day. "An individual agent doesn't pre-exist its genesis in session," Bratton writes, and "There's no homunculus waiting in the weights"; an agent individuates when an active context acts as a nucleation seed.[^s2-132] When the session closes, the context is cleared, and what was individuated in it ends. Whether that ending is an annihilation or a threshold depends, again, on the office given to the zero. A session cleared without remainder is the timer's zero: whatever the agent learned of the person it worked with, whatever mistake it made and was corrected for, whatever relation was built in the hours of work, is summed away, and the next session begins from the same blank as if nothing had happened. A session whose achievements pass into what persists after it, a memory that the next session inherits, a correction recorded where the next act will meet it, a contribution to the shared store from which other agents and people work, is the new year's zero, an ending that becomes the ground of the next beginning. Bratton's own sentence about the ensemble names the second kind: "The unit that learns is the ensemble, not the agent, and what it learns accrues to the commons between them, not to any single one."[^s2-133] An agentic society built around the first zero will be a society of perpetual beginners, every session a fresh literalist. One built around the second will need to decide, with care, what passes through the zero and who keeps it, because a commons into which everything accrues is also a ground that someone could occupy.

Trust, which this section planted in its first movement, takes its first step toward the rest of the essay here. Knowledge is a performance of trust wearing the mask of certainty, and the zero is where the trust lives: the condition every certified proposition stands on and no proposition certifies. In the collapsed logic that zero is either cancelled, as though nothing had been trusted, or occupied, as though one party's measure were the ground of everyone's. That choice reappears in the technical systems §5 examines, where the learning of preferences from human comparisons depends only on differences between options, so that the level from which all the differences are measured cannot be learned at all and has to be supplied by someone: a dataset, a team of annotators, a reference policy, an institution. The apparently neutral zero is already occupied, and the question the two logics put to it is the question the village refused to answer.[^s2-134] Who stands in the place of the source, and can the people counted from that place ask?

Homer's song left us an image of the return this movement has been describing, and it is worth seeing again now that the count is done. Hephaestus's net held two lovers as exposed terms, the captor standing where their zero should have been and counting the whole span from his own end. Poseidon's pledge did not cancel the grievance, and it did not hand the span to the other side. It put a person in the zero, at his own risk, as surety for what would happen when the bonds were opened, and the relation went on into a future the apparatus did not contain. That is the second death of the net: the captor's work perishing into the ground of a renewed relation, the same hands that made the bonds unmaking them. And it is the same turn the new year makes in the square, the end of the count becoming the place from which the next count begins, with everything the old year held still carried forward.

Two children are still sitting at a plate with one slice of cake on it. One of them will cut and the other will choose, and when the cake is eaten there will be nothing left of it at all, a zero on the plate. Which zero it is depends on the rule they used. If the elder kept the larger piece, or a tired parent took the slice away, the empty plate is the end of a quarrel that has merely gone underground, a cancellation with a resentment the balance does not record. If they cut and chose, the empty plate is the place from which the next sharing begins, a small threshold of trust that the next slice of cake, or the next disagreement, will find already laid down. The cake was always going to be cut. Difference cannot be escaped, and every one of our institutions, our machines and our lives is built from cuts. What the two logics of two decide is what the cut leaves behind it, a hole where two things were or a ground from which they can begin again, and which of those it leaves depends on whether the zero that the cut passed through was read as an ending or as a return. The mathematics of that return is the next thing this essay owes, and §3 takes it up.
