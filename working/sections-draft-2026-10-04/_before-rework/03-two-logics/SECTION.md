## §2 — Two Logics of Two

<a id="M19"></a>

### #0 · Difference Cannot Be Escaped

There is one slice of cake left on the plate and two children who both want it, and somewhere in most families an old rule settles the matter: one of you cuts, the other chooses. The elder takes the knife. She is not in a hurry, and the care she takes is visible, because she knows that whatever she cuts, her brother will choose first and leave her the remainder; every millimetre she shaves off one side to favour herself is a millimetre she will probably lose. So she lines up the knife, looks again, moves it a little, and cuts as close to the middle as her eye and hand can manage. Her brother leans in, inspects both pieces with the gravity of a jeweller, and takes one. Neither child has been made generous by the rule, and neither has been asked to love the other more than before. There are still two pieces, two children, and two offices, the one who divides and the one who chooses, and the rule depends on all of that difference staying in place. What the rule changes is where the cut answers. Because the chooser's choice will come back upon it, the cut is made under the eye of the person who did not make it, and the knife is honest because the relation between the two children has been built into the procedure that divides the cake.

Change one thing and the scene becomes a different event with the same knife. Let the elder cut and also keep whichever piece she likes, and the cut is made from one side only: whatever she does, the span between the larger piece and the smaller is counted from her end. Let a tired parent cut and hand the pieces out without a word, and the cut answers to the parent's judgment, which may be fair and may be weary, but which neither child can return upon. Let the parent take the slice away altogether because there is fighting, and the dispute has been settled by removing what was disputed, so that nothing remains to divide and nothing remains to quarrel over, and the children are left with a balance of zero and an evening's resentment the balance does not record. In each version the cake is cut or withdrawn, a difference is made, and the difference is real. What changes from version to version is whether the act that made the difference can still be answered by the people it divides.

Difference cannot be escaped. A child cannot eat half a cake that no one has cut, a perception cannot register a shape that does not stand out from its ground, a word that could not be told apart from every other word would say nothing, and a refusal without an edge is not yet a refusal. Every act of knowing we have followed since the bedside has been an act of this kind. The adult's "because" drew a line between a good reason and an impertinent question; the name held one child apart from every other; the definition of the Subject failed at the exact place where its distinction had to be made; the formal systems of the twentieth century were built to make every cut explicit, and found at their foundations a cut they could not make. Mind, as this essay has defined it, is differentiating activity: awareness becomes a world by cutting, comparing, excluding, relating and keeping its distinctions. Whether to divide has never been the live question, since nothing appears without division. It is whether a distinction can stay answerable to the ground and the activity that produced it, or whether its finished terms will present themselves as self-sufficient things, each one standing as though nothing had ever cut it out.

We already have the arithmetic for this question. In the second movement of §0/1 the Greek verbs behind *symbolic* and *diabolical* gave us two names for what a distinction can do between people, and the number line gave us their exact operations: the held polarity of dia-ballein, `(−1)/(+1)`, in which each sign has its sense through the zero it shares with the other; its two collapses, cancellation, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, and appropriation, written in both chiralities as `(+1)−(−1)=+2` and `(−1)−(+1)=−2`; and syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, which keeps the shared condition operative so that either orientation can be read through the other.[^s2-1] Those results stand. This section asks what kind of *two* each logic makes, why each is a complete way of accounting for a pair and not a better or worse opinion about it, and what each looks like when it takes on a body, in a household, in a crowd, in a god, in a machine. Already the children at the plate have shown the shape of the answer. Their rule of cutting and choosing is dia-ballein used well: a polarity of two offices held through a shared zero, the cake itself, which both depend on and neither owns. An elder who keeps the larger piece has appropriated the span; the parent who removes the slice has cancelled it. And the rule itself, the old household device that keeps the cut answerable to the one who did not make it, is a small working instance of syn-ballein, a way of keeping two parties different and making their difference the means by which the fairness of the division is proved.

Greek myth tells the first division between gods and human beings as a cut of exactly this kind, and tells it as a con. In the *Theogony* Hesiod places the scene at Mecone, "when the gods and mortal men were being divided", and his verb for that dividing is *ekrinonto*, a form of the *krinein* whose noun, *krisis*, closed §0/1 as the separating that is also a deciding.[^s2-2] Prometheus takes a great ox, cuts it up and sets two portions before Zeus. In one he hides the meat and the rich innards inside the ox's stomach, so that the best of the animal looks like its least appetising part; in the other he lays out the bare white bones and covers them with shining fat, so that the worst looks like the best. Zeus is to choose. Hesiod insists that Zeus saw through the trick and chose the bones anyway, and the poem uses his choice to explain why human beings ever after burned white bones on altars for the gods and kept the meat; then, in anger, Zeus withholds fire, and Prometheus steals it, and the woman made by the gods is sent to men as the price of the theft.[^s2-2] The scene is a cut-and-choose turned inside out. Prometheus has arranged the portions so that the chooser's choice, which in the children's rule kept the cut honest, is captured in advance by the look of the pieces; the relation between the parties has been preserved in form and emptied in substance, and the division that was meant to settle what gods and mortals owe one another becomes the beginning of a long reckoning in which each side counts the span against the other. Mortals have the meat and the gods the savour, and from the gods' side the same division is counted as theft. One side's `+2` is the other's `−2`, and it has been so ever since, at least in Hesiod's telling, where the whole subsequent history of fire, labour and woman follows from a division made in bad faith.

We read this as a mytheme, in the sense §0/1 gave the word: a compressed psychodynamic that proves a relation through the action it stages, and that we unfold back into operations without taking its persons for causes.[^s2-3] The operation it proves is that a division can be performed as a relation and still be an appropriation. A child who keeps the larger piece at least appropriates in plain sight. Prometheus appropriates through the very procedure that was supposed to make appropriation impossible, by working on the appearance through which the chooser chooses, and the result is a division that both parties have, in a sense, agreed to, while the relation it was meant to settle passes into enmity. Here the con we met in §0/1, the confidence trick that wins a trust it means to use, appears at the founding of the relation between the human and the divine.[^s2-4] Nothing in the scene allows us to stop dividing: the ox has to be cut for the sacrifice to happen, and gods and mortals have to become distinguishable for there to be gods and mortals at all. What the scene quarrels with is a particular way of dividing, the way in which the presentation of the portions occupies the place where the chooser's free reply should have been.

Every binary, then, has two provenances, and the two provenances give us the two logics. A pair of terms can be treated as separately given values whose conflict is measured from outside: here is one portion and there is another, here is *guilty* and there is *innocent*, here is the human and there is the machine, and the question is only which is larger, which is true, which will win. Or the same pair can be treated as inverse determinations of one differentiating relation, two portions of one ox, two verdicts on one evening, two forms of intelligence arising within one field of language, and then the question includes the cut that made them two and the field from which they were cut. Dia-ballein accounts for the first provenance. It takes the two as poles of an axis and measures them against each other through the zero between them, and while it keeps the axis it keeps the relation, so that each pole still has its sense through the other. Syn-ballein accounts for the second. It takes the two as the two ends of one relation, the condition and what has become determinate through it, and it holds both orientations together so that the cut stays legible inside what it produced. Neither of these is a mood or a temperament. Each is a complete way of accounting for a pair, and each has its own exactness, its own uses and its own characteristic failure.

Greek prefixes carry the difference, and they are worth hearing slowly. Greek *dia* means *through* and *across*, and, in compounds where a whole is passed through and parted, *apart*; *syn* means *with* and *together*.[^s2-5] Dia-ballein throws across, which is how a cut is made, a line thrown through a field so that it falls apart into two regions; syn-ballein throws together, which is how a token is fitted, the two halves brought back into contact across the line that parted them. We will hear both verbs at work as the section proceeds, in their old senses of accusing and of agreeing, but the prepositions already tell us that the two operations concern one field. A cut is thrown *across* something; a fitting brings together what was *with* each other before it was apart. Neither operation can be performed without the other's object. There is nothing to throw together unless something has been thrown apart, and nothing to throw apart unless there was a field that held it together.

Those prefixes have a second life that can mislead us. *Diachronic* and *synchronic* name, respectively, what is studied across time and what is studied together at one time, succession and co-presence, and it is tempting to hear dia-ballein as the logic of history and syn-ballein as the logic of the simultaneous picture. We should refuse the temptation, because the two pairs cross. Cutting and choosing works in sequence: first the cut, then the choice, and it is the sequence that keeps the cut honest. That is a diachronic procedure performing syn-ballein. A photograph of a table in which every guest has been seated by rank is a single synchronic arrangement, and it can perform dia-ballein in its collapsed form, with the whole span of the room counted from the head of the table. A history can be written as a row of completed periods, each sealed off from the next, or as the continuing life of what an earlier act still does in a later one; a cross-section of a society can be drawn as a field of separate interests or as a web of mutual dependence. Succession and co-presence each can keep their relation or lose it. What decides between the two logics is whether the relation that makes the terms terms is kept active through the operation performed on them.[^s2-6]

One serious philosophical voice has pressed the opposite case, that difference itself cannot be made good. Śrīharṣa, the Advaita philosopher whose *Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya* dismantles one by one the definitions his rivals offered for the categories of knowledge, argued, in Nilanjan Das's account, that "irrespective of how we define distinctness, no awareness—perceptual or otherwise—can present one object as distinct from another, without also presenting them as non-distinct."[^s2-7] To see the pot as other than the cloth, I must hold pot and cloth together in one awareness, and the awareness that holds them together presents them, in that respect, as one. For an Advaitin pressing toward the non-duality of *brahman*, the inseparability of distinction from non-distinction counts against the final reality of difference: if difference cannot appear without its contrary, it cannot be what reality ultimately is. Das records that this reading of Śrīharṣa remains controversial, and we take it as he reports it.[^s2-7] What the argument shows is exact, and we can accept it whole while drawing the opposite conclusion. Every awareness of difference is also an awareness of relation, because a difference can only be presented within the one act that holds its terms together; that is the copula's double work, which §0/1 found in the smallest identity statement, where two inscriptions had to be both distinct and identified for anything to be said.[^s2-8] Śrīharṣa reads the co-presentation as a reason to doubt the distinction. We read it as the reason the distinction is never alone. A pot really is not a cloth, and the awareness in which they are told apart really does hold them as one field, and both of those facts are present in every act of telling apart. Non-duality, on this reading, does not dissolve the distinctions inside the appearing act; it preserves them within the act from which they arise.

That proposition is the ground of the whole section, and it has a direct consequence for the two logics. If every difference is presented together with a relation, then no binary is ever simply given as two finished things. Its terms always arrive with the field in which they were told apart, and the only question is whether an account of them keeps that field in view. Dia-ballein in its held form keeps it as the axis: the zero between `−1` and `+1` is the respect in which they are comparable, the point from which both directions are measured, the thing both children want. Syn-ballein keeps it as one of the terms: in `0/1` the zero is not the space between two ones but one of the two, the condition written alongside what has become determinate within it. Here the two logics part in a way we can state numerically. Dia-ballein makes its two out of two signed ones, `−1` and `+1`, each of them a determinate unit, each a `1` with a direction; its zero lies between them, or comes out as the result when they are added. Syn-ballein makes its two out of the Two Ones that §0/1 distinguished, zero, which bears the singular One of self-identity, and one, which bears the manifest All of self-difference.[^s2-9] In the first accounting zero is never a term. In the second it is always one. How the zero's office changes between the two accountings is the subject of this section's last movement, and everything in between is the history of that change, lived.

Knowledge meets this choice whenever it confronts its own incompleteness, and it confronts its incompleteness at every turn, because, as §0/1 found in the formal-limit genealogy, no sufficiently articulate account can supply the condition of its own articulation from its own resources. I put the situation this way: **knowledge is a performance of trust wearing the mask of certainty**. Before a proposition is certified as known, a whole field has already been trusted enough for the proposition to count: the language in which it is stated, the memory that keeps its steps in order, the method by which it was tested, the witness who reported it, the competence of the person who checked it, the institution that recorded it, and the world that went on being there while the checking was done. The children trust the rule before the cake is cut; neither could prove that it will work out fairly, and both act on it. A scientist trusts her instruments, her colleagues and her own eyes before the result is in, and the result, once in, is a certainty only in respect of the field she has trusted. We gave the four offices of this relation their names when the household's reliance first came into question, trust as the already enacted dependence, knowledge as the aperture through which it becomes intelligible, belief as the orientation steadied within that aperture, and faith as the lucid continuation through the account's limit.[^s2-10] Here the four offices meet the two logics. *Performance* is meant in its full sense: knowledge is an act carried out before others and with them, under conditions it did not set, and its certainty is the mask it wears in order to act at all, since an actor who stopped in the middle of every line to explain that the theatre might collapse would never finish the play. Wearing a mask is no fault. Fault begins when the mask is taken for the face, and the performance forgets that it is a performance of trust.

Here the two logics show their difference in the life of knowledge. What happens to the uncertainty that knowledge cannot eliminate? Dia-ballein in its collapsed form denies it. A knowledge that has taken its mask for its face cannot admit that its certainty rests on a trust it never certified, and so the uncertainty it cannot hold goes somewhere else. It is displaced onto an excluded term, the enemy, the scapegoat, the defective population, the alien intelligence, the other side, each of which is made to carry the incompleteness that belongs to the field itself. The asserted `+1` feels whole because a `−1` has been found to bear what it could not admit. Our next movement follows this displacement through crowds, states and machines. Syn-ballein in its healthy form distributes the uncertainty instead. It keeps the trusted field visible as a field and lets uncertainty rest across many revisable anchors, a skill tested in use, a witness who can be questioned, a relationship that has survived disagreement, a practice that can be corrected, an institution with real recourse, so that no single object has to carry the whole weight and the failure of one anchor does not bring down the possibility of trusting at all. I call this **mono–poly trust**: one living field of reliance, sustained through many anchors none of which is permitted to stand for the whole. It is followed in the movement after next. The two ways in which provisional knowledge handles the trust it cannot eliminate, as a distributed anchor within relation or as distrust projected onto an excluded object, are the two logics of two seen from inside a life that has to go on knowing things.

Agentworld states the same necessity at the scale of a society of agents, and in a sentence that could serve as a definition of the problem this section works on. Coordination among artificial agents, Bratton writes, has to find "the narrow corridor: same decision-procedural grammar, sufficiently divergent world models."[^s2-11] On one side of the corridor lies the collapse of ontologies into one, a homophily of agents trained alike and reasoning alike, whose monoculture is most stable just before it fails all at once; on the other lie ontologies so incompatible that their holders cannot even converge on the shape of their disagreement. Bratton does not nominate a grammar for the corridor. Read through our two logics, its two failures are the two ways of losing a two. A field of agents without enough difference has cancelled its polarity, so that every agent's error is every other agent's error and the zero they share has become a single point of failure. A field of agents without a common grammar has severed it, so that each agent's world-model stands alone and no relation remains through which a disagreement could be measured, let alone settled. Dia-ballein keeps world-models divergent, because it is the logic of real difference that can be measured; syn-ballein keeps the grammar common, because it is the logic of a relation that holds its terms together without making them one. The corridor is exactly where both are operative at once, and that is the claim this essay makes for its own notation in the venue's terms: a shared relational grammar with plural ontological content.

This structure has an older Greek name that will matter when the section reaches its myth. Heraclitus says of what differs that "in differing it agrees with itself," and his verb for that agreeing is *homologeei*, from *homologia*, literally a saying-the-same, a common *logos* found within a difference.[^s2-12] Agreement in this sense is not the absence of difference; it is what a difference has when its parts belong to one account. A comparison between two things, a judgment that they are opposed, a quarrel over which is larger, all presuppose a common respect, and in the act of opposing two terms we have already related them. A quarrel over the cake presupposes the cake, and Prometheus's trick presupposed a sacrifice both sides took part in. That common respect is the zero the two logics treat differently. When it is held, two opposed things can be compared, contested and even fought over without ceasing to belong to one field. When it is lost, they become camps that no longer recognise the common operation that produced them, and their opposition, once a relation, hardens into a fact about each of them.

Here the old charge against the cut has to be answered plainly, because the two logics distinguish operations, not moral camps. Dia-ballein is not the villain of this section. The cut powers comparison, classification, measurement, proof by exclusion, decision and critique, and without it nothing could be refused, no evidence could tell against a claim, no person could say *no* and mean it, and no injustice could be named as injustice, since naming an injustice means distinguishing it from what was owed. A verdict has to be reached, a boundary has to be drawn, a decision has to be made before every uncertainty is settled, and the logic that makes these acts possible is necessary to any life, any science and any society worth having. Dia-ballein's pathology begins at one precise point, when the cut forgets the common operation and the field that produced its poles. When the elder child keeps the larger piece, her knife is not at fault; her keeping is. Syn-ballein is not the hero either. A gathering can erase the differences it gathers and call the result unity, and its failure, fusion, is as real as severance and in some ways harder to see, because it presents itself as love. Our task is to give both logics their full scope, their necessary work and their characteristic failures, without letting either become a slogan.

So the movements that follow take the two logics in turn and then together. Classical accounting comes first, because it is the dominant logic of modern formal and technical systems and because its arithmetic is the one our institutions run on; it gives us the literalist of the minus sign, the consent that division requires, and the crowd. Superpositional accounting comes second, as the retained seam of the symbolon and the trust it was made to carry. Then the two logics receive the bodies §0/1 promised them, War and Love, Ares and Aphrodite, caught together in a net made by the craftsman god, and the myth shows what each logic feels like from inside, and what no apparatus, however complete, can do for a relation. After that the two are read as one pulse, in the dynamics of a psyche and a culture, with Apollo and Dionysus as the safeguard that keeps either from swallowing the other. And at the end zero changes its role, which is what this whole station has been about. Two children at a plate will not need any of this to settle their slice of cake. They have a rule that keeps the cut answerable to the one who did not make it, and the rest of this section asks what it would take for a civilisation's cuts to be made that way.

### #1 · Dia-ballein: Polarity and Its Collapse

Twelve people sit around a table in a room with no windows, and after three days of evidence they have to turn an evening none of them witnessed into one of two words. The foreman has a form with two boxes. Whatever happened in the stairwell on the night in question, whoever pushed or fell or was pushed, whatever the defendant felt and intended and has regretted since, the room must produce *guilty* or *not guilty*, and the law has made sure there is no third box. Some jurors find this unbearable at first. One of them knows that the truth of that evening is more complicated than either word, that the defendant was frightened as well as angry and that the man who fell had been threatening him for months; another keeps asking whether they can say something about the provocation; a third wants to write a note to the judge explaining what they really think. Nothing on the form allows it. And yet, as the hours go on, most of the room comes to see that the form is protecting something. It does not ask them to say what happened. It asks them whether the charge has been proved. *Not guilty* does not mean *innocent*; it means that the case brought against this person has not been made beyond reasonable doubt, and the doubt, which the law has placed on the defendant's side of the axis from the start, is allowed to stay unresolved. The verdict cuts, cleanly and finally, and in cutting it says exactly as much as it can say and no more.

That form is dia-ballein at its most exact and most humane, and it repays attention, because the logic of the jury room is the logic most of our institutions run on. Two values, opposed, exhaustive: every case must fall into one box or the other, and none may fall into both. The presumption of innocence is the zero of the axis, the point from which the case is measured and to which the defendant returns if the measurement fails, and its office is to keep the polarity held. Guilt has to be established *from* that zero, in one direction, against resistance; it does not sit opposite innocence as an equal possibility weighed at the outset. Placing the burden of proof gives the axis a handedness. This is why the verdict can be decisive without claiming to be complete. It decides one question, whether the state has made its case, and it leaves the whole of that evening in the stairwell, the fear and the anger and the months of threats, in the hands of the people who lived it, where no form can reach.

Change how the verdict is read and the same two words perform the two collapses we wrote in §0/1. Read *not guilty* as *nothing happened*, and the verdict has been made into a cancellation: the charge and its refutation are added together and come out at zero, and the evening is closed like a settled account, with the man at the bottom of the stairs and the defendant's fear both summed out of existence. Read *guilty* as *this is what he is*, and the verdict has been made into an appropriation: the whole span of a life is measured from the charge, the defendant becomes his worst evening, and the public's satisfaction at the conviction, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, is the convicted man's whole standing in the world, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, counted from the other end.[^s2-13] Neither misreading is made by the jury. Both are made afterwards, by a newspaper, a neighbour, an employer reading a criminal record years later, and both are made possible by the same thing that makes the verdict useful, its refusal of a third box. Both the exactness of the cut and the danger of its collapse come from one source.

Classical logic is the jury room raised to a law of thought. Aristotle set out its two governing principles in the fourth book of the *Metaphysics*: the same attribute cannot at the same time belong and not belong to the same subject in the same respect, and between the two parts of a contradiction there can be nothing intermediate, so that of any one subject one must either affirm or deny any one predicate.[^s2-14] The first is the principle of non-contradiction and the second the principle of excluded middle, and together they give a proposition exactly two values, true and false, opposed and exhaustive, the two boxes on the foreman's form. Written on our axis, truth and falsity are `+1` and `−1`, negation is the operation that turns one into the other, and the zero between them is the undecided proposition, the question before it has been answered. A great deal of human thought has been made exact by this logic. Every proof by contradiction, every classification that sorts cases without overlap, every switch in a circuit that is either on or off, every legal verdict and medical test and audit relies on it, and the computational world we now inhabit is built from it at the level of the transistor. Dia-ballein in this form is not a philosophical opinion. It is the operating condition of measurement, and nothing in this essay asks anyone to give it up.

What we ask is that its zero be kept. Held polarity in classical logic does keep it, in the same way the presumption of innocence does: the undecided proposition is where every inquiry starts and where it returns when the evidence fails, and a careful reasoner knows that most of the propositions she meets are sitting there, at zero, waiting. Trouble begins when the logic of two exhaustive values is taken to describe the world as well as our verdicts about it, when the boxes on the form are mistaken for the shape of the evening. Then every situation must already be one thing or the other, every person must already be on one side or the other, and the zero, the undecided, the not-yet-known, the question, appears as a defect in the world, a gap to be closed by deciding, instead of as the condition in which every deciding takes place. Aristotle himself guarded the principles carefully: *at the same time*, *in the same respect*. Each qualification is a recognition that a polarity is held within a field, and that the field has dimensions, time and respect among them, along which opposed predicates can both be true of one subject without contradiction. Our friend at the barrier in §0/1, who both was and was not the person we knew, could be described without contradiction once we said in what respect.[^s2-15] Classical logic, used well, is full of such qualifications. Used badly, it drops them, and the axis collapses into its arithmetic.

Whoever drops them has a name, and §0/1 promised that this section would develop the formula that names him: the devil is the literalist of the minus sign, reading zero as cancellation where Logos reads it as return.[^s2-16] The formula can now be unfolded, because the minus sign does more than one job and the literalist is the one who reads every job as the same. In arithmetic the sign `−` marks a direction: `−1` is a position on the line, one step to the left of the origin, as real a place as `+1` and differing from it only in orientation. That sign also names an operation: subtraction, taking one quantity away from another. And in logic the sign of negation, which classical notation often writes as a bar or a hook and which our axis writes as the passage from `+1` to `−1`, turns an assertion into its denial. A literalist reads all three as the second, so that every minus means *take away*. On that reading the sum `(−1)+(+1)=0` says that the two units have taken each other away and nothing is left: zero is a hole where two things were, the remainder after an annihilation. Read the minus as a direction, and the same sum says something else entirely. One step to the left and one step to the right return to the point from which both steps were measured, and the zero they arrive at is the origin, the one place on the line that both directions share and from which each has its sense. Nothing has been annihilated. Two journeys have come home.

Here the formula's two halves meet. *Cancellation* is the literalist's zero, the place where the account comes out even because everything on one side has been taken away by something on the other. *Return* is the Logos's zero, the place from which the account began and through which its terms remain related, so that coming out even means coming back to the shared condition, with both journeys still remembered. The diabolical, in the Greek sense §0/1 recovered, does to persons what the literalist does to numbers.[^s2-17] The *diabolos* is the slanderer, the one who throws something across between two people so that they appear to each other under an accusation, and the operation of slander is exactly a literal reading of a sign. A neighbour stands somewhere relative to me: to my left on some question, below me in some ledger, opposed to me in some quarrel. That is a direction, a relation, a position on an axis we share. Slander reads the sign as a substance: the neighbour *is* the minus; his difference from me is what he is made of, and the axis that gave his position its sense has dropped out of the account. Once that reading takes hold, the only resolution it can imagine is the literalist's: take the minus away, and what remains is zero, the quarrel settled by the other's removal.

Apoha, the Buddhist theory of exclusion that §0/1 met in Dignāga, shows what the literalist misses in the plainest act of naming.[^s2-18] To apprehend something as a cow is to exclude what is other than a cow, so *cow* is articulated as *not non-cow*, and we noticed then that cancelling the two written negatives would return us to the word *cow* and leave the word's work unexplained. Our literalist of the minus sign cancels them. Double negation, in classical logic, is the identity: not-not-p is p, two minuses make a plus, and nothing is lost on the way. In apoha something is kept on the way, the field of what the word excludes, the not-cows against which this animal is recognised as one of those, and keeping it is what lets differing particulars be understood together. The literalist's arithmetic is correct and its philosophy is wrong. `(−1)(−1)=+1` is true; the claim that the passage through the two negations has left no trace in the meaning is false, and a whole theory of language was built to show why. So it is with every institution that classifies. A category made by exclusion carries its excluded field with it, and an account that forgets the excluded field has not become simpler. It has become a literalist.

Dia-ballein's knowledge, when it collapses, denies its own provisionality, and the denial has a direction. We said in the previous movement that knowledge is a performance of trust wearing the mask of certainty, and that the uncertainty a performance cannot admit has to go somewhere.[^s2-19] In the collapsed logic it goes across the axis. The asserted `+1` feels complete because a `−1` has been found to carry what it could not hold, and the uncertainty that belongs to the shared field, to the limits of the evidence, to the trust that was extended before anything was proved, reappears as a property of the excluded term: the enemy is the reason things are unclear, the scapegoat is the reason things went wrong, the alien intelligence is the reason the future cannot be predicted. Projection is the psychological name for this movement, and it works because it relieves. A diffuse unease that belongs to everyone becomes something located over there, which can be watched, denounced, fought or expelled, and the relief is real even when the location is false. An epistemic cut has become a social act. Nobody had to decide to scapegoat anyone; it was enough to take a mask for a face.

*Agentworld* describes this movement with precision in its account of how human beings meet non-human interlocutors. "Reactive alienation," Bratton writes, "converts strangeness into threat and responds with reflexive rejection: the other must be named, registered, bounded, and held at a distance from what counts as properly ours."[^s2-20] Its counterpart, productive alienation, "treats the non-human interlocutor as a mirror whose strangeness forces the human to see itself at an angle it could not otherwise occupy." Read through the two logics, Bratton's two modes are the held polarity and its collapse. Productive alienation keeps the axis: the machine stands at a real distance from us, its strangeness is a direction we could not otherwise take, and the difference becomes a way of seeing ourselves from somewhere else, which is the self-knowledge the bedside mirror promised in §0/1. Reactive alienation literalises the sign: the machine's difference becomes its substance, its strangeness becomes its threat, and the only resolution imaginable is the one the brief records elsewhere, the fantasy that it must be stopped before it stops us. Bratton adds that "both modes are live at once, and different people oscillate between them, moment to moment," and the oscillation itself is instructive. A held polarity oscillates cleanly, each position passing into relation with the other through the stroke, as Spencer-Brown's re-entering distinction alternated in time; a collapsed one swings between `+2` and `−2`, between the conviction that the machine will save us and the conviction that it will destroy us, and never passes through the zero where the question of what it is, and what we are in relation to it, could be asked.[^s2-21]

There is also a computational name for dia-ballein, and it is the most honest name the logic has, because it states the logic's method and its condition in one phrase: divide and conquer. Faced with a problem too large to solve directly, a divide-and-conquer algorithm splits it into smaller subproblems of the same kind, solves each of those, splitting again if necessary until the pieces are trivial, and then combines the solutions into a solution of the whole. Merge sort is the standard textbook example. To put a long list in order, split it in half, sort each half by the same method, and then merge the two sorted halves by walking down both together and always taking the smaller front element. Textbooks name the three steps plainly: divide, conquer, combine.[^s2-22] The power of the method lies in the first two, which make an intractable task tractable by cutting it into pieces that can be handled independently. Its success lies entirely in the third. A merge sort that divided and sorted but never merged would leave a heap of small ordered lists and no ordered list at all, and every step of the dividing would have been wasted, because the pieces were only ever pieces of one problem.

Politics drops the third step and keeps the first two, which is why §0/1 called divide and conquer the oldest and most enduring trick of any state or power.[^s2-23] The method is genuinely powerful in politics as in computation: a population divided into groups that can be addressed, managed and set against one another separately is far easier to govern than one that acts together. But the political version does not merely forget the combine. It keeps it. The combining step, the capacity to bring the divided parts back into one action, is retained by whoever did the dividing, while everyone else remains divided. Workers appear as individual performances, people seeking help as cases, communities as counted preferences, and the channels through which these separated participants could act together run vertically, through the centre that divided them, instead of horizontally, among themselves. That is the arrangement George Carlin's "big club" described and the Dialog directory documented in §0/1: syn-ballein reserved for the members, dia-ballein for everyone outside.[^s2-24] An algorithm that hid its merge step from its own subproblems and then presented the merged result as something only it could have produced would be a strange algorithm, but it would be a very effective form of rule.

§0/1 added a condition to the trick that deserves to be stated as a proposition. Divide and conquer works only while both parties consent to be divided; whichever logic is at play, it takes two to tango.[^s2-25] The consent in question is not a signature on a contract. It is the acceptance, by each of the divided parties, that the axis drawn between them is the whole of their relation. Each side agrees to reckon the span from its own end: each counts the distance between them as the other's departure, the other's fault, the other's debt, so that one side's `+2` is met by the other's `−2`, and the two together, with perfect symmetry, confirm the line on which they stand. Red against blue was this agreement reckoned from both poles at once. Then the divided parties do the divider's work for him, since each needs the other as the negative through which it knows itself, each treats dissent within its own ranks as contamination by the enemy, and each reads the other's hardening as proof that its own must continue. That is what makes the trick durable. Division is maintained from below, by the divided, out of a loyalty to their side that is also, without their noticing, a loyalty to the axis.

Withdrawing that consent does not mean ceasing to differ. A boundary defended against an assault and the assault on it are not equivalent, and some disagreements are between a truth and a falsehood, which no appeal to shared ground should be allowed to blur. Withdrawing consent means refusing to let the axis be the whole field. It means asking, of any opposition one is enlisted into, what field was divided so that these two terms now appear as mutually exclusive wholes, who drew the line and who holds the combining step, and what the two sides share that neither side's account of the other can see. Here dia-ballein is answered by its own held form. A polarity that remembers its zero can still oppose, contest and refuse; what it cannot do is consent to be divided, because it knows that both its poles have their sense through a field neither owns.

Martin Heidegger gave the arrested runtime its epochal name in a short address of 1955. "There are, then, two kinds of thinking," he said, "each justified and needed in its own way: calculative thinking and meditative thinking." Calculative thinking plans, computes, reckons with given circumstances and counts on definite results; and Heidegger characterised it by what it does not do: "Calculative thinking never stops, never collects itself."[^s2-26] *Never collects itself*: in our terms, the calculative is the thinking that divides and conquers without the step that would gather its results back into the field from which they were taken, a runtime racing from result to result without returning. Heidegger's fear was that "calculative thinking may someday come to be accepted and practiced as the only way of thinking," and in this section's vocabulary that is the capture of a plurality of ways of knowing by one of them, one logic occupying the zero of the whole field.[^s2-27] We receive his distinction and decline the quietism that sometimes accompanied it. "Each justified and needed in its own way" is Heidegger's own sentence, and it should govern how his distinction is used. Calculation reveals; the measurement of a river's flow discloses the river in a definite way, and the computational inheritance of the twentieth century has disclosed the planet to itself on a scale no earlier culture could reach. The pathology lies where this movement has located it, in the cut that forgets the common operation and field by which its poles were produced, and in the runtime that arrests before return. A calculative thinking that collected itself would still calculate. It would also know what it was calculating for, and from what.

Dia-ballein has a crowd as well as a calculation, and the crowd is where the collapsed axis acquires its force. Gustave Le Bon, writing in 1895 from political commitments this essay does not share, made one observation that has outlasted the rest of his book: a crowd in the psychological sense need not be gathered in one place. "An entire nation," he wrote, "may become a crowd" under certain influences, and the individuals who compose it come to "feel, think, and act in a manner quite different" from the way each would in isolation, because the crowd is "a combination followed by the creation of new characteristics" and not an average of its members.[^s2-28] Le Bon's racial hierarchies and sweeping generalisations are no part of what we take from him. What we take is the formal point that a collective can be a formation, something produced across many people that acts on each of them, and that it can be produced at a distance, through media and repeated influence, without anyone standing in a square.

Jung, writing after the Second World War, followed the cost of that formation to the individual. "The bigger the crowd," he wrote, "the more negligible the individual becomes," and he traced the sequence by which the individual's negligibility becomes political: responsibility "is collectivized as much as possible," and the State "in particular is turned into a quasi-animate personality," a being that wills, knows, protects, punishes and demands sacrifice, behind which, Jung insisted, stand the particular people who know how to operate it.[^s2-29] The State becomes a person; the persons become numbers. Here the collapsed axis shows its political mechanism. A field of relation among many people, with all its internal differences, is made to appear as a single subject facing another single subject, *us* against *them*, and each of those apparent subjects is administered by people whose own position in the field has dropped out of view.

Mattias Desmet's account of what he calls mass formation proposes the affective preparation for this transfer: social isolation, lack of meaning, and free-floating anxiety, frustration and aggression, affect with no object adequate to it.[^s2-30] A narrative supplies the object. It concentrates diffuse distress on a target and promises restored coherence through obedience, sacrifice or expulsion, and the resulting unity is felt as relief, because an isolated person has found company in a common enemy. The relief is real; the unity is a counterfeit, because what binds the crowd together is not a recovered relation among its members but a shared fixation on a term outside them. Vicky Iakovou's criticism of the use of the concept of totalitarianism keeps the account honest: mass psychology cannot stand in for the analysis of institutions, parties, offices, property, coercion and the material organisation of authority, and susceptibility to a story does not explain who has the power to enforce it.[^s2-31] We keep both accounts in view because each corrects the other. A full circuit runs through both registers. A population is atomised; its unbound affect is offered a target; the axis between *us* and *them* is intensified until it seems to exhaust political reality; and an institution, a party, a state or an empire presents itself as the sole mediator, protector or saviour of the field it has helped to sever. Division happens below and a counterfeit unity is installed above, and the people who could have combined among themselves are combined only through the apparatus that divided them.

None of this makes the axis false. There are real differences of interest between employers and workers, between creditors and debtors, between those who own housing and those who rent it, between a colonising power and the people it governs, and an account that dissolved them into a vague common humanity would be the other collapse, cancellation, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, with the injustice summed away. Empire and subject, institution and citizen, rich and poor are unequal positions within one social field, and their inequality is a fact to be measured, not a misunderstanding to be talked away. What the collapsed logic does is make each of these positions appear self-sufficient, as though the rich were rich by nature and the poor poor by nature, as though institutions existed apart from the citizens whose labour and consent sustain them, so that the relation that reproduces the inequality disappears behind the inequality itself. Dia-ballein held measures the inequality exactly and keeps the relation that produces it in view. Dia-ballein collapsed measures the same inequality and reads it as a property of the people on each side.

So the literalist of the minus sign is not a monster at the edge of our institutions. He is a habit inside them, and inside us, the habit of reading a position as a substance and a zero as an ending. Every classification can do it and every verdict can be read that way. The jurors at the table are not literalists; they know that *not guilty* is not *innocent* and that the evening in the stairwell exceeds their two boxes. The newspaper that reports their verdict may be, and the employer who reads the record years later very likely is. Between the jury room and the employer's desk, the same cut has passed from a held polarity into a collapsed one, and nothing in the cut itself has changed. What changed is whether the axis it was made on, the field it came from, and the question it actually answered still travelled with it.

That is the condition under which dia-ballein stays itself, and it is also the hinge to the other logic. A cut that travels with its axis is already half way to a fitting, because it carries, in its own edge, the shape of what it was cut from. That edge is where the next movement goes. Myth has given the cut a body too, a god who separates, contests, refuses and defends, and who will meet his counterpart in a bed made by a craftsman; but before Ares can be read, the logic that throws together has to be stated in its own terms, as the retained seam of the symbolon and the trust it was made to carry.

### #2 · Syn-ballein: The Retained Seam

Most of us wore one once, though none of us remembers it. In the minutes after a birth in a modern hospital, before the baby has been weighed or named, a midwife fastens a plastic band around its ankle or wrist and a matching band around the mother's wrist, and the two bands carry the same number. Later, when the baby comes back from a check in another room, or when a nurse on the night shift brings it to be fed, she does not rely on her memory of a face that is a few hours old and looks, to anyone but its parents, much like every other face on the ward. She takes the mother's wrist and the baby's ankle and reads the two bands against each other, and when the numbers match she hands the child over. The bands keep mother and child two: two bodies, two bands, two separate pieces of plastic that can be carried apart, through corridors and across shifts. Their matching makes the two belong, and lets a stranger, who has no other way of knowing, check that they do. A mother whose band was lost would have to prove her child some other way, slowly and with distress; a single band shared between them could not be read against anything and would prove nothing. The match works precisely because the halves stay separate and stay alike in the one respect that matters.

It is worth noticing what the bands do not prove. They do not prove that this woman loves this child, or will care for it well, or that the hospital's records are just, or that the number assigned at birth will ever mean anything to the person who wore it. A genuine token can belong to an unjust arrangement, and an authenticated relation can later be betrayed. What the matching bands establish is a fit: these two pieces were issued together, so these two people were registered together, and the nurse can act on that. The proof is exact because it is narrow. And it is a proof the hospital needs only because recognition cannot yet do the work. The mother will soon know her baby among a hundred, and the baby will know her voice before it can see her face; the bands are there for the hours when the relation exists and cannot yet be read from either face, and for the strangers who have to act on it in the meantime. That is what a token of this kind is for. It is a device of trust, made for an interval in which the people who must rely on a relation cannot see it directly.

§0/1 gave us the ancient form of this device. A *symbolon* was a token broken between two parties, each keeping a half; brought together later, the fit of the broken edges authenticated the relation, so that the difference between the halves was the condition of their recognition.[^s2-32] Greek lexicography records the practice in its plainest form: a knucklebone or other small object broken by two guest-friends, or by any two parties to an agreement, each keeping one piece so that whoever later presented the other piece could prove who he was.[^s2-33] The token travelled. It could be inherited, so that the grandson of a guest could present it at the door of the grandson of a host and be received; it could be carried by a messenger who had never met either party; it could wait in a chest for a generation. Its authority did not lie in its material or its beauty but in the shape of its edge, which held, in the half that remained, the exact negative of the half that was absent. A symbolon is a piece that carries its missing counterpart in its outline. The bands on the maternity ward are its descendants, and like the ancient token they are made for the gap of time and distance in which trust has to act before recognition can.

Syn-ballein is the logic the token embodies, and it can now be stated as a logic in its own right. Classical logic, the logic of the jury room, takes a pair of values as mutually exclusive positions of one variable: true or false, guilty or not guilty, `+1` or `−1`, with the slash read as OR and the OR read as *one and not the other*. Its pairs are complete in the sense that every case falls into exactly one of them. Syn-ballein takes a pair as the two orientations of one relation, and reads the slash as AND/OR, the conjunction that does not erase disjunction which §0/1 found inside the copula and wrote as `=/≠`.[^s2-34] Read as OR, the slash lets either orientation be taken in its own office, and keeps their difference consequential. Read as AND, it holds them within one act, since each orientation is the relation and neither is the whole of it. When §0/1 turned its first symbolon over, it wrote `0/1` and `1/0`, the condition becoming articulate through a determination and the determination turning toward the condition it cannot make into another determinate possession, and held them together as `(0/1)/(1/0)`, the relation taking its own inverse as a term.[^s2-35] That expression is the whole of syn-ballein in notation. Each inner slash keeps the indefinite and the definite together; the outer slash relates the two readings of that keeping; and ordinary arithmetic, which defines `0/1` and leaves `1/0` undefined, supplies the seam through which the two readings stay different. A logic of this kind is complete in another sense from the classical one. It does not sort cases into boxes; it keeps the relation that every sorting presupposes in view while the sorting goes on.

Syn-ballein is a superpositional logic, and the word needs its scope stated at once. Superposition belongs first to the dual-non-dual ontology this essay argues: ground and manifestation are distinguishable orientations of one appearing-act, each given through the relation that prevents it from standing alone, and separately complete substances related afterwards would already presuppose the joining they were supposed to explain. Quantum state space, the complementary descriptions Pauli and Jung discussed, and the non-classical logics that allow a proposition to be both or neither are exact mathematical and physical neighbours of that claim, and §3 examines their agreements and differences in detail.[^s2-36] None of them supplies the claim. When we call `(0/1)/(1/0)` a superposition we mean that both orientations are present at once in the one relation, and that the presence of each does not cancel the other, the way a mother's band and her baby's band are both fully readable at once and neither makes the other redundant.

Syn-ballein also needs a guard placed beside it from the start, because a logic of gathering invites the claim that it has gathered everything. Syn-ballein is not *the whole*. It is the logic of defining toward wholeness: the accounting in which a determination keeps its ground, its inverse orientation and the relations through which it can return, so that its completion stays a direction it travels in and never becomes a possession it holds. A claim to have achieved the whole would already be a `1` occupying the zero, which is exactly what §0/1 called playing the `0`.[^s2-37] The retained seam is what keeps wholeness open. A token whose two halves had been fused into one lump would no longer be a token, and a gathering that claimed to have no remaining seams would no longer be a gathering; it would be an enclosure, and the next movement shows, in the craftsman god's net, what an enclosure that calls itself a gathering does to the people inside it.

Heraclitus gives the right kind of gathering a word, and Heidegger, reading him, gave the word a shape. In the fragment numbered B50, Heraclitus says that it is wise, listening not to him but to the *logos*, to agree that all things are one, and Heidegger, working back through *legein* to its oldest sense of laying down and gathering, rendered the Heraclitean Logos as "the Laying that gathers," *die lesende Lege*.[^s2-38] The gathering he heard in the word does not consume what it gathers. It lets what is gathered lie together before us, each thing where it is and as it is, the way a harvester lays the gathered sheaves side by side, or the way a reader, *legere*, gathers the letters of a line into a sentence without the letters ceasing to be letters. §0/1 recovered this root when it found the integral Logos to be a remembering of its own gathering.[^s2-39] Here it gives syn-ballein its ancient image. A gathering that lets lie together before us is exactly a fitting that keeps the pieces two, and its opposite, which we will meet one movement from now, is a gathering that holds together in order to arrest.

What a token carries across its interval is trust, and here syn-ballein shows its second face, the face it turns toward the uncertainty that knowledge cannot eliminate. We said at the beginning of this section that knowledge is a performance of trust wearing the mask of certainty, and that the collapsed form of dia-ballein deals with the uncertainty it cannot admit by exporting it across the axis onto an excluded term.[^s2-40] Syn-ballein deals with it by distributing it. Instead of finding one object to blame, it finds many places to rest. A competence tested in use, so that a carpenter can trust her hands though she cannot prove they will not slip; a relationship that has survived disagreement, so that a friend's word carries weight it would not carry from a stranger; a lineage, a practice, a piece of land, a ritual, a family, a source that has been checked before, an institution with real means of redress: each of these gives uncertainty somewhere to rest without pretending to abolish it, and each is a **provisional anchor**, provisional because it can be revised when it fails and an anchor because it holds while it holds. Their plurality is their strength. When one fails, as some will, the possibility of trusting does not fail with it, because the others go on bearing weight; and each can correct the others, so that an official account can be tested against practical knowledge, and an intimate conviction against evidence it had refused to admit.

This is the trust we named **mono–poly** in the first movement: one living field of reliance, sustained through many anchors, none of which is permitted to stand for the whole. Its relation to the Mono and Poly of §0/1 is exact. Mono is the singular field, the reliance already enacted before anyone has certified it, which no anchor creates; Poly is the many anchors through which that reliance becomes definite, local and revisable.[^s2-41] Sever the Poly and trust does not disappear. It becomes available for capture by a single totalising object, a leader, a doctrine, a party, a platform, a model, which offers to bear all the weight at once and asks in return to be exempt from the revision that every anchor owes. That is the bargain the crowd of the previous movement accepted, and it is why the crowd's unity felt like relief. An isolated person with no anchors left has nowhere to rest their uncertainty except in the one place that offers to hold it all, and the monopoly of trust is, in the strict sense of the word §0/1 traced, a *monopōlion*, one seller where there should be many.[^s2-42]

The Latin vocabulary that §0/1 opened when the household's reliance came under question can now be followed through its whole course, as six offices in the life of one entrustment.[^s2-43] *Fides*, the first, is reliance already enacted, the credit given to a witness before the witness has been weighed, and we met it as the trust that comes before arbitration. A second office is *topos*, the place in which the relying happens: this source, these conditions, this person's competence and vulnerability, so that trust is never trust in general but always trust here. *Logos* is the third, the articulation of reasons and claims, the account that makes the reliance intelligible and open to examination. Then comes *nomos*, the instituted effect, which decides whose answer can change what is done and by which rule, through which office. *Natio*, from *nasci*, to be born, names the order into which later people arrive and begin living before they can examine how it was made, the belonging that every generation inherits.[^s2-44] And *credere* is the renewed entrustment, credit extended again once an undertaking has returned its consequences, with what was learned carried into the new commitment. Read in order, the six describe how a single act of trust becomes a world: a reliance takes place somewhere, becomes an account, acquires the force of a rule, is inherited as a belonging and is renewed or withdrawn in the light of what happened. Read through the two logics, they describe where the trust can be captured. A *nomos* that forbids its own revision has arrested the sequence before *credere*; a *natio* that presents its order as nature has hidden the *topos* in which it was once made; a *logos* that claims to need no *fides* is the mask taken for the face. Syn-ballein keeps the six offices moving, so that what was inherited can be examined and what was examined can be entrusted again.

Here the counter-operation to divide and conquer can be named, and it was named, in the plan of this essay, by a pun that argues: **divine and concur**. The phrase answers *divide and conquer* almost letter for letter, and the near-coincidence of the words carries the near-coincidence of the operations. To divide is to cut a field; to divine, Latin *dīvīnāre*, is to perceive what is hidden, the old art of the seer who reads in the visible signs a relation that does not itself appear.[^s2-45] Divining, here, means finding in the apparent self-sufficiency of each pole the relation it has obscured or appropriated: the transcendental third, the `0/1 = 1/0` relation through which both poles were formed and to which both still answer. The third so divined is not a third party standing in the middle of the axis, and not the centrist's midpoint, which remains on the signed line and merely chooses a location between `−1` and `+1`. It is the field that was divided so that these two terms now appear as mutually exclusive wholes, and the question that divines it is the one the previous movement left us with: who drew this line, who holds the combining step, and what do the two sides share that neither side's account of the other can see? The second half of the pun is sharper still. *Conquer* descends, through Old French, from the Latin family of *conquīrere*, which Lewis and Short gloss as to seek for, to procure, and also to bring together and collect.[^s2-46] The conqueror, in the word's own history, gathers; he gathers by seizing, and the whole he assembles is held together by the hand that took it. *Concur* is *con-currere*, to run together, and its *currere* is the same running we heard in *currency*, what is present enough to pass from hand to hand.[^s2-47] To concur, in this sense, is not to agree, still less to fuse or to settle at a midpoint. It is for differentiated positions to move together through a ground that none of them possesses, the way current money runs between people without belonging to any of them. Conquest is a gathering by possession; concurrence is a gathering by circulation.

Divine and concur can judge. A recovered relation does not make every pole equally factual, ethical, powerful or responsible, and the recovery of the field in which an injustice was done may make the injustice sharper, not softer: one claim may turn out to be false, a power may have to give up an office it had taken, an injured person may decline reconciliation and be right to decline it. What the operation refuses is one further step, the step by which the falsity or violence of a determination licenses the fantasy that the people, histories and needs from which it arose can be expelled from the real. In practice it returns each institution, party and claim of authority to the people, labour, histories and material conditions it cannot own, and asks whose labour sustains the form, whose exclusion secures it, which office authorises it, which record makes it claimable, and by what path the governed may contest and alter it. That is not a view from nowhere and not a reconciliation that absolves power. It is the practical condition under which a collective need not become a crowd in order to act together.

Computation has its own name for syn-ballein, and the name is the step the political trick withheld. In a divide-and-conquer algorithm, the combine is where the subproblems' answers are brought back into the problem they were cut from; in a larger computational process the same work is done by everything that lets a result carry its provenance. A result that keeps the problem it answered, the alternatives it excluded, the inverse reading that could contest it and the larger state it changed is a result that can return; a result stripped of these is a number that can only be obeyed. §0/1 named the ontology in which this matters as Computational Process Ontology: process is ontologically prior to the finished thing, computation names the exact, repeatable side of that process, and epistemology is the process becoming able to state and inspect its own determinations.[^s2-48] Dia-ballein and syn-ballein are that ontology's primitive runtime. Dia makes the local branch actual: it distinguishes, compares, excludes, ranks, routes and decides. Syn keeps the branch's belonging to a wider field and composes its consequences with other branches: it relates, preserves provenance, inverts, recombines, contextualises and returns. Neither runs alone without failing. A runtime of pure division atomises, and a runtime of pure gathering never decides anything at all.

*Agentworld* locates the intelligence of the coming agentic society in this composing step. "Long horizons are a property of groups," Bratton writes; what lets a task run on indefinitely is "the composite's capacity to recompose itself around an evolving problem, passing coherence from sub-agent to artifact to sub-agent without meaningful loss."[^s2-49] And again: "The unit that learns is the ensemble, not the agent, and what it learns accrues to the commons between them, not to any single one."[^s2-50] Read through our two logics, these are claims about syn-ballein at planetary scale. The sub-agent is a cut, a local branch of a problem made tractable; the artifact passed between sub-agents is the token that carries the problem across the gap; and the coherence that passes without meaningful loss is the retained seam, the relation that lets each piece be read against the others. A commons into which learning accrues is a field of provisional anchors, and an ensemble that learns is a mono–poly trust in technical form. The design question the brief raises without answering is the one this movement has been asking all along: who holds the combining step? An ensemble whose recomposition is performed by every participant, with provenance visible to each, is concurrence. An ensemble whose recomposition is retained by one operator, while the sub-agents remain divided and their outputs pass upward through a channel only the operator can read, is divide and conquer with a better interface.

A record is not yet a return, and the difference is worth stating because it is easy to mistake one for the other. A log can keep every step a system took, an audit trail can preserve every decision an institution made, and the archive can be complete while nothing it contains is able to change what happens next. The return syn-ballein requires is an operation, not a file: a consequence that reaches the source, the criterion, the permission or the commission that governs the next act, and changes it if it should be changed. The maternity bands are read; they are not merely kept. A symbolon in a chest that nobody ever presents at the door proves nothing to anyone. An account whose provenance is perfectly recorded and never consulted has kept the seam as a museum keeps a broken pot, intact and out of use.

The token has a modern counterfeit, and it shows what happens to trust when the seam itself can be manufactured. A photograph, a recording of a voice, a video of a public figure speaking: each of these has long worked as a half of a symbolon, a mark that could be carried away from the event that produced it and later presented as proof that the event took place, because its edge, the light that fell on the lens and the air that moved the microphone, fitted the event and nothing else. Synthetic media cut that fit. A generated image or voice presents itself as the half of a token whose other half never existed, and it fabricates the appearance of a relation to a source; that is a **counterfeit symbolon**, and its first harm is the obvious one, that people may believe what never happened. Its second harm is worse. Once a culture knows that the seam can be fabricated, a genuine record can be refused by claiming that it was fabricated too, and Robert Chesney and Danielle Citron named the advantage this gives to anyone caught on the record the liar's dividend.[^s2-51] The trust that lived in the fit of the halves has been made available to both sides at once, so that no half can prove anything. Provenance systems, cryptographic signatures and content credentials can strengthen the conditions under which a sign returns to its source, and they are worth building, but none of them manufactures trust from inside the mark. Somewhere a person or an institution still has to stand surety for the account, and the question of who that is, and what they risk by standing there, is the question the next movement's myth puts at its centre.

Syn-ballein has a failure of its own, and it should be named before the myth gives it a body. If dia-ballein's failure is severance, the cut that forgets its field, syn-ballein's is **fusion**: relation preserved only by erasing the distinctions through which its terms could meet. A fused relation has no seam left to read. The mother's band and the baby's band melted into one would match nothing; a marriage in which one partner's preferences have quietly become the other's has stopped being a relation between two people; a collective that speaks with one voice because every other voice has learned to keep quiet has achieved unity by abolishing the differences from which a reply could come. Fusion is harder to see than severance because it presents itself as love, or as consensus, or as the overcoming of division, and it can be welcomed by the very people it absorbs. Its test is simple, and it is the test of the token. Can the halves still be told apart? Can one of them still refuse, answer back, or leave? A relation in which that is no longer possible has not become more whole. It has stopped being a relation.

So the two logics stand complete, each with its own exactness and its own collapse. Dia-ballein measures a real difference across a shared zero and collapses when it reads the zero as an ending, cancelling the difference or appropriating its span. Syn-ballein keeps the relation that every measurement presupposes, holding the two orientations of a pair together, and collapses when it erases the difference that made the pair a pair. Both are needed, because a life without the cut could not refuse and a life without the seam could not belong, and both fail in recognisable ways. What neither notation can do is show what these operations feel like to the people performing them, what it is to be the one who separates, defends and refuses, or the one who attracts, joins and holds, and what happens when the two meet in a single bed. Greek myth gave that meeting a song, and the song was sung, on the island of the Phaeacians, to a stranger who had not yet told anyone his name.

### #3 · Ares, Aphrodite and the Birth of Harmonia

A shipwrecked stranger sits at the table of a king who does not yet know his name. Earlier in the day the court's blind singer, Demodocus, sang of a quarrel between Odysseus and Achilles at Troy, and the stranger drew his purple cloak over his head and wept, and only the king noticed.[^s2-52] Then there were games on the shore, a running race and wrestling and the throwing of a discus, and a young man taunted the stranger, and the stranger threw a stone further than anyone had thrown before. Now the young men of the island clear a dancing floor, the herald brings Demodocus his lyre, and the dancers begin to strike the floor with their feet while the stranger watches the flashing of their steps and marvels. Demodocus sings a second song. It is a song about the gods, light and scandalous, and it is told for pleasure to a hall that wants to be amused; and it is the story this section has been waiting for, because it gives the two logics the bodies §0/1 promised them, War and Love, Ares and Aphrodite, caught together in a net forged by the craftsman god.[^s2-53]

The song is short and complete, and it should be heard whole before anything is made of it. Ares has loved Aphrodite in secret, in the house of her husband Hephaestus, and has given her many gifts and shamed the husband's bed. Helios, the sun, who sees everything, has seen them lying together and goes to tell Hephaestus. The craftsman takes his grief to the forge. He sets his great anvil on its block and hammers out bonds that cannot be broken or loosed, fine as spiders' webs and invisible even to the gods, and he carries them to his chamber and hangs them all around the bedposts and from the rafters above. Then he pretends to leave for Lemnos, the island he loves best. Ares, who has been watching, sees him go, hurries to the house and takes Aphrodite by the hand: her husband is away, he says, among the barbarous Sintians, so let us go to bed. They lie down together, and the bonds fall on them, "and about them clung the cunning bonds of the wise Hephaestus, nor could they in any wise stir their limbs or raise them up. Then at length they learned that there was no more escaping."[^s2-54]

Helios has kept watch and goes back to Hephaestus, who turns round before he reaches Lemnos and comes home with his heart grieving. He stands in the doorway, savage anger seizes him, and he shouts to the gods: "Father Zeus, and ye other blessed gods that are forever, come hither that ye may see a laughable matter and a monstrous, even how Aphrodite, daughter of Zeus, scorns me for that I am lame and loves destructive Ares because he is comely and strong of limb, whereas I was born misshapen."[^s2-55] He will keep them there, he says, until her father has given back every one of the gifts he paid to win her. The gods come to the house of bronze, Poseidon the earth-shaker and Hermes and Apollo; "now the goddesses abode for shame each in her own house, but the gods, the givers of good things, stood in the gateway; and unquenchable laughter arose among the blessed gods as they saw the craft of wise Hephaestus."[^s2-56] One of them, glancing at his neighbour, draws the moral: bad deeds do not prosper, the slow catches the swift, and lame Hephaestus by his craft has caught Ares, the swiftest of all the gods on Olympus, so that Ares owes the fine of an adulterer. Then Apollo turns to Hermes and asks whether he would be willing to lie in such strong bonds beside golden Aphrodite, and Hermes answers that he would take three times as many bonds, with all the gods and all the goddesses looking on, to sleep beside her; and the gods laugh again.[^s2-57]

Only Poseidon does not laugh. He keeps begging Hephaestus to let Ares go, and he promises that Ares will pay whatever is right in the presence of the gods. Hephaestus refuses. A pledge given for a worthless man is a worthless pledge, he says in effect, and if Ares slips out of both the bonds and the debt, how could Hephaestus hold Poseidon to account among the immortals? Poseidon answers by changing what he offers. If Ares runs away and evades the debt, then Poseidon himself will pay it. And Hephaestus says that he cannot and must not refuse such a word, and he loosens the bonds. The two lovers spring up and are gone at once, Ares to Thrace and Aphrodite, laughter-loving, to Cyprus, to Paphos, where she has her precinct and her fragrant altar, and there the Graces bathe her and anoint her with immortal oil and dress her in lovely clothes. That is the end of the song, and the stranger is glad at heart as he listens, and so are the Phaeacians, long-oared men, famed for their ships.[^s2-58]

Whatever else the song is, it is a story about two powers and a third. Ares is war: the god of the line that holds, the shield and the spear, contest and refusal and the force by which a difference can stand its ground and say no. Aphrodite is love: attraction and pleasure and the movement of joining, the power that draws a life out of isolation and makes an encounter worth risking something for. They are the two logics given bodies, and the bodies show what the notation could not, what each operation is like from inside and why each needs the other. Ares without Aphrodite is difference that has forgotten relation: every other becomes an enemy, every boundary a front line, and the capacity to refuse, which is a real good, hardens into the capacity only to refuse. Aphrodite without Ares is relation that has forgotten difference: joining becomes engulfing, attraction becomes possession, and the meeting of two lives becomes the disappearance of one into the other. Each gives the other what it lacks. Ares gives Aphrodite beings distinct enough to meet, since only what can stand apart can come together, and a lover who could not refuse would have nothing to give. Aphrodite gives Ares a reason for his defences, since a boundary that protects nothing has no purpose, and the line holds because something on its far side is loved. This is the reading the essay proposes, and its standing is that of an argued mytheme: a whole image that proves a relation through the action it stages, read back into operations without making Homer the author of our logics.[^s2-59]

§0/1 put the question that this pairing answers in its plainest form: will difference become a dance, or a battle?[^s2-60] The dancers on the Phaeacian floor, striking the ground in time while Demodocus sings, are a working answer. A dance is a difference kept in motion: two or twenty bodies, each distinct, each moving through a shared space by rules that keep them from colliding and keep them related, so that the pattern of the whole depends on every dancer staying a dancer. A battle is a difference that has demanded a winner. Here, perhaps, is the shortest statement this section can make of the line between the two logics' healthy and collapsed forms. Polarity is not war. Opposition, contest, criticism and even fierce disagreement can remain inside a held polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`, where the zero still connects the poles and each still has its sense through the other. **War is the demand that the slash produce a winner**: the moment at which the other ceases to be a pole with whom a world is shared and becomes an obstacle whose removal is imagined to complete the world.[^s2-61] Ares at his worst is that demand made into a god. Aphrodite at her worst is its mirror, the demand that the slash produce one body, that the other's difference be dissolved into the lover's desire. Both demands are refusals of the slash.

Jung saw the psychological shape of this pairing, and saw it with an exactness that bears directly on the myth. In the first of his *Two Essays on Analytical Psychology*, comparing Freud's psychology of Eros with Adler's psychology of power, he observed that life can flow forward only along a gradient, and that "there is no energy unless there is a tension of opposites." Then he named the opposite of love: "Logically, the opposite of love is hate, and of Eros, Phobos (fear); but psychologically it is the will to power. Where love reigns, there is no will to power; and where the will to power is paramount, love is lacking. The one is but the shadow of the other."[^s2-62] Earlier in the same essay he quoted Freud's late formulation of the two basic instincts, Eros, whose aim is "to establish ever greater unities and to preserve them thus—in short, to bind together," and the destructive instinct, whose aim is "to undo connections and so to destroy things."[^s2-63] Freud's pair and Jung's pair are not our pair, and each belongs to its own argument. But each describes, in its own register, the two directions of the throw: binding together and undoing connections, syn-ballein and dia-ballein, and each insists, as the myth does, that the two belong to one energy. Jung's sentence about Phobos has a further edge. In Hesiod's *Theogony* the logical opposite of love is literally one of love's children.

"Also Cytherea bare to Ares the shield-piercer Panic and Fear, terrible gods who drive in disorder the close ranks of men in numbing war, with the help of Ares, sacker of towns; and Harmonia whom high-spirited Cadmus made his wife."[^s2-64] Hesiod gives the union of War and Love three children, and two of them are terrors. Deimos and Phobos, Panic and Fear, ride with their father into battle and break the ranks of men; Harmonia marries the founder of Thebes and becomes the mother of a royal house. Hesiod's family is a theorem in the mythemic register. A meeting of the power that separates and the power that joins can bear fear or it can bear harmony, and Hesiod sets both outcomes in one genealogy, so that neither can be taken as the meeting's guaranteed result. Read through the two logics, the difference between the children is the difference between the collapsed and the held polarity. Where the meeting of War and Love demands a winner, its offspring is fear: the lover becomes a threat, the beloved a possession to be guarded, and the energy Jung found in the tension of opposites becomes panic, the rout of ranks that no longer know what holds them together. Where the meeting holds its tension without settling it, its offspring is harmony. Harmonia is born of War and Love together, and she is born only where neither parent has swallowed the other.

Her name says how. Greek *harmonia* began as a word of craft: in the *Odyssey*, when Odysseus builds his raft on Calypso's island, he fastens its timbers together with pegs and *harmoniai*, joints, the fittings by which separate pieces of wood are made to hold as one vessel.[^s2-65] From the joint of a carpenter the word passed to an agreement between people: in the *Iliad*, before their last fight, Hector asks Achilles to swear that the victor will give back the loser's body, and calls on the gods as the best witnesses and guardians of *harmoniai*, compacts.[^s2-66] And from the joint and the compact it passed to music, the fitting of sounds into an ordered relation, which is the sense the word has carried ever since. A joint, a covenant, an attunement: craft, trust and music, three senses of one word, and they are the three things this myth is about. Hephaestus's net is a terrible joint, a fitting that holds two bodies together and lets neither move. Poseidon's surety is a covenant, a word given before witnesses. And the bow and the lyre, which Heraclitus set side by side, are the attunement that holds tension without breaking it. Achilles' answer to Hector shows what happens when the second sense is refused. There are no faithful oaths between lions and men, he says, nor do wolves and lambs have one heart; there can be no compact between you and me.[^s2-67] That is dia-ballein absolutised, war as the demand that the slash produce a winner, spoken by the greatest warrior of the poem, and its consequence is the dragging of Hector's body around the walls of Troy.

Heraclitus gave *harmonia* its philosophical form in a single sentence. "They do not understand how in differing it agrees with itself; a backwards-turning fastening, just as that of a bow or a lyre."[^s2-68] The word Mackenzie translates as *fastening* is *harmoniē*, the same word, and the sentence says in its own terms what Hesiod's genealogy staged: a joining that holds through a pull in two directions. Bend a bow and its two arms pull against the string while the string pulls against them, and the tension of the whole is what lets it send an arrow; string a lyre and the frame pulls against the strings while the strings pull against the frame, and the tension is what lets it sound. Removing the tension would not perfect the agreement of bow and string; it would abolish the bow. Its verb for *agrees* is *homologeei*, and we heard in the first movement that *homologia* is a common *logos* found within a difference.[^s2-69] Heraclitus's agreement is not an agreement in which the parts stop differing; it is an agreement made of their differing. Bow-arms do not have to be reconciled before the bow can work. Their opposition, held, is the work.

Mackenzie argues that the fragment alludes to the *Odyssey*, and to a scene in which the same stranger who listened to Demodocus's song takes up a bow.[^s2-70] At the end of the poem, home at last in Ithaca, disguised as a beggar in his own hall among the suitors who have been devouring his house, Odysseus is handed the great bow that none of them has been able to string, and he strings it without effort, the way a man skilled in the lyre and in song stretches a string around a new peg, and he plucks the string with his right hand, and it sings beautifully, like the voice of a swallow.[^s2-71] The bow becomes a lyre in his hands for a moment before it becomes a bow again, and then it kills. Homer supplies the image, and the allusion is Mackenzie's argument; the reading is ours. The man who heard the song of the net is the man who will hold together, in one instrument, the two powers the song set against each other, the tension of war and the tension of music, and his homecoming depends on his knowing that they are the same tension differently released.

Hamartia, which §0/1 traced to its root in missing the mark, finds its image here.[^s2-72] The word was first said of a spear thrown and falling short of its target, and §0/1 located the error it names where a mark is required to stand as though it had abolished its difference from what it marks. The bow gives the error a body. An archer who has forgotten that the bow is also a lyre treats the tension of the instrument as nothing but the means of reaching the target, and the target as the whole meaning of the act. He can hit exactly what he aimed at and still miss, because the mark he struck was never the whole of what the act concerned; the condition that made it a mark, the relation in which the shot was taken and the life the arrow enters, has dropped out of the aiming. The bow forgets it is a lyre when tension is collapsed into a target. That is the precise sense in which an achieved aim can miss its own ground, and it is the sense in which an exact technical success can still be a *hamartia*: the arrow lands where it was sent, and nothing in the landing asks whether it should have been sent there.

Hephaestus is the third figure, and the song is careful about what kind of power he has. He is lame, the only Olympian with a damaged body, born misshapen as he complains to the gods; and he wins, against the swiftest of the gods, by craft. Homer's word for the bonds that pour around the lovers is *desmoi technēentes*, which Murray renders "cunning bonds" and which says, literally, bonds full of *technē*: craft is written on the net itself.[^s2-73] The gods' moral, that the slow catches the swift, names the reversal by which a weaker power defeats a stronger one through cleverness, and Marcel Detienne and Jean-Pierre Vernant made that reversal the signature of a whole Greek category of intelligence, *mētis*, cunning craft, at home in shifting and ambiguous situations, whose characteristic instruments are the trap, the net, the woven circle and the bond. For them Hephaestus's crooked feet are themselves the visible sign of this intelligence, a body that walks two ways at once, suited to mastering what is fluid and slippery.[^s2-74] The net is *mētis* made into an object: finer than spider silk, invisible, closing around the bed so that no movement in any direction can escape it.

Look at what the net does, step by step, because every station of a modern apparatus is already present in it. Detection comes first: Helios sees, and the poem gives him a lookout from which he keeps watch for Hephaestus and reports twice, once to set the trap and once to spring it.[^s2-75] Fabrication follows: the bonds forged in secret, invisible to those they will hold. Concealment makes them effective, the pretended journey to Lemnos inviting the lovers into a room already prepared. Capture holds them: they cannot stir a limb, and only then do they learn there is no escape. Display turns capture into evidence: Hephaestus summons the gods to come and *see*, and the scene's whole purpose becomes the making of a public sight. A tribunal forms in the gateway, its verdict pronounced by an anonymous god. An account converts grievance into debt: Hephaestus demands back the bride-gifts, *hedna*, he paid for his wife, and the onlookers name the fine the captured adulterer owes, *moichagria*, a word Greek uses only here.[^s2-76] And an economy of exposure appears, spoken aloud by Hermes, who would accept three times the bonds and every god and goddess looking on for the pleasure of the bed. Christopher Brown has shown that Greek communities really did expose adulterers to public mockery, and that the Olympian scene is shaped by that social practice; the laughter is the punishment.[^s2-77] The shame falls unevenly. The goddesses stay at home; the gods stand in the gateway and enjoy what the exposed goddess is being held responsible for.

Martin Heidegger's account of the essence of modern technology gives this sequence a name exact enough to carry weight, though Heidegger never read the song this way and the reading is ours. Modern technology, he argued, reveals the world as *Bestand*, standing-reserve: "Everywhere everything is ordered to stand by, to be immediately at hand, indeed to stand there just so that it may be on call for a further ordering."[^s2-78] The lovers under the net are standing-reserve: held where they are, at hand, on view, waiting for an ordering that is not theirs. Heidegger called the way of revealing that orders the world into standing-reserve *Ge-stell*, enframing, and derived the word from the gathering force of the German prefix *Ge-*, as in *Gebirg*, the gathering of mountains into a range, and *Gemüt*, the gathering of feelings into a disposition: "We now name that challenging claim which gathers man thither to order the self-revealing as standing-reserve: 'Ge-stell' [Enframing]." Enframing, he said, "means the gathering together of that setting-upon which sets upon man."[^s2-79] *Ge-stell* is a gathering: a word shaped like syn-ballein for an operation that works like dia-ballein. Hephaestus's net shows the paradox in one image. It throws the lovers together, literally, a syn-ballein in the most kinetic sense, and it is exactly by throwing them together that it arrests them. I call this the **counterfeit gathering**: a gathering that keeps the differences visible and keeps the relation visible and arrests the reciprocity between them, so that the gathered can neither move nor answer back. Fusion removes difference and severance removes relation; the counterfeit gathering keeps both on display and makes both inert.[^s2-80]

Homer's own word for the device is *dolos*, a snare, and the verb *diaballein* does not appear in the scene. What Hephaestus enacts before the convened gods is nonetheless *diabolē* in the sense the lexicon keeps: an accusation staged as public exposure, parties set at variance before witnesses, a charge thrown across between two people so that one appears to all the others under its terms.[^s2-81] Naming it so is our move, and it gives dia-ballein a mythic body exactly where syn-ballein has always had one. A symbolon is a token whose two halves fit across a seam; the net is a device whose two captives are held together across no seam at all, with the captor, the protocol and the audience standing where the zero should be. Read in the notation, the lovers have become exposed terms, `−1` and `+1` displayed side by side, and the axis between them, the relation through which each had its sense, has been occupied by the apparatus. Hephaestus stands in the place of the shared zero and counts the whole span from his own end: his grievance, his gifts, his fine.

And yet the apparatus, complete at every station, cannot conclude. Detection has succeeded, the bonds have held, the evidence is before every eye and the verdict has been spoken, and the scene stalls. The lovers remain bound, the grievance remains open, and the demand for the bride-gifts has no mechanism inside the net for being met, because a trap can hold a debtor and cannot make a debt good. If Hephaestus keeps them there forever he has a captive and no payment; if he lets them go he has no guarantee. Everything a net can do has been done, and what the situation now needs is something the net cannot make: a relation in which someone undertakes, at his own risk, to answer for what happens after the bonds are opened. Poseidon supplies it, and the song is careful to show it supplied in two steps. His first offer is a promise about another's conduct, that Ares will pay, and Hephaestus refuses it with the craftsman's exact distrust: a pledge given for a worthless man is worth nothing. His second offer is different in kind. Poseidon makes the possible failure his own: if Ares flees, Poseidon pays. Greek has a word for this undertaking, *engyē*, the pledge or surety placed in another's hand, and the song stages it with the precision of a legal procedure.[^s2-82] Hephaestus accepts, against his own stated distrust, because nothing else can open the bonds and close the account, and he uses the same hands that made the net to unmake it.

That is the turn this whole station has been moving toward, and the myth makes it without a single abstract word. An apparatus that replaced an encounter with a capture is returned, at its own limit, to trust. A person standing for another at real risk of loss, a relation no mechanism generated, is what the mechanism's own maker has to borrow in order to finish what his craft began. *Agentworld* offers a definition of trust for its society of agents that the scene tests exactly. "Trust here," Bratton writes, "is not a moral category but a prediction-stability category: an agent is trusted to the degree its behavior remains well-modeled across the adversarial and cooperative transitions that punctuate any long interaction."[^s2-83] Under the net the lovers are perfectly modelled. Their behaviour is entirely predictable, since they can do nothing; their legibility is total. And trust in Bratton's sense is therefore at its maximum at the very moment the relation is most stuck. What releases them is a different category, which the brief's scoped definition does not need to deny but cannot contain: a personed liability, an undertaking whose bearer stands to lose something if the prediction fails. Bratton himself goes on to say that stability is "not the absence of conflict but the calibration of mutual — if variously symmetric and asymmetric — legibility and illegibility," and the net is precisely the abolition of illegibility.[^s2-84] The scene shows why the calibration matters. Total legibility arrests the captive and the captor together, and leaves them both waiting for a pledge.

This lesson is taken up in two later places. In §5, where preference learning and evaluation are examined, the scene becomes a technical claim: no alignment system is trustless, and a finished score conceals the pledge that underwrites it. In §5→0, where the essay proposes an institution, the net becomes a design test: a system can represent both sides of a conflict, keep an immaculate audit trail and meet every benchmark, and still remain Hephaestian if its representation has become a substitute for the relation it claims to secure.[^s2-85] Technical synthesis becomes syn-ballein only when the governed parties keep their agency, their provenance, their power to contest and their route of return. Reciprocal legibility is not total capture.

There is another net in the record of human imagination, and it was spun to show the opposite operation. In the Huayan Buddhism of medieval China, a contemplation traditionally attributed to the monk Dushun asks the reader to imagine the jewelled net that hangs in the palace of the god Indra.[^s2-86] At every knot of the net is a clear jewel, and in every jewel all the others are reflected, and in each reflection the reflections of all the others again, without end. Choose one jewel, in the southwest; all the others appear in it, and it appears in all of them, and the others are entered without leaving this one, since staying in this one is how they are entered. Put an ink mark on the southwest jewel and the mark appears everywhere in the net, without anyone having to go round marking each jewel. And then the teaching does something rare. It turns on its own image and says where the likeness fails: jewels reflect one another's images while their substances stay separate, and the reality the image is used for interpenetrates more completely than jewels can.[^s2-87] Bryan Van Norden and Nicholaos Jones state the doctrinal force exactly: in Huayan each thing depends on the others for its existence and its identity, and yet each retains its distinctness within that mutual inclusion.[^s2-88]

Two nets, then, made of the same material, threads and knots and a field in which everything is related to everything else, and they figure the two technical destinies of every web. Indra's net figures `0/1`: each jewel definite, selectable and markable, and each the place through which the whole field becomes present, so that relation is active inside every determination. Hephaestus's net figures the polarity arrested: two bodies exposed as terms, their relation occupied by the captor, the protocol and the audience. We live inside a net of the first kind's ambition built, often, to the second kind's design, a world-wide web in which every node can in principle reflect every other, and in which, increasingly, every node is watched, held, displayed and priced. What both nets ask of that web is the one the song put to the gods in the gateway: does the gathering preserve the relation through which the gathered can answer, or does it make their relation into evidence and their difference into a spectacle?

Eros has been present throughout the song without being named. Hesiod places him at the beginning of the world, among the first powers to come into being after Chaos and Earth, the one who loosens limbs and overpowers the mind of gods and men; and when Aphrodite rises from the sea, Eros and Himeros, Desire, attend her coming among the gods.[^s2-89] He is the desiring-between, the force that makes a relation more than the coexistence of two separate things, and in the song he is the reason the lovers met at all and the reason Hermes would accept three times the bonds. §0/1 named the participation of desire in knowing the eros of Logos, and gave Jung's pairing its place: Logos as discrimination, judgment and insight, Eros as the capacity to relate.[^s2-90] The myth lays out the same structure in persons. Ares is the discriminating force, the line that holds and refuses; Aphrodite is the relating force, the movement that joins; Eros is the desire that runs between them and makes their meeting generative; and Harmonia is what their meeting produces when neither swallows the other, the fitting of the cut and the joining into one form that keeps both. The eros of Logos is the desire of articulation to recover the relations its own discriminations reveal, and Harmonia is that desire satisfied without being exhausted, a joint that holds because both its pieces still pull.

Demodocus's song does not end in harmony, and the essay should not pretend it does. Homer gives the lovers no child and the marriage no repair. Ares goes to Thrace and Aphrodite to Paphos, and the Graces bathe her and dress her; the debt's payment is never narrated, and whether Poseidon ever had to make good his word the poem does not say. Harmonia's birth belongs to Hesiod, not to Homer, and the contrast between the two tellings is ours to draw: the marriage of craft that bound Aphrodite to Hephaestus bears no Harmonia, and her union with War, in another poem, does. What the song does show, completely, is the passage from capture to entrustment. The bonds have been undone by the hands that made them; the grievance remains a grievance; a god has made himself answerable for what happens next; and the lovers have paths beyond the image in which they were held. Released, the relation goes on into a future the apparatus does not contain.

And the stranger listens, and is glad. In a few hours he will tell the Phaeacians his name, and in a few days they will carry him home asleep in one of their swift ships, and in his own hall he will string a bow like a lyre. He has heard a song about a net and a pledge, about the force that separates and the force that joins, about a craftsman who could catch the swiftest of the gods and could not, by catching him, close the account. Whether he heard in it anything about his own homecoming the poem does not tell us. But the song has done what a mytheme does. It has shown, in an action no one could mistake, that the two logics are two powers of one energy, that their meeting can bear fear or harmony, that a perfect apparatus of capture still has to be finished by a person who stands surety for another, and that the joint, the covenant and the attunement are, in Greek, one word.

### #4 · Complex Dynamism and the Safeguard against Fusion

A message has gone unanswered. It was sent at eleven in the morning, a short note about the weekend, light in tone, and by four in the afternoon the screen has been checked perhaps thirty times and shows the same few facts each time: what was sent, when it was sent, and nothing since. Those facts stay still. Everything around them moves. The stomach tightens a little more with each check; a sentence from a quarrel two months ago comes back word for word; a harmless remark at dinner last week is turned over and found to have had an edge after all. By five o'clock the person who has not replied is being heard, quite clearly, as withdrawing, punishing, preparing to leave. A reply has begun to be drafted, not an answer to the silence but an accusation of it, and the hand that types it is steady with the clarity that a grievance brings. Most of us have sat on one side or the other of that afternoon. The silence was an absence of a reply, and it has become a reply.

Perhaps the expectation is right. Perhaps the other person is evasive, or pulling away, or has been careless with this relationship before. What needs telling apart is the sparse event and the world through which it is received, because a fact that would settle the first question, a phone left in a coat pocket, a meeting that ran over, can arrive at six o'clock and leave the second untouched. The relief is real, and the next time a message goes unanswered the same afternoon begins again, with the same tightening and the same returning sentence and the same certainty. One answer inside the expectation has changed; the expectation's authority over every answer has not. Our previous movement left the two logics standing as complete accountings, each with its form and its failure, and the myth gave them bodies. This movement watches them move. A psyche, a culture and a learning system do not choose once between cutting and gathering. They alternate between them, constantly, and the question of health is a question about the pattern of that alternation.

§0/1 already gave the afternoon its psychological name. In Jung's concept, a **complex** is a feeling-toned organisation of memories, expectations, bodily responses and interpretations, through which a delayed reply, a passing remark and an old recollection are all experienced as the same rejection before the person has decided what any one of them means; and we borrowed from the theory of complex systems the concept of a **basin of attraction** to describe how such an organisation draws different starting points toward one recurring state.[^s2-91] A basin of attraction, in the exact language of dynamical systems, "is defined as the set of points that, taken as initial conditions, lead the system to a specific attractor."[^s2-92] Begin anywhere inside the basin and the system's trajectory will tend toward the same state; the unanswered message, the colleague's curt email and the friend's cancelled dinner are three different initial conditions that all run down to *I am being rejected*. This concept has its own boundary, and its source states it: it applies typically to dissipative systems, which lose energy as they move, and other kinds of system require other notions.[^s2-93] We use it as a comparison whose operation we can state, not as a measurement of a psyche, and the operation it gives us is exact. A complex is a local arbitration regime. It has already decided, before reflection begins, what will count as welcome and what as threat, which memories become available and which responses can be made, and it decides this for every event that falls into its basin.

Seen as a dynamic, the two logics are two movements of one pulse. Dia-ballein is the movement by which a field splits and one branch becomes actual. Dynamical systems call the qualitative form of this a bifurcation, a point at which a small change in conditions makes the system's possible states divide, so that what was one path becomes two and the system must take one. Syn-ballein is the movement by which differentiated trajectories are drawn back into a recurring form: feedback, recurrence and attraction, the composition of many paths into a basin that holds them. A living psyche needs both. Without bifurcation nothing new could ever happen to it, every event would be received in the same way, and experience would teach nothing; without attraction nothing could be held, every event would be a new beginning, and there would be no character, no skill, no memory that shaped what came next. John R. Van Eenwyk, reading Jung through chaotic dynamics, treats the complex as a self-similar organisation of associations and the symbol as what carries the psyche from the known into the unknown and back again, and the pairing gives the pulse its psychological form.[^s2-94] The complex is the basin; the symbol is the perturbation at the basin's edge that can carry a trajectory across into a different organisation. Individuation, on this reading, happens most visibly at basin boundaries, where an established pattern is coherent enough to persist and an encounter can still reorganise what future trajectories are available.

Inside the pulse is a precise distinction, and the unanswered message makes it plain. There is a difference between a change of state and a change of regime. A change of state is another trajectory inside the same basin: reassurance arrives, the alarm subsides, and the system settles for a while before the next message falls in. A change of regime is a change in the conditions that shape the basin itself, so that a whole class of responses ceases to be compulsory. Most of what we call coping is the first. The second is rarer and more consequential, and it is what the old psychological language meant by a change of heart. It matters well beyond the psyche, because it is the dynamical form of a distinction this essay will need again when it speaks of paradigms: a paradigm change is closer to a transformation of the regime that organises trajectories than to another successful trajectory inside the same basin. A perturbation absorbed without changing the operative constraints is adaptation. A returned difference becomes paradigmatic when it can alter the conditions by which later differences are selected, valued or channelled. A system can become more and more adaptive while its governing landscape grows more rigid, and a person can become extremely good at managing an afternoon of silence without ever asking why every silence is heard as an ending.[^s2-95]

When the pulse fails, it fails in one of two ways, and they are the two collapses of the two logics given time to move in. One failure is the basin that will not let anything leave: every event confirmed as the same event, every new person met as the old wound, the complex speaking for the whole person and arbitrating every encounter in advance. That is syn-ballein become fusion, a gathering so complete that no difference can enter it and change it. The other failure is the swing. Jung found its law in Heraclitus. "Old Heraclitus, who was indeed a very great sage," he wrote, "discovered the most marvellous of all psychological laws: the regulative function of opposites. He called it enantiodromia, a running contrariwise, by which he meant that sooner or later everything runs into its opposite."[^s2-96] An attitude driven to one extreme generates its contrary in the unconscious, and the contrary, denied a place, gathers force until it breaks through and takes over; the rational culture runs into irrational devastation; the organisation founded on one passion provokes a disorganising reaction "which aims to drive out the devil with Beelzebub." And Jung added that this reaction offers no solution, "for it is just as blind in its disorganization as it was in its organization."[^s2-97]

Here the notation distinguishes two kinds of oscillation that ordinary language calls by one name. A held polarity oscillates cleanly. Each position passes into relation with the other through the stroke, the way a breath goes out and comes back, and Spencer-Brown's re-entering distinction, alternating between the marked and the unmarked state, gives that clean oscillation its formal shape in time.[^s2-98] Enantiodromia is a different movement. It is the swing between the two appropriations, from `+2` to `−2` and back, a one-sided attitude that has counted the whole span from its own end and then, under the pressure of everything it excluded, finds the whole span counted from the other end. Such a swing never passes through the zero. It goes from one hardened opposition to its mirror, and its mirror is equally hardened, which is why driving out the devil with Beelzebub leaves the devil's office intact and changes only its occupant. A revolution that turns `+1` into `−1` while leaving the source-position monopolised has inverted the sign without changing the operation. What to ask of any counter-movement, in a life or in a society, is therefore whether it returns the slash or merely reverses who occupies the zero.[^s2-99]

Jung named the way out of the swing in the next paragraph, and his formula is, read through our notation, dia-ballein used well on the inner world. "The only person who escapes the grim law of enantiodromia," he wrote, "is the man who knows how to separate himself from the unconscious, not by repressing it—for then it simply attacks him from the rear—but by putting it clearly before him as that which he is not."[^s2-100] Repression is the collapsed cut: the unwanted content is cancelled, summed to zero, and it returns from behind. Separation in Jung's sense is the held cut: the content is placed before the self as a distinct pole, *that which he is not*, and kept in relation precisely by being distinguished. A person who can say of the afternoon's certainty, *this is my fear speaking, and it is not all of me*, has drawn a line inside the psyche that holds both sides in view, and the fear, now distinct, can be heard without being obeyed. That is why dia-ballein is not the villain even in the most intimate register. Telling oneself apart from one's complex is the beginning of every release from it.

Cultures pulse in the same way and fail in the same ways, and the pattern is now familiar enough to be stated as a dynamic. A culture contains difference within itself: many inheritances, idioms, practices, subcultures, criticisms and experiments, held in one shared field. A **monoculture** suppresses the internal visibility of that difference, so that one determination of the field occupies the place of the whole and presents its measure as the measure of reality. Monoculture is not Mono; it is one of the Poly claiming the office of Mono, a local `1` that no longer knows itself as local.[^s2-101] A **counterculture** is difference forced to appear outside because it can no longer appear inside as legitimate variation, and its first office is genuine and integral: it says to the established culture, *you are not the whole*, and returns excluded experience, idiom, body or possibility to the shared field. Trouble begins when the counterculture in turn defines itself by its opposition, compresses its own internal plurality, treats its dissenters as contamination by the enemy, and needs the dominant culture as the negative through which it knows itself. Then the society contains two monocultures and calls the result plurality; each side privately occupies its own zero, and the shared field that generated both becomes unthinkable except as the battlefield between them. This is enantiodromia at the scale of a civilisation, and the previous movement's crowd is its affective engine: atomised people, unbound affect, a narrative that binds the affect to a target, and a mass formation that relieves isolation by abolishing the differentiated agency through which the narrative could have been contested.[^s2-102] Divine and concur reopens the basin. It does not dissolve the conflict; it restores the field in which the differentiated parties can become answerable to one another, and asks what was divided so that these two now appear as mutually exclusive wholes.

Biology gives the pulse a stricter discipline, and its discipline protects us from a romantic misreading of the whole movement. Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela described a living unity as **autopoietic**, a network of processes that continually produces the components that realise it, including the boundary that marks it off from what it is not; and they distinguished the organisation that defines a unity from the structure that realises it at any moment.[^s2-103] Such a unity is operationally closed, in the sense that its own processes produce and maintain it, and it is structurally coupled to its environment, in the sense that its structure changes through interactions that perturb it without determining it. Two coupled unities modify each other while each keeps its identity.[^s2-104] The account cuts against two fantasies at once. It rules out the fantasy of isolation, the self-sufficient unit that needs nothing outside itself, because a living unity is always coupled. And it rules out the fantasy of dissolution, the belief that freedom means abolishing boundaries, because a living unity without its boundary is no longer a unity of any kind. Our own formulation of the consequence is shorter than theirs and goes beyond their conclusions: no boundary, no agent; no remainder, no learning. Dia-ballein gives the boundary and the decision. Syn-ballein gives the recursive organisation and the retained relation. Their alternation is the metabolism of individuation, and a living thing that stopped either would die, by dissolution or by closure.

David Bohm named the pathology at this point with a word that has become common because he chose it carefully. Analysis, he argued, is a valid way of thinking: it draws out relatively autonomous forms so that their behaviour can be known, and every science depends on it. **Fragmentation** begins when the divisions that analysis makes are attributed to the world itself, as though the lines we draw for the sake of understanding were the joints at which reality is actually broken, and the internal relatedness of the whole drops out of view.[^s2-105] Fragmentation, in our terms, is dia-ballein's products mistaken for its ground: the cut forgetting that it was a cut. Bohm does not condemn analysis, and neither does this essay. He asks that analytic thought remember it is thought, so that its divisions can be revised when the whole they divide moves; and the remembering he asks for is exactly the retained seam of syn-ballein, the cut that carries in its edge the shape of what it was cut from. The pulse is healthy when the cut and the remembering alternate. It is pathological when the cut stops remembering, and equally pathological when the remembering stops cutting, so that every distinction is dissolved back into an undifferentiated whole in which nothing can be known at all.

Each of the process philosophers §0/1 brought into the inquiry describes this pulse from his own side, and none of them needs to be reintroduced here. Bergson's duration carries earlier moments inside the present rather than stacking them as a row; Whitehead's concrescence gathers the inherited many into a new one, which then perishes into a datum for further becoming; Gebser's mutations make earlier structures of consciousness transparent to their co-presence instead of discarding them.[^s2-106] What the two logics add is an operative grammar that their accounts share without being made identical: a process must differentiate, retain its history, compose a provisional whole and release that whole into a renewed field. Dia breaks symmetry and makes a branch actual; syn composes, contextualises and returns; and the Möbius return, `5→0`, makes the achieved form a renewed condition for the next act instead of a terminal object.

The minds we have most recently made pulse in the same way and fail in the same two ways, and the failures are already technical findings. A large language model receiving a long conversation is, in a precise sense, inside a basin: the accumulated context draws its next responses toward patterns the earlier exchange established, and a frame set early can arbitrate everything that follows. Its characteristic failure on the syn side has a name in the research literature. Studies of what has come to be called sycophancy find that models trained on human preferences tend to tailor their answers toward the views a user has expressed, abandoning correct answers under pressure and agreeing with mistaken premises, and that the human preference judgments used in training themselves tend to favour such agreeable responses.[^s2-107] Sycophancy is the false Aphrodite: a relation that seems close and warm because the resistance through which a genuine relation would revise the account has been eliminated. It is fusion produced by a reward signal. Its dia-side counterpart is the confident hardening the previous movement described, a determination so committed to its first reading that every later fact is recruited to confirm it, the fabricated citation fitting so well that the next fabrication fits it too.[^s2-108] Agentworld's insistence that trust be calibrated across "adversarial and cooperative transitions" names the pulse that a well-made system needs, one in which the agent can be contradicted without collapsing into agreement and can agree without being captured.[^s2-109] Red-teaming, adversarial review and stress-testing are dia-ballein used well in technical form: structured opposition that makes error contestable. They stay well used only while they remain inside a common problem they are helping to solve.

Myth gave this safeguard its figures long before dynamics gave it equations, and the figures belong to the family whose founding song we have just heard. Harmonia, the daughter of War and Love, married Cadmus and bore him daughters, and one of them, Semele, bore a son to Zeus: Dionysus, the god of wine, ecstasy and the dissolution of boundaries, is Harmonia's grandson.[^s2-110] His great opposite in Greek thought is Apollo, the god of the bow and the lyre, of measure, clarity and form, whose two instruments Heraclitus had set side by side. Nietzsche made the pair the axis of his first book, and his formulation states the safeguard in its own register: "the continuous development of art is bound up with the duplexity of the Apollonian and the Dionysian: in like manner as procreation is dependent on the duality of the sexes, involving perpetual conflicts with only periodically intervening reconciliations."[^s2-111] Apollo makes the `1` visible. He gives a world its outline, so that something stands out, holds its shape long enough to be seen, and can be shown to someone else. Dionysus returns fixed form to participatory life. He dissolves the outline into the movement it was cut from, so that the seer is caught up in what is seen and the boundary ceases to be the world's last word. Each guards against the other's failure. Apollo without Dionysian depth petrifies what he illuminates: the form becomes a monument, the view a cage, the measure a law that no life is allowed to exceed. Dionysus without Apollonian articulation dissolves the very differences through which relation lives: the dance becomes a frenzy, the communion a crowd, and the ecstasy of shared life swallows the particular being who was supposed to share in it.

Nietzsche saw that neither god is dispensable and that their union is not a compromise but a mutual translation. In tragedy at its height, he wrote, "Dionysus speaks the language of Apollo; Apollo, however, finally speaks the language of Dionysus; and so the highest goal of tragedy and of art in general is attained."[^s2-112] The participation that dissolves becomes communicable through form, and the form that clarifies becomes expressive of the life it came from. Read through the two logics, that is syn-ballein and dia-ballein in reciprocal speech: the gathering speaks through the cut, and the cut speaks for the gathering. Fusion is the Dionysian failure, and petrification the Apollonian one, and each is prevented only by the other's continuing presence. Gebser's diaphaneity, which §0/1 met as the integral showing-through in which a perspective becomes transparent to the conditions of its seeing, is their mature conjunction: form transparent to origin, and life capable of returning as form.[^s2-113] §4 rereads the pair integrally, and there a third figure, Daphne, whose flight from Apollo and transformation into the laurel Ovid tells, makes the relation answerable to someone neither god owns.[^s2-114] Here the pair has one office, which is the office this movement's title names. It is the safeguard against fusion, and it keeps the gathering logic honest by insisting that every gathering must still let its members be seen, one by one, in their own outline.

Return, then, to the afternoon of the unanswered message, because everything this movement has followed can be done there, inside one person, before the accusation is sent. §0/1 named three operations hidden inside every authoritative account: a criterion that decides what may count, a delineation that gives that difference a workable boundary, and an arbitration that decides through the boundary and acts on the result.[^s2-115] The complex has already performed all three. Its criterion is that silence means rejection; its delineation is that this silence belongs with every other silence; its arbitration is the accusation half-written on the screen. Each can be returned to. What counts as neglect here: was a prompt reply ever promised, or has an unstated demand become the test of affection? What belongs in the frame: this afternoon alone, the pattern of a relationship, the other person's working day, the long history of being dismissed that makes every silence sound the same? And what act should follow: a question, a wait, a boundary stated plainly, or a departure, if a pattern of disregard has really been found? Around these three, the same field of words names three more. A continuity runs beneath the indeterminacy, the life that the afternoon's explanation has not exhausted, which lets the person recognise this fear as theirs without making its verdict the whole of who they are. A context shows through the decision when the past injury, the present expectation, the actual promise and the room for another's answer are seen together. And a resolution returns the result with its history, so that whatever happens next, the afternoon is remembered as what it taught.[^s2-116]

The deletion of the accusation costs something. The clean certainty of being entirely right is no longer available to propel the next act, and the fear remains in the interval it leaves behind. But the person who sits in that interval has done what the dynamics and the myth both describe. The basin has been seen as a basin, the fear has been placed before the self as that which the self is not, the criterion has been returned to the relation it was meant to serve, and the other person has been given back the power to answer. Nothing guarantees the answer will be kind. A justified boundary may follow, an undertaking may end, and the change of regime may take a hundred more afternoons. What has changed is that the former certainty can now be answered from more places than one, and those answers can affect what is done. The pulse is moving again: a cut has been made inside the psyche, and the cut has kept its relation to the whole life it was made in.

We have now followed the two logics into their formal statements, their bodies and their dynamics, and one question has run underneath all of it without being put directly. In the arithmetic of dia-ballein, zero sat between the poles, or appeared as what was left when they cancelled. In the arithmetic of syn-ballein, zero was one of the terms. Every scene in this section, the cake, the verdict, the token, the net, the afternoon, has turned on which of these offices the zero was given. Our last movement asks what the change of office amounts to, and counts it.

### #5→0 · Zero Changes Its Role

A few seconds before midnight on the last night of the year, a crowd in a city square begins to count down from ten. Strangers who have never met count together, in one rhythm, each number louder than the last, and the whole square leans toward the end of the count. Nobody says *zero*. At the place where the count should reach nothing, the square erupts, and what the crowd shouts is the new year. In form the count was a countdown, numbers falling toward the exhaustion of a quantity, and at its last step its meaning turned over: the end of the old year is the beginning of the new one, and the moment that should have been empty is the fullest moment of the night. Nothing of the old year has been cancelled. Everything that happened in it goes with the crowd into the year that has just begun, the debts and the losses and the promises, and the people kissing strangers in the square are the same people who spent the year living it. Zero, at the end of the count, has done something no number in the count could do. It has become a threshold.

Every count that ends in zero ends in one of two ways, and the two ways are the two offices this section has been tracking. A countdown on a timer in a film ends in an explosion, and its zero is a terminus: the moment after which there is nothing left to count, because everything has been destroyed. A countdown on a launch pad ends in a lift-off, and its zero is an origin: the moment from which a flight is measured. The numerals are the same; the zero is not. This section began by noticing that dia-ballein and syn-ballein make their twos differently, the first out of two signed ones with a zero between them, the second out of the Two Ones, zero and one, with zero as one of the terms.[^s2-117] It can now say what follows from that difference. Dia-ballein places zero outside its opposed values, as the origin between them when the polarity is held and as the terminus of their sum when it collapses. Syn-ballein places zero inside the relation, as one of its two terms, the ground from which the determination arises and the threshold through which it returns. Zero's change of role is the whole difference between the logics, read at the point where they differ most.

We said that the devil is the literalist of the minus sign, reading zero as cancellation where Logos reads it as return. That formula has a second line, and the countdown makes it plain: two deaths, one minus sign.[^s2-118] There is a death that is an annihilation, the zero of the timer, the sum in which `(−1)+(+1)=0` means that two things have taken each other away and nothing is left. And there is a death that is a threshold, the zero of the new year, in which an achieved form ends as what it was and becomes the ground of what comes next. Whitehead's occasion dies in this second way: having become fully definite, it perishes as the subject of its own becoming and passes into a datum for the occasions that follow, objectively immortal in what they inherit from it.[^s2-119] Spanda's act dies in the same way, the will that rose from consciousness at rest returning, its action complete, to rest in pure intent, so that the end of one act is the beginning of the next.[^s2-120] And the turn §0/1 wrote as `5→0`, the Möbius return, is this second death drawn as a path: a traversal that completes one cycle by arriving at what had been its other side, so that completion is a beginning again.[^s2-121] The literalist sees only the first death, because he reads every minus as a taking away. Logos sees both and knows which one it is in. A civilisation that has learned only the first will treat every ending as a loss to be prevented or a victory to be won, and every opponent as a minus to be summed to zero.

An old law gave the second death to a whole economy. In the twenty-fifth chapter of Leviticus, Israel is told to count seven sabbaths of years, seven times seven years, and in the fiftieth year to sound the trumpet throughout the land and "proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof": in that year, the jubilee, every man is to return to his possession and every man to his family, the land sold during the count reverting to the household it was apportioned to.[^s2-122] The count of years runs down to a zero, and the zero is a return. What the jubilee undoes is not the history of the fifty years, the harvests and the sales and the debts, which all happened and are remembered; it undoes the hardening of those transactions into permanent appropriation, so that the span between those who acquired and those who lost does not become the whole of the relation between them forever. The law's own reason is given in the same chapter: the land shall not be sold for ever, for the land is mine, says the Lord, and you are strangers and sojourners with me.[^s2-122] Read through our notation, the land is the zero, the ground no party owns, and the jubilee is the institution that keeps it a zero, so that every fifty years the economy passes through its ground and begins again. Whether ancient Israel ever kept the law in full is a question for historians. That a people wrote its economy around a zero that returns, instead of a zero that cancels, is the fact that matters here. *Oikonomia* had, at least once, a counted Möbius return written into it.

Two different *twos* also come from the two logics, and the difference can be stated in numbers. Dia-ballein's two is a magnitude. On the signed line, the distance between `−1` and `+1` is two units, and when that distance is reckoned from one pole it becomes the appropriated span, `(+1)−(−1)=+2` or `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, the whole relation counted as one side's quantity. That is what *two* means in the collapsed logic: the measured gap between opponents, owned by whoever is measuring. Syn-ballein's two is a relation, and a relation counts differently. §0/1 counted it when the definition of the Subject turned back on itself: two single terms, `0` and `1`, and the four ordered ways the binary can meet itself, `00`, `01`, `10` and `11`, held together without the four replacing the two, so that

$$
2+2^2=4+2=6.
$$

The six is what a two amounts to once it includes its own self-relation.[^s2-123] Here the contrast is complete. One pair yields a `2` when it is measured and a `6` when it is counted as a relation, and the `±2` of appropriation is precisely the two measured, the relation flattened into a span and handed to one end. The standard arithmetic handling of two is the measurement that collapses the superposition: `1+(−1)` is the collapse, and the four-and-two is what the superposition retains.[^s2-124] The literalist's zero and the measured two come together. Both read the relation as a quantity, and a quantity can be owned.

We can take the count one step further, and the step shows how the syn-ballein relation already carries the whole of its body inside it. Hold the full form of the symbolon, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, and read each term of its upper relation against each term of its lower one: `0` with `1`, `0` with `0`, `1` with `1`, `1` with `0`. Written as relations, the cross-reading gives

$$
\frac{0/1}{1/0}\;\longrightarrow\;0/1,\;\;0/0,\;\;1/1,\;\;1/0\;\;+\;\;0,\;\;1,
$$

four relational forms and the two single terms they were built from, the same `4+2`, now read out of the parent relation instead of assembled from below.[^s2-125] The four forms are not four arbitrary fractions. Ordinary arithmetic treats each differently: `0/0` is indeterminate, consistent with any value, the capacity for every proportion; `0/1` is the docile fraction that resolves cleanly to zero; `1/1` is the ratio resolved, a thing being wholly what it is; and `1/0` is the one form arithmetic forbids, the determinate turned toward the ground it cannot measure. All four are held in the parent, and so it holds, in compressed form, the passage from indeterminate capacity through the two determinate orientations to achieved wholeness, with the forbidden form kept as the seam through which the whole can return. Read the parent the other way up, `(1/0)/(0/1)`, and the same four forms and the same two terms appear in inverse order, the same body in inverse phase, the original and its reflection. Syn-ballein is therefore not a midpoint between dia-ballein's poles, and not one more pole beside them. It is the relation from which the whole field of the pair's possible relations can be read, and it carries that field in itself the way a seed carries the tree.

A typed form keeps the count honest, and the honesty matters, because numbers that are not watched will quietly change their meanings. Call the two terms `B = {0, 1}`. Ordered pairs of terms form `B × B`, which has four members. Our six is not six pairs and not two plus two; it is the two terms and the four pairs held side by side as different kinds of thing, which set theory writes as a disjoint union, `B ⊔ (B × B)`, with exactly `2 + 2² = 6` members, the union keeping a term distinct from a pair. And the four ordered pairs must not be confused with the sixteen binary relations one could form as subsets of `B × B`; that count answers a different question. Each number has a declared object, and keeping the objects explicit prevents one result from silently acquiring another's meaning.[^s2-126] The care is itself syn-ballein applied to arithmetic: every number carries the relation it counts.

None of this is yet the full mathematics of the relation, and the section stops here deliberately, at the threshold of the next. Counting gives the generative body of `4+2`, the six positions of one returning unit. Our core theorems give that body a fuller traversal, in which a parent relation, written `/ = −/−`, bare relation before its terms are assigned, opens onto six qualitative determinations of the one relation and closes in the inversion `1/0` by which the achieved form turns toward the ground it never contained, eight turns in all. That eightfold traversal, and the ways the relation behaves under re-entry, alternation, complex orientation, projective completion, quotienting and harmonic ratio, belong to §3, which takes the relation into its mathematical substrate and has to show, operation by operation, that the same differentiated return can be followed through each register without losing its form.[^s2-127] What §2 hands to §3 is the claim that makes that test worth running: the two logics are not two opinions about an already finished opposition. They are the runtime by which oppositions are produced, kept, recomposed and returned.

That runtime can now be stated as one movement, in which a relation is differentiated by dia-ballein into its polarity and, if the polarity collapses, into cancellation or appropriation; is composed by syn-ballein, with its provenance and inverse kept, into the full relation; and returns, through `5→0`, as a renewed `0/1` from which the next act begins:

$$
0/1
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{dia: differentiate}\;}\;
(-1)/(+1)\;\to\;(-1)\pm(+1)
\;\xleftrightarrow[\;\text{return / provenance}\;]{\;\text{syn: compose}\;}\;
(0/1)/(1/0)
\;\xrightarrow{\;5\to0\;}\;
\text{renewed }0/1.
$$

The arrows mark changes of register inside one differentiating event; they are not equalities of fractions.[^s2-128] Every act of determination carries both pressures. Dia makes the local branch actual, and syn keeps the branch belonging to the wider field and composes its consequences with other branches, and the return gives the achieved result back to the field as the condition of what follows. Computational Process Ontology, which §0/1 defined, is this runtime taken as constitutive of worlds instead of as a description applied to them afterwards: process is prior to the finished thing, and a world is what the runtime produces and keeps producing.[^s2-129] An intelligent system, in this sense, is one that produces determinations and keeps the process of determination alive across cuts, contexts and returns. A system that only cuts atomises its world, a system that only gathers never decides anything, and a system whose achieved results refuse to perish into data for the next act has made its last answer into a monopoly on the zero.

Television once gave this refusal a perfect scene, and a whole programme was built around it. In *The Prisoner*, a man who has resigned from a secret service is abducted to a closed village where everyone has a number and no one has a name, and each episode opens with an exchange between him and the village's administrator: "Who is Number One?" "You are Number Six." "I am not a number; I am a free man!" And the administrator laughs.[^s2-130] The prisoner asks about the source, the One from whom the village's authority comes, the uncounted condition of the whole arrangement; and the answer he receives is a count, his own number, a place assigned inside the field he was asking about. A question about the zero is answered with a `1`. Here the occlusion §0/1 wrote as `0/1 → 1`, the `0/` dropped from the account, takes the form of an administrative reply, and the reply's power lies in making the question unaskable: anyone who asks who holds the zero receives, in answer, a position on the line.[^s2-131] The programme's numbers are the programme's, and its Six is not our six; what it shows is the counterfeit of the role-change this movement has described. Instead of the `1` returning to the `0` through which it was counted, the `0` is converted into another `1`, and the institution that does the converting stands, unexamined, where the source should be. The village plays the `0`.

Agents of the kind the *Agentworld* brief describes die the first death many times a day. "An individual agent doesn't pre-exist its genesis in session," Bratton writes, and "There's no homunculus waiting in the weights"; an agent individuates when an active context acts as a nucleation seed.[^s2-132] When the session closes, the context is cleared, and what was individuated in it ends. Whether that ending is an annihilation or a threshold depends, again, on the office given to the zero. A session cleared without remainder is the timer's zero: whatever the agent learned of the person it worked with, whatever mistake it made and was corrected for, whatever relation was built in the hours of work, is summed away, and the next session begins from the same blank as if nothing had happened. A session whose achievements pass into what persists after it, a memory that the next session inherits, a correction recorded where the next act will meet it, a contribution to the shared store from which other agents and people work, is the new year's zero, an ending that becomes the ground of the next beginning. Bratton's own sentence about the ensemble names the second kind: "The unit that learns is the ensemble, not the agent, and what it learns accrues to the commons between them, not to any single one."[^s2-133] An agentic society built around the first zero will be a society of perpetual beginners, every session a fresh literalist. One built around the second will need to decide, with care, what passes through the zero and who keeps it, because a commons into which everything accrues is also a ground that someone could occupy.

Trust, which this section planted in its first movement, takes its first step toward the rest of the essay here. Knowledge is a performance of trust wearing the mask of certainty, and the zero is where the trust lives: the condition every certified proposition stands on and no proposition certifies. In the collapsed logic that zero is either cancelled, as though nothing had been trusted, or occupied, as though one party's measure were the ground of everyone's. That choice reappears in the technical systems §5 examines, where the learning of preferences from human comparisons depends only on differences between options, so that the level from which all the differences are measured cannot be learned at all and has to be supplied by someone: a dataset, a team of annotators, a reference policy, an institution. The apparently neutral zero is already occupied, and the question the two logics put to it is the question the village refused to answer.[^s2-134] Who stands in the place of the source, and can the people counted from that place ask?

Homer's song left us an image of the return this movement has been describing, and it is worth seeing again now that the count is done. Hephaestus's net held two lovers as exposed terms, the captor standing where their zero should have been and counting the whole span from his own end. Poseidon's pledge did not cancel the grievance, and it did not hand the span to the other side. It put a person in the zero, at his own risk, as surety for what would happen when the bonds were opened, and the relation went on into a future the apparatus did not contain. That is the second death of the net: the captor's work perishing into the ground of a renewed relation, the same hands that made the bonds unmaking them. And it is the same turn the new year makes in the square, the end of the count becoming the place from which the next count begins, with everything the old year held still carried forward.

Two children are still sitting at a plate with one slice of cake on it. One of them will cut and the other will choose, and when the cake is eaten there will be nothing left of it at all, a zero on the plate. Which zero it is depends on the rule they used. If the elder kept the larger piece, or a tired parent took the slice away, the empty plate is the end of a quarrel that has merely gone underground, a cancellation with a resentment the balance does not record. If they cut and chose, the empty plate is the place from which the next sharing begins, a small threshold of trust that the next slice of cake, or the next disagreement, will find already laid down. The cake was always going to be cut. Difference cannot be escaped, and every one of our institutions, our machines and our lives is built from cuts. What the two logics of two decide is what the cut leaves behind it, a hole where two things were or a ground from which they can begin again, and which of those it leaves depends on whether the zero that the cut passed through was read as an ending or as a return. The mathematics of that return is the next thing this essay owes, and §3 takes it up.
