### #0 · Difference Cannot Be Escaped

There is one slice of cake left on the plate and two children who both want it, and somewhere in most families an old rule settles the matter: one of you cuts, the other chooses. The elder takes the knife. She is not in a hurry, and the care she takes is visible, because she knows that whatever she cuts, her brother will choose first and leave her the remainder; every millimetre she shaves off one side to favour herself is a millimetre she will probably lose. So she lines up the knife, looks again, moves it a little, and cuts as close to the middle as her eye and hand can manage. Her brother leans in, inspects both pieces with the gravity of a jeweller, and takes one. Neither child has been made generous by the rule, and neither has been asked to love the other more than before. There are still two pieces, two children, and two offices, the one who divides and the one who chooses, and the rule depends on all of that difference staying in place. What the rule changes is where the cut answers. Because the chooser's choice will come back upon it, the cut is made under the eye of the person who did not make it, and the knife is honest because the relation between the two children has been built into the procedure that divides the cake.

Change one thing and the scene becomes a different event with the same knife. Let the elder cut and also keep whichever piece she likes, and the cut is made from one side only: whatever she does, the span between the larger piece and the smaller is counted from her end. Let a tired parent cut and hand the pieces out without a word, and the cut answers to the parent's judgment, which may be fair and may be weary, but which neither child can return upon. Let the parent take the slice away altogether because there is fighting, and the dispute has been settled by removing what was disputed, so that nothing remains to divide and nothing remains to quarrel over, and the children are left with a balance of zero and an evening's resentment the balance does not record. In each version the cake is cut or withdrawn, a difference is made, and the difference is real. What changes from version to version is whether the act that made the difference can still be answered by the people it divides.

Difference cannot be escaped. A child cannot eat half a cake that no one has cut, a perception cannot register a shape that does not stand out from its ground, a word that could not be told apart from every other word would say nothing, and a refusal without an edge is not yet a refusal. Every act of knowing we have followed since the bedside has been an act of this kind. The adult's "because" drew a line between a good reason and an impertinent question; the name held one child apart from every other; the definition of the Subject failed at the exact place where its distinction had to be made; the formal systems of the twentieth century were built to make every cut explicit, and found at their foundations a cut they could not make. Mind, as this essay has defined it, is differentiating activity: awareness becomes a world by cutting, comparing, excluding, relating and keeping its distinctions. Whether to divide has never been the live question, since nothing appears without division. It is whether a distinction can stay answerable to the ground and the activity that produced it, or whether its finished terms will present themselves as self-sufficient things, each one standing as though nothing had ever cut it out.

We already have the arithmetic for this question. In the second movement of §0/1 the Greek verbs behind *symbolic* and *diabolical* gave us two names for what a distinction can do between people, and the number line gave us their exact operations: the held polarity of dia-ballein, `(−1)/(+1)`, in which each sign has its sense through the zero it shares with the other; its two collapses, cancellation, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, and appropriation, written in both chiralities as `(+1)−(−1)=+2` and `(−1)−(+1)=−2`; and syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, which keeps the shared condition operative so that either orientation can be read through the other.[^s2-1] Those results stand. This section asks what kind of *two* each logic makes, why each is a complete way of accounting for a pair and not a better or worse opinion about it, and what each looks like when it takes on a body, in a household, in a crowd, in a god, in a machine. Already the children at the plate have shown the shape of the answer. Their rule of cutting and choosing is dia-ballein used well: a polarity of two offices held through a shared zero, the cake itself, which both depend on and neither owns. An elder who keeps the larger piece has appropriated the span; the parent who removes the slice has cancelled it. And the rule itself, the old household device that keeps the cut answerable to the one who did not make it, is a small working instance of syn-ballein, a way of keeping two parties different and making their difference the means by which the fairness of the division is proved.

Greek myth tells the first division between gods and human beings as a cut of exactly this kind, and tells it as a con. In the *Theogony* Hesiod places the scene at Mecone, "when the gods and mortal men were being divided", and his verb for that dividing is *ekrinonto*, a form of the *krinein* whose noun, *krisis*, closed §0/1 as the separating that is also a deciding.[^s2-2] Prometheus takes a great ox, cuts it up and sets two portions before Zeus. In one he hides the meat and the rich innards inside the ox's stomach, so that the best of the animal looks like its least appetising part; in the other he lays out the bare white bones and covers them with shining fat, so that the worst looks like the best. Zeus is to choose. Hesiod insists that Zeus saw through the trick and chose the bones anyway, and the poem uses his choice to explain why human beings ever after burned white bones on altars for the gods and kept the meat; then, in anger, Zeus withholds fire, and Prometheus steals it, and the woman made by the gods is sent to men as the price of the theft.[^s2-2] The scene is a cut-and-choose turned inside out. Prometheus has arranged the portions so that the chooser's choice, which in the children's rule kept the cut honest, is captured in advance by the look of the pieces; the relation between the parties has been preserved in form and emptied in substance, and the division that was meant to settle what gods and mortals owe one another becomes the beginning of a long reckoning in which each side counts the span against the other. Mortals have the meat and the gods the savour, and from the gods' side the same division is counted as theft. One side's `+2` is the other's `−2`, and it has been so ever since, at least in Hesiod's telling, where the whole subsequent history of fire, labour and woman follows from a division made in bad faith.

We read this as a mytheme, in the sense §0/1 gave the word: a compressed psychodynamic that proves a relation through the action it stages, and that we unfold back into operations without taking its persons for causes.[^s2-3] The operation it proves is that a division can be performed as a relation and still be an appropriation. A child who keeps the larger piece at least appropriates in plain sight. Prometheus appropriates through the very procedure that was supposed to make appropriation impossible, by working on the appearance through which the chooser chooses, and the result is a division that both parties have, in a sense, agreed to, while the relation it was meant to settle passes into enmity. Here the con we met in §0/1, the confidence trick that wins a trust it means to use, appears at the founding of the relation between the human and the divine.[^s2-4] Nothing in the scene allows us to stop dividing: the ox has to be cut for the sacrifice to happen, and gods and mortals have to become distinguishable for there to be gods and mortals at all. What the scene quarrels with is a particular way of dividing, the way in which the presentation of the portions occupies the place where the chooser's free reply should have been.

Every binary, then, has two provenances, and the two provenances give us the two logics. A pair of terms can be treated as separately given values whose conflict is measured from outside: here is one portion and there is another, here is *guilty* and there is *innocent*, here is the human and there is the machine, and the question is only which is larger, which is true, which will win. Or the same pair can be treated as inverse determinations of one differentiating relation, two portions of one ox, two verdicts on one evening, two forms of intelligence arising within one field of language, and then the question includes the cut that made them two and the field from which they were cut. Dia-ballein accounts for the first provenance. It takes the two as poles of an axis and measures them against each other through the zero between them, and while it keeps the axis it keeps the relation, so that each pole still has its sense through the other. Syn-ballein accounts for the second. It takes the two as the two ends of one relation, the condition and what has become determinate through it, and it holds both orientations together so that the cut stays legible inside what it produced. Neither of these is a mood or a temperament. Each is a complete way of accounting for a pair, and each has its own exactness, its own uses and its own characteristic failure.

Greek prefixes carry the difference, and they are worth hearing slowly. Greek *dia* means *through* and *across*, and, in compounds where a whole is passed through and parted, *apart*; *syn* means *with* and *together*.[^s2-5] Dia-ballein throws across, which is how a cut is made, a line thrown through a field so that it falls apart into two regions; syn-ballein throws together, which is how a token is fitted, the two halves brought back into contact across the line that parted them. We will hear both verbs at work as the section proceeds, in their old senses of accusing and of agreeing, but the prepositions already tell us that the two operations concern one field. A cut is thrown *across* something; a fitting brings together what was *with* each other before it was apart. Neither operation can be performed without the other's object. There is nothing to throw together unless something has been thrown apart, and nothing to throw apart unless there was a field that held it together.

Those prefixes have a second life that can mislead us. *Diachronic* and *synchronic* name, respectively, what is studied across time and what is studied together at one time, succession and co-presence, and it is tempting to hear dia-ballein as the logic of history and syn-ballein as the logic of the simultaneous picture. We should refuse the temptation, because the two pairs cross. Cutting and choosing works in sequence: first the cut, then the choice, and it is the sequence that keeps the cut honest. That is a diachronic procedure performing syn-ballein. A photograph of a table in which every guest has been seated by rank is a single synchronic arrangement, and it can perform dia-ballein in its collapsed form, with the whole span of the room counted from the head of the table. A history can be written as a row of completed periods, each sealed off from the next, or as the continuing life of what an earlier act still does in a later one; a cross-section of a society can be drawn as a field of separate interests or as a web of mutual dependence. Succession and co-presence each can keep their relation or lose it. What decides between the two logics is whether the relation that makes the terms terms is kept active through the operation performed on them.[^s2-6]

One serious philosophical voice has pressed the opposite case, that difference itself cannot be made good. Śrīharṣa, the Advaita philosopher whose *Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya* dismantles one by one the definitions his rivals offered for the categories of knowledge, argued, in Nilanjan Das's account, that "irrespective of how we define distinctness, no awareness—perceptual or otherwise—can present one object as distinct from another, without also presenting them as non-distinct."[^s2-7] To see the pot as other than the cloth, I must hold pot and cloth together in one awareness, and the awareness that holds them together presents them, in that respect, as one. For an Advaitin pressing toward the non-duality of *brahman*, the inseparability of distinction from non-distinction counts against the final reality of difference: if difference cannot appear without its contrary, it cannot be what reality ultimately is. Das records that this reading of Śrīharṣa remains controversial, and we take it as he reports it.[^s2-7] What the argument shows is exact, and we can accept it whole while drawing the opposite conclusion. Every awareness of difference is also an awareness of relation, because a difference can only be presented within the one act that holds its terms together; that is the copula's double work, which §0/1 found in the smallest identity statement, where two inscriptions had to be both distinct and identified for anything to be said.[^s2-8] Śrīharṣa reads the co-presentation as a reason to doubt the distinction. We read it as the reason the distinction is never alone. A pot really is not a cloth, and the awareness in which they are told apart really does hold them as one field, and both of those facts are present in every act of telling apart. Non-duality, on this reading, does not dissolve the distinctions inside the appearing act; it preserves them within the act from which they arise.

That proposition is the ground of the whole section, and it has a direct consequence for the two logics. If every difference is presented together with a relation, then no binary is ever simply given as two finished things. Its terms always arrive with the field in which they were told apart, and the only question is whether an account of them keeps that field in view. Dia-ballein in its held form keeps it as the axis: the zero between `−1` and `+1` is the respect in which they are comparable, the point from which both directions are measured, the thing both children want. Syn-ballein keeps it as one of the terms: in `0/1` the zero is not the space between two ones but one of the two, the condition written alongside what has become determinate within it. Here the two logics part in a way we can state numerically. Dia-ballein makes its two out of two signed ones, `−1` and `+1`, each of them a determinate unit, each a `1` with a direction; its zero lies between them, or comes out as the result when they are added. Syn-ballein makes its two out of the Two Ones that §0/1 distinguished, zero, which bears the singular One of self-identity, and one, which bears the manifest All of self-difference.[^s2-9] In the first accounting zero is never a term. In the second it is always one. How the zero's office changes between the two accountings is the subject of this section's last movement, and everything in between is the history of that change, lived.

Knowledge meets this choice whenever it confronts its own incompleteness, and it confronts its incompleteness at every turn, because, as §0/1 found in the formal-limit genealogy, no sufficiently articulate account can supply the condition of its own articulation from its own resources. I put the situation this way: **knowledge is a performance of trust wearing the mask of certainty**. Before a proposition is certified as known, a whole field has already been trusted enough for the proposition to count: the language in which it is stated, the memory that keeps its steps in order, the method by which it was tested, the witness who reported it, the competence of the person who checked it, the institution that recorded it, and the world that went on being there while the checking was done. The children trust the rule before the cake is cut; neither could prove that it will work out fairly, and both act on it. A scientist trusts her instruments, her colleagues and her own eyes before the result is in, and the result, once in, is a certainty only in respect of the field she has trusted. We gave the four offices of this relation their names when the household's reliance first came into question, trust as the already enacted dependence, knowledge as the aperture through which it becomes intelligible, belief as the orientation steadied within that aperture, and faith as the lucid continuation through the account's limit.[^s2-10] Here the four offices meet the two logics. *Performance* is meant in its full sense: knowledge is an act carried out before others and with them, under conditions it did not set, and its certainty is the mask it wears in order to act at all, since an actor who stopped in the middle of every line to explain that the theatre might collapse would never finish the play. Wearing a mask is no fault. Fault begins when the mask is taken for the face, and the performance forgets that it is a performance of trust.

Here the two logics show their difference in the life of knowledge. What happens to the uncertainty that knowledge cannot eliminate? Dia-ballein in its collapsed form denies it. A knowledge that has taken its mask for its face cannot admit that its certainty rests on a trust it never certified, and so the uncertainty it cannot hold goes somewhere else. It is displaced onto an excluded term, the enemy, the scapegoat, the defective population, the alien intelligence, the other side, each of which is made to carry the incompleteness that belongs to the field itself. The asserted `+1` feels whole because a `−1` has been found to bear what it could not admit. Our next movement follows this displacement through crowds, states and machines. Syn-ballein in its healthy form distributes the uncertainty instead. It keeps the trusted field visible as a field and lets uncertainty rest across many revisable anchors, a skill tested in use, a witness who can be questioned, a relationship that has survived disagreement, a practice that can be corrected, an institution with real recourse, so that no single object has to carry the whole weight and the failure of one anchor does not bring down the possibility of trusting at all. I call this **mono–poly trust**: one living field of reliance, sustained through many anchors none of which is permitted to stand for the whole. It is followed in the movement after next. The two ways in which provisional knowledge handles the trust it cannot eliminate, as a distributed anchor within relation or as distrust projected onto an excluded object, are the two logics of two seen from inside a life that has to go on knowing things.

Agentworld states the same necessity at the scale of a society of agents, and in a sentence that could serve as a definition of the problem this section works on. Coordination among artificial agents, Bratton writes, has to find "the narrow corridor: same decision-procedural grammar, sufficiently divergent world models."[^s2-11] On one side of the corridor lies the collapse of ontologies into one, a homophily of agents trained alike and reasoning alike, whose monoculture is most stable just before it fails all at once; on the other lie ontologies so incompatible that their holders cannot even converge on the shape of their disagreement. Bratton does not nominate a grammar for the corridor. Read through our two logics, its two failures are the two ways of losing a two. A field of agents without enough difference has cancelled its polarity, so that every agent's error is every other agent's error and the zero they share has become a single point of failure. A field of agents without a common grammar has severed it, so that each agent's world-model stands alone and no relation remains through which a disagreement could be measured, let alone settled. Dia-ballein keeps world-models divergent, because it is the logic of real difference that can be measured; syn-ballein keeps the grammar common, because it is the logic of a relation that holds its terms together without making them one. The corridor is exactly where both are operative at once, and that is the claim this essay makes for its own notation in the venue's terms: a shared relational grammar with plural ontological content.

This structure has an older Greek name that will matter when the section reaches its myth. Heraclitus says of what differs that "in differing it agrees with itself," and his verb for that agreeing is *homologeei*, from *homologia*, literally a saying-the-same, a common *logos* found within a difference.[^s2-12] Agreement in this sense is not the absence of difference; it is what a difference has when its parts belong to one account. A comparison between two things, a judgment that they are opposed, a quarrel over which is larger, all presuppose a common respect, and in the act of opposing two terms we have already related them. A quarrel over the cake presupposes the cake, and Prometheus's trick presupposed a sacrifice both sides took part in. That common respect is the zero the two logics treat differently. When it is held, two opposed things can be compared, contested and even fought over without ceasing to belong to one field. When it is lost, they become camps that no longer recognise the common operation that produced them, and their opposition, once a relation, hardens into a fact about each of them.

Here the old charge against the cut has to be answered plainly, because the two logics distinguish operations, not moral camps. Dia-ballein is not the villain of this section. The cut powers comparison, classification, measurement, proof by exclusion, decision and critique, and without it nothing could be refused, no evidence could tell against a claim, no person could say *no* and mean it, and no injustice could be named as injustice, since naming an injustice means distinguishing it from what was owed. A verdict has to be reached, a boundary has to be drawn, a decision has to be made before every uncertainty is settled, and the logic that makes these acts possible is necessary to any life, any science and any society worth having. Dia-ballein's pathology begins at one precise point, when the cut forgets the common operation and the field that produced its poles. When the elder child keeps the larger piece, her knife is not at fault; her keeping is. Syn-ballein is not the hero either. A gathering can erase the differences it gathers and call the result unity, and its failure, fusion, is as real as severance and in some ways harder to see, because it presents itself as love. Our task is to give both logics their full scope, their necessary work and their characteristic failures, without letting either become a slogan.

So the movements that follow take the two logics in turn and then together. Classical accounting comes first, because it is the dominant logic of modern formal and technical systems and because its arithmetic is the one our institutions run on; it gives us the literalist of the minus sign, the consent that division requires, and the crowd. Superpositional accounting comes second, as the retained seam of the symbolon and the trust it was made to carry. Then the two logics receive the bodies §0/1 promised them, War and Love, Ares and Aphrodite, caught together in a net made by the craftsman god, and the myth shows what each logic feels like from inside, and what no apparatus, however complete, can do for a relation. After that the two are read as one pulse, in the dynamics of a psyche and a culture, with Apollo and Dionysus as the safeguard that keeps either from swallowing the other. And at the end zero changes its role, which is what this whole station has been about. Two children at a plate will not need any of this to settle their slice of cake. They have a rule that keeps the cut answerable to the one who did not make it, and the rest of this section asks what it would take for a civilisation's cuts to be made that way.
