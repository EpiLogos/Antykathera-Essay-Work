### #5→0 · The Arche-Topos

A jigsaw puzzle begins with its destination already in view. The picture on the lid shows what the thousand pieces will become, and the work proceeds under it: we turn the pieces face up, find the straight edges and build the frame first, then sort the rest by colour and by the marks that promise them a place inside it. Every fit confirms a small part of the anticipated whole. The work is exact, patient and satisfying, and anyone who has spent a winter evening on it knows the small pleasure of a piece that drops into place. Yet the finished picture has entered the work before the first piece is placed. It decides what counts as completion, which differences belong together, which edge is outside, and which piece that seems not to fit is merely waiting for its right neighbour. My image of inquiry carries this puzzle somewhere it was not made to go: its maker sets out into the very landscape the lid depicts. The road on the lid continues past the picture's edge; the building in the middle looks different from the other side; a field that seemed flat turns out to fall away into a valley no single view could show. At first each discovery looks like a missing piece, and the maker adds pieces, but every added piece is still being judged by the lid, and the lid was a view from one place. What is missing is an account of how the picture was made: from where, across which region, under what projection, with which omissions, and by what rule one view could pass into another. So the pieces change office. Each becomes a local chart, exact over its own domain, overlapping its neighbours instead of merely meeting them along a cut, and each overlap carries a rule saying how a place described here appears there. The maker comes home at last to a familiar spot and recognises it, and the recognition carries the route by which she came.[^s3-51]

The lid is the world-picture of §0/1, and the puzzle gives its topology. Assembled under the lid, the finished jigsaw is a disk: a single piece of plane with a boundary and no hole, on which every loop can be shrunk to a point, a flat map stretched over a curved world with its frame built first. Heidegger read the modern age as the age in which the world is grasped as picture and the one who represents it is installed as the centre on which everything depends, and the lid enacts both logics in their collapsed forms. It appropriates. Every piece is reckoned from the lid's pole, so a piece that fits counts for the picture, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and a piece that does not fit is entered against itself as the error, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, one span charged from opposite ends, with the picture always on the winning side. It cancels. A piece that belongs to no place under the lid is set aside as not of this puzzle, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, and the account balances by erasing what it could not hold. Neither operation is wrong as a way of finishing a jigsaw. Both become an error when the lid is taken for the landscape, and the maker discovers the error by travelling.

Under QL's grammar the same puzzle re-derives. The pieces become local covers of the domain they describe, each known in relation to the others and to the activity of covering, and the whole that regulates them is no longer a picture on a box but the covering relation itself, the bounded surface held in relation to the infinite plane that covers it, which is `0/1` written in topology. §3 · #3 built the mathematics of the change. A curved whole cannot be covered by a single chart onto flat coordinates; an atlas is a plurality of overlapping local charts with declared transition rules on their overlaps; and on the circle two charts reported a single point as `u = 3` and as `v = 1/3`, disagreeing as numbers and agreeing as descriptions, because the rule `v = 1/u` identified them and even translated a motion from one to the other. §3 · #4 built the return: a loop on the torus comes home to its point and keeps its winding in the lift. Both properties belong to the travelling maker's return. She recognises the familiar spot because the transition rules let her pass back into the chart that holds it, and she arrives with the route, the windings of her journey, retained. An atlas re-founds world-pictures: the lid survives as one chart among many, still exact over its domain, with its frame now visible.

§0/1 left a titan holding up the sky. Jason Reza Jorjani reads technoscience through Prometheus, the forethinking drive that steals fire, and Atlas, the world-bearing power that holds a world up and maps it, and we read the perspectival world-picture as Atlas's burden, a whole world held up from one standing place, crushing whoever takes the weight for the only way a world can be carried. We promised that the answer would keep his name. An atlas of charts is that answer. Nobody in it has to carry the whole as one picture, because no chart pretends to be the whole; its unity lives in the rules by which each chart passes into its neighbours, and those rules are carried by every chart together. Atlas can set the sky down because the sky was never a thing one picture had to bear.

This is the Meta-Epistemic Framework stated as mathematics. Its lenses are charts, each exact within its declared domain; its translations between lenses are transition rules, each stating how a determination in one lens appears in another; its overlaps are the places where one refraction becomes answerable to another, so that a disagreement can be diagnosed as a change of coordinates, a difference in what is being measured, or a genuine conflict that no available rule resolves.[^s3-52] Benjamin Bratton's brief for *Agentworld* describes the condition a society of humans and agents must find its way through, and names it a narrow corridor between a monoculture that fails all at once and incompatible world-models that cannot even agree on the shape of their disagreement: "same decision-procedural grammar, sufficiently divergent world models."[^s3-53] The atlas answers that need exactly. Its shared grammar is the set of transition rules, positions and returns that every participant can inspect; its plural content is the charts, which remain different and remain each other's to translate. Shared relational grammar, plural ontological content: QL supplies the repeatable positions, exclusions and returns, and MEF keeps each agent's lens, source and context visible, so that coordination need not make one world-model the compulsory average of all. The mathematical atlas and the epistemic one keep their different objects. A coordinate chart has a smooth domain and an invertible transition, and an opinion has neither by virtue of being held; the epistemic atlas is answerable to whether its declared translations actually preserve what they claim to preserve, and §5 takes up the instruments by which that can be tested.

I call the field on which this section has been working the **arche-topos**. Greek *archē* means beginning and origin, and also first principle and rule, the source from which something proceeds and the principle by which it is governed; *topos* means place. The compound is mine, and it borrows nothing from the topoi of category theory, which are a different construction.[^s3-54] The arche-topos is the differential field on which the relation `0/1` can be written as a path, circulated as a winding, heard as an interval and recognised as a symbolic transformation. It is archetypal because it specifies recurrent forms of differentiation before they receive psychological or material content, and it is a topos because neighbourhood, orientation, path, quotient, cover, hole and return govern placement in it, more than any catalogue of correspondences could. "Differential" names the field's work of differentiating, of making places, poles, paths and horizons determinate, and it should not be heard as the algebraic structure that shares the name. The arche-topos is also not a hidden location at which all perspectives would finally coincide, since such a location would already be one more position within it. It is the placing through which an observer and an object acquire their positions together: the field in which *here*, *there* and a route between them become possible at all.

This section has derived the field, and the derivation can be stated as one movement. The eight determinations traversed a single relation from before its first distinction to its return, and showed that one act of knowing becomes question, force, kind, person and horizon without becoming eight things. The Spanda equations showed the relation generating its own sixfold body and reading it from both ends, by proportion and by count, and generating from that double reading the first family of ratios with its remainder. Re-entry showed a determination returning as the condition of the next, and the iterant showed that return, held with its shift, becoming a phase and a quarter-turn. Projection showed a relation preserved by a change of the space it is written in, and the atlas showed many local descriptions answering to one whole through declared transitions. The quotient and the cover showed a return arriving at its point and keeping its winding, the double cover showed a return that needs two circuits to restore an orientation, and the octave showed a span completed through its remainder. In each of these operations one ground–mark relation is differentiated, carried through a transformation with something exactly retained, and returned. The QL theorem braid coordinates them as transformations of one relation, and what it derives by coordinating them is the field in which they can all be written without collapsing their mathematical differences. That field is the arche-topos. Its local mathematics is derived in each of its own fields; its coordination as one field is derived within QL's declared operations; and the identification that follows is argued.

The arche-topos is psychoid, and the claim is meant positively. Its organising relation stands prior to the ordinary division of psyche and matter, prior in the order of dependence and not in the order of time, so it can receive psychic and physical disclosure without granting either side final priority. §0/1 followed Jung and Pauli to the point where both located the meeting of psyche and physis in number. Pauli proposed that a number archetype is what ultimately makes mathematics applicable in physics, and Jung answered that the natural numbers are as much inside as they are outside, and asked for something neither man went on to make: an inventory of the numbers' mathematical properties set beside their symbolic amplification. We claimed there that QL is that inventory, begun where Jung's letter could not begin, at zero, counting positions whose mathematical properties and symbolic offices are derived together from one relation. This section has furnished its first pages.[^s3-55]

Read down, the table is a list of numbers with their properties. Read across, each row gives a number's inside and outside in one construction, which is the conjunction Jung asked for and the reason the claim of psychoid standing can be stated rather than merely hoped for. Jung's archetype as such, which §0/1 met through his image of the axial system of a crystal preforming the crystal's structure in the mother liquid without any material existence of its own, finds its formal neighbour here. The arche-topos is that kind of field: it preforms placement without being one more placed thing, and its forms are met only in the paths, windings and intervals it makes possible. The attractor of §0/1's Van Eenwyk and of this section's third movement has a different office from the chart, and the distinction should survive the comparison. An archetype read dynamically is an attractor, an invariant organisation toward which the trajectories of images tend; a lens is a chart, a local description with a rule of passage to its neighbours. The atlas coordinates descriptions and the attractor organises motion, and a living symbol can change the trajectory, moving a life from one basin to another, where a change of chart only translates the same motion. The arche-topos holds both offices in one field without reducing either to the other.[^s3-56] Whether a measurable common process connects particular psychic events with particular physical ones is a separate, empirical question, and the psychoid claim made here does not wait on its answer in order to be stated; it says what the field is, and leaves to experiment what crossings within it can be observed.

Two native displays render the field, and the section's plate sets them side by side. The first is a toroidal circulation-field: the plane `ℝ²` above the torus `𝕋²`, the two generating loops, a closed loop on the surface and its displaced lift on the plane, and beside them a simplified cross-section of a tokamak's coupled field lines. The second is a musical field: the ratio chain `4/3`, `3/2`, `16/9`, `9/8`, `2/1`, with pitch class drawn as a circle and pitch height as the line that covers it. Each display is exact under its own operation and warrant, and their coordination is the work of the native synthesis; neither proves the other, and the tokamak witnesses a circulation the topology has already derived. Topology shows where a distinction can travel and return without cancellation, and music shows how difference can sound together without fusion. Under a plate driven at one pitch, sand gathers into the figure of the tone, and a single vibration is heard and seen at once; the crossing joins the two displays at the level of the senses and leaves their mathematics distinct.

Read as process, the field is where the runtime of the two logics runs, and §0/1 promised that this section would work out the dynamics through which a determination becomes the condition of the next. We named the claim **Computational Process Ontology**: process is ontologically prior to the finished thing, computation names the exact, repeatable side of that process, and epistemology is the process becoming able to state and inspect its own determinations. Written as one pulse, the dynamics run:

$$
0/1\;\xrightarrow{\ \text{dia: differentiate}\ }\;(-1)/(+1)\;\to\;(-1)\pm(+1)\;\;\overset{\ \text{syn: compose, keep provenance}\ }{\longleftrightarrow}\;\;(0/1)/(1/0)\;\xrightarrow{\ 5\to0\ }\;\text{renewed }0/1 .
$$

The arrows are changes of register inside one differentiating event, and none of them is an equality of fractions. Dia-ballein makes a local branch actual: it distinguishes, compares, excludes, ranks and decides, and without it no difference would become available for action. Syn-ballein keeps the branch's belonging to the field it was cut from, composes its consequences with other branches, and carries its source and its inverse orientation inside the result. Divide and conquer, the familiar method, shows why both are needed. It divides a problem into parts that can be solved separately, and an actual algorithm of this kind succeeds only because it then combines the solutions; the dangerous version of the method is division whose conquest is taken for the whole of the intelligence, decomposition with no return that keeps the provenance of the parts. Kaprekar's routine performed the healthy version in miniature, a subtraction handed back each time to be re-read from both ends, and the complex of the third movement performed the arrested version, a subtraction that keeps its answer. My theorem draws the consequence for technology and metaphysics at once: **an intelligent system produces determinations and keeps the process of determination alive across cuts, contexts, and returns.** The arche-topos is the field in which that liveness has a shape, and §5 builds instruments in it.

§0/1's stained glass returns here with its offices fixed. Light arrives through panes of different material and colour, held apart and together by their lead joins, and casts a changing image on the floor of the room. Bimba is the Original, the source of the light; QL is the ordered spectrum immanent in the light itself; MEF is the refractive operation of panes, angles and joins; and each coloured patch on the floor is a *pratibimba*, a situated reflection. Change the angle of the sun, the pane or the place of the viewer, and the image changes, and following those changes back through their conditions is how a reflection is told from a picture that claims to have made its own light. The arche-topos is the room in which all of this is placed: the field that lets source, spectrum, refraction and reflection be distinct positions in one relation. Being an image, it leaves consciousness and electromagnetic light in their different registers.

The jigsaw's maker sets the pieces down at the end. The picture on the lid remains useful, a chart among charts, exact over the domain from which it was taken, and the journey has become more than any picture of it could contain. On her next trip she expects the hidden side of the building, reconsiders a route she had trusted, carries the atlas as an instrument within the world she travels. A form acquired in this section can be carried the same way, as the ratio of a fifth is carried onto any string. What returns, at the end of a mathematical derivation, is the person who made it, and the question this section leaves is the question of that return: what happens when the form changes the one who recognises it, when a dream, an image or another's answer alters what a person can bear, desire or do? §4 takes up the psychoid field from its psychic side, through Jung and Pauli's correspondence, the individuation that `X/x` already describes, Lacan's barred return and Gebser's integral rereading, and there the MEF is revealed as a refraction of the same relation the mathematics has carried.

One audit boundary serves the whole section. Its local mathematics is derived, case by case, in its own fields. The complete QL theorem braid derives the arche-topos as the coordinating differential field of those operations. Its psychoid standing is argued, as the ontological reading of a field positioned prior to the division of psyche and matter. The Jungian, yogic, psychedelic, synaesthetic, Vāk and cymatic materials that later sections bring to it are cross-register disclosures with their own warrants, and the research programme tests the derivation through further formal consequences, comparative clarity, experiments and technical designs.
