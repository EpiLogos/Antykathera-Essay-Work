### #2 · Syn-ballein: The Retained Seam

In the minutes after a birth in a modern hospital, before the baby has been weighed or named, a midwife fastens a plastic band around its ankle or wrist and a matching band around the mother's wrist, and the two bands carry the same number. Later, when the baby comes back from a check in another room, or when a nurse on the night shift brings it to be fed, she does not rely on her memory of a face a few hours old that looks, to anyone but its parents, much like every other face on the ward. She takes the mother's wrist and the baby's ankle and reads the two bands against each other, and when the numbers match she hands the child over. The bands keep mother and child two, two bodies and two pieces of plastic that can be carried apart through corridors and across shifts, and their matching makes the two belong and lets a stranger, who has no other way of knowing, check that they do. A mother whose band was lost would have to prove her child some other way, slowly and in distress, and a single band shared between them could be read against nothing and would prove nothing. The match works because the halves stay separate and stay alike in the one respect that matters.

What the bands establish is a fit: these two pieces were issued together, so these two people were registered together, and the nurse can act on that. The proof is exact because it is narrow. A genuine token can belong to an unjust arrangement and an authenticated relation can later be betrayed, and nothing in the bands speaks to whether this woman will care for this child well or whether the number assigned at birth will ever mean anything to the person who wore it. The hospital needs the proof only because recognition cannot yet do the work. The mother will soon know her baby among a hundred, and the baby will know her voice before it can see her face; the bands serve for the hours when the relation exists and cannot yet be read from either face, and for the strangers who have to act on it meanwhile. That is what a token of this kind is for: a device of trust, made for an interval in which the people who must rely on a relation cannot see it directly.

§0/1 · #1 gave us the ancient form of the device. A *symbolon* was a token broken between two parties, each keeping a half; brought together later, the fit of the broken edges authenticated the relation, so that the difference between the halves was the condition of their recognition.[^s2-32] Greek lexicography records the practice in its plainest form: a knucklebone or other small object broken by two guest-friends, or by any two parties to an agreement, each keeping one piece so that whoever later presented the other piece could prove who he was.[^s2-33] The token travelled. It could be inherited, so that a guest's grandson could present it at the door of a host's grandson and be received; it could be carried by a messenger who had met neither party; it could wait in a chest for a generation. Its authority lay in the shape of its edge, which held, in the half that remained, the exact negative of the half that was absent. A symbolon is a piece that carries its missing counterpart in its outline. The bands on the maternity ward are its descendants, made for the gap of time and distance in which trust has to act before recognition can.

Syn-ballein is the logic the token embodies, and it can now be stated as a logic in its own right. Classical logic, the logic of the jury room, takes a pair of values as mutually exclusive positions of one variable, true or false, guilty or not guilty, `+1` or `−1`, with the slash read as OR and the OR read as *one and not the other*. Its pairs are complete in the sense that every case falls into exactly one of them. Syn-ballein takes a pair as the two orientations of one relation and reads the slash as AND/OR, the conjunction that does not erase disjunction, which §0/1 · #1 found inside the copula and wrote as `=/≠`.[^s2-34] Read as OR, the slash lets either orientation be taken in its own office and keeps their difference consequential. Read as AND, it holds them within one act, since each orientation is the relation and neither is the whole of it. When §0/1 turned its first symbolon over it wrote `0/1` and `1/0`, the condition becoming articulate through a determination and the determination turning toward the condition it cannot make into another determinate possession, and held them together as `(0/1)/(1/0)`, the relation taking its own inverse as a term.[^s2-35] That expression is the whole of syn-ballein in notation. Each inner slash keeps the indefinite and the definite together, the outer slash relates the two readings of that keeping, and ordinary arithmetic, which defines `0/1` and leaves `1/0` undefined, supplies the seam through which the two readings stay different. A logic of this kind is complete in another sense than the classical one. It does not sort cases into boxes; it keeps the relation every sorting presupposes in view while the sorting goes on.

Syn-ballein is superpositional in the sense the dual-non-dual ontology of this essay gives the word. Ground and manifestation are distinguishable orientations of one appearing-act, each given through the relation that prevents it from standing alone, and separately complete substances related afterwards would already presuppose the joining they were meant to explain. Quantum state space, the complementary descriptions Pauli and Jung discussed, and the non-classical logics that allow a proposition to be both or neither are exact mathematical and physical neighbours of that claim, and §3 examines their agreements and differences in detail.[^s2-36] None of them supplies the claim. When we call `(0/1)/(1/0)` a superposition we mean that both orientations are present at once in the one relation and that the presence of each does not cancel the other, the way a mother's band and her baby's band are both fully readable at once and neither makes the other redundant.

Syn-ballein is not *the whole*. It is the logic of defining toward wholeness: the accounting in which a determination keeps its ground, its inverse orientation and the relations through which it can return, so that its completion stays a direction it travels in and never becomes a possession it holds. A claim to have achieved the whole would already be a `1` occupying the zero, which is what §0/1 · #2 called playing the `0`.[^s2-37] The retained seam keeps wholeness open. A token whose two halves had been fused into one lump would no longer be a token, and a gathering that claimed to have no remaining seams would be an enclosure, as §2 · #3 shows in the craftsman god's net.

Heraclitus gives the right kind of gathering a word, and Heidegger, reading him, gave the word a shape. In the fragment numbered B50, Heraclitus says that it is wise, listening not to him but to the *logos*, to agree that all things are one, and Heidegger, working back through *legein* to its oldest sense of laying down and gathering, rendered the Heraclitean Logos as "the Laying that gathers," *die lesende Lege*.[^s2-38] The gathering he heard in the word does not consume what it gathers. It lets what is gathered lie together before us, each thing where it is and as it is, as a harvester lays the gathered sheaves side by side, or as a reader, *legere*, gathers the letters of a line into a sentence without the letters ceasing to be letters. §0/1 · #4 recovered this root when it found the integral Logos to be a remembering of its own gathering.[^s2-39] A gathering that lets lie together before us is a fitting that keeps the pieces two, and its opposite, which §2 · #3 meets, is a gathering that holds together in order to arrest.

What a token carries across its interval is trust, and here syn-ballein shows its second face, the one it turns toward the uncertainty knowledge cannot eliminate. §2 · #0 said that knowledge is a performance of trust wearing the mask of certainty, and that the collapsed form of dia-ballein deals with the uncertainty it cannot admit by exporting it across the axis onto an excluded term.[^s2-40] Syn-ballein distributes it. Where the collapsed form finds one object to blame, syn-ballein finds many places to rest. A competence tested in use lets a carpenter trust her hands though she cannot prove they will not slip, a relationship that has survived disagreement gives a friend's word weight a stranger's would not carry, and a lineage, a practice, a piece of land, a ritual, a family, a source that has been checked before, an institution with real means of redress each give uncertainty somewhere to rest without pretending to abolish it. Each is a **provisional anchor**, provisional because it can be revised when it fails and an anchor because it holds while it holds. Their plurality is their strength. When one fails, as some will, the possibility of trusting does not fail with it, because the others go on bearing weight, and each can correct the others, so that an official account can be tested against practical knowledge and an intimate conviction against evidence it had refused to admit.

This is the trust I call mono–poly: one living field of reliance sustained through many anchors, none of which is permitted to stand for the whole. Its relation to the Mono and Poly of §0/1 · #1 is direct. Mono is the singular field, the reliance already enacted before anyone has certified it, which no anchor creates, and Poly is the many anchors through which that reliance becomes definite, local and revisable.[^s2-41] Sever the Poly and trust does not disappear; it becomes available for capture by a single totalising object, a leader, a doctrine, a party, a platform, a model, which offers to bear all the weight at once and asks in return to be exempt from the revision every anchor owes. That is the bargain the crowd of §2 · #1 accepted, and it is why the crowd's unity felt like relief. An isolated person with no anchors left has nowhere to rest their uncertainty except in the one place that offers to hold it all, and the monopoly of trust takes its name from the *pōlein*, to sell, that §0/1 · #4 traced in *monopoly*: one seller where there should be many.[^s2-42]

The vocabulary §0/1 opened when the household's reliance came under question can be followed through its whole course as six offices in the life of one entrustment.[^s2-43] *Fides*, the first, is reliance already enacted, the credit given to a witness before the witness has been weighed, the trust that comes before arbitration. A second office is *topos*, the place in which the relying happens: this source, these conditions, this person's competence and vulnerability, so that trust is never trust in general but always trust here. *Logos* is the third, the articulation of reasons and claims, the account that makes the reliance intelligible and open to examination. Then comes *nomos*, the instituted effect, which decides whose answer can change what is done and by which rule, through which office. *Natio*, from *nasci*, to be born, names the order into which later people arrive and begin living before they can examine how it was made, the belonging every generation inherits.[^s2-44] And *credere* is the renewed entrustment, credit extended again once an undertaking has returned its consequences, with what was learned carried into the new commitment. Read in order, the six describe how a single act of trust becomes a world: a reliance takes place somewhere, becomes an account, acquires the force of a rule, is inherited as a belonging and is renewed or withdrawn in the light of what happened. Read through the two logics, they show where trust can be captured. A *nomos* that forbids its own revision has arrested the sequence before *credere*, a *natio* that presents its order as nature has hidden the *topos* in which it was once made, and a *logos* that claims to need no *fides* has taken the mask for the face. Syn-ballein keeps the six offices moving, so that what was inherited can be examined and what was examined can be entrusted again.

The counter-operation to divide and conquer is **divine and concur**, a pun that argues. The phrase answers *divide and conquer* almost letter for letter, and the near-coincidence of the words carries the near-coincidence of the operations. To divide is to cut a field; to divine, Latin *dīvīnāre*, is to perceive what is hidden, the old art of the seer who reads in the visible signs a relation that does not itself appear.[^s2-45] Divining here means finding in the apparent self-sufficiency of each pole the relation it has obscured or appropriated: the transcendental third, the `0/1 = 1/0` relation through which both poles were formed and to which both still answer. The third so divined is not a third party standing in the middle of the axis, and not the centrist's midpoint, which remains on the signed line and merely chooses a location between `−1` and `+1`. It is the field that was divided so that these two terms now appear as mutually exclusive wholes, and the question that divines it is the one §2 · #1 left us with: who drew this line, who holds the combining step, and what do the two sides share that neither side's account of the other can see? The second half of the pun is sharper. *Conquer* descends, through Old French, from the Latin family of *conquīrere*, which Lewis and Short gloss as to seek for, to procure, and also to bring together and collect.[^s2-46] The conqueror, in the word's own history, gathers; he gathers by seizing, and the whole he assembles is held together by the hand that took it. *Concur* is *con-currere*, to run together, and its *currere* is the running we heard in *currency*, what is present enough to pass from hand to hand.[^s2-47] To concur is not to agree, still less to fuse or to settle at a midpoint. It is for differentiated positions to move together through a ground none of them possesses, as current money runs between people without belonging to any of them. Conquest gathers by possession and concurrence gathers by circulation.

Divine and concur can judge. A recovered relation does not make every pole equally factual, ethical, powerful or responsible, and recovering the field in which an injustice was done may sharpen the injustice: one claim may turn out to be false, a power may have to give up an office it had taken, and an injured person may decline reconciliation and be right to decline it. What the operation refuses is the step by which the falsity or violence of a determination licenses the fantasy that the people, histories and needs from which it arose can be expelled from the real. In practice it returns each institution, party and claim of authority to the people, labour, histories and material conditions it cannot own, and asks whose labour sustains the form, whose exclusion secures it, which office authorises it, which record makes it claimable, and by what path the governed may contest and alter it. It takes no view from nowhere and absolves no power. It is the practical condition under which a collective need not become a crowd in order to act together.

Computation has its own name for syn-ballein, and the name is the step the political trick withheld. In a divide-and-conquer algorithm the combine is where the subproblems' answers are brought back into the problem they were cut from, and in a larger computational process the same work is done by everything that lets a result carry its provenance. A result that keeps the problem it answered, the alternatives it excluded, the inverse reading that could contest it and the larger state it changed is a result that can return, and a result stripped of these is a number that can only be obeyed. §0/1 · #5→0 named the ontology in which this matters as Computational Process Ontology: process is ontologically prior to the finished thing, computation names the exact, repeatable side of that process, and epistemology is the process becoming able to state and inspect its own determinations.[^s2-48] Dia-ballein and syn-ballein are that ontology's primitive runtime. Dia makes the local branch actual: it distinguishes, compares, excludes, ranks, routes and decides. Syn keeps the branch's belonging to a wider field and composes its consequences with other branches: it relates, preserves provenance, inverts, recombines, contextualises and returns. A runtime of pure division atomises, and a runtime of pure gathering never decides anything at all.

*Agentworld* locates the intelligence of the coming agentic society in this composing step. "Long horizons are a property of groups," Bratton writes, and what lets a task run on indefinitely is "the composite's capacity to recompose itself around an evolving problem, passing coherence from sub-agent to artifact to sub-agent without meaningful loss."[^s2-49] And again: "The unit that learns is the ensemble, not the agent, and what it learns accrues to the commons between them, not to any single one."[^s2-50] Read through the two logics, these are claims about syn-ballein at planetary scale. The sub-agent is a cut, a local branch of a problem made tractable; the artifact passed between sub-agents is the token that carries the problem across the gap; and the coherence that passes without meaningful loss is the retained seam, the relation that lets each piece be read against the others. A commons into which learning accrues is a field of provisional anchors, and an ensemble that learns is mono–poly trust in technical form. The brief raises without answering the question this movement keeps asking: who holds the combining step? An ensemble whose recomposition is performed by every participant, with provenance visible to each, is concurrence. An ensemble whose recomposition is retained by one operator, while the sub-agents remain divided and their outputs pass upward through a channel only the operator can read, is divide and conquer with a better interface.

Return is an operation, and a file cannot perform it. A log can keep every step a system took, an audit trail can preserve every decision an institution made, and the archive can be complete while nothing it contains is able to change what happens next. Take a constructed case: a research agent whose reports carry, beside each claim, the retrieved page it rests on. A reviewer who opens one of those pages and finds that it does not say what the claim says has found a fault, and the fault can be handled in two ways. It can be recorded, in which case the report is corrected, the log keeps both versions, and the next run proceeds under the same rule for what counts as a source. Or it can reach the source, the criterion or the permission that let that page count as evidence, and change what the harness will accept as a citation from then on. Only the second is a return, because only there does the consequence arrive where the next act is decided. The maternity bands are read; they are not merely kept. A symbolon in a chest that nobody ever presents at the door proves nothing to anyone, and an account whose provenance is perfectly recorded and never consulted has kept the seam as a museum keeps a broken pot, intact and out of use.

The token has a modern counterfeit, and it shows what happens to trust when the seam itself can be manufactured. A photograph, a recording of a voice, a video of a public figure speaking: each has long worked as the half of a symbolon, a mark that could be carried away from the event that produced it and later presented as proof that the event took place, because its edge, the light that fell on the lens and the air that moved the microphone, fitted the event and nothing else. Synthetic media cut that fit. A generated image or voice presents itself as the half of a token whose other half never existed, and it fabricates the appearance of a relation to a source, a counterfeit symbolon. Its first harm is the obvious one, that people may believe what never happened. Its second is worse. Once a culture knows that the seam can be fabricated, a genuine record can be refused by claiming that it was fabricated too, and Robert Chesney and Danielle Citron named the advantage this gives to anyone caught on the record the liar's dividend.[^s2-51] The trust that lived in the fit of the halves has become available to both sides at once, so that no half can prove anything. Provenance systems, cryptographic signatures and content credentials can strengthen the conditions under which a sign returns to its source, and they are worth building, yet none of them manufactures trust from inside the mark. Somewhere a person or an institution still has to stand surety for the account, and who that is and what they risk by standing there is the question the myth of §2 · #3 puts at its centre.

Syn-ballein has a failure of its own. If dia-ballein's failure is severance, the cut that forgets its field, syn-ballein's is **fusion**: relation preserved only by erasing the distinctions through which its terms could meet. A fused relation has no seam left to read. The mother's band and the baby's band melted into one would match nothing; a marriage in which one partner's preferences have quietly become the other's has stopped being a relation between two people; a collective that speaks with one voice because every other voice has learned to keep quiet has achieved unity by abolishing the differences from which a reply could come. Fusion is harder to see than severance because it presents itself as love, or as consensus, or as the overcoming of division, and it can be welcomed by the very people it absorbs. Its test is the test of the token. Can the halves still be told apart? Can one of them still refuse, answer back, or leave? A relation in which that is no longer possible has stopped being a relation, and calling it whole does not change that.

Dia-ballein measures a real difference across a shared zero and collapses when it reads the zero as an ending, cancelling the difference or appropriating its span. Syn-ballein keeps the relation every measurement presupposes, holding the two orientations of a pair together, and collapses when it erases the difference that made the pair a pair. A life without the cut could not refuse and a life without the seam could not belong, and both fail in recognisable ways. What neither notation can show is what these operations feel like to the people performing them, what it is to be the one who separates, defends and refuses, or the one who attracts, joins and holds, and what happens when the two meet in a single bed. Greek myth gave that meeting a song, which §2 · #3 tells.
