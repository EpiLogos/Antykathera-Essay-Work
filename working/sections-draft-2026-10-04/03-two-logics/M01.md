### #0 · Difference Cannot Be Escaped

One slice of cake is left on the plate and two children want it, and somewhere in most families an old rule settles the matter: one cuts, the other chooses. The elder takes the knife and is in no hurry. She knows that whatever she cuts, her brother will choose first and leave her the remainder, so every sliver she shaves off one side to favour herself is a sliver she will probably lose, and she lines the knife up, looks again, moves it a little, and cuts as near the middle as her eye and hand can manage. Her brother leans in, inspects both pieces with the gravity of a jeweller, and takes one. Nobody has been made generous. There are still two pieces, two children and two offices, the one who divides and the one who chooses, and the rule depends on every one of those differences staying where it is. What the rule changes is where the cut answers: because the choice will come back upon it, the cut is made under the eye of the person who did not make it, and the knife is honest because the relation between the two children has been built into the procedure that divides the cake.

Change one thing and the same knife performs a different event. Let the elder cut and also keep whichever piece she likes, and the cut is made from one side only, so that the span between the larger piece and the smaller is counted from her end. Let a tired parent cut and hand the pieces out without a word, and the cut answers to a judgment that may be fair and may be weary but that neither child can return upon. Let the parent take the slice away because there is fighting, and the dispute has been settled by removing what was disputed: nothing remains to divide and nothing remains to quarrel over, and the children are left with a balance of zero and an evening's resentment that the balance does not record. In every version a difference is made and the difference is real. From version to version the thing that changes is whether the act that made the difference can still be answered by the people it divides.

Difference cannot be escaped. A child cannot eat half of an uncut cake; a perception cannot register a shape that does not stand out from its ground; a word that could not be told apart from every other word would say nothing; a refusal without an edge has not yet refused anything. Every act of knowing we followed through §0/1 was an act of this kind. The adult's "because" drew a line between a good reason and an impertinent question; the name held one child apart from every other; the definition of the Subject failed at the place where its distinction had to be made; the formal systems of the twentieth century were built to make every cut explicit and found at their foundations a cut they could not make. Mind is differentiating activity: awareness becomes a world by cutting, comparing, excluding, relating and keeping its distinctions. Since nothing appears without division, whether to divide has never been the live question. The live question is whether a distinction can stay answerable to the ground and the activity that produced it, or whether its finished terms present themselves as self-sufficient things, each standing as though nothing had ever cut it out.

We already hold the arithmetic of that question. §0/1 · #2 recovered the two Greek verbs behind *symbolic* and *diabolical*, *syn-ballein*, to throw together, and *dia-ballein*, which we take as throwing apart and which also meant setting at variance and accusing, and it wrote their operations on the number line.[^s2-arithmetic] Dia-ballein in its held form is the polarity

$$
(-1)/(+1),
$$

in which each sign has its sense through the zero it shares with the other. When the relating stroke gives way to an operation on two isolated terms, the polarity collapses in one of two ways. Addition cancels,

$$
(-1)+(+1)=0,
$$

and subtraction appropriates, the whole span reckoned from one pole and owned by it, in either chirality,

$$
(+1)-(-1)=+2 \qquad\qquad (-1)-(+1)=-2,
$$

so that one party's `+2` is the other's `−2`, one span charged from its two ends. Syn-ballein is the relation that keeps the shared condition operative so that either orientation can be read through the other,

$$
(0/1)/(1/0).
$$

Those results stand, and the children at the plate already show them working. Cut-and-choose is dia-ballein held well: two offices on one axis, with the cake as the zero both depend on and neither owns. An elder who keeps the larger piece has appropriated the span; a parent who removes the slice has cancelled it. And the rule that keeps the cut answerable to the one who did not make it is a small working syn-ballein, a household device that keeps two parties different and makes their difference the very means by which the fairness of the division is proved.

Greek myth tells the first division between gods and human beings as a cut of exactly this kind, and tells it as a confidence trick. Hesiod sets the scene at Mecone, "when the gods and mortal men had a dispute", and the verb Evelyn-White renders as having a dispute is *ekrinonto*, a form of the *krinein* whose senses run from separating and putting asunder to deciding a dispute: the noun is *krisis*, the separating that is also a deciding, on which §0/1 · #4 closed.[^s2-mecone][^s2-krino] Gods and mortals at Mecone are being sorted, and the sorting is to decide what each owes the other. Prometheus is "forward to cut up a great ox and set portions before them, trying to befool the mind of Zeus." Hesiod's participle for the cutting, *dassamenos*, means having divided out, having portioned. In one portion Prometheus lays the flesh and the rich innards on the hide and covers them with the ox's paunch, so that the best of the animal looks like its least appetising part; in the other he sets "the white bones dressed up with cunning art and covered with shining fat", so that the worst looks like the best. The Greek for the cunning art is *doliēi epi technēi*, a *technē* bent to *dolos*, craft in the service of a trick, and both words return in §2 · #3, wrapped around a bed. Zeus sees it at once: "Son of Iapetus, most glorious of all lords, good sir, how unfairly you have divided the portions!" and Hesiod's verb for that unfair dividing, *diedassao*, carries the prefix *dia-* on the act of portioning itself.[^s2-mecone]

Prometheus answers smiling, "take which ever of these portions your heart within you bids", and Hesiod insists that Zeus "saw and failed not to perceive the trick" and took the fat-covered bones with both hands anyway, angry at heart; "because of this", the poem says, "the tribes of men upon earth burn white bones to the deathless gods upon fragrant altars."[^s2-mecone-choice] The rest follows in a chain of counter-reckonings. Zeus withholds fire; Prometheus steals it in a fennel stalk; Zeus makes "an evil thing for men as the price of fire", and the one who makes it is "the very famous Limping God", Hephaestus, who forms a maiden from earth.[^s2-mecone-choice] The craftsman god whose net §2 · #3 will follow has his hands in the first division already, fashioning the price that one side charges the other.

Read through our notation, the scene is cut-and-choose turned inside out. In the children's rule the chooser's choice kept the cut honest. At Mecone the look of the portions captures the choice in advance, so that the relation between the parties is preserved in form and emptied in substance, and the division meant to settle what gods and mortals owe each other opens a reckoning in which each side counts the span against the other. Mortals keep the meat and the gods the savour; from the gods' side the same division is counted as theft, and fire, labour and the woman sent as its price follow from it. The mortals' `+2` is the gods' `−2`, one span reckoned from either end, and in Hesiod's telling it has stayed so. The mirror is exact, and it is why the scene is more than a story about a clever thief: each party's gain is legible only as the other's loss, and the zero they shared, the sacrifice that both gods and mortals were to take part in, has dropped out of both accounts. Zeus's choice adds the hardest detail. He saw through the trick and chose the bones anyway, because choosing them gave him a grievance, and a grievance is a span counted from one's own end. The division had already become an accusation before anyone spoke one; it was diabolical in the first sense of the word, a charge thrown across between two parties so that each appears to the other under its terms.

We read Mecone as a mytheme in the sense §0/1 · #4 gave the word, a compressed psychodynamic that proves a relation through the action it stages, unfolded back into operations without taking its persons for causes.[^s2-mytheme] The operation it proves is that a division can be performed as a relation and still be an appropriation. A child who keeps the larger piece appropriates in plain sight. Prometheus appropriates through the very procedure that was supposed to make appropriation impossible, by working on the appearance through which the chooser chooses, and the result is a division that both parties have in a sense agreed to while the relation it was meant to settle passes into enmity. This is the con of §0/1 · #2, the confidence trick that wins a trust it means to use, set at the founding of the relation between the human and the divine.[^s2-con] And nothing in the scene lets anyone stop dividing. The ox has to be cut for there to be a sacrifice, and gods and mortals have to become distinguishable for there to be gods and mortals at all. Hesiod quarrels with one way of dividing, the way in which the presentation of the portions occupies the place where the chooser's free reply should stand.

Every binary, then, has two provenances, and the two provenances give us the two logics. Our core theorems put the fork in two phrases. A pair can be "One and *some* Other One": two separately given values whose conflict is measured from outside, one portion here and another there, *guilty* and *innocent*, the human and the machine, so that the only question left is which is larger, which is true, which will win. Or a pair can be "One and its own Other": inverse determinations of one differentiating relation, two portions of one ox, two verdicts on one evening, two forms of intelligence arising within one field of language, and then the question includes the cut that made them two and the field from which they were cut.[^s2-own-other] Dia-ballein accounts for the first provenance. It takes the two as poles of an axis and measures them against each other through the zero between them, and while it keeps the axis it keeps the relation, so that each pole still has its sense through the other. Syn-ballein accounts for the second. It takes the two as the two ends of one relation, the condition and what has become determinate through it, and holds both orientations together so that the cut stays legible inside what it produced. Each is a complete way of accounting for a pair, with its own exactness, its own uses and its own characteristic failure, and we come to know each by what it does to a zero.

The Greek prefixes carry the difference in small. *Dia* means through and across, and in compounds where a whole is passed through and parted it comes to mean apart; *syn* means with and together.[^s2-prefixes] Dia-ballein throws across, which is how a cut is made, a line thrown through a field so that it falls into two regions; syn-ballein throws together, which is how a token is fitted, the halves brought back into contact across the line that parted them. Each operation needs the other's object. Nothing can be thrown together unless something has been thrown apart, and nothing can be thrown apart unless there was a field holding it together, so the two prepositions already tell us that the two logics work one field between them.

The same prefixes have a second life that can mislead. *Diachronic* names what is studied across time and *synchronic* what is studied together at one time, and it is tempting to hear dia-ballein as the logic of history and syn-ballein as the logic of the simultaneous picture. The two pairs cross, and the crossing has consequences. Cut-and-choose is a sequence, first the cut and then the choice, and the sequence is what keeps the cut honest: a diachronic procedure performing syn-ballein. A banquet table seated by rank is one synchronic arrangement, and it can perform dia-ballein in its collapsed form, the whole span of the room counted from the head of the table. A history can be written as a row of sealed periods or as the continuing life of what an earlier act still does in a later one; a cross-section of a society can be drawn as a field of separate interests or as a web of mutual dependence. Succession and co-presence each can keep their relation or lose it.[^s2-dia-syn-time] What decides between the two logics is whether the relation that makes the terms terms stays active through the operation performed on them.

The strongest case against difference itself was made by a philosopher pressing toward non-duality, and his argument is worth receiving whole, because we can accept every step of it and draw the opposite conclusion. Śrīharṣa, a philosopher and poet of twelfth-century northern India whose *Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya*, *The Sweets of Refutation*, takes apart one by one the definitions his rivals offered for the categories of knowledge, argued, in Nilanjan Das's account, that "irrespective of how we define distinctness, no awareness—perceptual or otherwise—can present one object as distinct from another, without also presenting them as non-distinct."[^s2-sriharsa] To see the pot as other than the cloth I must hold pot and cloth together in one awareness, and the awareness that holds them together presents them, in that respect, as one. For an Advaitin the inseparability of distinction from non-distinction counts against the final reality of difference: if difference cannot appear without its contrary, it cannot be what reality ultimately is. Das records that this reading of Śrīharṣa "remains controversial", and we take the argument as he reports it.[^s2-sriharsa] What it shows is exact. Every awareness of difference is also an awareness of relation, because a difference can be presented only within the one act that holds its terms together, which is the copula's double work that §0/1 · #1 found in the smallest identity statement, two inscriptions both distinct and identified for anything to be said at all.[^s2-copula] Śrīharṣa reads the co-presentation as a reason to doubt the distinction; I read it as the reason the distinction is never alone. The pot really is not the cloth, and the awareness in which they are told apart really does hold them as one field, and both facts are present in every act of telling apart. Non-duality, on this reading, preserves distinction within the appearing-act from which its terms arise.

That proposition is the ground of this section, and it has a direct consequence. If every difference is presented together with a relation, then no binary is ever given as two finished things: its terms always arrive with the field in which they were told apart, and an account of them either keeps that field in view or loses it. Dia-ballein in its held form keeps the field as the axis. The zero between `−1` and `+1` is the respect in which they are comparable, the point from which both directions are measured, the cake both children want. Syn-ballein keeps the field as one of the terms. In `0/1` the zero stands as one of the two, the condition written alongside what has become determinate within it, where on the number line it was the space between two ones. Here the two logics part in a way we can count. Dia-ballein makes its two out of two signed ones, `−1` and `+1`, each a determinate unit with a direction, each a `1`; its zero lies between them, or comes out as the result when they are added. Syn-ballein makes its two out of the two senses of *one* that §0/1 · #1 distinguished: zero, which bears the singular One of self-identity, and one, which bears the manifest All of self-difference.[^s2-two-ones] In the first accounting zero is never a term. In the second it always is one. How the zero's office changes between the two accountings is what §2 · #5→0 counts, and everything between here and there is that change lived in a formula, a body, a myth and a mind.

Knowledge meets the same fork whenever it confronts its own incompleteness, and it confronts that incompleteness at every turn, because, as §0/1 · #3 found, no sufficiently articulate account can supply the condition of its own articulation from its own resources. I put the situation this way: **knowledge is a performance of trust wearing the mask of certainty.**[^s2-performance] Before any proposition is certified as known, a whole field has already been trusted enough for the proposition to count: the language in which it is stated, the memory that keeps its steps in order, the method by which it was tested, the witness who reported it, the competence of the person who checked it, the institution that recorded it, and the world that went on being there while the checking was done. A haematologist reading a blood count trusts the analyser's calibration, the technician who drew the sample, the label on the tube and the reference ranges printed beside the numbers, and the result, once in, is a certainty only in respect of everything she trusted to get it. An agent answering a question with three citations trusts the retrieval that found them, the documents' own provenance and the instructions that told it what counts as a source, and its fluent answer wears the same mask. We gave the four offices of this relation their names in §0/1 · #2: trust, the already-enacted dependence; knowledge, the aperture through which it becomes intelligible; belief, the orientation steadied within that aperture; faith, the lucid continuation through the account's limit.[^s2-four-offices] The field that has been trusted in every one of these cases is Objective Internality, the means through which a life knows and acts within a world, and the logics we are about to follow are the operations by which those means handle what they cannot certify. *Performance* is meant in its full sense: knowledge is an act carried out before others and with them, under conditions it did not set, and its certainty is the mask it wears in order to act at all, as an actor who stopped in every line to explain that the theatre might collapse would never finish the play. Wearing the mask is no fault. The fault begins when the mask is taken for the face, and the performance forgets that it is a performance of trust.

What happens, then, to the uncertainty that knowledge cannot eliminate? Here the two logics show their difference inside the life of knowing. Dia-ballein in its collapsed form denies it. A knowledge that has taken its mask for its face cannot admit that its certainty rests on a trust it never certified, so the uncertainty it cannot hold goes somewhere else: it is exported across the axis onto an excluded term, the enemy, the scapegoat, the defective population, the alien intelligence, the other side, each made to carry the incompleteness that belongs to the shared field. The asserted `+1` feels whole because a `−1` has been found to bear what it could not admit, and the public's `+2` is the scapegoat's `−2`. Syn-ballein in its healthy form distributes the uncertainty instead. It keeps the trusted field visible as a field and lets uncertainty rest across many revisable anchors, a skill tested in use, a witness who can be questioned, a relationship that has survived disagreement, a practice that can be corrected, an institution with real recourse, so that no single object has to carry the whole weight and the failure of one anchor does not bring down the possibility of trusting at all. These are the two ways provisional knowledge handles the trust it cannot eliminate, as a distributed anchor within relation or as distrust projected onto an excluded object, and they are the two logics of two seen from inside a life that has to go on knowing things. §2 · #1 follows the projection into crowds, states and machines; §2 · #2 gives the distributed anchors their name.

The same fork decides whether a society of artificial agents can coordinate without collapsing into one world-model or splintering into many that cannot even measure their disagreement, the corridor between ontology collapse and unbounded conflict that §5 · #5→0 reads through these two logics. Heraclitus had already given the structure a Greek name. He said of what differs that in differing it agrees with itself, and his word for agreeing is *homologeein*, a saying-the-same, a common *logos* found within a difference; §2 · #3 hears the whole fragment.[^s2-homologia] Agreement in that sense is what a difference has when its parts belong to one account. A comparison between two things, a judgment that they are opposed, a quarrel over which is larger, all presuppose a common respect, and in the act of opposing two terms we have already related them: a quarrel over the cake presupposes the cake, and Prometheus's trick presupposed a sacrifice both sides took part in. That common respect is the zero the two logics treat differently. Held, it lets two opposed things be compared, contested and even fought over without ceasing to belong to one field. Lost, it leaves camps that no longer recognise the common operation that produced them, and their opposition, once a relation, hardens into a fact about each of them.

Both logics therefore need their full scope, because they distinguish operations, not moral camps. The cut powers comparison, classification, measurement, proof by exclusion, decision and critique, and without it nothing could be refused, no evidence could tell against a claim, nobody could say *no* and mean it, and no injustice could be named as injustice, since naming one means distinguishing it from what was owed. A verdict has to be reached, a boundary drawn, a decision made before every uncertainty is settled, and the logic that makes these acts possible belongs to any life, any science and any society worth having. Its pathology begins at one point: when the cut forgets the common operation and the field that produced its poles. When the elder child keeps the larger piece, her knife has done nothing wrong; her keeping has. Syn-ballein has a pathology of its own, and it is as real as severance and harder to see. A gathering can erase the differences it gathers and call the result unity, and its failure, fusion, presents itself as love, consensus or the overcoming of division, and can be welcomed by the very people it absorbs. Two children at a plate do not need any of this to settle their slice of cake. A civilisation does: it cuts a great deal more than cake, and its rules for cutting decide whether its divisions stay answerable to the people they divide.
