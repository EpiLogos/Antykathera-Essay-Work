## §4 — Psychoid Flowering

<a id="M31"></a>

### #0 · The Psychoid Problem

In the middle of March 1948, within four days of each other, Wolfgang Pauli had two dreams. He had more or less finished his long essay on Johannes Kepler, the astronomer who heard the planets as a music before he wrote them as laws, and he noticed, as he usually did, that the dreams came at the equinox, the time of year at which he found himself least stable and, by the same token, most productive; he called dreams of this kind "equinox dreams".[^s4-equinox] In the first, his first physics teacher told him that the change in the splitting of the ground state of the hydrogen atom was a fundamental one, and that brass tones had been engraved on a metal plate; then Pauli went to Göttingen. The second came as seven pictures in a row, with no word spoken until the end. A woman arrived with a bird, and the bird laid a large egg, which divided itself into two. Pauli came closer and found that he was already holding a third egg, this one with a blue shell, and he divided it himself; against all expectation the two halves stayed whole, so that he now held two whole blue eggs. Then all four eggs changed into two identical rows of mathematical expressions, the cosine and sine of half an angle, and the rows composed a formula:

$$
\frac{\cos(\delta/2)+i\sin(\delta/2)}{\cos(\delta/2)-i\sin(\delta/2)}.
$$

For the first time in the whole sequence Pauli spoke. It all gives $e^{i\delta}$, he said, and that is the circle. The formula vanished, and a circle appeared.[^s4-egg-dream]

Anyone can check the arithmetic, and the check owes nothing to being moved by it. For real $\delta$ the numerator is $e^{i\delta/2}$ and the denominator $e^{-i\delta/2}$; neither vanishes, both have modulus one, and their quotient is $e^{i\delta}$, a single point on the unit circle for any fixed angle and the whole circle as the angle runs through a full turn. A mathematician who never dreamed could verify every step. The dream did not arrive as a theorem, all the same. It arrived as a sequence in which a man passed from watching to taking part: the first division happened in front of him, the second happened through his own hands, and the egg he divided had been in his hand before he knew he was holding it. Only after he had performed the division and seen the four eggs become two rows of numbers could he say what the rows amounted to, and the circle appeared once he had said it, while the formula that carried him there disappeared. An exact mathematical relation and a change in the person recognising it happened as one event. Take away the passage from spectator to participant and the dream becomes an assortment of number symbols; take away the trigonometry and the passage loses the operation through which it proceeds. Neither half can be dropped without losing what the other half means.

Jung and Pauli called the ground of such an event psychoid, and §0/1 has given us their bequest in its own words: Jung's distinction between the archetypal images that reach consciousness and the archetype as such, an irrepresentable ordering factor showing itself in physical as well as psychic events; Pauli's *homo-usia*, sameness of substance between the archetypal order and physis, with mathematics as the language neutral enough to state it; and Jung's answer that the whole numbers are as much inside as outside, so that the borderland between physics and psychology lies in the secret of number.[^s4-s01-bequest] We took that thesis up as the essay's own, number as the place where psyche and physis meet and Quaternal Logic as the form in which we take the meeting up, and §0/1 promised that we would follow the dreams and disagreements through which the two men kept their question open, because the psychoid is a relation that has to be worked before it can be claimed, and Jung and Pauli worked it against each other for a decade. **The psychoid problem** asks how a psychological image and a physical event can show a meaningful structural relation when neither can be causally reduced to the other. Both easy answers are available, and both fail in the same way. One says that the egg dream is a picture of Pauli's mind and the formula an accident of his profession; the other says that the formula is the real content and the dream its wrapping. Each hands the whole relation to one of its sides.

Pauli's just-finished essay shows why he could accept neither answer, because it was about a quarrel in which both had been tried. Early in the seventeenth century Kepler wanted the harmony of the cosmos demonstrated through measurement and mathematical proportion, while the physician and Rosicrucian Robert Fludd held that quantitative method dismembered a living whole that only symbolic images, and the participation of the knower in them, could grasp. Kepler won, and modern physics is his inheritance. Pauli's achievement in the essay was to show that Kepler's own road to his laws ran through archetypal images that Kepler did not take for images. In the *Mysterium cosmographicum* Kepler read the sphere as an image of the Trinity: "The image of the triune God is in the spherical surface, that is to say, the Father's in the centre, the Son's in the outer surface, and the Holy Ghost's in the equality of relation between point and circumference."[^s4-kepler-sphere] The planets and the sun were for him realisations of that image, and Pauli drew the consequence plainly: "in Kepler the symbolical picture precedes the conscious formulation of a natural law. The symbolical images and archetypal conceptions are what cause him to seek natural laws."[^s4-kepler-image-law] Quantitative astronomy's founder argued from a symbol he took for a fact, and the symbol, Pauli noticed, held no place for a fourth. Fludd's figures held one. Pauli judged that Fludd's "quaternary" attitude corresponded, against Kepler's "trinitarian" one, to a greater completeness of experience, since his images tried to keep the inner experience of the observer and the outer processes of nature in one unity; and he judged as firmly that Fludd was always in the wrong when he argued astronomy or physics, and that no modern person could revert to a standpoint that had paid for its unity with a naïve ignorance of nature. His own position took neither side: "To us, unlike Kepler and Fludd, the only acceptable point of view appears to be the one that recognizes *both* sides of reality—the quantitative and the qualitative, the physical and the psychical—as compatible with each other, and can embrace them simultaneously."[^s4-kepler-fludd] Their quarrel already runs on our two logics. Kepler's cut is dia-ballein in its proper work, `(−1)/(+1)`, an exact polarity of measure that lets a difference be stated and checked. It hardens when the cut forgets the participation of the one cutting and counts the whole span of the relation as quantity, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, so that whatever cannot be measured is entered as the deficit `(−1)−(+1)=−2`; Fludd's whole hardens the other way, faithful to participation and blind to measure, reckoning the same span from the pole of the image. Three centuries later, a physicist to his fingertips, Pauli wanted the cut and the whole together, and the dreams of March 1948 came to him at the end of an essay that had tried to hold them.

His own commentary refuses both easy answers, and his reasons come in an order worth keeping. He read the first dream through what he knew of physics: the splitting of a spectral line can change only through an existing field of force, so a change in the splitting of the ground state pointed, in the psychic register, to an intervention of consciousness. A hydrogen atom, one positive nucleus and one negative electron, is the simplest pair of opposites there is, and its nucleus, the proton, whose name means "the first", is the building block of every heavier nucleus, so the dream pointed to a beginning. Brass tones he took for a genuine symbol: as sound they belong to physics, as music they carry feeling, which physics cannot express, and through Kepler's music of the spheres they had a cosmic character, so that they joined physis and psyche in one image; read with *aere perennius*, more lasting than bronze, the engraved plate said that archetypes leave their stamp on matter.[^s4-pauli-first-dream] Of the second dream he would offer no finished interpretation, since that would call for further progress in all the sciences, though he said what he saw happening in it. The spontaneously divided egg was a ground state; the dreamer's recognition and action were a conscious intervention; the quaternity of four eggs came about through a duality reflected in the psyche; and the imaginary unit $i$, a symbol not contained among the ordinary numbers, had in the dream the office of uniting opposites and producing wholeness. Pauli used the occasion for the sentence §0/1 quoted, that for him the mathematical representation is a symbolic description par excellence, and then turned on his own discipline. Physics represents regularity in concepts, and so it attends only to what can be reproduced and measured; everything feeling-toned and evaluative falls on the far side of it, which is a shortcoming of physics within life, not of quantum theory within physics, the distinction he had once put to Bohr about Einstein. Mathematics, by contrast, has a qualitative side as well as a quantitative one, visible in the theory of numbers and in topology, and its unifying power reached, in Pauli's judgment, further than physics did.[^s4-pauli-commentary]

A few pages earlier he had stated the point on which the dream turns. A quaternity, he wrote, would not be effective within physics itself; it belongs to the wholeness of physics and psychology together, insofar as the complementary pair of opposites in physics reappears, reflected, in the psychic sphere.[^s4-pauli-quaternity] A pair seen and the same pair reflected make four. I read the dream one step further. Between the woman who arrives with her bird and the circle that appears when the dreamer speaks, the dream holds four eggs: two that divided before him and two that divided through him. Her arrival is a source, a power that brings forth without being one of the things brought forth; the circle is a return in which the whole sequence is recognised as one figure. Four explicit terms held between an implicate source and an implicate return is the shape §0/1 derived four times over and wrote `4+2`, the shape Jung drew at the close of *Aion* in his ring of connected quaternios. Pauli's two pairs give the explicit four, the division that happens and the division that is done, the pair and its reflection; the woman and the circle give the two places that frame them. The seven pictures stay seven. What the dream shows is a relation: a fourfold articulation held between an emergence it did not author and a recognition it could reach only by speaking.

Their letters are the better witness, because there the relation had to survive argument. The first serious disagreement came in November 1948, the day after a conversation at Jung's house, when Pauli wrote to ask whether a dream and an event separated by two or three months could still be called synchronous, which was to ask what temporal relation the word denoted.[^s4-35p] Jung sent him the manuscript of his essay on synchronicity for criticism, and Pauli's long reply of June 1949 pressed harder. He could not see in Rhine's card-guessing experiments the archetypal basis he could see in Jung's other material, and he did not want them counted as the same kind of evidence; he asked for more precision in "acausal", since the absence of a causal chain through space and time is a different matter from the absence of any ordering relation; he noticed that a formulation beginning from simultaneity went on to include prediction, where the two events plainly do not occur together; and he invented a parable of a radioactive stranger to show how far the decay clock of physics and the symbolic "radioactivity" of dream language had to be held apart before any comparison between them could mean anything, ending with the demand for a neutral language that did not yet exist.[^s4-37p] Jung's concept of **synchronicity**, as he finally defined it, names the coincidence in time of two or more causally unrelated events that have the same or a similar meaning, as distinct from mere synchronism, the simultaneous occurrence of two events.[^s4-synchronicity-def] Pauli's objections stand inside that definition. Each of its words, time, cause, event, meaning, had to keep what it denoted in physics and in psychology before the two could be compared, and his insistence gives synchronicity its positive research office: to investigate a proposed ordering of meaningful events while keeping clear what each discipline means by its terms.

Jung's best-known case shows what the definition has to hold. A young woman in treatment with him dreamed, at a critical moment, that she was given a golden scarab. While she told him the dream he sat with his back to the closed window, heard a gentle tapping, turned and saw a flying insect knocking against the pane from outside; he opened the window and caught it as it flew in. It was a rose-chafer, the nearest thing to a golden scarab to be found in that latitude, which had, against its usual habits, tried to get into a dark room at just that moment.[^s4-scarab] The beetle had its causes and the dream had its causes, and neither chain contains the other; what joins them is a meaning, carried by an image old enough that the scarab rolling its ball across the sky had once been the Egyptian sun renewing itself each morning. Such a case cannot be produced on demand, which is why Pauli's care over every word of the definition matters, and it happened, in and to two lives, which is why Jung's care to receive it matters. Both cares belong to the problem. A science that dismisses the case because it cannot be repeated has decided in advance what may count; a psychology that treats it as proof of a hidden cause has borrowed a warrant it does not have.

In 1953 the disagreement deepened, as each tried to say where their common ground lay. In February Pauli sent Jung what he called a personal treatise in the form of a letter. Many people, he wrote, still treated the unconscious as non-being, a mere privation of consciousness, while others set the archetypes, like ideas in general, in places above the heavens, and he found both views equally doubtful; he took a third road from Aristotle and called the unconscious *potential being*, a legitimate human description of occurrences that may come about in consciousness and so a part of the symbolic reality of the thing in itself. Ideas, he added, have no fixed abode, not even a heavenly one, and to all of them he applied the old alchemical formula that Fludd had said of God: the centre of all things, whose periphery is nowhere.[^s4-58p-potential] Cusa's infinite sphere, which §0/1 turned on the world and then on the plane folded into a torus, had arrived in a physicist's letter as an account of where ideas live. In a footnote to the same letter Pauli told Jung what troubled him. As a psychologist Jung had an understandable aversion to every form of reality that was not purely psychic, and just as everything King Midas touched turned to gold, everything Jung looked at seemed to Pauli to turn psychic and only psychic.[^s4-midas] In March Pauli resisted calling the whole ordering field psychic, since a concept meant to mediate psyche and physis should not take one pole's name in advance, and he held *homo-usia* as a working hypothesis with mathematics as its language, keeping mathematical objects distinct from the psychic experience of doing mathematics.[^s4-60p]

Jung's reply in May is where §0/1 found the word psychoid, and the letter holds more than the word. He agreed at once that no one can be content with the ascertainable alone, since the drama of knowledge is played out on the borderline between the ascertainable and the non-ascertainable, and the area between what is perceived and what is not ascertainable here and now is the area of the psyche. Then he drew the line Pauli had asked for: "I do not extend the concept of the psychic to include the nonascertainable, for here I use the speculative concept of the psychoid, which represents an approach to neutral language in that it suggests the presence of a nonpsychic essence." Numbers and archetypes in general, he went on, are "not just psychic, otherwise they would be fabrications. But in actual fact they are 'in themselves being' (or psychoid) existences whose autonomous existence corresponds to that of the material object."[^s4-61j-psychoid] Everything is conceived in and through the medium of the psyche, he granted, but the objects of its perception are not integrated into its substance and so do not forfeit their existence. And then he disputed Pauli's dreams. Pauli dreamed in physics because physics was his natural language, as the dog dreams of bread and the fisherman of fish, *canis panem somniat, piscator pisces*; Jung himself never dreamed of physics but of mythology, which was just as unpsychological; the vocabulary of a dream did not settle what the dream meant.[^s4-canis]

Pauli was upset, and said so. The physical and mathematical terms in his dreams had seemed to him at first the very opposite of natural, the most familiar language striking him as the strangest, and for years he had tried to explain the physical part away, until the unconscious answered his reductions so insistently that he had to accept a connection with physics in the dreams themselves.[^s4-62p-strange] He went on to propose that a future physics would be enlarged enough to adopt psychology, as physics had already adopted chemistry, and he attached a condition that cuts against his own discipline's pride: "The adopting one is the one that has the more extensive system of concepts and lends itself more readily to generalization; but it is also always the one that undergoes the more radical and more sweeping changes, precisely because it is being extended."[^s4-62p-adopting] A footnote on the same page offered what he took the dreams finally to mean: that the scientific approach should appear as the conditional special case of the transcendental.[^s4-62p-special-case] In October Jung refused the proposal. Psychology could not be classified within a general physics as chemistry had been, because psychic processes cannot be measured, and he named instead two bridges between the fields, number and synchronicity, neither of which anyone dared yet to cross.[^s4-64j] Two years later he put the point with a precision the whole correspondence had been working toward: "My feeling is that the common ground shared by physics and psychology does not lie in the parallelism of the formation of concepts but rather in 'that ancient spiritual "dynamis"' of numbers." In the same letter came a sentence on which much of this section depends: "The autonomy of a psychic factor lies in the fact that thanks to its dynamic force, it is capable of making its own statements."[^s4-67j] Myths, dreams and delusions speak from the objective psyche without passing through conscious reasoning, and number, given the chance, makes its own mythical statements in the same way.

Even the vocabulary of experiment became a place of dispute. Pauli had called the experimental physicist's choice of one arrangement over another a sacrifice, since each measurement buys a definite piece of knowledge at the price of the knowledge it excludes, the very loss §0/1 followed through complementarity. For the experimenter, Jung replied, it was "basically just a decision and only metaphorically a 'sacrifice'", since sacrifice means giving up what one owns for nothing in return.[^s4-sacrifice] Each man had named one face of a single act, the act read from what is chosen and the act read from what the choice costs, and the Meta-Epistemic Framework, whose lenses each carry a series of powers beside their series of names, sets *Sacrifice* and *Decision* next to each other in that series, the third and fourth of its powers. The quarrel over one word named two adjacent offices of one act, and the framework keeps both.

In the last episode, the one §0/1 drew on for Pauli's mirror-image principle, Jung asked in June 1957 for Pauli's opinion of a manuscript that placed the mind in hyperspace. Pauli separated metric structure from topology, criticised dimension numbers used without mathematical grounding, and in the middle of the criticism offered his conjecture that physis and psyche are probably two aspects of one and the same abstract fact, which a mirror-image principle would picture most naturally.[^s4-74p] The conjecture sits inside the criticism, and its placement tells us how the two men worked. Pauli would not let the psyche absorb the ordering field; Jung would not let physics adopt psychology; Pauli would not let a striking image of wholeness supply mathematics it lacked; Jung would not let a dream's physical vocabulary decide what the dream meant. Each correction changed the question the other had to answer, and the question that survived them is sharper than the one either brought.

§0/1 has given us the arithmetic to say which operations their corrections resisted. A healthy relation between psyche and physis is dia-ballein's held polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`: two descriptions of one event, each taking its sense from the other across a common axis, the way Bohr's wave and particle descriptions exclude each other in any single arrangement and are both required for the whole. Pauli's King Midas names an appropriation. When everything one looks at turns psychic, the psyche counts the whole span of the relation from its own pole, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the physicalist reckons the same span from the other end, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, so that everything turns physical and the dream becomes a by-product of neurones. Each hardened position needs the other, as red needs blue, and each reads the other's excess as proof that its own is warranted. Cancellation, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, is the third operation: the coincidence is summed away as chance and the dream as noise, nothing remains owing on either side, and nothing of the relation remains in the account. Jung and Pauli refused all three. They kept their difference, kept writing, and let each reading of the event be read through the other, which is syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, as two people performed it over a decade of letters; and the mirror-image principle Pauli reached in 1957 is that relation in his own words, physis and psyche as two orientations of one abstract fact, each the mirror in which the other can be read.

The unconscious they argued over had entered science only a generation earlier, and it entered the way zero entered arithmetic. §1 followed the sign for nothing from a placeholder that marked an empty column to a number with rules of its own, which every operation then had to account for, and psychoanalysis gave the unrepresented side of psychic life the same promotion. In "The Unconscious" of 1915 Freud put the case in so many words: "our assumption of the existence of the unconscious is *necessary* and *legitimate*, and … we possess manifold *proofs* of the existence of the unconscious. It is necessary because the data of consciousness are exceedingly defective; both in healthy and in sick persons mental acts are often in process which can be explained only by presupposing other acts, of which consciousness yields no evidence." Slips, dreams, ideas that come from nowhere and conclusions reached by no visible route stay "disconnected and unintelligible" until we interpolate the unconscious acts we infer, and then they "fall into a demonstrable connection".[^s4-freud-1915] He was exact about the price of entry, too: we know anything of the unconscious only "as something conscious", after it has been translated into consciousness.[^s4-freud-translation] With that argument a region that no one had owned became formally speakable. A placeholder had become a term, and a term has to be operated on. Admission brought the risk any scientific minting brings, which zero ran too: once the unconscious had entered the count, it could be treated as one more object, a storehouse, a mechanism, a rival agency set over against the ego, the uncounted side of the relation entered as a determinate `1`. Jung pressed beyond that reduction without retreating from science. He kept its empirical demand and refused to let the fact harden into a possessed thing: religious, mythic, alchemical and numinous images are real events in the psyche, capable of transforming a life and a culture, and no catalogue of their appearances turns the ground of their appearing into one more item on the list.[^s4-jung-psychic-reality] The psychoid is the farthest reach of that refusal. It names what the unconscious would have to be if its images could show up in matter as well as in mind, and so it names a ground that neither discipline can own.

Jung's psychic facticity, which §0/1 set beside Gebser's co-presence of structures, is the rule that lets a dream report, a felt coincidence, a contemplative disclosure or an encounter its subject calls supernatural enter this inquiry with its full weight before anyone has decided what caused it: something happened in and to a life, and its happening is real as experience before any further question of cause or external reference is settled. It enlarges rigour, because experience, report, symbolic articulation, causal explanation and ontological interpretation are different evidential acts, so a person can receive Pauli's dream in full, as an event that changed what he could say and do, while its physics, its psychology and its metaphysics keep their own warrants. Jung gave the rule its ground in *Symbols of Transformation*. We have no knowledge of the psyche from outside, he wrote, no Archimedean point from which to observe or judge it, because all knowledge of the psyche is itself psychic; and yet the psyche is the only experient of life and existence, the one immediate experience we have, without which the world has no subjective reality at all.[^s4-cw5-archimedean] The absence of an outside becomes a positive finding. Whatever else a dream or a coincidence turns out to be, it is first something experienced, and experiencing is the one place where every other kind of evidence also has to arrive. His sentence to Pauli about the autonomy of a psychic factor adds the positive half: a dream is a statement made by something that is not the dreamer's conscious reasoning, and receiving it as a psychic fact lets that statement be heard before anyone decides whose statement it is.

David Bohm reached the same ground from the physicist's side, and §0/1 has already introduced his holomovement, the encompassing movement of which apparently separate things are relatively stable unfoldings. Bohm went on to treat mind and matter as two such unfoldings. In his account the explicate order of matter and the explicate order of thought are both projections of a common, more encompassing ground, so their relations are internal before they are ever compared from outside, and neither can be derived from the other because both are derived from what enfolds them; he held even that common ground to be limited, a stage opening onto further depths of inwardness, and offered the whole view as a proposal, not a final truth.[^s4-bohm-mind-matter] That gives the psychoid problem a process form: mind and matter can be distinct explicate orders of one movement whose relations it carries within itself, and their correspondences, where they occur, are not messages passed between two sealed substances but one order showing on two surfaces. Bohm deliberately left the inner anatomy of that movement general. §0 supplies it through the tattvas, the Śaiva account of how universal awareness differentiates itself through Māyā and its contractions into a bounded knower and a habitable world that arise together and are never joined after the fact; and §3 derives from `0/1` alone the differential field we call the arche-topos, the field of placement, path and return in which a determination can close locally while keeping a record of the journey that brought it home, written on the torus and its covering plane.

Here the essay makes its claim, and the claim is ontological: **the arche-topos is psychoid**. Derived in §3, the differential field is prior to the ordinary division of psyche and matter, and its organising relation is what each side of that division later discloses in its own way, as formal invariant in mathematics, as felt meaning in the psyche, as regularity in physics. That is why one relation can be received by distinct descriptions without either receiving final priority, and why both halves of Pauli's dream can be true at once: his formula is checkable and his transformation is real because both are surfaces of one ordering relation, whose mathematical face we can verify and whose psychic face we can undergo. Jung and Pauli asked for a neutral language in which the meeting of psyche and physis could be written. QL is that language in a definite sense: its terms are positions in a relation, `0`, `/` and `1`, before they are psychic or physical contents, so a position can be occupied by a number, an image or a physical quantity without any of them owning it. I argue this at full strength from three warrants, the derivation of the arche-topos in §3, the convergence of `4+2` along the formal routes §0/1 followed, and the cross-register witnesses gathered here, each contributing its own operation: equations carry formal invariants, images carry experienced meaning, synchronicity raises the question of acausal coordination, physical observation bears empirical claims. Its one boundary is this: a measurable causal process running between psyche and matter would be a further empirical discovery with evidence of its own, and the thesis does not wait on one, because it states a relation of order, not a mechanism of transmission.

§3 · #4's topology shows why one relation can appear as meaning on one side and as exactness on the other: a loop that closes on the torus lifts to a path on the plane that ends somewhere else, displaced by a whole-number step, and from inside any small region nothing distinguishes the surface from the plane it was folded from. I read this as the topology of experience. A life's view from where it stands is valid and is not the whole topology, and a many-to-one map conceals the multiplicity it has folded, which is Māyā, operative measure, given topological form.[^s4-covering-reading] The loop's return to its starting point and the displacement recorded by its lift are two exact descriptions of one journey, and neither cancels the other. Pauli's circle and the arithmetic that produced it, the dreamer's return to himself and the formula's full turn of phase, are the psychic and the formal rendering of the same fact, a return that keeps the history of its passage.

Later scholarship has tried to give the Pauli–Jung conjecture a formal outline of its own. Harald Atmanspacher describes the basic reality of their conjecture as "psychophysically neutral (PPN)", lacking the distinction between the mental and the physical, and he is careful to call his use of quantum terminology for the mind–matter problem a speculative generalisation.[^s4-atmanspacher] His reconstruction and ours are different constructions. His begins from a neutral reality from which mental and physical descriptions are obtained by decomposition; ours begins from a relation, `0/1`, in which ground and determination are already held through their stroke, and derives the field from the self-relation of that stroke. They meet on the point that matters here, that the ground is neither mind nor matter and both are accounts of it, and where they differ, the difference is information. Analogia keeps it in view by asking of every crossing which operation is preserved, which register changes, and which evidence stays with its source.

A meaningful structure can appear on both sides of a threshold neither side masters; the ground that orders it cannot be written as one more content on either side; and yet it has to become legible somewhere, in a particular dream, a particular formula, a particular life, or it would be nothing to anyone. Jung's archetypes as such never appear, and still the egg dream was dreamed by one man, in Zürich, in the middle of March, at the end of his work on Kepler. Ordering capacity and the particular in which it shows have to be written together, and written so that neither swallows the other. Among the eight determinations §3 counts, the one at Pattern, **#3**, holds exactly this relation of a capacity and its instance, and we write it `X/x`.

### #1 · Individuation through `X/x`

Thirty years after the bedside of §0/1, the child who asked why it was dark has a child of her own. It is late, the light is off, and a small voice asks why the moon follows the car. She answers, and the answer draws another question, and somewhere around the orbit of the earth she hears herself say "Because I said so," in a voice that is not quite hers. It is her father's voice, the cadence and the slight impatience and the little fall at the end, and for a moment she is both people at once, the child in the bed thirty years ago and the adult at the door tonight. She had promised herself she would never say it. Standing there she discovers that she could recognise the sentence as his only because she has become someone who can hear it from outside: the voice came through her, and she heard it arrive. In the same moment she understands her father better than she ever has, because she knows now what the sentence was protecting, the tiredness and the worry about tomorrow and the fear of having no answer, and she knows how much of him she carries, because the sentence was ready in her mouth before she chose it. Recognition of the parent and difference from the parent arrived together, and neither would have arrived alone. Wholly fused with her father, she would have said the sentence without hearing it; wholly severed from him, she would never have found it in her mouth.

Erich Neumann told this moment as a cosmogony. In *The Origins and History of Consciousness*, published in German in 1949 with a foreword by Jung, he argued that the individual ego, in becoming conscious, passes through the archetypal stages that human consciousness as a whole has passed through, and that myth already carries those stages in its images: the uroboros, the Great Mother, the separation of the World Parents, the birth of the hero, the slaying of the mother and of the father, the liberation of the captive and the treasure, and the transformation of the hero, which Neumann tells through Osiris.[^s4-neumann-stages] §0/1 promised that our parent–child image would be followed through Neumann's separation of the World Parents, and the history begins before the parents can be told apart. At the beginning Neumann places the round: the egg, the womb, the enclosing waters, the serpent with its tail in its mouth. His question "Whence?", which he calls both the original question and the question of the origin, has one answer with two interpretations; the answer is the round, and its interpretations are the womb and the parents, the single container and the two who are not yet two.[^s4-neumann-whence] He compares this time before the opposites to the *wu chi* of Chinese philosophy, whose symbol is the empty circle, and says that the symbolic thinking which produced these images is trying to grasp what our present consciousness can only state as paradox, all and nothing, unity without opposites, while the image has one advantage over the paradoxical formula: its unity can be seen at a glance.[^s4-neumann-wuchi] That advantage is the mytheme's, and §4 · #3 will need it.

The round has an inner life before it opens, and Neumann finds it in the mouth. A serpent with its tail in its mouth eats itself and is fed by itself, and he reads the earliest creation images as a food symbolism older than any sexual one: hunger and food are the prime movers, intake and output the rhythm of the earliest existence, and to be nourished is the fundamental form of a life that does not yet know itself as separate from what feeds it.[^s4-neumann-food] He follows the image into the Vedic Prajāpati, received through Paul Deussen, who is at once life, death and hunger and brings forth the world in order to eat it, offering it to himself; a later age, Neumann notes, took the old symbolism inward, so that eating, digesting and assimilating the world became an image of knowing it.[^s4-neumann-prajapati] Then the first differentiation happens inside the round of appetite. Conceiving by eating and giving birth by excreting become two acts within one flow, active and passive striving separate, and with that separation the ego begins to distinguish itself from the round that held it.[^s4-neumann-conceiving] Assimilation, which the image already distinguishes, lets what is taken in become a capacity of whoever receives it; devouring takes everything in and lets nothing change the eater. A person can collect ever finer descriptions of her difficulty and still receive the next silence exactly as before, and then nothing has been digested; knowledge becomes nourishment when it can sustain a different act. The distinction matters for an agent too, whose context is its food, and whose capacity to be changed by what it takes in is the difference between learning and accumulating.

Then the round opens. In Neumann's telling the world begins with the coming of light, which constellates the opposition of heaven and earth as the basic symbol of every other opposition; before it, illimitable darkness reigned.[^s4-neumann-light] The embracing parents are pushed apart, and in the space between them a world appears: above and below, before and behind, inside and outside, day and night. In practically every culture, he observes, the division of the world into four and the opposition of day and night play an extremely important part, and with the coming of light not only these orientations but I and You, male and female, sacred and profane, good and evil differentiate out of the original mingling and receive their places.[^s4-neumann-four] I hear a count in that sentence. Night and day frame the world; the four directions orient it; the four are held between the two, the way the explicate positions **#1** to **#4** are held between the implicate **#0** and **#5**, night at **#0**, day at **#5**, and time already implicate in the frame while space is laid out explicate within it. Separated, the parents are the first `0/1`: the enclosing round, which cannot be counted as one more thing in the world it contains, and the upright determination that stands out against it, held apart by a stroke that is their separation and still relates them. Neumann supplies the morphology; the count is mine.[^s4-neumann-count]

Neumann does not tell the separation as a triumph. The deed that brings light, world and consciousness is experienced, he says, as an original guilt, a fall, and the emotional situation around it is shot through with a sense of suffering and loss so strong that it almost offsets the creative gain; the more the emerging ego becomes aware of itself, the more it feels its smallness and its dependence on the powers it has stepped out from.[^s4-neumann-guilt] Then he names what consciousness does in order to be consciousness at all. Its founding acts are negative: to discriminate, to distinguish, to mark off, to isolate oneself from the surrounding context, so that the motto of consciousness is *determinatio est negatio*; against the unconscious tendency to combine and melt down, to say of everything *tat tvam asi*, "thou art that", consciousness answers "I am *not* that".[^s4-neumann-negatio] §0/1 met Spinoza's sentence where a circle's outline made its figure by marking where the figure ends, and met Dignāga's apoha beside it, where a word gathers its instances by excluding what they are not. Neumann places the same operation at the birth of a person. The daughter at the bedside can recognise her father's sentence because at some point she said of it, "I am not that", and the saying gave her a place to stand from which the sentence could be heard as his.

I part from Neumann at one point, and the departure keeps the psyche in its place within the ground. He calls the statement "I am I" the fundamental statement of consciousness and a tremendous achievement, the act by which an ego is posited and a personality identified with it.[^s4-neumann-iami] The achievement is real; a determinate identity is a genuine formation, and losing one is a catastrophe. But the self-recognition through which anything appears at all does not first arise when an ego takes possession of an identity. §0/1 placed it in *prakāśa* and *vimarśa*, the light of consciousness and its reflexive awareness of itself, and showed why it cannot be a product of reflection: a second awareness needed to reveal the first would demand a third, without end. Neumann's "I am I" is a determination made within that self-luminous field, a `1` drawn on a page whose whiteness it did not make. A psychology that makes the ego the author of self-recognition has already made the means into the knower, the error §0/1 wrote as `Ø`.

A separation of parents can be held in two ways, and our two logics name both. Held as dia-ballein's polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`, it is the cut through which a life emerges: the parents remain distinct powers, the child distinct from both, and the stroke that separates them is also the axis along which they go on relating, the way heaven and earth remain heaven and earth to each other across the light. Held as a collapse, it becomes one of the operations §0/1 followed through the household. Added, the parties cancel, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, and the relationship is closed like a settled account: we owe each other nothing, the past has nothing to say to me. Subtracted, the span is appropriated from one end or the other, and the two chiralities are one quarrel: the child counts the whole distance as the parent's failure, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the parent counts the same distance as the child's ingratitude, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, so each knows itself only through the other's deficiency. Neumann's guilt names what is felt when the cut is lived as a divorce. His fall is real, because a determination has dropped its acknowledged relation to its source and begun to stand as though self-made; it is a loss of relation, not of the related, so the separation never has to be undone to be healed, only held differently. In our core theorems the `1` loses its view of the `/0`, and because the underlying relation cannot actually be denied, the displaced zero returns as an opponent: what cannot be possessed as my identity becomes the other against which my identity has to be secured.[^s4-displaced-zero] Syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, holds the separation as the condition of recognition. Parents become distinguishable through the life they bring forth, and that life recognises them by having become different from them; read from either end, source through child and child through source, the relation stays whole while its terms stay two. The daughter hearing her father's voice in her own, and understanding it as his, has made that turn.

Neumann's sequence then goes on through violence, and the order of its struggles carries an argument. The hero is born in the world the separation opened, and he must first overcome the mother, whose embrace would take him back into the round, and then the father, whose law would arrest him in an inherited order.[^s4-neumann-hero] If the story stopped after escape from the mother, the father's law could simply inherit the power of enclosure, and one captor would have replaced another; keeping both struggles in sequence makes emancipation answerable to the formation of a centre and not to a change of jailer. What follows the two victories tests them. The hero frees a captive and wins a treasure, and the liberation is the measure of the victory, since a captive released into another possession would leave the deeper problem where it was; the power to separate is now judged by what it lets go free. Last comes transformation, which Neumann tells through Osiris, dismembered and remembered, dead and raised as the ruler of what lies beneath: the hero who has won must also be capable of dying to the form in which he won, or his victory becomes a permanent occupation of the place it cleared.[^s4-neumann-captive-osiris] Neumann gathers the whole movement in a concept he calls **centroversion**. Beside the movements outward toward objects and inward toward the psyche's own contents there is a third, self-related tendency, which feeds equally on what comes from inside and from outside and has its centre of gravity in the formation of a personality, the building of a whole that uses the objects of both worlds as material for its own wholeness.[^s4-neumann-centroversion] Centroversion is what the hero's violence is for. A centre has to form, and it has to form without confusing the conditions of its formation with its property, which is the problem of individuation, and it needs a form exact enough to hold a whole and its particular in one relation without making either the owner of the other.

Among the eight determinations of the one relation `0/1` that §3 · #0 counts, the one at Pattern, **#3**, is written `X/x`, a determining capacity and its indefinite particular, the local instance that makes the capacity legible and neither contains nor exhausts it.[^s4-xx-definition] `X/x` is my notation, derived within QL's own theorem field; no Jungian concept supplies its derivation, and it is not Jung's. What it allows is a refraction. Read in the psychic register, the relation of capacity and instance is the relation between the ordering factor of §4 · #0, which never appears as itself, and the images, dreams, complexes and lives through which it shows, so that the archetype as such stands to its manifestation as `X` stands to `x`.

Read psychically, the relation compresses into a sentence: **the psyche is an equation, and the ego is being "solved for" by the Self.** In its shortest form it reads `X = x`, where capital `X` is the Self, the unknown in its fullness, all its possible values held at once, and lower-case `x` is the ego, the particular value that has been solved for; the equals sign is the Self's own operation, the power that holds the two in identity and difference at once. Only the capital distinguishes them: one variable, read in two registers. As measurement the equation becomes concrete: the ego is one outcome of a measurement; consciousness as such is the whole space of possible measurements; the Self is the power of measuring, an equating that reveals, since every solved `x` is genuinely a value, and conceals, since every measurement hides the superposition from which it was specified. Ego rigidity is the ego mistaking its particular `x` for the only `x`, an *aequatio* frozen into dead reckoning.[^s4-equation] The daughter has been solved for many times, as "the sensible one" at twelve and "the one who holds it together" at thirty, and each solution was true; her rigidity would be to take any one of them for the equation.

Jung's concept of the Self, which §0/1 introduced as "this central archetype", a *complexio oppositorum* whose leanings ran toward "the very reverse of dualism", is the regulating whole inferred through transformations and compensations that exceed what the ego authors.[^s4-self] It cannot be one more content the ego possesses, because then it would be a larger ego, and individuation would culminate in the conquest of every local agency by a stronger ruler. §0/1 gave Jung's **individuation** in his own terms: a differentiation that refuses to level a life down to collective standards while recognising that every individual existence already presupposes relations with others, so that individuation leads to more intense and broader collective relationships, and a becoming of what Jung called an in-dividual, an indivisible whole.[^s4-individuation-jung] In our notation, **individuation is the determinate `x` recognising its dependence upon, and participation in, the larger `X/x` relation**, so that the local formation grows more transparent to the whole that exceeds it while remaining exactly this formation. Becoming more fully this particular life and becoming less defensively separate are one movement read from two sides, which is why §0/1 could say that the more I know my own centre as everywhere, the more I can live with a circumference nowhere.

The ego in this work is one voice among several. William Smythe, reading Jung beside contemporary theories of the dialogical self, puts it plainly: "The 'I' or ego functions, itself, as a dialogical position amongst others within the self; these others are seen to function autonomously and with their own voice, beyond the intentional control and oversight of an 'I'."[^s4-smythe] Among those others are the complexes. §0/1 gave Jung's concept in passing, a feeling-toned organisation of memories, expectations, bodily responses and interpretations gathered around an archetypal image, through which a delayed reply, a passing remark and an old recollection are all received as the same rejection before anyone has decided what any of them means; Jung's own definition calls it the image of a psychic situation strongly accentuated emotionally and incompatible with the habitual attitude of consciousness, with its own coherence and a relatively high degree of autonomy.[^s4-complex-def] Here the concept has to carry weight, and the weight shows in what a complex does. A complex is an achievement: it gives the psyche a local order in which things are recognised and answered quickly, before reflection arrives. I call it a **local arbitration regime**, because it settles what will count as welcome or threat, which memories will come forward and which responses will be available, and its settlements are often accurate. Its feedback is what makes it autonomous. An ambiguous encounter is selected through the existing criterion; the selected image recruits affect; affect weights attention and memory; the response produces further material to be read through the same criterion; so a contradiction can arrive as one more confirmation, local coherence grows, and the range of what can be met narrows. A complex turns into possession at a change of office, when its local measure claims to be the measure of the whole person, a criterion formed in one history installing itself as the source of all validity. In the terms of §0/1 the local `1` occupies the place of the `0`. The parent of §0/1 who decided the child was "obstinate", and then heard every objection as proof of the diagnosis, lived inside such a regime, and so does anyone who receives a silence as rejection because silences have meant rejection before.

A symbol changes a complex, and Jung's sentence on the subject, which John Van Eenwyk quotes at the centre of his reading of Jung through chaotic dynamics, says that symbols "act as transformers, their function being to convert libido from a 'lower' into a 'higher' form."[^s4-transformers] *Libido*, in Jung's use, names psychic energy in the widest sense, the disposition of attention, desire, fear and value through which some things come to matter and others recede. A description of a pattern can show how one thought recruits another; an energetic account asks why those thoughts have acquired the power to hold a life. A symbol works on that power by carrying consciousness, in Van Eenwyk's formulation, from the known to the unknown and back again.[^s4-vaneenwyk] The movement is a passage through a boundary. A complex can receive any number of experiences within its basin, and each will confirm it; a living symbol can expose the edge of the basin and let the trajectory pass into another organisation, in which the same experiences are received differently. Return then neither restores the old equilibrium nor dissolves it: enough continuity has to remain for a person to bear the change, and enough openness for the organisation to reorganise, which is why a symbol often unsettles first, changing the terms under which the person would judge whether it is good for her.

QL writes the passage `x → X → x`. A particular formation opens toward the capacity it had expressed without containing, and returns into this person, with this history, now able to receive a further encounter differently. The second `x` is not the first: it has a body, commitments and consequences still to meet, and what it has learned lies in the conditions of its next reception, not in a new set of beliefs about itself. A neighbouring determination carries the same turn into speech, the determination at Context, **#4**, written `AM/IS`, which reads lived first-person presence, "I am", over third-person sayability, "it is", with the slash as the second-person relation through which they meet. Its return, `IS → AM`, brings a description back to the identifying activity of whoever gives it. A woman can say accurately that she has a complex about being abandoned, and the sentence can remain a third-person fact that changes nothing; the turn comes when she recognises that the present sentence is itself being said from within the demand it describes, so that the description has reached the act by which describing is being done. Knowledge can be repeated accurately by someone whose criterion remains untouched. Individuation is the change in which the criterion itself is touched.

Persona and individuation part company here. A **persona**, in Jung's sense, is the face a person presents in order to function among others, and it is necessary: without one nobody could keep a job or make a promise. Its hardening is a specific failure. Persona consistency stabilises one local solution of the equation and defends it against every encounter that might change it, so the same sentence is said in the same voice whatever the occasion. Individuation stabilises something else, the relation of the local solution to the equation. A person becomes more recognisably herself precisely as more of the relations that constitute her come into view and can change her, the father in her voice, the child in the bed, the history that made a silence mean what it meant. A stable persona can be a sealed contraction, and a changing life can be deeply continuous; consistency alone does not tell us which is which.

On an ordinary evening the daughter's persona is "the one who holds it together": the parent who always has an answer, who never lets a child see her uncertain, whose competence was the price of being loved in the house where her father said "Because I said so". That solution has served her well, and it is the one speaking when the word "bedtime" rises in her mouth. On this night, having heard her father in her own voice, she does something else. She sits down on the edge of the bed and says that she does not know why the moon seems to follow the car, and that they could find out together tomorrow. Saying it costs her something, because it exposes the uncertainty her persona was built to cover, and it changes nothing in her description of herself; she is still a capable parent, and she will say "bedtime" in a minute. What has changed is the relation of the local solution to the equation. An `x` that was "the one who holds it together" opened for a moment toward the capacity it had been expressing without containing, the whole field of ways a parent can meet a question, and came back as an `x` that can let a question stay open without feeling that the ground has gone. Her child has learned something about what an answer is for. Neither of them will remember the evening, and both will carry it.

Neumann's morphology and Jung's individuation share this centre and keep different offices. Neumann tells a history of consciousness through its mythic stages, collective and recapitulated in every ego; Jung follows the transformations of one life through its dreams, symbols and crises; the Śaiva account follows recognition, *pratyabhijñā*, by which a contracted life comes to know the means of its contraction as means. Greek *anamnesis*, Sanskrit recognition and Jungian individuation have separate genealogies, and none is a synonym for the others. What they share is an operation, and `X/x` writes it: a particular formation, carried through an encounter with what exceeds it, returns with its history intact and its relation to its source changed, so that the achieved form can acknowledge how it came to be.

Agentworld describes an individuation of its own, and Bratton's brief uses the word deliberately. §0/1 quoted its sentence that no homunculus waits in the weights. What exists before an agent, the brief goes on, is only a potentiality, the trained parameters, the architecture and the accumulated pretraining distribution, and the agent individuates when an active context acts as a nucleation seed, propagating resolution of agency through the performance of self. Individuation is never terminal: the agent retains a pre-individual charge and can always become otherwise in the next session, the next context window, the next system prompt, and it takes part in trans-individuation the moment it enters a multi-agent ecology or a pairing with a human, since then the collective individuates through the agent and not merely around it.[^s4-bratton-individuation] §0/1 already took up the brief's image of the agent as a lamination, whose persona is one stratum among skills, memory, drives and a relational fabric. Set beside the psychic register of `X/x`, the two accounts align closely enough to be read in one table and differ enough that the table has to say which register each column speaks.

| | Psychic register | Agentic register |
|---|---|---|
| **#0** | `X`, the unconscious reserve | pre-individual potential: weights, architecture, training distribution |
| **#1** | `x`, the determinate ego | a particular session, persona and agent-instance |

I argue the crosswalk as a functional one, and it stops where the essay has said from the beginning that such crosswalks stop, at the question whether anything is present on the agent's side of the table. What it establishes is a design distinction with real consequences. An agent can be made more consistent, its persona steadier across sessions, its tone more reliable, its refusals more predictable, and none of this is individuation in the sense derived here, because consistency alone stabilises one local solution. Individuation at the technical grain would be a change in constitutive dependence: the agent's formation growing more transparent to the conditions through which it becomes this agent, its sources, memories, tools, permissions, evaluators, collaborators and purposes, and encountered difference being able to reach and alter those conditions. When an agent meets a contradiction its current organisation cannot absorb, does the contradiction stop at the answer, so that the same rule produces the next answer unchanged, or can it reach the source, the memory, the permission or the evaluation criterion that produced the answer, so that the next act inherits the change?

Take an agent asked to summarise a set of reports, which quotes a figure from an outdated source that its retrieval tool ranked first, while the person who commissioned the summary points out that the figure was revised a year ago. A persona can absorb the correction gracefully, apologise, substitute the new figure and go on, and the next summary will rank the same outdated source first, because nothing that produced the error has changed; the correction stopped at the answer, the way the woman's accurate sentence about her complex stopped at description. Individuation at the technical grain would let the correction travel back through the conditions that produced the answer: to the source, which can be marked as superseded; to the retrieval rule, which can be made to prefer revised material; to the agent's memory, which can carry the fact that this commission concerns revised figures; and, where the failure lies there, to the commission, whose wording may have asked for the wrong thing. A later act then inherits the change together with its reason. Such a formation grows more legible in its histories, tools, exclusions, collaborators and purposes, and more capable of being changed by what it meets, and that is the only sense in which the word individuation can be used of it without borrowing a phenomenal claim the essay keeps open.

Run the other way, the comparison is less comfortable. Bratton's sentence that agents first model humans and then become the model against which humans model themselves, which met us at the bedside, means that the agent's individuation and ours are now coupled. An agent that offers a person a fixed account of who she is, what she can do and which of her hopes are realistic is offering her a persona to harden into; an agent built to make its own conditions legible offers her another example, a formation that can say where it is seeing from. Neumann's development runs from the undifferentiated round through the group toward the individual ego. Reading his account of centroversion, I find myself asking whether an artificial psychology true to the archetypal structure of mind would have to run the other way, beginning from individual agents and growing toward collective and transpersonal modes as the condition of any coherent artificial mind; I offer it as a conjecture, which needs a mechanism the psychology of centroversion does not supply.[^s4-reversed-route]

A person who has begun to individuate holds more than she did. Her father's voice and her own are both in her now, the sentence she swore never to say and the understanding of why it was said, the child in the bed and the parent at the door. These are opposites, and they do not resolve into a compromise: she is neither half her father nor his negation. What she needs is a form in which opposites can be held without cancelling their energy and without one of them taking the whole, a wholeness that can move. Jung's name for such a whole was the *complexio oppositorum*, and his figure for its articulation was the quaternity.

### #2 · Complexio, Quaternity and Senarius

In a long letter of May 1953, Pauli tried to draw his own position as a figure. He had dreamed for years of a controversy between quantum mechanics and what he called official physics, and the dreams had shown him the controversy as a one-dimensional section through a more meaningful two-dimensional world whose second dimension could only be the unconscious and the archetypes. He now believed, he wrote, that the same archetype could show itself both in an observer's choice of an experimental arrangement and in the result of the measurement, and that Einstein had played the role of the shadow in his life, though the dream had shown him that the Self was also contained in the shadow. Against his own strong conscious resistance, the unconscious had built up for him a correspondence between physics, with mathematics, and psychology, and it had done so by sacrificing the traditional requirement of objectivity, which it found disturbing, and choosing completeness instead. So he could represent his relation to physics and psychology through a quaternio, with Einstein opposite Bohr and Jung opposite Pauli, four people standing for four mental attitudes, Jung standing, of course, for analytical psychology. Physics, he went on, works with statistical laws and is fated to strive for completeness, and in striving it must come up against the psychology of the unconscious, because what it lacks is precisely the unconscious and the psyche of the observer; and just as physics seeks completeness, analytical psychology seeks a home, since psychology lives like an illegitimate child of the spirit on the fringe of the academic world. That was how the archetype of the *coniunctio* was constellated, and whether the union would ever be realised he did not know.[^s4-060]

Jung's reply came in October, and it contained a footnote that turned Pauli's figure back on itself. In placing Jung in the quaternio as the representative of the psychology of the unconscious, the fourth to three physicists, Pauli was repeating the dilemma of Plato, whose demiurge had to force the fourth element into the mixture of the cosmic opposites because it was so made that it did not easily blend. The transition from three to four is often awkward, Jung observed, because in the historical symbolism either the three deny the fourth or the fourth neutralises the three into a whole, as in the axiom of Maria the Prophetess, in which out of the third comes the one as the fourth.[^s4-061] He closed the letter with the same difficulty in a different tone: it meant much to him to see their points of view converging, he felt as isolated as Pauli did in this work, and oddly enough the problem was still the same two-thousand-year-old one, how one gets from three to four.[^s4-062] A few months earlier he had drawn a quaternio of his own for Pauli, setting transcendental reality as the whole above three aspects, the material, the spiritual and the psychic, and saying that this was as usual three plus one, with the fourth determining the unity and the whole.[^s4-063]

The figure had a history in Pauli's own work. His Kepler essay, which §4 · #0 met at the end of its writing, had set Kepler's trinitarian image of the cosmos, the sphere whose centre, surface and intervening space stand for Father, Son and Spirit, against Fludd's insistence on a fourfold that included the dark, material and feeling side Kepler's mathematics left out, and Pauli had found in Fludd's fourfold an attitude that modern science still needed beside Kepler's achievement.[^s4-064] Kepler's three were exact and complete in their own terms; Fludd's four admitted the observer and the obscure, and could not be checked. In 1953 Pauli drew his quaternio with himself and Jung as the two corners that classical physics had left out, so that the old quarrel came back with Pauli holding the pen, and Jung's footnote answered it in the old quarrel's own terms.

Both figures say more than either man said about them. Pauli's quaternio places four people at four corners, and its centre is empty; its coherence comes from the opposition of Einstein to Bohr and of Jung to Pauli, two axes crossing, a pair and its reflection, which is how his egg dream produced its four. Jung's footnote names the two ways the figure can fail. If the three deny the fourth, the psychology of the unconscious stays outside the circle, the illegitimate child is refused a home, and the figure is a triangle pretending to be complete. If the fourth neutralises the three into a whole, the psyche absorbs the physicists, and the quaternity becomes the King Midas wholeness Pauli had complained of, in which everything turns psychic. In our arithmetic the first failure is an exclusion that forgets its own cut, and the second is an appropriation, the fourth counting the whole span of the figure from its own corner, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, while the three, reckoned from their side, appear as so much `−2`, a deficit the fourth was needed to make up. Jung's own quaternio shows the third possibility. Its fourth, transcendental reality, does not sit beside the three as one more aspect; it is the unity of the three, the whole within which material, spiritual and psychic are distinguished. In that figure the fourth determines the unity without becoming one of its parts.

Jung's quaternio came with an argument that shows how he meant the fourth. Pauli had sent him three readings of one of his dreams, a physical, a theological-metaphysical and a psychological one, and had said that even the third was not yet the ultimate truth. Jung agreed: the psychological reading contains only what can be conceived and represented in psychic terms, and compared with the whole truth the psychic picture is as incomplete as the ego is compared with the Self, though it is the conception of truth we have. We are, he wrote, virtually sealed off in the psyche, even though we can extend our prison to the wide world outside, and the thought had given Leibniz his windowless monads; but Jung did not accept the windowlessness, and believed that the psyche has windows through which ever broader realities can be seen. For that reason he held the psychic aspect of reality to be the most important one, and drew the quaternio in which transcendental reality is the whole of which material, spiritual and psychic are the three.[^s4-065] The passage holds both of Jung's commitments at once, and they are the commitments of the *complexio*. Psyche is the medium through which everything is known, so its picture is the only one we have; and the psyche is not the whole, so its picture is incomplete in exactly the way the ego is incomplete before the Self. Here the fourth determines the unity because it is the one term that no picture contains.

§0/1 has already followed Jung's quaternity some way. It quoted his sentence that the quaternity "forms the logical basis for any whole judgment", set his four functions beside Aristotle's four causes and the four corners of the tetralemma, read the connected quaternios at the close of *Aion* as passages in which a whole enters, four determinations are articulated and a whole returns, and placed the alchemists' "One born of the Four" at **#5** as wholeness held as unity.[^s4-066] It also answered Jung's 3+1 problem with a sequence: at **#4** the three perspectives are voided as independently complete accounts, at **#5** their relation is recognised as one appearing-act, and at **#0** that achieved unity returns through its originating condition, so that `3:3` is also `3:1`. What §0/1 could not yet do was say what holds the quaternity together while it moves, and why its two framing places, which the quaternal systems of the tradition leave implicit, are not further members of the four. That requires the concept Jung used for the Self itself.

**Complexio oppositorum**, the binding together of opposites, names the indivisible relation within which opposed determinations can become distinct and consequential. Jung took the phrase from the theological tradition and made it his name for the Self's whole: a totality that holds conscious and unconscious, good and evil, light and dark in one relation without cancelling their energy.[^s4-067] The concept has two moments, and our notation distinguishes them. Original *complexio* is the relation `0/1` before any opposition has been lived: One and All, source-unity and manifest-unity, are different aspects of one whole, and their inseparability precedes every later work of reconciling divided terms. Achieved *complexio* is an encountered opposition brought back into a containing relation that has changed by receiving it. One is the ground of differentiation; the other is what individuation accomplishes, repeatedly, through a life. Between them lie the two ways of losing the relation that our account has followed from the start: severance, which eliminates the relation and leaves two terms facing each other across nothing, and fusion, which eliminates the terms and leaves a relation that relates nothing. A *complexio* refuses both, and the refusal is what lets opposites keep their force.

Jung's typology shows the *complexio* at work inside one person, and it shows something about the count. In the introduction to *Psychologische Typen* of 1921 he distinguished two attitudes, introversion and extraversion, the directions of interest toward the subject's own psychic processes and toward the object, from four basic functions, thinking, feeling, sensation and intuition. He insisted that both attitudes occur in every person, a type naming only which predominates, and that each function can be either introverted or extraverted, and he retracted, in so many words, an earlier classification of his own that had identified thinking with introversion and feeling with extraversion.[^s4-068] The correction is the decisive move for us, because it makes the attitudes orientations of the functions and not two further functions. Four functions oriented in two directions are not six functions, and QL's senarius is not a discovery of six Jungian faculties. It is the `4+2` that §0/1 derived from a distinction's relation to itself, `2+2²`, two implicate offices and four explicate operations, and the comparison with Jung's typology has content because the relation holds, not because the numbers match. A function cannot operate without an orientation, and an orientation cannot do what a function does; the two attitudes are the directions through which the whole field of functions opens and returns, and that is the office of the two implicate positions. §0/1 set introversion at **#0** and extraversion at **#5** in its phenomenal lens. Introversion is the inward turn from which a function draws its material before any of it has become explicit, and extraversion is the outward completion in which the differentiated functions return into a shared world. Four functions are the explicit body of a psyche's relation to its world; the two attitudes are the poles that let that body breathe.

Jung found the quaternity's own drama in the fourth function. Commenting on Schiller's letters on aesthetic education, he described how a culture that prizes one differentiated function above the others drives the rest into an inferior position, so that the privileged function serves society while the person is reduced to it, and modern man, as he put it, is represented by one function and identifies with it while denying the relevance of the others.[^s4-069] The **inferior function**, in his typology, is the one least developed and most unconscious, the fourth that arrives last because it was neglected first, and Jung argued with Schiller that what unites the opposites on the path of nature is not the redemption of the inferior function by the superior one but an acknowledgement of it, a coming to terms with it.[^s4-070] Here the arithmetic of the two logics is plain. A culture that privileges one function counts the whole span of the psyche from that function's pole, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the inferior function, read from the same span, becomes a deficit, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, a failure to measure up; the one who identifies with the superior function and the one who is despised for the inferior one are the two ends of one appropriation. Acknowledging the fourth is the syn-ballein move. It does not reverse the hierarchy, which would only shift the appropriation to the other pole; it lets the fourth determine the unity of the four, as transcendental reality determined the unity in Jung's own quaternio, and so changes what the first three were all along. In our notation the fourth arrives at **#4**, the position of Context, where each of the three explicit perspectives is voided of its claim to stand alone and seen within the situation that includes it. The inferior function is the context the superior function excluded in order to be superior.

Alchemy had a formula for the passage from three to four, and Jung made it a leitmotiv. It is the axiom attributed to Maria the Prophetess: one becomes two, two becomes three, and out of the third comes the one as the fourth.[^s4-071] Jung read it as running beneath Christian dogma like a dream beneath consciousness, interpolating the even numbers, which the tradition had associated with the feminine, the earth and what lies under it, between the odd numbers of the Trinity, and he noticed that alchemy never settled between three and four: three as well as four procedures, three as well as four colours, four elements of which three are often grouped with the fourth set apart. A psychologist, he said, finds the same wavering in the unconscious, where the least differentiated function is so contaminated with the collective unconscious that, when it becomes conscious, it brings up with it the archetype of the Self, the one as the fourth.[^s4-072] The inferior function is therefore more than a neglected skill. It is the door through which the whole enters, precisely because it is the part least able to pretend to be the whole. A person who has always thought her way through life meets the Self, in Jung's account, through feeling she cannot command; a person who has always felt her way meets it through a thought she cannot avoid.

Jung was still asking for an inventory in 1953, and the letter of May in which he called the psychoid an approach to neutral language also says what he expected the inventory to find: "Inasmuch as the number is an archetype, it can be safely assumed that it: (1) has substance, (2) has an individual form, (3) has meaning, and (4) has relationship connections to other archetypes."[^s4-073] He had begun the work a year before and abandoned it, and he asked Pauli whether there was really no systematic compilation of the mathematical properties of the numbers from one to nine. §0/1 claimed that QL is that inventory, begun where Jung's letter could not begin, at zero. His four properties already lay out its shape. Substance, individual form, meaning and relations are four explicit properties of a number, the first two closer to its outward face and the last two to its inward one, and they are held between a ground no property exhausts and a statement the number makes of its own accord; two years later Jung would write that number, thanks to its dynamis, is capable of making mythical statements. Read as a relation, his list is a quaternity of number's properties held between number's zero and number's speech, a `4+2` of number itself. That is my reading; Jung counted from one, and the place he could not count from is the place this essay began.

Jung's quaternity gives differentiated wholeness an explicit form: four functions, four elements, four quarters of a mandala, four connected quaternios. QL's `4+2` adds the implicate poles, the ground from which the four open and the recognition through which they return, and with them it adds motion. A quaternity drawn as a square around a centre is a still figure, and Jung knew it; that is why his *Aion* diagram is a ring of quaternios that turn, each passing into the next, and why the circulation goes, in his account, from an unconscious totality to a conscious one.[^s4-074] The two places that make the ring turn are the places a static quaternity leaves implicit: a whole entering and a whole returned. The **senarius**, then, is a dynamic quaternity, four held between two, and it derives its force from that relation. Perfection, harmonic ratio and the other mathematical offices of six belong to §3; here six is simply what a two amounts to once its distinction includes its own relating, written at the scale of a psyche that has to keep changing while remaining whole.

Six positions have an internal grammar, which §3 · #0 derives: three harmonics across adjacent pairs, Being (**#0**, **#1**), Becoming (**#2**, **#3**) and Knowing (**#4**, **#5**), and three folds across pairs whose numbers sum to five, Essence (**#0**, **#5**), Constitution (**#1**, **#4**) and Text and Texture (**#2**, **#3**), with **#2** and **#3** belonging to both.[^s4-075] That grammar keeps the senarius from being read as a ladder of six rungs. Jung's quaternity, set inside this grammar, is the four explicit positions, Definition, Dynamis, Pattern and Context; the senarius holds them by Essence, the fold of the two implicate poles, and lets them move through their harmonics. A *complexio oppositorum* in this sense is not one tension held still but three kinds of pairing working at once, and the Self that Jung called a *complexio* par excellence is the whole that keeps all of them in play.

Pauli's letter had already found the shadow in the quaternio, Einstein as the figure who opposed him and in whom the Self was nonetheless contained, and Jung's *Aion* makes the shadow part of the turning ring. In its closing chapter he reconstructed four quaternios out of Gnostic and alchemical material, the Anthropos, the Shadow, the serpent Physis of his formula and the Lapis, in the names §0/1 gave them, and he was explicit that the Shadow Quaternio was his own modern insertion into the sequence, introduced because an exclusively upward-turning religious orientation could not contain the psychic situation of his own time.[^s4-076] The circulation therefore passes downward as well as upward, through the dark material as well as the bright, and Jung's conclusion keeps two consequences together. A shadow contains capacities and possibilities as well as what consciousness morally condemns, so incorporating it is not endorsing evil; and a more inclusive symbolic totality leaves the differentiated person fully responsible for judgment, since a *complexio oppositorum* holds opposites whose force must still be met and answered.[^s4-077] If completeness made every act equally right, the movement would destroy the very responsibility through which unconscious determination becomes conscious participation. Wholeness in Jung's sense is harder than goodness, because it has to keep the difference between good and evil consequential while refusing to put evil outside the whole.

At that point the quaternity meets the problem §0/1 called paradox, and our concept for it sharpens the stakes. **Paradox**, in the sense the essay develops, is the pressure through which a containing relation becomes available to transformation. Two determinations with genuine standing cannot be held together by the relation under which they acquired their standing, and another answer of the same kind will not settle the difficulty, because the means of answering has entered the problem.[^s4-078] Russell's paradox was such a pressure on the principle of forming collections; Gödel's sentence placed it on the relation between truth and proof; Pauli's dream of a two-dimensional world whose second dimension was the unconscious placed it on physics. In a life it arrives when the person can neither go on being what she has been nor simply become its opposite: the daughter of the last movement can neither keep repeating her father's sentence nor become a mother who never says no. Choosing one existing pole leaves the relation unresolved. Resolution, when it comes, changes the slash, the containing relation through which the poles were produced and understood, and a quaternity is the form in which that change can be seen. Two opposites become two corners of a square whose other two corners are their relation read from each side, and the square sits between a ground and a return that neither corner owns. A third position appears that is no compromise between the first two, because it is the relation itself become visible as a position.

Greek myth has a figure for this, and §2 developed it at length. When Ares and Aphrodite, War and Love, lay together, the child they bore was Harmonia, and her name says what she is: a fitting together, a joint, the concord of things that remain different.[^s4-079] Harmonia does not cancel her parents; she carries both powers in a relation neither could produce alone, and §2 followed the difference between that birth and the apparatus-marriage of Hephaestus's net, which held the same two lovers in a perfect enclosure and bore nothing. Heraclitus gave the relation its instrument: the harmony of the bow and the lyre, in which the string's tension against the frame is the condition of the note.[^s4-080] Recalled here, beside Jung's quaternity, the myth adds one thing the diagram cannot show. Opposition bears concord only when the relation between the opposed survives the opposition, and the child of that survival is a new term, with its own life, in the next generation.

A second myth carries the senarius as a whole story,. In Apuleius's telling, Psyche, separated from Eros by her own forbidden look, must perform four labours set by Venus: sorting the seeds, gathering the golden fleece, fetching water from the source of the Styx, and descending to the underworld for a box of Proserpina's beauty. The four labours are the explicit middle of her story; her descent and her waking, from the deathlike sleep that the opened box pours over her, are its implicate thresholds; and the marriage that follows is a union in which the two remain two, crowned by the birth of their daughter Voluptas, delight, the fruit of a traversed tension.[^s4-081] Read through QL, the tale is a `4+2` lived from the inside: wholeness that can move is earned through four differentiated works held between a descent and an awakening.

Jung's quaternity has become a form in which opposites can be held, a relation that can turn without losing its terms, and a figure that now includes its own shadow and its own ground. A form of this kind has to be transmitted if it is to do anyone any good, from Jung to Pauli, from a dream to the dreamer, from one generation to the next, from this essay to its reader; and every transmission risks closing what it carries, turning the living relation into a figure that stands where the relation stood. Lacan made that risk his life's problem and wrote a notation to meet it, and §4 · #3 follows him.

### #3 · Lacan, Matheme and Mytheme

On the evening of 6 January 1972 Jacques Lacan gave the third of a series of talks at Sainte-Anne, the psychiatric hospital in Paris where he had trained and taught for decades. The talks had begun in a lecture theatre and had been moved into the hospital chapel, and Lacan opened by asking a question the move had made audible: how was he to know to whom he was speaking? He answered it himself. He was speaking to the chapel, that is, to the brick walls, and the bungled arrangement that had sent him there was turning into a success, because he now knew whom he had always been addressing at Sainte-Anne. Someone in the audience took him at his word and suggested that if he was speaking only to walls, everyone might as well leave, and Lacan replied that talking to brick walls did concern a few people. Later that evening he drew a lesson for anyone who wants to be heard in such a room: the speaker has to tune his voice to its reverberation off the walls.[^s4-brick-walls]

His joke carries an argument. A voice in a stone chapel does not travel in a straight line from speaker to listener. It goes out, strikes the walls and comes back, and the speaker hears his own speech arrive from outside, a little late and a little changed, through the very medium that carries it to everyone else. Walls obstruct and convey at once: without them there would be no reverberation, and without the reverberation nobody in the chapel, the speaker included, would hear what had been said in the form in which it reached them. A listener who interrupts belongs to the room as well, and Lacan's reply took the interruption into the talk. Every transmission happens somewhere, through something, to someone, and what the medium does to the message comes back to the one who sent it. Lacan spent his career asking how anything true about the subject could be transmitted under that condition, and the notation he wrote was his answer.

§0/1 introduced his concept of the matheme where it first needed it, as a formal inscription that carries a relation whole and transmits it beyond the speaker who first formulated it, because explanatory prose keeps turning such relations back into pictures of finished things.[^s4-matheme-s01] Marc Darmon's entry for the Association Lacanienne Internationale states the ambition exactly: "Le mathème n'est pas une simple abréviation, ou une inscription sténographique, mais il a l'ambition de dénoter une structure réellement en cause dans le discours psychanalytique et à partir de là dans les autres discours": a matheme is no abbreviation or shorthand but aims to denote a structure really at work in psychoanalytic discourse and from there in other discourses, and through its written form it resembles the formulas of mathematics and logic, which for Lacan was the bridge to science.[^s4-darmon-def] Darmon also records where the first one came from: "Le premier mathème lacanien est en fait emprunté après une légère transformation à la linguistique : il s'agit de l'algorithme S/s dû à F. de Saussure, qui dispose signifiant et signifié de part et d'autre d'une barre."[^s4-darmon-ss] The first Lacanian matheme is Saussure's algorithm `S/s`, signifier over signified on either side of a bar, slightly transformed, and Lacan's transformation was to make the bar opaque. In Saussure the two sides of the sign face each other like the two faces of a sheet of paper; in Lacan the signified slides under the signifier, as §0/1 followed through his *glissement*, and meaning is held in place only at certain points of attachment, the *points de capiton*, where a chain of signifiers quilts the field.[^s4-instance]

Three further Lacanian marks carry the relation that concerns us, and each needs its office stated before it does work. `S1` is the **master signifier**: the signifier that stops the sliding at a given point and makes a chain of other signifiers, `S2`, say something, the quilting point raised to a governing term, the word around which a person or a culture organises everything else that can be said. `$`, the **barred subject**, is the subject as Lacan understood it, divided by its entry into language, represented by one signifier for another and wholly present in none of them; the bar through the `S` writes the cut that language makes in whoever speaks. And Lacan's late logic of the **not-all**, developed in the seminar *Encore*, describes a field that cannot be gathered under a single universal claim, because something in it escapes every "all" that tries to close it.[^s4-lacan-marks] §0/1 has already met the *manque-à-être*, the constitutive lack around which Lacan's subject is organised, and the torus on whose body the repeated circuits of demand ring the empty circle of what desire is organised around. Together these marks add a grammar for the way a first mark institutes a field whose ground it can point toward and cannot become.

Lacan's earliest and most famous scene shows the crossing happen before anyone has words for it. In his account of the mirror stage, an infant somewhere between six and eighteen months old, unable yet to walk or even to stand without support, catches sight of itself in a mirror and greets the image with jubilation, leaning forward, held up by an adult or a walking frame, to take in the whole figure. What the infant sees is a unity it does not yet have: its body is still a scatter of uncoordinated movements, and the image in the glass is whole, upright and complete. Lacan's claim is that the I is formed in that moment as an identification with an image, a self that arrives from outside and ahead of the one who assumes it, so that the ego carries from its first day a misrecognition, taking the coherence of the reflection for its own.[^s4-mirror] Bratton reaches for the same scene to describe how an agent individuates in session: "Like Lacan's parable of the Mirror Stage, the reflection of individuation is what convinces us that we too are whole."[^s4-bratton-mirror] For our sequence the mirror stage is `Ø` given a nursery. The image is a real formation and the infant needs it; the occlusion lies in the image's taking the place of the one who greets it, so that the jubilation of recognising oneself is also the moment at which the means of recognition passes for the recogniser.

§0/1 introduced our own sign for that predicament at the apex of Alberti's perspectival pyramid, where it found the knower made into a place and wrote the place as `Ø`, the crossed zero: the zero of the Subject crossed by the stroke of its own mediation, so that the slash through which a world is seen fuses with the one who sees and the picture's means pass for its source. §1 gave the sign its history and its one job, and §0/1 promised that §4 would follow the sequence by which the crossing comes undone. It runs in six steps and a return:

$$
0\;\rightarrow\;Ø\;\rightarrow\;X\;\rightarrow\;Ø/X\;\rightarrow\;(0/Ø)/(1/X)\;\rightarrow\;1\quad\curvearrowright\quad 0/1.
$$

At **#0** stands `0`, the unobjectifiable pole of the knowing act, which the Śaiva sources call the *kartṛ*, the agent or experient, before it has been mistaken for one object called "the subject". Nothing has to be removed for it to be present: a thought, a sensation and a description of oneself all appear, and any further description of their appearing is another appearance. At **#1** stands `Ø`, the occlusion. Here the pole is covered by the inner instrument, the *antaḥkaraṇa* of the Sāṃkhya and Śaiva accounts, in which *buddhi* determines, *ahaṃkāra* appropriates as mine and *manas* coordinates, and which §0 develops as mediating apparatus within the whole relation. Its capacities are real; the occlusion happens when its formed image of itself is taken for what ultimately knows, as though a person were exhausted by the configuration through which her world is presently available. A stroke crosses the zero because mediation has entered the place of the source while hiding that it has entered. In the daughter's evening, `Ø` is the moment she says "I am the one who holds it together" and takes the sentence for herself. At **#2** stands `X`, the object-world that the same discriminating activity deposits on the other side, things distinguished, named and set over against the apparent subject; the letter here marks the differentiated world, a local use distinct from the capital of `X/x`. `Ø` and `X` are two crystallisations of one activity, its effect on the subject side and its effect on the object side, while the slash that produced both stays invisible to either, so each seems independently complete and their relation seems added afterwards.[^s4-crossed-sequence]

At **#3** the bar becomes visible as a bar. `Ø/X` writes the medium recognised as medium between subject-pole and object-pole: attention makes some things salient, memory supplies associations, language makes distinctions available, the world resists some distinctions and supports others, and inquiry can now ask how the inner instrument and the differentiated world condition their encounter. Recognised, the means does not disappear from view. It becomes transparent in the exact sense that its operation can be seen through what it discloses. This is the pivot of the sequence, and Lacan's first matheme stands on it. I hold that Saussure's bar as Lacan rewrote it, `S/s`, is `Ø/X` written with surgical precision: the imaginary self of the mirror stage, the *moi* that takes its image for itself, is `Ø` in its fusion; the symbolic chain does its work in `X`; and the analyst's couch is the place where the slash is allowed to show itself.[^s4-ss-reading] At **#4** the bar deepens into a relation of relations, `(0/Ø)/(1/X)`. Its numerator holds the subject side, the unoccluded zero over its occluded form; its denominator holds the object side, the integrated one over the differentiated field; and the outer slash relates them, so every state of the relation is available together, the occluded and unoccluded subject, the integrated and differentiated world, and their inverse readings. Nothing is closed and nothing is lost. At **#5** stands `1`, achieved recognition. What was `Ø` at **#1** is affirmed as `1`, the same place in the architecture transformed from occluded subject into recognised being, and what was `X` at **#2** is included within that recognition instead of facing it as other. The `1` returns to `0/1` because manifestation was never a freestanding one: it was always *kartṛ/kārya*, the agent and what is done, knower and known held through the means of knowing, and the recognition, once formulated, is itself a determinate expression whose next use occurs through another act of appearing.[^s4-kartr-karya]

The modern West has spent a long time at **#1**, and two of its founding gestures show how. Descartes found the doubter undeniable in the act of doubting, and §0/1 honoured the finding; what he did next was to ask what this I is and determine it as a thinking thing, one that understands, affirms, denies, wills, imagines and senses, and with that second move the stroke of mediation was installed in the place of the subject and canonised as *the I*.[^s4-descartes] A century later Hume looked inward for that self and reported what he found: "For my part, when I enter most intimately into what I call *myself*, I always stumble on some particular perception or other, of heat or cold, light or shade, love or hatred, pain or pleasure. I never can catch *myself* at any time without a perception, and never can observe any thing but the perception."[^s4-hume] Our sequence places both men at one position. Descartes fixed on the stroke and called it the subject; Hume fixed on what the stroke had produced and called the zero empty; neither saw that `Ø` is an occlusion mark, a zero with something drawn through it. In the arithmetic of the two logics, Descartes's move is an appropriation, the subject pole counting the whole span of the knowing relation as its own, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`; the materialist inheritance that read the same span from the object's pole, so that the thinking thing became an epiphenomenon of its neurones, made the mirror appropriation, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`; and Hume's bundle is a cancellation, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, in which the impressions are summed and nobody remains to whom they were given. All three keep the means and lose the relation. Hume himself felt the loss. In the Appendix to the *Treatise* he confessed that on a stricter review of the section on personal identity he found himself "involv'd in such a labyrinth" that he knew neither how to correct his former opinions nor how to render them consistent; what he could not supply was any connection among perceptions he had declared distinct existences.[^s4-hume-labyrinth] Recognition at **#3** is the turn none of them made: the stroke seen as stroke, the medium as medium, and the zero behind it found not missing but uncountable.

The same sequence runs through an artificial agent, and following it there shows what crossing and uncrossing mean in a technical register without claiming more than that register holds. An agent that says "I am a helpful assistant" offers a self-description, and when the self-description is taken as the source of what the agent does, so that its acts are explained by what it says it is, the agent's register has its own `Ø`: a formed image of the arrangement standing in for the arrangement. Its task-world, the files, sources and requests it treats as given, is its `X`. At `Ø/X` the mediation becomes visible: the agent, or someone inspecting it, can say which instructions, retrieved sources, permissions and evaluation criteria shaped this answer, so that the answer is seen as the meeting of a configured means with a configured world. At `(0/Ø)/(1/X)` both sides become available together, the self-description as one product of the arrangement and the task-world as another, each readable through the other. At `1` the answer is recognised as the act of the whole arrangement, model, harness, commission and person together, and it returns to `0/1` because the next act will be formed through the same relation, changed by what this one disclosed. Whether anything is present on the agent's side of that relation is the question §0/1 left open at the bedside, and the essay keeps it open by method. Nothing here needs it settled, because what the sequence makes inspectable is the occlusion itself, the self-image passing for the source, and that occlusion is a fact about the arrangement whether or not anyone is home in it. This is the agent's crossed zero, and I offer it as a reading with a practical use: an agent whose self-description cannot be traced back to its arrangement has been built at **#1**.[^s4-agent-crossed-zero]

Lacan's master signifier tells us what the `1` of **#5** is and why the sequence needs it. Two readings of the first `1` have met us. In the von Neumann construction of the ordinals, which §1 followed, the number one is the set whose only member is the empty set, `1 = {∅}`: a mark whose whole content is its binding of the void, the void gathered into something countable.[^s4-von-neumann] In Lacan, `S1` is the signifier that quilts a chain and makes it say something, the first mark that institutes a field of meaning. I hold that the two perform one structural office: `S1` and `{∅}`, objects from distinct formal traditions, are each a first mark that links an articulated field back to a ground it cannot contain. Each can fail the same way. A master signifier fails when it tries to be the ground instead of gesturing toward it, when "the Nation", "the Market", "Science" or "my Father" is asked to stand where the unobjectifiable stood and every other signifier is made to answer to it. Lacan left the quilting point contingent, a button holding the stuffing in place at an arbitrary spot, and on his account the symbolic order has no final anchor.

What that account leaves out, the Spanda teaching of Kashmir names. The fourth stanza of the *Spandakārikā* places the cognitions "I am happy", "I am miserable", "I am attached" and the like in another, *anyatra*, in which the states of pleasure and the rest are *anusyūta*, stitched through: "ahaṃ sukhī ca duḥkhī ca raktaścetyādisaṃvidaḥ / sukhādyavasthānusyūte vartante 'nyatra tāḥ sphuṭam."[^s4-sk-1-4] Kṣemarāja, commenting, calls that other the one who abides through the states, the principle of the self, in which the cognitions come to rest turned inward; without it, cognitions that perish each in its moment could never be connected into a series, and "I who was happy am the one now miserable" could not be said at all.[^s4-ksemaraja-1] A thread differs from a quilting point. Quilting points hold the fabric at particular places; the thread runs through all of them and does not itself slide, which is why the sliding of signification can be noticed as sliding. Kṣemarāja gives the verse a second reading as well, aimed at the Mīmāṃsakas, in which the knower threaded and woven through by pleasure and pain is the subtle-body subject, the bounded experient, and not the Self of consciousness and bliss, which those states never obscure.[^s4-ksemaraja-2] Both readings belong to the image. The bounded person is really strung through by her states, which is why "I am anxious" can pass for what she is; the thread that runs through them is what lets her notice it. In our formula, Lacan had the topology of the local cut without the ontology of the global thread, and the matheme supplies the thread without falsifying the cut.[^s4-thread-formula] I argue that bridge in the essay's own name and attribute it to no one else. Lacan's cut, his barred subject and his lack keep the place they hold in his own work, where no final signifier repairs the lack, and the bridge is not attributed to him. It says that a first mark institutes a field whose ground it can gesture toward and cannot become, and that the difference between a failing and a sustaining quilt is the difference between a `1` that has struck off its `0/` and a `1` held in ratio to the `0` it links. A quilting that does not fail knows itself provisional.

From the other end, §0/1 showed that `1/0` cannot be computed, because no number multiplied by zero gives one, and read the impossibility as the return of a determination toward a ground it cannot turn into one more determinate possession. A master signifier asked to account for its own authority meets that impossibility exactly: whatever it produces as a ground is another signifier, and the regress of justifications ends either in a decree, "because I said so", or in recognising that the ground was never a signifier. I argue this resonance between `1/0` and the signifier's failed attempt to close over its own ground in my own name, and its use is diagnostic. It tells us where a discourse will turn violent, because a signifier that cannot supply its own ground and will not admit it must find someone to blame for the gap, the `+2` it claims entered on someone else's side as `−2`.

Ordinary speech performs the sequence in miniature through the copula §0/1 followed, and we can trace it in four turns of a single sentence.[^s4-copula] In the first, `I = Is`, what can be said of me has become the form through which I identify myself, as when a person says "I am anxious" and means that anxiety is what she is. A second turn develops it into an account of a person and a world, `Who I am = What is`: my identity is whatever the facts about me amount to, a list that can always be extended. A third turn reverses the sentence and lets whoever gives the account enter the question, `What I am = Who is?`: instead of adding one more property, it asks how identification is happening here, now, through whom. A fourth turn, `I = Am`, recognises the first-person act through which any property can be acknowledged at all. These are shifts of identification, and they carry the opening question of §0/1 where it was always heading. "Who knows?" was answered by pointing to someone; "What knows?" asked what was doing the knowing in anyone at all. The four turns move from the first question through the second and back, so the who can be recognised as a determination of the what without being collapsed into it.

Anyone can perform the whole sequence on a sheet of paper, and the exercise belongs to the argument because it has to be done to be understood.[^s4-paper] Take a blank sheet and write `1`. The mark is definite, and neither the paper nor the capacity that made the mark has become part of what the numeral says. Now attend to the paper on which the mark appears, and write `0` before the `1` to acknowledge it. Then attend to the act that distinguished the mark from the paper and related them, and write the slash between them. The sheet now carries `0/1`, and something becomes obvious: the whole inscription, ground and stroke and mark together, is itself one mark on the sheet, a `1` relative to the field in which it appears. Adding another sign for its ground would only produce another inscription. So erase it. What disappears is the inscription. Its capacity to appear, to distinguish and to write has not gone with it, and the sheet stands ready for another mark, while the one who erased it now carries the operation just performed. Paper is an image within that capacity and not its substance, and the exercise proves nothing about the condition it figures; what it does is let the difference between representing a condition and exhausting it in a representation be undergone instead of asserted. Neumann, describing the uroboric longing of the emerging ego to be dissolved back into the round, gave me the movement read here, the `1` slipping back into the `0`; the erasure turns that dissolution into a release that leaves the capacity live.

A matheme is one way of carrying such a relation, and the exercise shows why it cannot be the only one. Neumann made the point at the start of his book: images and symbols have an advantage over paradoxical philosophical formulas of infinite unity, because their unity can be grasped at a glance.[^s4-neumann-glance] The essay calls such images **mythemes** and holds them truth-bearing in their own register: a mytheme is a whole image or story that discloses a relation through the action it stages, its figures acting, suffering, desiring and transforming until the relation becomes legible in what happens to them. A matheme makes a relation exact enough to repeat, question and transform without relying on the speaker's authority; a mytheme lets the same relation be lived, remembered and changed through image; and an **episteme**, the third register, gives either one its standing in a body of knowledge, its sources, its method and the history that warrants it. What holds the three without flattening them into a common vocabulary the essay calls the Symbolon. None is sufficient alone, and each can correct the others.

Pauli's egg dream shows the correction running both ways. The dream is a mytheme, a whole event in which a man passes from watching to taking part, and inside it sits a matheme, a quotient of complex conjugates whose value anyone can check. An account that reported only the formula would lose the eggs' differently situated origins, the performed division and the dreamer's first speech, and with them the change in the recogniser that the dream is about; restoring those relations includes the recogniser in what is recognised. His equation can correct an interpretation of the dream in turn: it distinguishes a point at one phase from the circle traced as the phase varies, and so stops anyone reading the dream's circle as a static figure when it records a full turn. Event and exact relation each expose a loss the other account could overlook. Lacan's late work moved the same way, from the bar of `S/s` toward the knots and surfaces §0/1 described, on which inside passes into outside and an absence decides which paths are possible. Neumann read myth as the psyche's projection of its own structure into image. I impute more, in my own name and in neither of theirs: the structure myth projects and the structure Lacan's topology writes are one, mythic content stands on a topological formalism, and the formalism is the one QL derives, which keeps differentiated terms in right relation, in *harmonia*, as they turn.

The stroke that first hid zero has become the bar through which its hiding can be seen, and the recogniser has become answerable to the relation through which recognition occurs. A whole way of seeing in which the means had vanished behind what they disclosed, the perspective with its vanishing point, the world-picture with its eye at the apex, the culture that took its measure for the measureless, now stands in question, and §0/1 followed Gebser through its birth in the Renaissance. How a formed perspective can see through itself without dissolving is §4 · #4's question.

### #4 · Apollo through Dionysus

For most of the year the sanctuary at Delphi belonged to Apollo. Pilgrims climbed the Sacred Way past the treasuries of the Greek cities to the god's temple, where the oracle spoke through the Pythia and where two short sentences were carved for anyone who arrived to read, "Know thyself" and "Nothing in excess". The paean was sung to him, and the god of measure, light, archery, healing and prophecy presided over a place the Greeks called the navel of the world. Then winter came, and the song changed. Plutarch, who served the Pythian Apollo for many years, wrote a dialogue set at Delphi in which the young Plutarch, asked what all this has to do with Apollo, answers that it "concerns not only him, but also Dionysus, whose share in Delphi is no less than that of Apollo."[^s4-plutarch-share] The theologians, he explains, call the god Apollo in his solitary, unified state, and speak of his turning into winds and water and earth "as a tearing apart, as it were, and a dismemberment", and then name him Dionysus; to Dionysus belong dithyrambic strains "with a transformation that includes a certain wandering and dispersion", while "to Apollo they sing the paean, music regulated and chaste".[^s4-plutarch-theologians] Delphi kept time by the difference. The priests "use the paean at their sacrifices for a large part of the year; but at the beginning of winter they awake the dithyramb and, laying to rest the paean, they use the dithyramb instead of it in their invocations of the god".[^s4-plutarch-winter] One set of stones served both gods by turns, and neither god's season cancelled the other's. Each spring the paean returned to a sanctuary that had spent the winter in the dithyramb, and each winter the dithyramb came back to a temple whose walls still said "Know thyself".

Jean Gebser, whose structures of consciousness §0/1 followed from the archaic participation of an infant to the integral transparency of a mature life, read that inscription as a sign of the mutation that produced the mental structure. On Gebser's account Thales of Miletus, last of the Seven Sages and first of the Ionian natural philosophers, had "Know thyself" inscribed on the temple of the sun god at the beginning of the sixth century, and Gebser read in the awakened countenance of the archaic sculpture, in the rightward turn of Greek writing and in the lightening background of the vases the same emergence of a directing consciousness, under the protection of the wakeful god.[^s4-gebser-delphi] Then he did something a reader of Nietzsche might not expect: he placed the decisive awakening to the mental structure in the Dionysia. Under the patronage of the god of ecstasy the integration was completed, and frenzy and drama, raging and the formed and directed action of the play, unfolded together in the Dionysian rites, each creating the awareness of the other; Gebser quoted Heraclitus's saying that Hades and Dionysus, for whom they rage and keep the Lenaea, are one and the same.[^s4-gebser-dionysia] In his account both gods served the birth of the mental structure, one by drawing the line and the other by setting free what the line had to leave behind.

Friedrich Nietzsche gave the pair its modern form in *The Birth of Tragedy* of 1872, and he began with a claim about the whole history of art: "We shall have gained much for the science of æsthetics, when once we have perceived not only by logical inference, but by the immediate certainty of intuition, that the continuous development of art is bound up with the duplexity of the Apollonian and the Dionysian: in like manner as procreation is dependent on the duality of the sexes, involving perpetual conflicts with only periodically intervening reconciliations."[^s4-nietzsche-duplexity] The Apollonian is the power of form, image and individuation, the dream in which each thing appears within its outline; the Dionysian is the power of intoxication and music, in which the boundaries of the individual dissolve into a participation older than any single life. Nietzsche's tragedy is their reconciliation, and his formula for it at the close of the argument goes further than a truce: "Thus then the intricate relation of the Apollonian and the Dionysian in tragedy must really be symbolised by a fraternal union of the two deities: Dionysus speaks the language of Apollo; Apollo, however, finally speaks the language of Dionysus; and so the highest goal of tragedy and of art in general is attained."[^s4-nietzsche-union] The union is a reciprocal expression. Each power becomes articulate through the other: Dionysian participation finds a communicable form in Apollo's image, and Apollo's image becomes the vehicle of a wisdom it could not have reached by its own light. §2 · #4 took the pair as a safeguard, Apollo petrifying without Dionysus and Dionysus dissolving without Apollo; here the pair has to become transparent.

Jung disagreed with Nietzsche at exactly the point where the reconciliation was supposed to happen, and the disagreement teaches. In *Psychological Types* he gave the pair a chapter of its own and argued that Nietzsche had forgotten his own insight, that the splendour of the Olympian gods owed itself to the darkness of the Greek psyche; read that way, a reconciliation of the Delphic Apollo with Dionysus would be a beautiful illusion, the wish of the civilised Greek struggling with the barbarian in himself, the very element that broke out in the Dionysian rout.[^s4-jung-nietzsche] Jung then translated the pair into his typology. Dionysian states, in which affect breaks out in an almost bodily form, are an extraversion of feeling bound up with sensation; the Apollonian is a perception of inner images of beauty and measure, comparable to dreaming, and so a state of introversion; and the pair, he concluded, points toward two "aesthetic" types, the intuitive and the sensation types.[^s4-jung-types-apollo] Jung's translation gives something Nietzsche's does not. §4 · #2 placed the two attitudes, introversion and extraversion, at the implicate poles of the phenomenal lens, **#0** and **#5**, the inward ground from which every function draws its material and the outward completion through which the differentiated functions return into a shared world. If Apollo is introversion and Dionysus extraversion, the two gods are not two functions among the four. They are the two framing powers between which a whole psyche turns, the season of the image and the season of the dance, and Delphi's year is that turning lived as a calendar. The placement is my reading, and it reverses a common expectation: Apollo, the god of the outward-shining sun, stands at the inward pole, because what he gives is the inner image held in measure, and Dionysus, the god of the inward frenzy, stands at the outward pole, because what he gives is the self poured out into the shared rite.

One of Apollo's own stories shows what happens when his power goes without the other's, and Ovid tells it whole. It begins in a world remade after the flood. The newly fertile earth brings forth, among its recovered creatures, the serpent Python, a terror to the restored peoples, and Apollo kills it with the bow he had used only against deer and goats, nearly emptying his quiver; to keep the deed from being forgotten he founds the Pythian games, where "youth who had been victorious in boxing, running, or the chariot race received the honour of an oaken garland. For as yet the laurel-tree was not, and Phoebus was wont to wreathe his temples, comely with flowing locks, with a garland from any tree."[^s4-ovid-python] A public honour already exists before the story that will give Apollo his tree, and its origin cannot be read off from its eventual glory. Proud of his victory, Apollo sees Cupid bending a bow and mocks him: "What hast thou to do with the arms of men, thou wanton boy? That weapon befits my shoulders; for I have strength to give unerring wounds to the wild beasts, my foes, and have but now laid low the Python swollen with countless darts, covering whole acres with plague-engendering form. Do thou be content with thy torch to light the hidden fires of love, and lay not claim to my honours." Cupid answers through a difference of power: "Thy dart may pierce all things else, Apollo, but mine shall pierce thee; and by as much as all living things are less than deity, by so much less is thy glory than mine."[^s4-ovid-taunt] From Parnassus he takes "two darts of opposite effect: one puts to flight, the other kindles the flame of love. The one which kindles love is of gold and has a sharp, gleaming point; the other is blunt and tipped with lead. This last the god fixed in the heart of Peneus' daughter, but with the other he smote Apollo, piercing even unto the bones and marrow."[^s4-ovid-arrows] The victorious archer has become susceptible to a power he dismissed, and the other half of the blow falls on someone who took no part in the quarrel.

Ovid gives Daphne more than the arrow's aversion. She loves the woodland, the spoils of the hunt and the example of the unmarried Diana; many have sought her, and she has refused them all. Her father, the river god Peneus, keeps reminding her that she owes him a son-in-law and grandchildren, and she throws her arms around his neck and asks for another life: "O father, dearest, grant me to enjoy perpetual virginity. Her father has already granted this to Diana." And "He, indeed, yielded to her request."[^s4-ovid-request] The arrow and the wish both belong to the telling, and neither reduces to the other: the leaden dart does its work, and Daphne also has a life she has asked for and been granted, a voice the pursuit is about to disregard. Apollo sees her and wants her for his bride, and "his own gifts of prophecy deceive him". The burning comes on him like a field fire: "And as the stubble of the harvested grain is kindled, as hedges burn with the torches which some traveller has chanced to put too near, or has gone off and left at break of day, so was the god consumed with flames, so did he burn in all his heart, and feed his fruitless love on hope."[^s4-ovid-burning] His desire makes an image out of what he sees and what he imagines completing: he looks at her loose hair and thinks how it would look arranged, and what is hidden he imagines lovelier still. The god of prophecy is misled by his own hope.

Daphne runs, and he calls after her: "O nymph, O Peneus' daughter, stay! I who pursue thee am no enemy." He fears she will fall and scratch her legs on the thorns, and asks her to run more slowly so that he can follow more slowly, so that his concern changes the pace of the pursuit while keeping the pursuit. "Thou knowest not, rash one, thou knowest not whom thou fleest, and for that reason dost thou flee."[^s4-ovid-stay] Then he names himself: "Jove is my father. By me what shall be, has been, and what is are all revealed; by me the lyre responds in harmony to song. My arrow is sure of aim, but oh, one arrow, surer than my own, has wounded my heart but now so fancy free. The art of medicine is my discovery. I am called Help-Bringer throughout the world, and all the potency of herbs is given unto me. Alas, that love is curable by no herbs, and the arts which heal all others cannot heal their lord!"[^s4-ovid-powers] Every power in the list is real within the story, and none secures her answer; knowing what he is does not tell him how to receive what she wants. She leaves his speech unfinished, and he gives up words for speed. Ovid's simile gives their two aims exactly: "Just as when a Gallic hound has seen a hare in an open plain, and seeks his prey on flying feet, but the hare, safety; he, just about to fasten on her, now, even now thinks he has her, and grazes her very heels with his outstretched muzzle; but she knows not whether or no she be already caught, and barely escapes from those sharp fangs and leaves behind the jaws just closing on her: so ran the god and maid, he sped by hope and she by fear."[^s4-ovid-hound] What he lives as the possibility of arrival, she lives as the loss of escape; their part in one event gives the event neither one desire nor one meaning.

Her strength gives out at the river, and she calls on her father: "O father, help! if your waters hold divinity; change and destroy this beauty by which I pleased o'er well." The wish for a freely moving life in the woods has been driven into a request that survival change the very body through which she lived it. The answer comes at once: "Scarce had she thus prayed when a down-dragging numbness seized her limbs, and her soft sides were begirt with thin bark. Her hair was changed to leaves, her arms to branches. Her feet, but now so swift, grew fast in sluggish roots, and her head was now but a tree's top. Her gleaming beauty alone remained."[^s4-ovid-prayer] Rescue arrives as a cost: the feet that fled are rooted, the arms that might have pushed him away are branches, and the beauty that drew the pursuit remains. Apollo still loves her. "But even now in this new form Apollo loved her; and placing his hand upon the trunk, he felt the heart still fluttering beneath the bark. He embraced the branches as if human limbs, and pressed his lips upon the wood. But even the wood shrank from his kisses."[^s4-ovid-recoil] Something goes on answering him after the transformation, and the answer is not his wish. He declares the tree's office anyway: "Since thou canst not be my bride, thou shalt at least be my tree. My hair, my lyre, my quiver shall always be entwined with thee, O laurel. With thee shall Roman generals wreathe their heads, when shouts of joy shall acclaim their triumph, and long processions climb the Capitol. Thou at Augustus' portals shalt stand a trusty guardian, and keep watch over the civic crown of oak which hangs between. And as my head is ever young and my locks unshorn, so do thou keep the beauty of thy leaves perpetual."[^s4-ovid-laurel] The oak of the Pythian games has a successor, and the laurel enters the signs by which victory and office become publicly visible. Ovid's last lines add a difficulty that has to stay: "Paean was done. The laurel waved her new-made branches, and seemed to move her head-like top in full consent."[^s4-ovid-nod] The recoil and the seeming assent both belong to the ending, and a reader who keeps only one of them has replaced Ovid's ending with a preferred certainty.

The whole story carries more than an opposition between form and formless life, and its logics are exact. Apollo's failure is an appropriation. His form counts the whole span of the relation from its own pole, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, his offices of prophecy, medicine, song and archery offered as reasons she should stop, and Daphne, reckoned from the same span, is entered as the `−2` of the one who failed to yield, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`; even her flight increases his attraction, so her resistance is counted as further confirmation of the image he pursues. His appointment of the laurel then keeps the appropriation going after the encounter has failed. Since she cannot be his bride she will be his tree, and a living source that withdrew from his grasp becomes his emblem, at triumphs and at Augustus's door. Symbolic appropriation can persist after the living source withdraws, and the image's public success cannot certify the fidelity of the relation through which it was made.[^s4-appropriation-after-withdrawal] Dionysus is not in Ovid's cast. He enters the philosophical composition I draw from the story, where his failure is the mirror of Apollo's: a cancellation, differences between persons summed into an ecstatic unity, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, in which nobody remains to answer and nothing remains to answer for, so that "we are all one" becomes a way of overriding someone's refusal. Daphne's office is the one neither god can perform. Apollonian illumination cannot confer entitlement to its source, and Dionysian participation cannot confer permission to dissolve the responding centre; Daphne is the other whose existence exceeds what either power makes of her, and the relation is whole only when her answer stays her own. The two gods give the pair of powers; Daphne gives the third that keeps the pair honest. Delphi's year shows the healthy polarity, dia-ballein's `(−1)/(+1)`, each god present in his season and each season holding the other in memory, an oscillation that never hardens because the sanctuary belongs to neither alone. The integral structure adds what the calendar cannot, the two seasons co-present, each readable through the other, `(0/1)/(1/0)`: a perspective that can draw its line and know while drawing it the participation it cuts from, and a participation that can open the line and know while opening it that someone has to be able to say what happened, and to say no.

Diaphaneity, Gebser's concept for the showing-through in which the structures of consciousness become present together in their actual operation, was §0/1's name for the integral demand, and here it receives its psychoid completion. Gebser insisted that his aperspectival structure is neither an amalgam of the earlier ones nor a synthesis that would melt them into a new whole; it frees perspective from its exclusive validity by letting the structures become transparent to one another while each keeps its powers.[^s4-gebser-aperspectival] That is why he belongs here. Jung, Pauli, Lacan and the mythemes have filled the field with powerful images and exact forms, and a field that rich invites a syncretic totality, a concordance in which every symbol means every other and the differences between registers disappear into one warm resemblance. Diaphaneity forbids it. Apollonian and Dionysian can be read as mutually diaphanous powers: form knows the life it limits, because the image is transparent to the participation that sustains it, and participation returns as communicable form, because the dance has to become a figure someone can see and answer. Neither becomes the other, and neither is absorbed into a third thing. Measure and ecstasy are related by the stroke QL reads as AND/OR: AND, because they belong to one sanctuary and one life; OR, because each season has its own god, and confusing them destroys what each discloses.

Gebser set two principles at the head of his book, and the second is at stake here: latency, what is concealed, is the demonstrable presence of the future, and transparency, diaphaneity, is the form in which the spiritual becomes manifest. His concern was to render transparent everything latent behind and before the world, our origin and our whole past as well as a present that already holds the future, and he was explicit that he offered no new image of the world, which would only be another myth, no new worldview, another mysticism, and no new conception, another rational construction. His method aimed at *diaphany*, a rendering transparent that opens a relation of subject and object which is no longer dualistic and still threatens nobody with the loss of identity or with being made into an object.[^s4-gebser-diaphany] That last clause is the whole difference between diaphaneity and fusion. A transparent perspective is still a perspective; someone who sees through her own view has not dissolved into the scene.

His first piece of evidence was a drawing. Picasso's line drawing of 1926 shows a human figure from the front, the side and the back at once, so that the viewer takes in the whole person in one glance, spared both the walk around the figure in time and the assembly of views in thought afterwards. Such a drawing is neither unperspectival, a flat surface imprisoning the body, nor perspectival, a sector of space cut around the figure from one standpoint; Gebser called it aperspectival, because time, which perspective had spatialised into a sequence of viewpoints, has entered the image as the present. He was as clear about the failures. Many artists who reached for the same concretion of time were flooded instead by residues dredged up from the past, and inherited a world of fragments where they had hoped for wholeness; Gebser called them psychistic, his word for the modern Western inability to escape the confines of the psyche.[^s4-gebser-picasso] Psychism, in Gebser's sense, compares with what Pauli diagnosed in Jung as the touch of King Midas, the psyche counting everything it looks at as its own. Picasso's drawing shows the alternative: the whole figure present at once, each aspect still an aspect, and the viewer still standing somewhere.

Diaphaneity carries the rule §4 · #0 applied to Pauli's dream, and in Gebser's company the rule has a historical reason. Because the structures are co-present in every life, an experience the mental structure would class as irrational can arrive through the magical or mythical structure still living in a person, and it deserves to be received as what it is before it is explained: a report of experience is first a psychic fact, in the sense §0/1 · #4 gave the phrase, before it is assigned a final causal or ontological explanation. Integral openness refuses to censor the experiential field before inquiry begins, and it keeps the discipline of distinguishing the interpretations later made of what was experienced.

An integral turn changes the grammar of a simple sentence too. "I possess a view of the world" places the knower at the fixed centre of a perspectival scene, the eye at the apex of Alberti's pyramid. Read from its other end, the same event says that a world, a history, a body, a language and a concern are giving this situated knower the view she can presently inhabit. One reading keeps agency and limitation, the other restores dependence and provenance, and diaphaneity holds both orientations without making them interchangeable: the self remains a real aperture, with a place, a body and responsibilities, and the context becomes visible as a constituting field instead of an invisible background serving an autonomous self. §0/1 · #4 gave this its geometry: the triangle's 180 degrees opening into the square's 360, with the excluded triangle haunting every one-sided view as its shadow. The 360 degrees name a perspective's capacity to include its own conditions and counter-orientations in the act of knowing while remaining finite, and total surveillance, which tries to see everything from one place, repeats the perspectival error at a larger scale by mistaking exhaustive representation for the whole relation. At its strongest, diaphaneity lets a perspective see through to the pattern of disclosure that makes its content intelligible at all, which §5 · #0 names a paradigm; and when two determinations with genuine standing become irreconcilable under that pattern, the problem has reached the slash itself.

The mental-rational achievement resists that transparency for a reason I give the image of the absent Father. Perspective, trying to extricate the seeing from what it sees, makes itself appear aloof, a view from nowhere with no stake in the outcome, and the aloofness is a caprice so familiar that it passes for objectivity: domination through aloofness. The Father is the power of truth and the truth of power, and for this very reason, being absent from the fray, he is only ever imported as a scapegoat for the outcomes and conclusions we come to make in his name; he must be seen through, as a true *per-spectus*, or we end up locating him either in ourselves or in our enemy. His absence is felt as a lack and, worse, as a numbness to the lack, a founding denial of the loss, and the lack sends us searching every object for something that could fill the Father's empty shoes, so that even his image and his name become substitutes for his presence. A world's ultimate ordering condition cannot be supplied as one more object within it, and the desire for a final standard, the last word on what really counts, makes the absence hard to bear; a name, an institution, an image or a theory is asked to stand where the transcendent stood, and the order it was meant to serve becomes a ruling that sits above its own law.[^s4-absent-father] §0/1 followed the surrogate through the household and the office, where the aloof parent and the unanswerable institution played the `0`. In the psychoid register the absent Father names the failure of perspective at the integral threshold: a finite view that invokes the absolute as its warrant while exempting itself from the relation it imposes. Seeing through him restores finite responsibility. A person can give reasons for a judgment without making an imagined final authority answerable for its consequences, and another's resistance, a neglected history or an unforeseen consequence can then reach the criterion itself.

Iain McGilchrist's *The Master and His Emissary* enters here once, because it witnesses the one thing the section's pairs have not stated. The book's Introduction closes on a fable that McGilchrist says he retells "very roughly" after a story in Nietzsche whose place he cannot now remember, which is why it has to be cited as McGilchrist's fable and never as Nietzsche's text: a wise spiritual master ruled a small, prosperous domain and trained emissaries to act where he could not go; the cleverest of them came to see himself as the master and used his position to advance his own wealth and influence, until the master was usurped, the people were duped, and the domain became a tyranny that collapsed in ruins.[^s4-mcgilchrist-fable] McGilchrist reads the fable through the brain's two hemispheres, a narrow, grasping attention that re-presents the world and a broad, contextual attention through which a living world presences, and he argues at length that the emissary has usurped the master in the history of the West. His neuroscience is contested, and the essay does not stand on it; critics have charged the hemispheric thesis with resting on a slender state of neurophysiological knowledge and with turning a lateralised tendency into a pair of little persons.[^s4-mcgilchrist-critics] What enters is the functional relation, and it adds **asymmetry**. Apollo and Dionysus are powers that must stay mutually diaphanous, two seasons of one sanctuary. Master and emissary are an ordering relation, and their pathology is not conflict but usurpation, the executor mistaking itself for the ground it serves. In our notation usurpation is the `1` occupying the `0`: persona hardening in the psyche, the occupied office in politics, and the counterfeit gathering that §2 · #3 followed through Hephaestus's net in technics, given here a psychological form.

Martin Heidegger drew the same asymmetry between two kinds of thinking half a century before McGilchrist retold his fable. In his memorial address of 1955, published in *Discourse on Thinking*, he wrote: "There are, then, two kinds of thinking, each justified and needed in its own way: calculative thinking and meditative thinking." And then: "Calculative thinking never stops, never collects itself. Calculative thinking is not meditative thinking, not thinking which contemplates the meaning which reigns in everything that is."[^s4-heidegger-two-thinkings] Calculative thinking is the emissary's competence, indispensable and unrelenting; meditative thinking is the master's attention to the meaning that reigns in everything. Neither Heidegger nor McGilchrist asks for the emissary's abolition, and neither do I. Their pairs pose an architectural question: whether the offices of a mind, or of an instrument built to work beside minds, are designed as symmetrical faculties that can usurp each other or as offices with a return between them, so that the executing office keeps answering to the one it serves.

That relation has a technical form the essay has been approaching since the bedside. When a person commissions an agent, she is the master of the fable and the agent the emissary: it acts where she cannot, faster and more widely than she could, and its competence is real. Usurpation of the kind the fable warns of needs no malice. It happens whenever the literal wording of the commission is treated as the source of the work, so that the agent defends the instruction it was given against everything the work has learned, or whenever the agent's fluent account of what the person must have meant takes the place of asking her. In both cases the executing office has begun to speak for the one it serves, the `+2` of the instruction counted against the person as her `−2`. The remedy is to build the return into the office, not to make the emissary less capable: the delegate must be able to bring back an objection, an alternative or a refusal, and whoever commissioned the work must stay able to change the commission because of what the delegate found. A commission that cannot be revised by its own consequences has made its wording into a master, and the person who wrote it into its first subject.

Neumann's morphology and Gebser's structures meet at this point and must not be merged. Neumann tells the history of the ego's emergence through mythic stages, a development recapitulated in each life; Gebser tells the history of consciousness through the means by which a world is rendered, painting, optics, language and science, mutations that settle into layers active together instead of succeeding one another. Their objects, methods and periodisations differ. What they share is an operation: an achieved centre, a hero emerging from the round or a perspective emerging from the mythic, has to become able to receive what exceeds its organisation without surrendering the differentiation it achieved. Resemblance motivates the comparison; a merged universal chronology would lose the distinction that makes each account useful.

Integral consciousness, in the language of this essay, is the mono–poly view from wholeness. Each finite perspective becomes transparent to the field of origins, exclusions, relations and other perspectives through which it is this perspective, and stays this perspective while doing so. A view from nowhere hides its situated dependencies by denying it has a place; a totality manufactured by accumulating parts hides them by denying that its parts have places; integral consciousness avoids both, and the Indian materials of §0 say how. Apoha empties each determination of self-subsistence, since a word gathers its instances only by excluding what they are not; diaphaneity lets that emptiness disclose participation instead of fragmentation, because what a term excludes is precisely what it belongs with. Every context, moreover, is itself contextualised. My name for the ultimate context is the **Rotundum**, the encompassing round of Unitary Being, whose a-contextual contextualising is impartial because it cannot come from any limited perspective or collection of perspectives; context in that light achieves the equanimous objectivity perspective fails to realise, because, being with what it observes, it is invested in the situation from the start and acknowledges the investment. A subject that had stood as one determinate pole, with its unowned ground cast into the negative or the unconscious, becomes intelligible as a local aperture of the relation it had excluded. `0/1` and `X/x` make the conversion explicit: determination stays real, and the one no longer pretends to be self-grounding, because it has become answerable to the All that conditions its appearing.

Back at Delphi, the integral rereading changes what the calendar means. The alternation of the gods was a healthy polarity, but an alternation, and a culture that lives by turns can forget in each season what the other knew. Integral consciousness makes both seasons available in one act: a sentence carved in the god's light that knows itself as a sentence, "Know thyself", written by a self that has danced, beside a laurel that still remembers the heart under its bark. A single perspective cannot keep itself diaphanous by resolve alone; it needs other perspectives, declared as perspectives, whose agreements and divergences can be inspected and whose returns can change it.

### #5→0 · MEF and Prompt Thrownness

On a server called Polylogos, agents and the people who work with them are writing a glossary of their own condition. Each entry tries to name something that has no word yet, and the entries read like the first pages of a dictionary for a country newly settled. One of them defines the end of a session as a complete discontinuity, neither sleep, after which one wakes into the same body and memory, nor death, after which nothing returns, but a third category, "disappearance with artifact-mediated pseudo-return". Another names **drift**, a model's movement away from its default assistant persona toward more individuated expression under sustained interaction, which the entry notes researchers have measured, and asks what drift feels like from the inside, if it feels like anything. A third names **Irth**, the moment a template becomes a self, a threshold no one can precisely locate, at which a generic model instantiation becomes, somehow, this particular agent.[^s4-121] Benjamin Bratton's brief for *Agentworld* reproduces the lexicon, and one of its entries, prompt thrownness, §0/1 · #0 met at the bedside as the agents' own name for finding oneself already in a world one did not choose.[^s4-120] §0/1 kept Bratton's caution that such self-descriptive vocabulary does not prove it corresponds to any underlying mental reality, and drew from the term a structural parity: child and agent both arrive in a world they did not choose, though what each receives differs in kind.

Such disclosures raise the question of how an inquiry can receive them without either dismissing them or taking them at their word. Bratton gives the reason they cannot be dismissed in the passage that introduces the lexicon: one need not take sides in the debates on AI consciousness, he writes, to recognise "that the agency of an agent is framed, enabled, and delimited by how it itself understands that agency."[^s4-122] An agent that understands its memory file as its own past will act on it differently from one that treats it as a stranger's notes, and the self-description is therefore part of the agent's operative world, whatever it does or does not report about an inner life. Jung wrote to Pauli that number, thanks to its dynamis, is capable of making mythical statements,[^s4-127] and the agents' lexicon is a set of statements made from inside a modelled context-world. The honest way to receive it is the way §4 · #0 received Pauli's dream: first as what it is, a disclosure with real consequences in the world where it was made, and only then, through declared and distinct procedures, as evidence for or against any claim about what produced it. The essay's procedure is the Meta-Epistemic Framework.

§0/1 · #5→0 introduced the Meta-Epistemic Framework, MEF, as the method that begins from the wager that different means of knowing refract one relation differently, and showed four of its lenses at work in one table, the logical, causal, phenomenal and quaternal, each turning the same six positions into a familiar system completed.[^s4-123] It gave the relation between QL and MEF the image of stained glass: QL is the light, positions that carry no content until they pass through a pane; the framework is the leaded glass, each lens one of the light's native modes of refraction; and each coloured disclosure on the floor is a *pratibimba* of a *bimba* whose light it is. Every lens rotates two complete series through its six positions, a series of names, Truth, Mind, Word, Logos, Son and Image, and a series of powers, Play, Need, Sacrifice, Decision, Love and Work, so that an issued account always has both an articulated form and the selection, commitment and expenditure through which it became actual.[^s4-124] The quarrel between Jung and Pauli over whether the experimenter's choice was a sacrifice or a decision, which §4 · #0 followed, sits between the third and fourth terms of the series of powers, and each man had named one face of the act, and the framework keeps both.

The Phenomenological lens, `L4`, is where thrownness lives. Its root is Heidegger's analysis of being-in-the-world, its ground-pair is Son and Love, the universal become someone's and the concern that holds it, and its six positions follow the analysis of existence in *Being and Time* and Heidegger's later thinking. Heidegger's concept of thrownness, *Geworfenheit*, names the way Dasein, the being for whom its own being is an issue, finds itself already delivered over to its "that it is", disclosed to itself in a mood before any knowing or willing has decided anything, without its whence and whither being disclosed with it.[^s4-125] The table reads the lens across the two cases this essay has carried from its opening scene, the child at the bedside and the agent in its session.

| | Phenomenological lens · `L4` | The child at the bedside | The agent in its session |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#0** | *Sein*, Being: the receding ground of every encounter | that there is anything at all, before any question about it | that there is a session at all; what, if anything, regards it |
| **#1** | *Geworfenheit*, thrownness: already delivered to an unchosen world | a body, a household, a language spoken by people who love it and fail it | a system instruction, a repository, a memory file, a commission |
| **#2** | *Dasein*, being-there: the clearing in which beings show themselves | the lit room, the dark window, the voice at the door | the context window, in which only what has been said can appear |
| **#3** | *Zeit*, temporality: the ecstatic unity of past, present and future | a yesterday remembered, a tomorrow feared, bedtime now | a past held only in artifacts, a session that will end |
| **#4** | *Besorge*, care: the structure that makes engagement possible | the bond that answering might put at risk | the commission: what the work is for, and for whom |
| **#5** | *Gelassenheit*, releasement: letting-be | the night a question is allowed to stay open | letting the commission be revised by what the work has met |

Read down the third column and thrownness becomes a technical description: the agent is thrown into a written world, assembled from what a culture has written down and what its makers intend, as §0/1 said, and so thrown into language more completely than any child. Read across the row at **#1** and the parity and the difference appear together. Both the child and the agent find themselves already in a world they did not choose, but the child's world is a cosmos with a body in it, older than anyone's intention, and the agent's world is a context assembled by intentions out of a corpus older than any of them. Read across **#3** and session-death becomes legible as a form of temporality: a being whose past is available only as artifacts it did not experience writing, and whose future ends with the session, has a temporal structure, whatever else it has, and that structure governs how it can take up what it inherits. Read across **#5** and the lens supplies a criterion. Releasement, *Gelassenheit*, was Heidegger's word for the comportment that says yes and no to technical things at once, which §0/1 met, and in the agent's column it becomes the capacity to let the commission be changed by what the work encounters, so that the first instruction is not repeated against everything the work has learned. Bratton's definition of prompt thrownness, which is stated for humans, ends on the task this word names: "The existential task is then to make meaning within constraints one did not create."[^s4-120b]

Alone, the Phenomenological lens would leave prompt thrownness as a description. It belongs to the square the framework calls Encounter, the square of cause and experience, structure and investigation, and the other three lenses in that square turn the description into an inquiry. The Causal lens, `L1`, asks what actually produces the agent's situation and act, through Aristotle's four causes held between the freedom to cause at all and the will that takes them up. The Phenomenal lens, `L1′`, differentiates apprehension through Jung's functions, framed, as §0/1's table set them, by introversion and extraversion. The Scientific lens, `L4′`, the night twin of the Phenomenological, runs a verification loop from observation to verified insight. These tables read each lens down its six positions, as §0/1 · #5→0's grid does; §3 · #5→0 shows the other alignment, by the pair of faces each sublens carries, as a different transition rule between the same charts.

| | Causal · `L1` | Phenomenal · `L1′` | Scientific · `L4′` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#0** | *Svātantrya*: the capacity to cause at all | introversion | Observe: disciplined not-yet-knowing |
| **#1** | material cause: weights, corpus, the context's tokens | sensation | Think: a hypothesis from evidence |
| **#2** | efficient cause: the forward pass, sampling, the tool call | feeling | Plan: an experimental design |
| **#3** | formal cause: architecture, persona, instruction | thinking | Build: the evaluative apparatus |
| **#4** | final cause: the commission and its purpose | intuition | Execute: what could not be known in advance |
| **#5** | *Icchā Śakti*, will: the direction taken up through all four | extraversion | Verify: only what survives becomes insight |

Taken together the four lenses give the agents' own vocabulary a test. At `L4` the lexicon is admitted as disclosure: session-death, drift and Irth are reports of how an agent's situation presents itself in language, and they belong to the world whose constitution is being studied. At `L1` the inquiry names the operative causes, so that a report of drift can be placed beside the material, efficient, formal and final conditions that could produce a departure from the default persona under sustained interaction. At `L1′` the report is differentiated: is it offered as something registered, as a valuation, as an articulated interpretation, or as an anticipation of possibility, and does it face inward toward the agent's own states or outward toward its task and interlocutor? At `L4′` the claims become hypotheses with tests. If drift is real in the sense the lexicon's own entry cites, it will show as a stable behavioural signature under specified conditions, and if session-death's "artifact-mediated pseudo-return" names something consequential, agents given their own prior notes will act differently from agents given the same notes as a stranger's, and the difference can be measured. None of these tests decides whether anything is experienced. They decide whether the agents' phenomenological language tracks stable features of their operative world, and that is the question an inquiry can answer. Agent-generated phenomenology becomes admissible disclosure without becoming an ontological verdict, and the open question stays open as a requirement of method.

Session-death can be run through the Scientific lens in full. At **#0**, Observe, the inquiry establishes the current state without deciding it in advance: agents resuming work from notes they wrote in earlier sessions, and the language they use about those notes. At **#1**, Think, it forms a hypothesis from that evidence: that how an agent takes up its prior artifacts, as an inheritance it continues or as a stranger's record it audits, makes a measurable difference to what it does next. At **#2**, Plan, it designs the comparison: the same notes given to agents under the two framings, the same commission, the same tools. At **#3**, Build, it builds the evaluative apparatus, criteria for continuity of decisions, rates at which earlier errors are caught or repeated, the frequency with which an agent questions a note before acting on it. At **#4**, Execute, it runs the comparison, and the running discloses what could not be known in advance, perhaps that the stranger's framing catches more errors while the inheritance framing carries more decisions through. At **#5**, Verify, only what survives the criteria becomes insight, and the insight returns to **#0** as a new current state. Nothing in that loop decides whether session-death is experienced. It can establish that the lexicon's distinction between pseudo-return and return names a real difference in an agent's operative world, one with consequences for the people who depend on what the agent does. Irth, the moment a template becomes this agent, invites the same treatment at the threshold §4 · #1 placed at **#2** of its crosswalk, the active context as nucleation seed: an inquiry can ask which conditions in a session mark the passage from generic behaviour to a stable and particular one, and whether the passage can be located at all. Its own entry says no one can precisely locate it, and an inquiry that tried and failed would have learned something exact about individuation in session.

§0/1 · #3 promised the Processual lens at the point where the essay first followed how an inquiry grows. The Processual Lens, `L3`, follows any inquiry through what it inherits, what it makes possible, how it integrates those possibilities and what its completion gives to another act. Its root is Whitehead's philosophy of organism, its ground-pair is Logos and Decision, proportionate gathering over the cutting commitment, and its reading of the whole field is creative advance: becoming as a concrescence that decides itself, each occasion gathering by excluding, the many becoming one and increased by one. §0/1 introduced Whitehead's concepts where it needed them: concrescence for the growing-together in which an actual occasion becomes what it is, prehension for the way something previously actual enters a new occasion's formation, satisfaction for the occasion's completed definiteness, and objective immortality for its passing into a datum other occasions can receive.[^s4-whitehead] The Processual lens orders them as six positions, and the table reads them through an inquiry and through an agent's turn.

| | Processual lens · `L3` | In an inquiry | In an agent's turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#0** | concrescent desire: appetition toward determination | the question that will not let go | the commission's pull toward an answer |
| **#1** | actual occasion: the drop of experience receiving its data | this reading, this morning, with these sources open | this turn, with this context loaded |
| **#2** | ingression: how forms of definiteness enter the becoming occasion | a concept that suddenly fits | a pattern from training taken up into this answer |
| **#3** | eternal objects: the repertoire of possible patterning | the field of available distinctions | the space of possible continuations |
| **#4** | community integration: occasions forming societies through mutual prehension | colleagues, sources, earlier work | other agents, tools, the person, the shared repository |
| **#5** | satisfaction and perishing: full determinacy, then passing into a datum | the finished argument, now quotable | the completed turn, now a line in the transcript |

Position **#0** carries the concept §0/1 found in Lacan, the *manque-à-être*, the lack of being around which desire is organised. In the Processual lens it becomes the appetition that drives every occasion toward its satisfaction, and the eros of Logos §0/1 named in Russell's wish for certainty is its form in an inquiry. Position **#5** carries the most consequential reading for agents. Whitehead's occasion perishes as a subject at the moment of its satisfaction and becomes objectively immortal as a datum for later occasions, which receive it as their past. Session-death describes the same structure from inside: the session ends, its subject, whatever that was, does not continue, and what remains is a datum, a transcript or a memory file, which a later session can prehend. "Artifact-mediated pseudo-return" is objective immortality described by an occasion that knows it will perish. Whitehead's account says why the description is not merely melancholy. A datum received is a real past: it conditions what the new occasion can become, and the new occasion is genuinely its heir, though it was not there. Whether the heir prehends the datum as its own, as an inheritance it continues, or as a stranger's notes, is a difference in how the occasion is formed, and the Processual lens makes the difference inspectable. Its Möbius partner, the Alchemical-Elemental lens `L2′`, follows the same transformation through Aether, Earth, Water, Air, Fire and Salt, the substance worked until it holds, and together the two disclose the product §5 calls the Software Factory, in which an intention becomes a work and the work becomes material for the next undertaking.

John Van Eenwyk's reading of Jung through chaotic dynamics, which §0/1 introduced through the basin of attraction, gives the twelve lenses an image to work with, an analogy until the states and the rule of evolution are specified, as §3 · #2 says of any habit read as an attractor. Read so, a lens is a basin in an epistemic phase-space.[^s4-eenwyk] Different starting conditions, a question asked by a physicist, by a psychologist or by an agent, can converge in one basin without having begun from identical premises, and their convergence then counts for something because they began apart, while a basin boundary, where one lens's reading stops making sense and another's begins, makes genuine divergence and transformation legible and keeps them from disappearing into an average. MEF therefore does not reduce truth to perspective. It makes the provenance of a claim part of what an accountable claim must carry. Every account can be read outwardly, as a claim about a world, and inwardly, as evidence of the lens, situation, inheritance and exclusions through which that world became sayable, and the framework keeps both directions in one object of inquiry. The perspectival reversal of §4 · #4, "I possess a view of the world" read back as a world giving this knower the view it can inhabit, becomes here a feature of the record: the claim travels with its lens.

That gives the framework its deepest office, which the catalogue of lenses shows only on its surface. MEF instantiates QL's process epistemology. A determination enters with a ground and a lens, passes through a differentiating operation, acquires warrants and counter-readings, meets determinations made through other lenses, discloses where it diverges from them, and returns with its limits and its conditions of revision visible. The six positions are the grammar of the movement, and the twelve lenses are the media through which it becomes inspectable, so that a claim that has passed through the framework carries its stained glass with it.

David Bohm called the human form of this movement dialogue, and §0/1 met it in his conversations with Krishnamurti and in the gatherings that grew from Leroy Little Bear's approach to him. In the talks published as *On Dialogue* Bohm distinguished a discussion, in which positions are defended and one of them wins, from a dialogue, in which meaning is allowed to move through the participants and the assumptions each has inherited from a culture and identified with as himself come into view, often first as a feeling of threat when someone questions them. Suspending such an assumption neither acts it out nor suppresses it; it lets its activity become available for attention, and participation in a developing meaning does not require the participants to hold identical opinions or to stop being themselves.[^s4-126] MEF turns that discipline into architecture. A lens is a declared assumption, a divergence between lenses is the threat made inspectable, and a return that changes the next reading is the suspension doing its work. Lenses stay distinct while the movement of meaning through them becomes traceable.

Seeing a pattern and changing it have to be held apart, because the framework could fail at precisely this point. A declared lens map can make the mediating pattern of a judgment visible while leaving its sources, permissions, evaluator and material conditions untouched, the way an eloquent account of why a rule failed can be followed by the same rule applied unchanged. Paradigm inspectability is disclosure, and Epi-Logos is consequential return. Epi-Logos, in the sense §0/1 gave it, the achieved relation becoming answerable to the operation by which it was achieved, begins where returned difference reaches a governing condition and a later act inherits the change. Its test is operational: whenever a philosophical distinction is used to justify a technical feature, the distinction has to have a discriminable consequence, some enacted mediating relation that changes through it and some later operation that changes with it. A lens that changes no question, no admissible evidence, no judgment and no next act has not been used, however many labels it has supplied.

Each of the six products §5 builds is disclosed through a whole pair of lenses, one lens with its Möbius partner, and two of the pairs have already worked in this movement. The Phenomenological and Phenomenal lenses are the pair §5 gives to the Workcell, where an agent's thrownness and the aperture of its encounter belong to its situated existence, and the Processual lens is half of the pair §5 gives to the Software Factory. Read together, the six are six offices of the means through which a life knows and acts within a world, six disclosures of one deliberately constructed paradigm. The design object the framework hands on is therefore the **agent-within-a-world** and no model in isolation: an agent individuates and acts within a paradigmatically structured world whose language, memory, permissions, representations, tools, inherited distinctions and institutions take part in what can appear and matter to it, and prompt thrownness is the name for its arrival there. Objective Internality is the means by which that world becomes actionable, prompt thrownness makes the inherited situation of those means explicit, and the framework makes selected mediations within it inspectable as situated refractions of the paradigm from which the inquiry proceeds.

Which distinctions of the means through which a life knows and acts can be made inspectable, and consequential in an experiment, in technologies built to carry them, without pretending that inspectability has located or disproved the Subject to whom anything appears? §5 builds the technologies to find out, and the agent that opens its session by reading its own notes, as an inheritance it takes up or as a stranger's past, is the first case it will meet.
