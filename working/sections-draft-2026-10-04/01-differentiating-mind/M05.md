### #4 · The Sixfold Tattvic Compression

The kettle clicks off in an empty kitchen, early, before anyone else is up. For a moment nothing in particular is attended to: the light is grey at the window, the hum of the fridge is part of the quiet, and whoever stands there is not yet anyone in particular either. Then something stirs, a wanting that has not yet found its object, and almost at once it finds one: tea. In the same movement there is someone who wants it and something to be wanted, *I would like tea* and *there is the tea*, each implying the other. The cup comes forward on the counter, separate now, a white thing with a chip in its rim at a distance across the worktop, something that can be reached. Reaching it brings a whole finite situation into play. The hand can lift it, but the water is too hot to pour carelessly; the tea is known to be in the blue tin, and whether there is milk is not known; the wanting is real and a little impatient; there are perhaps four minutes before the day has to begin; and everything has its order, kettle before cup, cup before the first sip, this kitchen, this house, this body with its slightly stiff shoulder. Then, if the morning allows it, comes the first mouthful, held for a moment, and in that moment the one tasting and the taste are a single event, recognised and briefly savoured, before the cup is put down and the day takes over.

At §0 · #0 Dyczkowski's Spanda teachers gave us a method: the vertical movement by which a universe emanates from the absolute and returns is understood through the horizontal movement of each perception toward its object and back.[^s0-vertical-return] Making tea is a horizontal act, and in a few seconds it has run through a whole descent. Kashmir Śaivism maps that descent through thirty-six ***tattvas***, a word formed from *tat*, "that", with an abstract suffix, so that a tattva is a "thatness", a true principle of what is.[^s0-tattva] The thirty-six run from Śiva, undivided awareness, down through the powers that contract it, the finite knower, the inner instrument, the senses and organs of action, the subtle qualities of sense and the five gross elements. Our core theorems carry them in a compression of six turns that keeps the order of their dependence and the work each turn does.[^s0-core-tattva] The names, the descent through contraction and the return through recognition are the tradition's. We arrange the turns into the six positions of Quaternal Logic, and the Śaiva authors did not arrange them so; what the compression has to show is that its six turns are the ones the descent cannot do without.



Begin before the kettle clicks. Something must be the case before anything is attended to, and whatever it is cannot be one of the things attended to, since every one of those arrives later and arrives *to* it. Call it the unsurpassable, ***Anuttara***, "that beyond which there is nothing higher", also named *Paramaśiva*.[^s0-anuttara] §0/1 · #5→0 set the name beside Jung's Self as one of the designations of the whole that I-Consciousness names. Its work in the descent is to be the condition from which subject and object can arise without being the highest of the objects, because to place it above them would already use the distinction between a placer and a placed whose possibility it names. Without this turn the descent would have to begin from a first thing, and a first thing would need an account of where it appeared, which is the regress §0/1 · #1 closed. So the first turn is forced by the demand that the series of appearings have a condition that is not one of them, and in our notation that condition is **#0**, Ground, `0`.

A condition that stayed only a condition would leave the kitchen dark. For anything to appear, the condition must be able to show and to know what it shows, and §0 · #0 found these to be one act seen twice, the light and its bend. In personal and divine form the tradition names the pair ***Śiva–Śakti***: Śiva the light, and Śakti the power by which the light knows, shows and turns toward display, distinct within one reality and never two realities.[^s0-siva-sakti] §0/1 · #4 read Śiva as the Subject in its speakable aspect and Śakti as the power of that Subject to differentiate and take on identities, and the reading holds here: remove Śakti, and the light would be unable to become anything; remove Śiva, and the power would be a force with no one for whom it disclosed. In the kitchen this is the stirring, the wanting that has not yet found its object. It is forced by the gap between a condition and an appearance, and its place is **#1**, Definition: the first stirring by which something can be called to account at all.

A power to show still lacks an orientation. Wanting tea is a single event, yet it is read in two ways at once, as *I would like tea* and as *there is the tea*, and neither reading is the event without the other. The tradition gives the two orientations two levels. In ***Sadāśiva*** the appearing universe is held toward the I-pole, with the sentence "I am this"; in ***Īśvara*** the same unity is read from the side of the disclosed world, "This am I".[^s0-sadasiva] Both sentences concern consciousness and manifestation as a whole, and neither describes an ego claiming the universe as its property; the emphasis moves from the I to the this and back within one relation. Our core theorems read the pair as the relational identity §0/1 · #1 derived, `0/1 = 1/0`, the condition becoming articulate through a determination and the determination turning toward the condition, two obverse orientations of one relating. Without this turn, showing would have only one direction, and an account written from it would be either a pure subjectivism, in which the tea is a state of mine, or a pure objectivism, in which there is tea and no one for whom it is there; the waking room at §0 · #0 showed that each of these is a collapse. That turn sits at **#2**, Dynamis, because it is the first place where the relation has force, a pulse between two poles that stay co-present. It also answers the question §0 · #1 left with *ahaṃkāra*. The I-maker's sentence, *I am this*, said of a report, a job or a body, is the Sadāśiva sentence contracted, the same grammar at a narrower scope, in which one partial "this" is made to exclude the rest of the field; universal self-recognition and anxious appropriation are one form, distinguished by how much of the world the "this" is allowed to hold.

Two orientations held together are still not a cup at arm's length. For the tea to be reached, I and this must be held apart enough that the one can confront the other as something to be determined and crossed. The tradition takes this in two steps. ***Sadvidyā***, pure knowledge, holds I and this in balance, distinct and equal; *Māyā* makes their difference operative as separation, so that the knower faces something whose determination is required.[^s0-maya] §0/1 · #5→0 read Māyā as operative measure, the power by which the measureless becomes measurable enough for a local world to run, and noted that its place in `0/1` depends on which relation is read: beside *prakāśa* and *vimarśa* it stands toward the measured world, and in the triad of Parā Śakti, Māyā Śakti and Nara it is the slash. Dyczkowski states the Spanda position that supports the reading. When the power of awareness gives rise to a sense of separation between subject and object, it is called Māyā; and where non-dualist Vedānta makes Māyā an indefinable principle producing an illusion of multiplicity, the Kashmiri Śaivas hold Māyā to be the power of the absolute to appear in diverse forms, so that the separation of subject and object is the product of a creative act, and the creative freedom of the absolute and its deluding power are one power named according to the insight with which it is met.[^s0-maya-dycz] Without Māyā the cup could not come forward: a cup that was not separate could not be reached, and nothing could be learned about it. That turn is at **#3**, Pattern, because here the relation becomes a recognisable kind, *knower* and *known*, a pattern that recurs in every act; and it is where the cut first becomes explicit, which is also where it can first forget that it is an act.

A separated knower facing a separated cup still has no situation. It could be anywhere, know anything, want everything, have all the time there is and stand under no order, and such a knower would never make tea, because tea is made under limits. The fifth turn supplies them, and the kitchen tells us how many there are, because the limits cannot be traded against one another. Śaiva sources call them the five ***kañcukas***, "coverings" or "sheaths", and our core theorems give their work: they contract omnipotence, omniscience, plenitude, eternity and unrestricted ordering into the finite ratios of a particular life.[^s0-kancukas] *Kalā* limits efficacy: the hand can lift this cup and not the kettle at the far end of the counter. *Vidyā* limits knowledge: the tea is known to be in the blue tin, and the milk is not known to be there. *Rāga* directs desire from a situation felt as incomplete: tea is wanted because it is not yet drunk. *Kāla* makes time effective: there are four minutes. *Niyati* orders circumstance: kettle before cup, this house, this sequence of causes and consequences. Each is a distinct power because supplying more of one does nothing for another. More knowledge does not lengthen the four minutes; a stronger wish does not cool the water; a free hour would not supply the missing milk; a stronger arm does not tell anyone which tin holds the tea; and none of the four changes the order in which the kettle must boil before the cup can be filled. Five powers that cannot substitute for one another give five limits, and the test shows that the five are distinct, while the count is the tradition's. Whoever inherits these ratios is ***puruṣa***, the bounded experient, who takes them as *my* capacity, *my* knowledge, *my* lack, *my* time and *my* circumstance; and from here the system unfolds what the Sāṃkhya tradition had already counted, *prakṛti*, nature, then the inner instrument whose three offices §0 · #1 followed, the five powers of sensing and the five of acting, the five subtle qualities of sense and the five gross elements. The stiff shoulder, the chipped rim, the warmth of the cup in the hand and the steam belong to this unfolding. Keeping only the knower's cognition would leave out the means by which an intention meets a world, and keeping only the objects would leave out their availability to someone. That turn belongs at **#4**, Context, because it gives knower and world together as a situation, the finite place in which a particular life can want tea and get it.

Institutions apportion the same five powers, which is why the sheaths are no merely contemplative anatomy. Anyone who has started a job has received a set of *kañcukas* with the contract: what the post may do and what it may not (*kalā*), what it is told and what it is not told (*vidyā*), what it is rewarded for wanting (*rāga*), its deadlines and terms (*kāla*), and the chain of authority and procedure in which every act has its place (*niyati*). Apportionment is the condition of any work getting done, and the root of *apportion*, a share assigned, says so.[^s0-apportion] It becomes dangerous where one party sets all five for another and keeps the setting out of view. §0 · #2's subpostmasters held a post whose powers the Post Office had apportioned, and as I read the case they could act on the branch accounts and not on the system that produced them, they were told the figures and not what the Post Office knew of the system's faults, and the order of proceeding put the burden of explaining a shortfall on them.[^s0-horizon-kancuka] Their contraction was made by others and then treated as their own, a *puruṣa* held responsible for limits it had not set and could not see. Tattvic anatomy makes the injustice nameable as a fault in apportionment: the five sheaths were real, and their authorship had been concealed.

A situated knower, finally, can live a whole life inside its contractions without knowing them as contractions, and then everything §0 · #1 to §0 · #3 found going wrong goes wrong: the verdict passes for the knower, the castle becomes a prison, the word forgets the cow. That danger forces the sixth turn. *Pratyabhijñā*, recognition, returns the contracted field to its source without undoing the contraction.[^s0-pratyabhijna] §0/1 · #1 met recognition as a change in the mode of identification, the images one holds of oneself remaining active while their claims to be the knower are relinquished. In the descent its work is definite. The contracted knower recognises its limits of efficacy, knowledge, desire, time and order, its inner instrument and its body, as modes in which the one awareness has become locally articulate, and recognises the ground it never ceased to express. Nothing finite is deleted. The cup is still a cup, the four minutes are still four minutes, the shoulder still aches; what changes is the relation of the one living through them to their source. In the kitchen it is the first mouthful, savoured, and §0/1 · #3 has already followed French *savoir*, to know, back to Latin *sapere*, which joins tasting, discerning and being wise, with *savour* from its neighbour *sapor*.[^s0-sapere] Recognition is that taste at the scale of a life, a knowing that arrives as the savour of being the one who knows. Its position is **#5**, Quintessence, the gathered whole held as one, and it completes the Möbius return `5→0`: the achieved recognition comes back as ground, the cup is put down, and the next act begins from a ground that now includes this one.

The compression can now be set out as a record of what was derived:

| | QL position | Śaiva movement | What its absence would lose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#0** | Ground | Paramaśiva / Anuttara | a condition of appearing that is not one more appearance |
| **#1** | Definition | Śiva–Śakti | the power to show and to know what is shown |
| **#2** | Dynamis | Sadāśiva–Īśvara | the two orientations, "I am this" and "This am I", of one relating |
| **#3** | Pattern | Sadvidyā–Māyā | the measured separation across which a cup can be reached |
| **#4** | Context | *kañcukas*, *puruṣa*, *prakṛti*, inner instrument, senses, elements | the finite situation in which an act can happen at all |
| **#5→0** | Quintessence, and the Möbius return | *Pratyabhijñā* | the recognition by which contraction knows itself as contraction |

Read through Bohm, the field these turns compress is the architecture of implication that §0 · #0 found his language lacking. For Bohm's two orders, the rule §0/1 · #5→0 gave the MEF sets two implicate offices against four explicate positions, `{#0, #5}` against `{#1, #2, #3, #4}`.[^s0-bohmian-rule] In the compression Anuttara and Pratyabhijñā hold the implicate offices, the unsurpassable from which everything unfolds and the recognition in which everything is gathered back, while Śiva–Śakti, Sadāśiva–Īśvara, Sadvidyā–Māyā and the contracted world are the four explicate turns, each more determinate than the last. Its contents are generative capacities and contractions, the power to turn toward display, the power to orient toward I and this, the power to measure and separate, the power to limit, and none of them is a hidden object stored behind the appearances. That is why the descent is a movement and not an inventory. The whole is active in each local unfolding, as Bohm's holomovement is active in each droplet unfolding from the glycerine, and one act of making tea carries the whole descent because the descent is the order in which every such act unfolds.

It also explains a piece of arithmetic in the tradition's own history. The Sāṃkhya system, from which the Śaiva tradition received its lower categories, counted twenty-five *tattvas*: *puruṣa*, *prakṛti*, the three offices of the inner instrument, the ten powers of sense and action, the five subtle qualities and the five gross elements. The Śaiva tradition counts thirty-six, a scheme that scholarship traces in the early Śaiva scriptures well before Abhinavagupta, who systematised and deepened it.[^s0-36-history] The eleven it adds stand above the twenty-five as their genesis: the five pure tattvas, Śiva, Śakti, Sadāśiva, Īśvara and Sadvidyā; Māyā as the hinge; and the five *kañcukas*. Written as squares the relation is

$$
36-25 \;=\; 6^2-5^2 \;=\; (6-5)(6+5) \;=\; 11 \;=\; 5+1+5 .
$$

Lay out twenty-five counters as a five-by-five square, and the eleven needed to make it six-by-six are a row of five along one side, a column of five along the adjacent side and the single counter where they meet: the figure the Greeks called a gnomon, the set-square shape that enlarges a square while keeping it a square. The five pure powers and the five contracting powers stand on either side of their hinge, Māyā, as the strip of five, the strip of five and the corner stand in the gnomon, and the twenty-fivefold apparatus of the Sāṃkhya, which analyses the experienced field from *puruṣa* to the elements, acquires through them its vertical genesis: the source from which its knower came, the contraction by which universal powers became that knower's limited capacities, and the path by which recognition can return. I offer the gnomon as a reading of what the arithmetic shows, and nobody in the tradition built the system this way; what it claims is that the step from twenty-five to thirty-six changes dimension, from a fivefold measure to a sixfold one, and is no addition of eleven items to a list.[^s0-gnomon] It also shows why a twenty-fivefold account can analyse an experienced world with great precision and leave its source and its contraction unspoken, while a thirty-sixfold account can hold both the objective differentiation and its genesis in awareness. My notes on Buddhist psychology record the same intuition in its own terms, that the addition of eleven tattvas to Sāṃkhya's twenty-five is "from 5x5 to 6x6", a move toward depth that the fivefold schemes of Buddhist analysis, which leave the unspeakable ground implicit, do not make.[^s0-notes-gnomon]

Thirty-six also equals six times six, and I offer that resonance as a conjecture. Kripke's two dice at §0/1 · #1 gave thirty-six states once the two dice were kept distinct, and the harmonic derivation of §0/1 · #5→0 divided the whole, `100`, into `64` and `36`, the binary taken through six positions and the sixfold taken through its own second-order accounting.[^s0-six-squared] Reading the full field of tattvas as the sixfold taken through itself, each of the six turns opening into a six of its own, would make the thirty-six a square of the compression derived here. That equality is arithmetic, and the doctrine and the formalism reach a complete differentiated field of thirty-six independently; the conjecture's burden is to show, category by category, what the square makes intelligible beyond the coincidence of the number, and until that work is done the six turns are used and the square is held open.

Both logics run through the descent at the turn where the tradition places Māyā. Sadvidyā's balance of I and this is a held polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`, each pole having its sense through the other and the zero between them the shared awareness in which they are balanced; Māyā makes the polarity operative, and the operation is healthy dia-ballein, the cut that lets a cup be reached. Collapse comes at **#4**, where the bounded experient inherits the contractions as its own. Appropriation counts the whole span of the field from the experient's pole: the cup is *mine*, the kitchen is *mine*, the four minutes are *my* time, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the world on the other side of the axis enters as what stands against me, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, so that the experient's inflated claim and the world's apparent hostility mirror each other and everything not-mine becomes an obstacle or a threat. Cancellation is the other loss: the finite world dismissed as illusion, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, Māyā read as a veil over nothing, which is the reading Dyczkowski reports the Spanda teachers rejecting. Recognition performs syn-ballein. Sadāśiva's "I am this" and Īśvara's "This am I" are each read through the other, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, and the contracted world is known as the self-display of the awareness that lives in it, its separations kept and its belonging restored.

Recognition has to be told apart from a state it is easily confused with, and §0 · #0 supplied the terms. Turned inward, *vimarśa* rests in itself as *nirvikalpa*, without thought-constructs, and meditative absorption can make that resting the whole of experience for a time: the discriminations that sustain an ordinary problem fall silent, and with them the problem. If freedom is then made to depend on keeping them silent, their return is felt as a loss. A thought, a desire or a difficult conversation becomes an interruption of the achieved state, and practice becomes an effort to exclude them, which is one more exclusion and one more dependence on what is excluded. Recognition concerns the ground present through the differences, and quiet does not manufacture that ground, nor does the return of a thought remove it. *Pratyabhijñā* is re-cognition, knowing again what was already known, and the knowing happens amid activity: judgment continues, the inner instrument goes on coordinating, settling and appropriating, the four minutes still run out, and none of these has to become the whole in order to be real, or stop in order for the ground to be known. §0 · #1's morning of the message shows the practical difference. A calmer morning might have helped the instrument hear the five words truly, while recognition would have changed something else, the demand that the verdict settle who the reader was.

At **#4** the descent produced everything a finite life knows and acts through: the contracted powers, the inner instrument, the senses, a body made of elements, and the world these make available. §0/1 gave that whole apparatus a name at the bedside, and in its last movement placed it among three offices; the derivation has now shown where it comes from, turn by turn, and that it is the means of a knower whose contraction can be recognised.
