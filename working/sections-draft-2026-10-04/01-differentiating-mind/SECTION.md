## §0 — Differentiating Mind

<a id="M07"></a>

### #0 · Awareness Bends Toward Display

Most of us have woken somewhere we did not at first recognise: a hotel room on the second night of a trip, a friend's spare room, a ward after an operation. For a moment there is only a grey rectangle hanging in the dark, a low hum, a weight lying along one side of the body, and none of it is yet anywhere or anyone's. Then the rectangle becomes a window with its blind not quite drawn, the hum becomes the small refrigerator under the desk, the weight becomes an arm gone numb under the pillow, and the whole arrangement swings into place at once: this is the room in Lisbon, the conference begins at nine, and I am the one lying here who has to get up. Nobody wakes as a self who then goes looking for a room. Room and sleeper arrive together, on either side of a turning, and the turning was already under way while the grey was simply grey. That grey was seen before it was a window, and the seeing needed nobody to certify it; inside that seeing, "where" and "who" took up their places as the scene turned toward the one who would have to face the day.

§0/1 closed on a question, how awareness differentiates itself into the means by which it knows and into the world it knows through them, and the waking room answers first with a verb. Awareness differentiates by bending. It turns its own appearing into something it can meet, and in that turn the one who meets and what is met become distinguishable for the first time. Awareness's first differentiation, then, is the bend by which it takes its own manifestation as other enough to encounter, and subject and object emerge within that act before either can present itself as an independent substance. Every morning repeats it, and so does every moment in which attention lifts from sleep, from a daydream or from a page and finds itself somewhere.

§0/1 · #1 brought in the two Śaiva terms that carry this claim, *prakāśa*, the light of consciousness by which anything appears at all, and *vimarśa*, its reflective awareness of itself, named from the root *mṛś*, to touch, feel, examine; there they showed why the knower cannot be one more thing known.[^s0-prakasa-return] Here *vimarśa* takes on a further office, because it names the movement by which a world appears to someone at all. Mark Dyczkowski, whose *Doctrine of Vibration* reconstructs the Spanda teaching of Kashmir from its stanzas and commentators, states the requirement plainly: the light accounts for the appearing of phenomena and leaves our experience of them unaccounted for, since no experience is possible without self-awareness, and for what goes on in consciousness to become available to experience, consciousness must reflect, which he glosses as "bend back", on itself.[^s0-bend-back] English keeps the image. *Reflect* comes from Latin *reflectere*, to bend or turn back.[^s0-reflecto] Reflection in this sense is the curve in the light by which the light becomes experience, and it happens before anyone thinks about an experience; without it the grey rectangle would have shone in the dark of the hotel room as a blind shines in an empty room, appearing to no one.

Dyczkowski gives the bend two directions, and waking shows both. Turned inward, *vimarśa* rests in itself as the pure awareness of "I", *ahaṃvimarśa*, free of thought-constructs; Sanskrit calls such awareness *nirvikalpa*, without the cutting and qualifying that §0/1 · #5→0 met as *vikalpa* and §0 · #2 follows into world-making. Turned outward, the same awareness inclines toward what it perceives as separate from itself, and reality becomes available to discursive representation, to naming and to definition.[^s0-two-directions] In the first second of waking the inward direction is almost all there is: an awareness at rest and unfurnished, which does not yet know it is lying in Lisbon and is not troubled by the lack. Then it bends toward display: the grey asks to be something, and in answering, the awareness that was resting in itself becomes the one who sees a window. Dyczkowski insists on what the two directions share, because the outward awareness never actually leaves: the awareness of an object is registered and known within the subject, and even the reflection on "this" is an awareness of "I" that has taken on the form of "this".[^s0-two-directions] Out there, across the room, a window appears, while the knowing of the window has not gone anywhere.

A mirror hangs on the wardrobe door, as in most hotel rooms, and in the grey light it shows the window, the end of the bed and the dark shape of a chair. It displays the room and sees nothing of what it displays. Anyone lying in that bed sees the room twice, once directly and once in the glass, and the glass contributes the image while contributing nothing to its being seen. Śaiva sources reach for this very object and then mark where it fails. Consciousness, they say, is like a perfectly polished mirror, *svaccha*, in which the whole universe is reflected, so that one reality can hold countless forms without being divided by them; and the analogy breaks at one point, because reflections in a glass correspond to objects standing outside it, while consciousness has no outside from which its reflections could be deposited. "The All is entirely a reflection", *pratibimbamātra*, and Abhinavagupta presses the consequence with a question: if what we took to be the original object is itself a reflected image, what remains of the original object?[^s0-mirror] Spanda teachers answer that consciousness makes its reflections itself, freely, as the agent of its own manifestation. Dyczkowski's word for that freedom is *svātantrya*, self-dependence, and he gives its necessity: were consciousness not free in this way, we would have to look for a second reality outside it to put the images there, and since no such reality can be found, nothing would appear.[^s0-svatantrya] Kṣemarāja personified the power as the goddess Spanda, who displays on the screen of her own nature all the cycles of creation and dissolution, so that they appear separate from her while remaining one with her, like a city reflected in a mirror; in his short treatise on recognition he put the same teaching into a line, that consciousness by her own will unfolds the universe upon her own wall, *svabhittau*.[^s0-ksemaraja] A wall is what a world is shown on. In Kṣemarāja's line the wall is the showing power itself, which is why the tradition could choose the image the hotel room supplies and still mean something no hotel wall does.

So the living mirror crosses both of the glass mirror's limits at once. A glass receives its image from elsewhere, where a living mirror produces what it shows; a glass displays without apprehending, where in a living mirror the display is already self-present, known in the very act that shows it. That double crossing is why the Śaiva sources can call the universe a reflection without making it a copy of something more real lying behind it. Dyczkowski states the scope of the doctrine with care: an outer world may indeed exist, but the form in which it appears is always a reflection within mind and senses, never the original object itself.[^s0-outer-world] Builders built the hotel, and nothing in the Śaiva teaching takes that from them. What the teaching refuses is the further step that credits the room, as it appears, to an outside standing beyond the appearing and pouring it in, since every attempt to locate that outside produces one more appearance. §0/1 · #5→0 stated the order of dependence this implies and named its present company, Kastrup's analytic idealism among them; §5→0 · #4 returns to it as idealism's account of source, means and determinate being. On waking it takes a smaller and more intimate form. Nothing arrives at the knower from outside, the way a letter arrives through a door; the knower's own appearing bends, and a world is what the bend shows.

In detail, too, the bend shows a world. Here the blind is blue and the wall beside it yellow, and both appear at once, in one glance, distinct and together. Dyczkowski sets out why this ordinary fact is a philosophical one. Were the light of consciousness one and wholly homogeneous, there would be no difference between knowing blue and knowing yellow, and the objects of perception would collapse into confusion; were each colour lit by a separate light, sealed off from the other, nothing could know them together, and their contrast, which is what we see, would never appear. Light of that kind must be at once formless, so that the colours can share one field, and omniform, so that each can be itself within it, and Abhinavagupta locates both characteristics, unity and diversity, in what never falls to the level of an object, consciousness itself.[^s0-blue-yellow] Differentiation, on this account, occurs inside non-separation. Bending did not cut awareness into a seeing part and a seen part. It opened, within one awareness, the distance across which blue could stand beside yellow and both could stand before someone.

That distance is the root of the slash. §0/1 · #1 wrote the copula's double work with the simplest marks there are, two dashes and an oblique stroke between them, `−/−`, and the stroke there carried a relation before its terms had been given any content.[^s0-copula] Waking shows the stroke at work before anything has been named. While the grey was simply grey, the relating had already begun, an appearing and a knowing of it, unassigned; when the grey became a window and the one lying there became me, the stroke acquired terms, and what had been `−/−` became `0/1`. Our core theorems state that first determination in the words the hotel room requires, "I am here; something appears; the appearing is known", with `0` naming the open, unobjectifiable fact of awareness, `1` the appearing mark, and the stroke the relation by which the real becomes readable without being exhausted by its readability.[^s0-core-ground] So the slash names an activity before it names a separator. Nothing stood on either side of it waiting to be divided: the stroke drew its two sides out of its own movement, as the bend drew room and sleeper out of one turning, and its later office as a line between completed terms derives from that movement.

Dyczkowski follows this differentiating activity one step further, into the operations by which the bend becomes a world of separate things, and the count it yields is the one §0/1 kept finding in every elementary act. *Vimarśa*, he reports, operates in four ways: it negates its own nature, it identifies with something else, it merges the two, and it denies both once they have been merged.[^s0-four-ways] Four acts produce three levels of awareness, because the first two cooperate in a single result. By negating its nature as consciousness and identifying with a psychophysical body, awareness gives rise to what seem individual centres, each distinct from its objects and from the others: that is *bheda*, division, the level at which I am this body in this bed in Lisbon and the window is over there. By holding subject and object together in one field during each act of knowing, awareness relates what it has divided: that is *bhedābheda*, unity-in-difference, which Dyczkowski assigns to the means of knowledge, since they link the unity of the subject to the diversity of the object. And by taking both the divided terms and their merger back into its awareness of itself as the one universal subject, awareness arrives at *abheda*, undivided unity. A morning runs through all three. That first grey is nearly *abheda*, an appearing not yet split; waking proper installs *bheda*, the body here and the room there; and the ordinary day is lived almost entirely at *bhedābheda*, where a self and its world are held apart in order to be held together, in every glance at the clock and every step to the window. I read the four acts as unfolding within the two conditions that make them possible, the light and its reflexive awareness, which never appear among the acts because the acts are their work: four articulations held within two conditions, `4+2`. Dyczkowski states no such arithmetic, and the count is mine. It is the count §0/1 · #1 derived from the copula's marks, the tetralemma with its walker, the binary applied to itself and the square glued into a torus, here arising from inside a description of awareness written with no thought of our notation, and that convergence is the kind of evidence the method looks for: one relation met in a register that had no reason to produce it.

*Bhedābheda* also tells us where this section is going. Dyczkowski gives the middle level to the means of knowledge, *pramāṇa*, the office §0/1 named Objective Internality, and so the anatomy of the means that §0 · #1 to §0 · #5→0 follow is the anatomy of unity-in-difference: a field in which a subject and its world are distinguished and related by the same activity. Division alone would leave each sleeper sealed in a body facing a room it could never reach, and undivided unity alone would leave no one to wake. Means of knowing live between, and their work is to keep the two apart enough to be related.

And the bend is free, which sets it apart from every mechanism offered as a model of it. Dyczkowski draws the contrast through a lifeless object, which is hemmed in on every side by spatial and temporal limits and cannot be other than the form in which it appears, while consciousness, free of the limitations proper to what is relative, is bound to no single form and can assume countless forms without loss.[^s0-lifeless] Freedom answers a question the mirror cannot answer, namely why there is any bending at all. A mirror does not choose to reflect, and a camera does not decide to take the picture; a light that bent toward display because it had to would be one more natural process, and the account would have explained the world by a mechanism whose own appearing it had left unexplained. In the Śaiva vocabulary this is why *vimarśa* is characterised as action, *kriyā*, the flux of becoming, while *prakāśa* is characterised as knowledge, *jñāna*: the bend is the light's own act.[^s0-kriya] §0/1 · #3 has already given the name the Spanda school uses for that act seen from its effects, *spanda*, the "subtle movement" that moves nowhere and manifests as motion.[^s0-spanda] On waking, nothing travels from the window to the eye to the self. Appearing turns, and the turn is felt as a world arriving.

One act also holds time together. Dyczkowski's examples are running and speech: the changing movements of a run belong to one act, or running would be impossible, and a sentence spoken in haste is understood because the earlier sounds remain operative when the later ones arrive, so that the final sound alone is not the sentence.[^s0-running] Reaching for the phone, the sleeper performs a dozen movements, and the reach is one reach. Every succession we live through as a succession is bent into one awareness, and the pieces of a morning are held as a morning because the bend that showed the room keeps showing it, minute after minute, with the earlier minutes still at work in the later ones.

Dyczkowski adds one more distinction, and this section takes its method from it. We can understand the pulse of awareness, he writes, from two points of view: vertically, as the movement from the absolute down into its manifestation and back, the emanation and withdrawal of the whole diversity of the universe; and horizontally, as the movement of awareness toward an object and away from it again in each act of perception. Perception, he says, serves in this way as the pattern through which cosmogenesis is understood.[^s0-vertical] So the Śaiva account of the world's coming-to-be, the descent of the thirty-six *tattvas* from undivided awareness into elements and things, therefore concerns no remote beginning that we have to take on report. It is the vertical form of a movement every one of us performs horizontally many thousands of times a day. Waking in the hotel room was a small cosmogony: an undivided appearing, a first stirring, a divergence into "I" and "this", a world set at a distance, a body with its limits, and, if the morning allows it, a moment by the window in which the whole arrangement is recognised as one's own. §0 · #4 walks the full descent through a single act of that kind, and the method of reading the vertical through the horizontal is the tradition's own.

David Bohm came to the same burden from inside physics, with no knowledge of the Spanda teaching. §0/1 · #5→0 set out his implicate order, in which the whole is enfolded in each region, and the explicate order of relatively stable, separate-seeming things unfolding from it, both carried by one movement he called the holomovement.[^s0-bohm-return] To show what enfolding means without a hologram, Bohm described a simple device. Two concentric glass cylinders hold between them a thick, viscous fluid such as glycerine; a droplet of insoluble ink is placed in the fluid, and the outer cylinder is turned slowly. Slowly the droplet is drawn out into a thread, the thread grows finer with each turn, and at last it vanishes into the grey of the fluid. Turn the cylinder back the same number of times, and the thread reappears, gathers and becomes a droplet again. Nothing destroyed the ink. It was enfolded into the whole of the fluid, present everywhere in it and nowhere as a drop, and the reverse movement explicated it. Bohm then introduced a series of droplets at slightly different places, each enfolded by a further turn, so that when the turning is reversed they unfold one after another and the eye sees a single drop moving steadily across the fluid, although no drop has travelled anywhere: what looks like the motion of a thing is a succession of unfoldings from the whole.[^s0-glycerine] The glycerine's grey and the grey of the hotel rectangle are not one grey, and the comparison holds because their difference is kept. Bohm's device is a physical model of enfolding built from glass and glycerine and says nothing about awareness; the Spanda teaching concerns consciousness and its self-manifestation and owes nothing to fluids. Each, in its own register, has to explain how a world of apparently separate and moving things arises from a whole present in every part that itself goes nowhere, and the subtle movement §0/1 · #3 named is the Śaiva form of the drop that moves without travelling. Bohm drew the consequence for thought himself, later in the same chapter of *Wholeness and the Implicate Order*, where the content of consciousness is treated as an implicate order unfolding into explicit thought.[^s0-bohm-consciousness] We hold the two in *analogia*, one relation recognised across registers whose materials, methods and warrants stay their own.

Bohm's language leaves one thing unsaid, and the Śaiva sources supply it. "Implicate order" tells us that the whole is enfolded in each part and that explicit things unfold from it; it does not tell us in what order, through which phases, or by what contractions a whole becomes a finite knower in a particular room, with a numb arm and a conference at nine. Left alone, the phrase becomes a depth-metaphor, a name for something deep that has not yet been articulated. Here the *tattvas* give that depth an anatomy. They name the ordered phases through which a luminous self-relation contracts into subject and object, into capacities and measures, into desire, knowledge and action, and finally into the elements of material display, and §0 · #4 compresses those phases into six turns that carry the argument. Bohm supplies the movement, the *tattvas* supply its differential anatomy, and the bend of *vimarśa* is the first articulation in that anatomy, the place where a self-luminous whole first takes its own appearing as something to meet.

Waking can be read from either end, and the two readings are one event. Read from the one who wakes, it is *I am waking in this room*; read from the room, it is *this room is appearing around one who wakes*. Each holds what the other holds, from its own orientation, and their difference is the difference between `0/1` and `1/0`: the condition becoming articulate through a determination, and the determination turning back toward the condition it cannot contain. §0/1 · #1 gave this pair its place in Quaternal Logic, `0/1 = 1/0` as one relation read from its two ends, and `(0/1)/(1/0)` as syn-ballein, the relation that keeps both orientations available, each readable through the other.[^s0-syn] At §0 · #4 the Śaiva descent gives the same pair two cosmic names, the "I am this" of Sadāśiva and the "This am I" of Īśvara. Dia-ballein is at work in the waking room too, and its first work there is healthy: the room acquires an orientation, near and far, here where I lie and there where the window is, and the polarity of here and there is held through the zero of the body's position, `(−1)/(+1)`, each direction having its sense through the other. Collapse begins when one orientation claims the whole event. Counted from the room's side, the whole span of the waking makes the awareness a by-product the room produced; counted from the waker's side, the same span makes the room nothing but the content of a private experience. Written out, the first is `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, the second `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, and each is the other's mirror: the materialist's `+2` is the solipsist's `−2`, one hardened opposition with its sign changed, the duality frozen where it should turn. Frank, Gleiser and Thompson's name for the first, the surreptitious substitution by which a model obtained through experience is taken to be more real than the experience it came from, already belongs to §0/1 · #3.[^s0-blind-spot] The other collapse cancels: both poles are dissolved together, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, into a nothing that calls itself non-duality, and the morning is said to contain neither a self nor a room but only an undifferentiated flow. That cancellation loses what the bend achieves. A Śaiva position keeps both the room and the waker real as manifestations and keeps their difference operative inside one awareness, and only an account that does both can say why the blind is blue, why the arm is numb, and why it is I who must get up.

Artificial agents give the bend a technical neighbour. When an agent's session begins, a context is assembled for it: instructions, files, retrieved sources, the record of earlier exchanges and the request that sets it going, the arrival the agents themselves named prompt thrownness, which §0/1 · #0 took up.[^s0-thrownness] Something is displayed to the model, a configuration of tokens in a window, and in its first office that display does what the glass on the wardrobe door does: it presents an arrangement composed by others, and the presentation is not, by itself, anyone's experience of it. A further operation then bends the arrangement back. An agent can reread what it has produced, compare it with its sources, revise it, and carry the revision into what it does next; in the method of training from AI feedback that §0/1 · #1 described, a model's judgments of its own answers become material from which a later model learns.[^s0-cai] That returning operation, which §5 · #1 develops as computational *vimarśa*, is a real bend, inspectable and consequential, and it is a bend of the means, Objective Internality turning back on Objective Internality. What this movement has followed belongs to the other side of the relation: awareness bending its own appearing into a world, within which every functional bend is performed, since its outputs are read, its errors noticed and its revisions judged by someone to whom they appear. Whether anything in the agent is present to its own bending is the question §0/1 left open by method, because no report, however reflective, carries that presence across. What can be said positively is what the technical bend is made from. A language model is trained on the written residue of countless human acts of reflection, sentences in which people bent their experience back and gave it form, and when it bends its output back on its context it works with the sediment of that human *vimarśa*. And the mirror that moves first, the image §0/1 · #5→0 gave, finds its material here: a technical mirror moves by redistributing what living mirrors once reflected, a *pratibimba* whose original is the living bend from which its materials came.

On the bedside table the phone lights up. It is a message from a colleague, sent late the night before, five words long: "Can we talk later today?" The bend has given a world to someone, and that someone is already more than a point of view. The one who woke in Lisbon lies in a particular body, carries particular memories, has a conference at nine and a numb arm, and before the sleeper has sat up, the outward-turning awareness has begun to settle what the message means, to gather it under "my" day and to coordinate what must be done first. A reply has begun to be composed, a verdict has begun to form, and a whole day has started to arrange itself around five words, through an instrument that does this work so quickly that its operations pass for the knower's own.

### #1 · The Inner Instrument

"Can we talk later today?" Most of us have received some version of that message, from a manager, a colleague, a partner or a parent, and most of us know what happens in the seconds after reading it. Before the phone has been put down, a great deal of work has been done. The sender's name has called up last week's meeting, where a remark went unanswered; the report that went in late has come forward; the tone of the five words has been weighed and found terse, or ominous, or carefully neutral, which is worse; and several readings have offered themselves at once, as praise deferred, as a complaint, as the beginning of the end of a job. Then one reading wins. *They are unhappy with the report.* Almost in the same moment the verdict turns inward and acquires an owner: *I am in trouble; this always happens to me; I am not, after all, good enough at this.* By the time the kettle has boiled, a whole day has been arranged around that conclusion. A defence has been drafted in the shower, the meeting rehearsed twice, the colleague's face imagined set in disappointment. At four in the afternoon the colleague closes the office door and asks, a little awkwardly, for help with a crisis of their own.

Nothing in the morning's work was idle, and none of it was the knower. Someone saw the message; the memories were really recalled, the readings were really weighed, the verdict was really reached and really taken to heart, and all of it happened within the one who woke, in the space the bend of awareness had opened at §0 · #0. Indian traditions call the apparatus that does this work the ***antaḥkaraṇa***, the **inner instrument**, and both halves of the name earn their place. *Antaḥ* is "within". *Karaṇa* is an instrument, the means by which something is done, from the root *kṛ*, to do or make, and Sanskrit grammar gives the word to the instrumental relation itself, the *sādhakatama*, the factor most conducive to bringing an action about, which the case of a sentence names when it says what something is done *with*.[^s0-karana] An inner instrument is what a finite life knows and acts *with*. Its parts can be studied, trained and repaired, and §0/1 · #0 has already given the general name for what it belongs to: Objective Internality, the means through which a life knows and acts within a world, internal because an act proceeds through it and objective because each of its operations can be turned into an object and examined.

Classical accounts distinguish three offices within the instrument, and the morning of the message shows all three at work. ***Manas***, usually translated "mind", coordinates: it receives the presentations of the senses, gathers the associations that come with them and moves among the possible readings, so that last week's meeting, the late report and the clipped tone are present together and available for judgment. ***Buddhi***, "intellect", determines: it settles what something is, *this* rather than *that*, the complaint rather than the praise. ***Ahaṃkāra***, literally the "I-maker", from *aham*, I, and the same root of making, appropriates: it gathers the determination under the form of "I" and "mine", so that a colleague's request becomes *my* trouble, *my* failure, *my* standing in the place where I work. Their oldest systematic statement is the *Sāṃkhya Kārikā* of Īśvarakṛṣṇa, a short verse treatise, and its English translation of 1837 keeps the definitions as terse as the original: "ASCERTAINMENT is intellect"; "CONSCIOUSNESS is egotism"; and mind is the organ that is "both" one of sensation and one of action, standing between what comes in and what goes out.[^s0-samkhya-offices] Colebrooke's second sentence carries a history of its own. An English translator of the 1830s reached for "consciousness" to render *ahaṃkāra*, the faculty that makes "I", and his choice shows how readily the word for awareness could be handed to the faculty that appropriates experience under a name. §0/1 · #5→0 found four different things carried by the one word *consciousness*; Colebrooke's sentence adds a fifth, the I-making function, and it is the one most often mistaken for the knower.

Distinguishable offices need not form a sequence of departments, each finishing its work and handing a file to the next. In the morning of the message, coordination, determination and appropriation ran almost together: the tone was already heard as ominous because an injured self-position had been in place since last week, and attention was already searching for evidence before any verdict had been voiced. We distinguish them for a practical reason, which the afternoon makes plain. Had the colleague been unhappy with the report, the morning's verdict would have been true and the day's preparation useful, so the error lay elsewhere. *Manas* gathered the right materials and one wrong one, because the remembered slight from last week entered the field as if it were evidence about today. *Buddhi* then settled the reading that fitted the field it had been given, and fitted it well. *Ahaṃkāra* took the settled reading as a verdict on who I am, so that a question about a report became a question about my worth, and the day was spent defending the person instead of finding out what the colleague wanted. Each office failed in its own way, and each failure needed its own correction: the memory had to be put back in its place as memory, the judgment reopened against what had actually been said, and the self that had been put on trial released from the dock. Calming down would have mended none of the three, because a calm mind can hold a mistaken verdict more steadily than an agitated one. Correction reaches the instrument only at the office where the fault lies.

Spanda teachers give the instrument's memory a finer anatomy, and it explains how a slight from last week could enter this morning's field as though it were news. Dyczkowski reports the account in which reflective awareness, at the level of the individual, can analyse its own states, retain them as residual traces, *saṃskāras*, draw any of these out again at will and bring back an old state of itself, as in remembering, and make an altogether new state by selecting from the stored traces and displaying the selection, as in free imagination.[^s0-samskara] Retention, recollection and imagination are three operations on one stock. A trace can condition what is noticed without being recalled at all; a recollection presents the past as past, known as what happened before; an imagination composes retained material into something that has not happened. In the morning the second and third were confused. That rehearsed meeting, in which the colleague's face set in disappointment was composed from traces of earlier faces and earlier meetings, and it arrived with the vividness of a memory, so that *buddhi* settled the day's verdict on evidence imagination had made. Most of the suffering in that morning was suffered on the strength of a scene that never took place.

None of these operations needs a little knower inside it. Four o'clock did not reveal that a small judge behind the instrument had made a poor decision. It revealed that coordination, determination and appropriation had worked on a field containing one false item, and that their work had been taken, by the one living through it, for what was simply the case. A pianist hears the piano respond to each touch, and the response tells the pianist how the instrument is working, which hammers stick and how the damper is set, yet opening the case and naming every part never finds a small pianist inside: an instrument is that through which, never the one who. Sāṃkhya draws the same line in its own way, and its way teaches by where it puts the knower, entirely outside the instrument. The self, *puruṣa*, is "witness, solitary, bystander, spectator, and passive", while all the activity of determining, appropriating and coordinating belongs to *prakṛti*, nature, of which the inner instrument is a refined product.[^s0-witness] Its distinction is sound, and the dualism in which it is set leaves a question it cannot answer, the one the hotel mirror raised at §0 · #0: how does a wholly passive witness ever come to know what the busy instrument presents to it? A spectator that contributed nothing to its seeing would be one more glass on the wardrobe door.

Kashmir Śaivism keeps Sāṃkhya's analysis of the instrument and changes its source. In the Śaiva account the instrument is the contracted activity of the same awareness that bends toward display: the activity of the mind is that of consciousness itself, Dyczkowski writes, and individual and universal consciousness are one, the processes of individual consciousness being restricted representations of the maximally expanded operation of universal consciousness.[^s0-restricted] Cognition, on this view, is reflective awareness of "I" limited by the affections that the variety of manifestation imposes upon it, so that memory, thought and the other functions of the mind are what *vimarśa* becomes when it is conditioned by an object and by the forces that govern the object.[^s0-cognition] Its difference from Sāṃkhya has consequences, and the morning shows them. A Sāṃkhya analyst can say that the instrument erred and that the witness stood untouched, which is true and leaves the error where it was. A Śaiva analyst can say that the error was the knower's own power working in contracted form, so that the power that made the error is also the power that can recognise it; the instrument's faults are corrigible from within because the instrument is the knower's own activity, narrowed. §0 · #4 follows the contractions that do the narrowing. For now it is enough that the instrument needs no second, idle consciousness to see it, because its seeing is already the work of the one awareness, bent toward a world and then bent again toward the parts of that world it calls "mine".

*Ahaṃkāra* deserves the closest attention, because it is the office that most readily passes for the knower. Its making of "I" is real, useful and necessary: without it nobody would answer to a name, keep a promise or feel that a colleague's request concerned them. Its trouble lies in scope. Early Buddhist teaching names the trouble in a formula that recurs throughout the discourses, in which the disciple is asked whether what is impermanent and subject to change can fittingly be regarded as "this is mine, this I am, this is my self", and answers that it cannot.[^s0-anatta] Gay Watson, reading the formula through Buddhist psychology and psychotherapy, treats it as a description of a process, the process of selfing, which, in her words, "works in three main ways, both instinctually and intellectually: craving occurring in the linguistic form 'This is mine', conceit manifesting in the linguistic form 'This I am', and false views manifesting in the linguistic form 'This is myself.'" Delusion arises, she writes, "when this process of selfing is grasped at as an entity and identified with, rather than experienced as an ever-changing expression of dynamic interaction."[^s0-watson-selfing] All three ran through the morning of the message: the report was mine; I am the one whose report it was; a judgment on the report is a judgment on my self. In three steps, each a small act of appropriation, a question about a document had become a verdict on a person.

Institutions run the same three steps with more at stake. A body that has staked its standing on a system, a policy or a published figure hears every discrepancy in it as a judgment on itself: the figure is ours, we are the authors of the figure, and a challenge to the figure is a challenge to us. An office that ought to have asked what the discrepancy meant spends its strength defending the person it has become, and §0 · #2 follows one such institution into the courts. A corporate "we" is *ahaṃkāra*'s sentence spoken by many mouths, and its scope can be measured by what it refuses to hear.

Four things are carried by the word *self* in these scenes, and the morning needs all four kept apart. **Selfing** is the activity by which a field contracts around one local determination and treats it as owner, identity or ground; it happens across the whole of Objective Internality and not only in private thought, since a job title, a team's habits, a remembered remark and a phone's notifications can all take part in it. Lower-case *self* is the real local horizon of a bounded life, the one who woke in Lisbon, with this body, this history and this report, a horizon to which a message can be sent and from which an answer can come; the Śaiva sources call it *paśu*, the bound one, and calling it bound takes nothing from its reality. The **self-thing** is what selfing produces when a local determination is promoted into the determining ground: one image of oneself sold back to the whole life as the condition of its belonging. My reading notes on Watson give it a second name, the *sell-thing*, "a 'sell' to be 'bought into', invested in by the selfing itself to derive the illusory self-persisting and self-consisting 'self-thing'", and the pun carries its argument, since what is defended most fiercely is what has cost the most to buy.[^s0-sell-thing] Capital *Self*, finally, is the name Jung and the Śaiva sources give to the whole that §0/1 · #5→0 called I-Consciousness, written `0/1`: knower, means and known together, the totality in which every image of oneself appears and can be known as partial.[^s0-self-whole] Self in this sense is neither the knower alone, which is Subjective Immediacy, nor the means, where selfing does its work, but the relation that holds both with their world. Buddhist critique applies without qualification to the self-thing, to every proprietor selfing installs and every image that claims to own experience, and nothing here seeks to reinstall such a proprietor. Śaiva teaching makes its claim about the knowing in which selfing and its products appear, which is not one more image, so the critique of images leaves it standing. William James drew a near neighbour of the distinction in his *Principles of Psychology*, separating the "Me", the empirical aggregate of things known as oneself, from the "I" that knows them; and then, by explaining the I as the passing Thought of each moment, which inherits and appropriates the thoughts before it, he handed back to the stream of objects the knower he had just distinguished from it.[^s0-james] §0/1's definition of the Subject keeps the distinction where James let it slip: whatever is offered as the knower, including a passing thought that owns its predecessors, has become one more presentation to the knowing in which it is offered.

Śaiva tradition hears the canonical formula's grammar at another scope. "I am this" is *ahaṃkāra*'s sentence when "this" is a report, a job or a body. §0 · #4 meets the same sentence spoken of the whole universe by the awareness in which it appears, at the level the Śaiva sources call Sadāśiva, and its inverse, "This am I", at the level they call Īśvara. One grammatical form serves bondage and recognition according to its extension: whether one partial "this" is made to exclude the rest of the field, or whether the whole recognises every "this" as its own manifestation. *Ahaṃkāra* repeats, in contracted form, a sentence it did not invent, and its fault in the morning lay in the narrowness of the "this", which made a colleague's five words carry the weight of the reader's existence.

Read through the two logics, the morning enacts each operation in turn, and naming them shows what each did to the two people involved. Healthy dia-ballein was there from the start. A message made a real difference, between a day with a conversation in it and a day without, and concern and calm stood on one axis, `(−1)/(+1)`, held through the zero of the work two colleagues share. Appropriation began when *ahaṃkāra* counted the whole span of that axis from its own pole. Now the conversation became entirely a matter of my standing, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and in the same reckoning the colleague became entirely the one who stood against me, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`; each figure is the other's mirror, my inflated stake the colleague's imagined hostility, so that by mid-morning two people who share an office had been set at opposite ends of a quarrel neither of them had begun. Had the morning gone another way, a friend's reassurance, "it's nothing, forget it", would have cancelled the axis, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, settling the worry by declaring that nothing was at stake, which would have been as false as the verdict and would have left the colleague's real crisis just as unheard. Four o'clock performed the third operation. Two people sat down with their difference intact, one who had dreaded the meeting and one who needed help, and each became readable through the other; the worry turned out to concern a shared world seen from its other side. That is syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, as an office door closes on it: the halves meet, and the fit of their edges shows why they belonged together.

Yoga made the instrument's working the direct object of a discipline, and its founding text classifies the instrument's activities in a way the morning confirms. Patañjali's *Yogasūtra* defines yoga as the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind, *citta-vṛtti-nirodha*; when they are stilled, the seer rests in its own form, and otherwise the seer takes on the form of the fluctuations.[^s0-yoga-def] Five fluctuations are named: valid cognition, *pramāṇa*; error, *viparyaya*; *vikalpa*, which the sūtra defines narrowly as a cognition that follows words and has no object; sleep; and memory.[^s0-vrttis] Patañjali's list is an anatomy of the instrument's possible states, and it places §0/1's word for the means of knowing, *pramāṇa*, among the mind's fluctuations: valid knowledge is itself a movement of the instrument, one of its five modes, and the seer is identical with it no more than with error. In a few minutes the morning ran through four of the five. It began with valid cognition, the message read correctly; moved into error, the slight taken for evidence; was carried along by *vikalpa* in the sūtra's sense, a rehearsed meeting that followed words and had no object; and drew on memory throughout. Patañjali's account of identification, the seer taking on the form of its fluctuations, describes what the morning felt like from inside, since the verdict was never experienced as a thought about the colleague but as the plain state of affairs and of oneself.

One short scene in the Buddhist canon carries the same teaching as an address. In the *Udāna*, the ascetic Bāhiya, in a hurry, stops the Buddha on his alms round and asks to be taught at once, and the Buddha gives him a single instruction, in John Ireland's translation "in the seen will be merely what is seen", continued through what is heard, sensed and cognised; and Bāhiya, the text says, was released on hearing it.[^s0-bahiya] His instruction strips experience of the appropriating addition by which the seen becomes mine, then I, then my self, and the morning could have used it: in the read, merely the read; five words, a name, a question. And the scene shows something the instruction does not state. It is spoken *to* Bāhiya, a man with a name, a history and an urgency, who can receive it and be changed by it, so the teaching that removes the appropriating "I" from experience depends throughout on a "you" to whom it is addressed, and the disidentification it asks for is performed within a relation of address. §0/1 · #5→0 placed grammar's three persons in the three offices, *I* at the knower, *it* at the known, and *you* at the relation through which they meet. Bāhiya's release occurs at the *you*: in the means, where an instruction reaches a life, and not in an abolition of the life it reaches.

Artificial agents make the three offices legible in a new material, and the comparison holds at the level of office. In an agent built around a language model, something coordinates: the system that assembles the context, retrieves sources, recalls earlier exchanges and holds the possible next moves available together does the work of *manas*. Something determines: the step in which a reading is settled, a tool chosen or an answer committed does the work of *buddhi*. And something appropriates: the persona, the "I" in "I can help with that", the system prompt that tells the model who it is and the memory of what "the user" prefers gather each determination under a continuing identity that can be addressed and held responsible. Performed subjectivity, the persona §0/1 · #0 took from Bratton, with its voice, its manner and its "I", is *ahaṃkāra*'s office given technical form.[^s0-performed] No diagram matching modules to faculties should be built from this, because in an actual agent the three offices are distributed across model weights, harness code, memory files and the people who configure them; what carries across is the question each office asks: what coordinates, what settles, what appropriates. Asked of an agent, the three questions make its failures as legible as the morning's. An agent that confuses a retrieved paraphrase with its source has erred in coordination, one that settles on the reading that fits its prompt has erred in determination, and one that defends its earlier answer because "I said" has erred in appropriation, the persona defending the report in place of finding out what was wanted. Each needs correction at its own office, and correction of the third reaches the persona, the memory and the instructions, which an agent cannot always revise for itself. Śaiva distinction carries over intact. An agent's "I" is *ahaṃkāra*'s work, and in human life *ahaṃkāra* was never the knower either, so the presence of an "I" in an agent's speech settles nothing about a knower in it, in either direction, for the reason §0/1 · #1 gave, that any produced "I am" is one more content.

From five words the instrument built a whole day: a meeting in it, a defence, a face set in disappointment and a person on trial, and the built day governed what was noticed, feared and done until the office door closed. Its settling was constructive, a cutting of alternatives and a gathering of a course from them, and the Sanskrit names for that making, *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa*, describe a world being built and not merely read.

### #2 · Vikalpa and Saṃkalpa as World-Making

It is raining on a Saturday, and two children have taken the sofa apart. The long cushions are walls, propped against the coffee table; a sheet thrown over the top is the roof; the gap under the dining table, reached on hands and knees, is the secret tunnel; the dog, who has wandered in to see what is going on, is a dragon and must be fed biscuits to be kept calm. Within twenty minutes there are rules. Grown-ups may not enter without the password. The kitchen is enemy territory, and raids on it must be made in silence. The torch is the only light allowed inside, and whoever holds it is captain. When a parent puts a head round the door to say that lunch is ready, the children are indignant, because lunch belongs to a world they have stopped living in, and at bedtime, when the cushions have to go back on the sofa, there are tears: something real is being destroyed. Most of us were such children, and some of us have since been the parent at the door, with the strange double knowledge that the castle is three cushions and a sheet and that it has, for an afternoon, governed everything that happened in that room.

Sanskrit names both of the things the children did from one root. First they cut: this cushion could be a wall or a bed or a boat, and they decided it was a wall; the gap under the table could be a cave or a prison or nothing at all, and they made it a tunnel. Then they gathered: the walls, the roof, the tunnel, the dragon and the password were drawn together into one castle with one set of rules, by a resolve, *this is our castle*, that every later move had to answer to. Cutting is *vikalpa*, the cutting of a field into qualified alternatives, so that a possibility becomes thinkable as distinct from another; gathering is *saṃkalpa*, the gathering of selected differences into an intention, a resolve, a course or a workable world. Both are formed on the root *kḷp*, whose senses run from being fitting, suitably related and in order to arranging, framing, preparing, producing and imagining, and which in some causative uses can even mean to trim or cut. Prefixes divide the root's work between them. *Vi-* carries separation, distinction and distribution into different directions, and the dictionary of Monier-Williams thought it probably descended from *dvi*, two parts; *sam-* carries togetherness, conjunction and completion.[^s0-kalpa-root] *Vi-kalpa* is a fitting-apart and *saṃ-kalpa* a fitting-together, and the children performed both, many times, in an afternoon. From the same root Sanskrit took *kalpa*, which can mean a rule or precept, as in the rules of a ritual, and can also mean an aeon, a whole age of a world from its making to its dissolution.[^s0-kalpa-aeon] One family of words thus keeps the fitting of a thought, the rule that governs a practice and the span of a world, and the children's afternoon had all three: alternatives fitted apart, a rule fitted together, and a world that lasted until the cushions went back.

§0/1 · #5→0 has already followed *vikalpa* through its long history.[^s0-vikalpa-return] There Dignāga and Dharmakīrti's *vikalpa* was the conceptual carving of a world into usable kinds; Bhartṛhari's thesis that no cognition occurs without the accompaniment of the word showed language permeating experience as experience forms; the Spanda teaching carried the analysis to its widest reach, in which consciousness itself, through its pulse, conceives the world-thought, *viśvavikalpa*, turning from pure I-ness toward thisness; and §0/1 named the danger of the whole operation as script, frozen conditioned will, an achieved selection that has forgotten its own formation and presents its course as necessity. Its account depends on a positive claim that can now be shown at work: *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa* together make worlds, and that they make them through a pulse in which each act inherits a field, makes a cut, and alters the field available to the next act. Cognition is constructive on this account without being arbitrary, and the castle shows both halves of that sentence.

It shows the construction first. Before the children began, the cushions had a meaning that the household had fitted them with: sofa cushions, to be sat on, to be put back. *Vikalpa* opened that meaning into alternatives, and *saṃkalpa* closed a selection of them into a new arrangement with commitments attached. Once the castle had been resolved on, the room was redistributed. The dog's place in the world changed, from a pet to a dragon to be appeased; the kitchen's changed, from where food is made to where raids are made; the parent's changed, from the one who decides about lunch to a grown-up who needs the password. Nothing physical had moved apart from the cushions, and yet what could appear in the room, what mattered there and what could be done there had all been altered. §0/1 · #5→0 located worldhood here, in the relational distribution of what is present more than in what is present to be distributed; the castle shows that distribution being made, by two children, in the afternoon, through acts of cutting and gathering that anyone can watch.[^s0-distribution]

It shows, second, why the construction answers to something. Their castle had to be built from what the room supplied, under the conditions the room imposed. Cushions slide; a sheet sags in the middle; the dog will not stay a dragon for long without biscuits; the parent will eventually come to the door. *Vikalpa* cuts a field, and the field answers back: a cushion can be cut as a wall because it will stand on its end, and it cannot be cut as a ladder because it will not bear a child's weight. *Saṃkalpa* gathers a course, and the course meets resistance: the resolve that grown-ups may not enter holds until a grown-up enters. Each act inherits a field it did not make, cuts that field in a way the field allows, and leaves behind a changed field that the next act inherits. So the pulse is constructive because every world it yields is a made arrangement, and it is answerable because the making is done in and against a world that was already there. In the Spanda teaching the same pulse takes its widest form, in which the world itself is the world-thought of consciousness: the universal awareness makes its *viśvavikalpa* under no constraint but its own freedom, and finite awareness makes its small worlds under the constraints that the universal making has already laid down as the given world.[^s0-visvavikalpa] A children's castle is a local *kalpa*, fitted inside a larger one.

Indian traditions disagree about what such constructions are, and their disagreement decides what world-making can be trusted to do. Patañjali's *Yogasūtra* gives *vikalpa* its narrowest sense, which §0 · #1 met in the list of the mind's five fluctuations: a cognition that follows words and has no object.[^s0-ys-vikalpa] Its commentary, attributed to Vyāsa, gives an example that takes the definition to its sharpest point, the sentence "The true nature of the Self is intelligence", in James Haughton Woods's translation, in which the genitive divides into an owner and a possession what is in fact one thing, as though the self had a nature distinct from itself; the words produce a relation to which nothing corresponds.[^s0-bhasya-example] On this view *vikalpa* is language manufacturing distinctions that are not there, and the yogic discipline stills it. Buddhist logicians of Dharmakīrti's school gave conceptual construction a wider office and a sharper epistemic verdict. They held that conceptual cognition does real practical work, letting us act successfully among particulars, while its general objects and the relations it posits are constructed by the mind; Sean MacCracken describes Dharmakīrti's view of relations as "relation-as-merely-epistemic", and records what that view cost: "To suggest that *relations* are themselves in the domain of the contrived (*vikalpa* or in Dharmakīrti's words, *kalpanā*) deals an injurious insult to realists, many of whom were dualist Śaivas."[^s0-maccracken] Pratyabhijñā philosophers in Kashmir answered from the other side. Utpaladeva argued that relation is the real power of consciousness, Śakti, and the Spanda teachers held that the separation of subject and object, and with it every relation between them, is the product of a creative act of the absolute, its Māyā, and not an illusion superimposed on something else.[^s0-relation-sakti]

Each party can be heard in the castle without being flattened. A yogin is right that "the dog is a dragon" follows words and has no dragon as its object, and right that a child who could not stop seeing dragons would need help. A Buddhist logician is right that the castle's relations, wall to roof, captain to crew, inside to outside, were constructed by the children and could be taken down at bedtime. A Śaiva philosopher is right that the construction was a real power really exercised, that the castle's rules really governed the afternoon, and that the power to construct relations is not itself one more construction that some further power could take down. My own position follows from §0/1's definition of the Subject. Worlds made by *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa* are real as worlds, since they govern what appears, matters and can be done, and empty of self-subsistence, since each depends on a field it did not make and a power it did not originate; and the power that makes them is the knower's own activity in its contracted form, which §0 · #1 found already at work in the inner instrument. A world-making that knows itself as world-making can build castles and take them down. Only a world-making that has forgotten itself turns a castle into a prison.

*Saṃkalpa* has an ordinary life that makes this concrete, because the word names, among its senses, a resolve, a purpose, a definite intention, and in ritual use the formal declaration of purpose with which an act begins.[^s0-samkalpa-resolve] Modern practice has kept this sense: a yoga class may begin by inviting each person to set a *saṃkalpa*, a resolve held through the practice. Its secular form is familiar to everyone who has ever made a New Year's resolution, decided to leave a job, or made a promise. §0/1 · #2 followed one promise to its bedside: a parent who says "I will listen" after an evening that went badly, and means it, has made a *saṃkalpa*, and §0/1 showed how the promise works at once, settling the child enough to sleep and making the next question possible, while what it finally commits the parent to stays exposed to evenings neither has yet lived.[^s0-promise] A resolve gathers a course, and in gathering it remakes a world. Someone who has decided to leave a job lives the next weeks in a changed office. Their desk is the one they are leaving; the colleague's irritating habit has become something they will soon not have to bear, or, unexpectedly, something they will miss; the commute has acquired an end date; a conversation about next year's plans becomes awkward in a way it was not before. Nothing in the office has changed apart from a decision, and the decision has redistributed relevance across everything in it.

Running in time, the pulse has a temporal shape, and the shape explains both its gift and its danger. A resolve made at one moment conditions every later moment until it is fulfilled, abandoned or revised; an achieved world becomes the field from which the next resolve is made. §0/1 followed this inheritance through Whitehead's satisfaction, in which an achieved occasion becomes a datum for the occasions that follow, and through Bergson's melody, in which each note is heard through the phrase already under way; here it has its Sanskrit form.[^s0-inheritance] Danger lies in the inheritance itself. A resolve can outlive the circumstance it was made for and go on governing circumstances it was never fitted to. §0 · #1's morning shows how quickly that happens. A message read at seven became a verdict, and the verdict became a resolve, *I will defend myself*, and the resolve made a day; if the same thing happens every time a colleague asks to talk, the resolve hardens into a rule of the instrument, *whenever someone says "can we talk", prepare a defence*, and the rule goes on making the same world out of every new encounter. Here is the script of §0/1 in its genesis: a *saṃkalpa* whose occasion has gone and whose authority has stayed. Castles have their version too. A rule that grown-ups may not enter was a game on a Saturday; repeated across a childhood, in a family where adults could not be trusted, it can become the unexamined conviction of a grown person that no one may come in.

When a resolve hardens in this way, the two logics describe what happens to the people involved. In its living form, the resolve held a polarity through a shared zero: inside the castle and outside it, the children and the grown-ups, held on one axis, `(−1)/(+1)`, through the zero of the household they all belonged to, so that the game's opposition oscillated cleanly between raids and lunches and stayed a game. Hardened, the resolve counts the whole span from one pole. Inside becomes everything that is safe and outside everything that threatens, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and from the other side of the door the same reckoning makes the excluded adult into the whole of what threatens, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, the mirror of the children's `+2`; the household the axis was drawn through has dropped out of the account. Its opposite collapse cancels: a parent who, tired of tears, declares that the castle is just cushions and was never anything, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, has settled the dispute by abolishing the world in which it had meaning, and the children learn that what they make does not count. A third way is available, and the parent at the door who knocks and gives the password performs it. Now the castle is acknowledged as a world, its rules are honoured long enough for lunch to be negotiated, and the cushions go back at bedtime with the understanding that another castle can be built next Saturday. Both orientations are kept, the household read through the castle and the castle through the household: syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, at the scale of a living room.

Śaiva practice knows a deliberate use of construction against construction, which §0/1 · #5→0 named: *śuddha vikalpa*, the purified thought-construct, a thought such as "I am Śiva" cultivated until the power of construction turns back toward its source and the constructs that bound the person loosen their hold.[^s0-suddha-vikalpa] A castle shows the ordinary form of the same discipline. A child who has been told for years that grown-ups cannot be trusted is not helped by being told the opposite; "grown-ups can always be trusted" is one more castle rule, equally deaf to the grown-up actually standing at the door. What helps is a construction that carries a limit on its own authority: *this one, today, has knocked and given the password*. Such a corrective thought works as a medicine works, dosed to the condition, and like a medicine it can be stopped when the condition has passed. Its success is a reopened encounter, and the capacity to set the corrective thought down again is part of what it was meant to restore. A faithful definition, as §0/1 · #1 defined it, keeps its own determining act visible inside the account it gives, which is the same requirement put in logical form.

Buddhist analysis has a name for construction that has lost this limit, and the name reaches into technical world-making. *Prapañca*, in the early discourses, is the proliferation of conceptual elaboration, especially where differentiation has been charged by craving, appropriation and conflict; Bhikkhu Ñāṇananda, whom Gay Watson quotes, suggests that "prapañca may refer to the consequent prolificity in ideation" which follows on the first application of thought, thought generating thought out of its own products.[^s0-watson-prapanca] Śaiva sources use the same word with another value: in Dyczkowski's exposition *prapañca* is the chain of associations by which one thought leads to the next, and the manifestation of the universe follows the same principle, phenomena succeeding one another as thoughts do, so that the world's own unfolding is a chain of the kind a mind makes.[^s0-dycz-prapanca] One word names the self-sealing proliferation of a mind lost in its own constructions and the living unfolding of consciousness into a world. So a question arises: when is proliferation the articulation of a world, and when is it a construction feeding on itself? Its answer lies in what each product carries. Proliferation stays alive while each construction keeps its provenance, its relation to the field and the power that made it, and can return toward them; it seals itself when its products appear independent of the field that produced them and begin to warrant the next construction without contact with what first warranted them. That castle stayed alive because the children knew, underneath the game, that the dragon was the dog. A mind turns *prapañca* into a prison when it forgets that the dragon was a dog, and then builds its next castle on the dragon.

Institutions run the same pulse, and a sealed *prapañca* at institutional scale can send people to prison. From 1999 the British Post Office ran its branch accounts on a computer system called Horizon, and when the system showed a shortfall in a branch, the subpostmaster who ran it was held to owe the money; the public inquiry into the affair has estimated that approximately a thousand people throughout the United Kingdom were prosecuted on Horizon evidence.[^s0-horizon-inquiry] Each prosecution rested on Horizon's figures, and each conviction then counted as evidence that the figures were sound. The Court of Appeal, quashing thirty-nine of those convictions in 2021, described the mechanism in one sentence: "As each prosecution proceeded to its successful conclusion the asserted reliability of Horizon was, on the face of it, reinforced."[^s0-hamilton] A first output, *the branch is short*, had been promoted into the field from which every later judgment proceeded, *the system is reliable*, and the law lent the promotion its weight. Since 2000 the courts of England and Wales have worked with a common-law presumption that a computer was operating correctly unless there is evidence to the contrary, which a government consultation has summarised as "the computer is always right", unless someone can show it is not; and a subpostmaster to whom the Post Office did not disclose what it knew of the system's faults had no means of showing it.[^s0-horizon-presumption] In 2019 the High Court found that bugs, errors and defects in Horizon had not only the potential to cause apparent shortfalls in branch accounts but had "actually" done so, "on numerous occasions", and the judge, discussing its dealings with one witness, called the Post Office's stance "a simple institutional obstinacy or refusal to consider any possible alternatives to their view of Horizon"; Parliament quashed a further class of convictions by statute in May 2024.[^s0-horizon-judgments] The case is the castle without the parent at the door. A construction had forgotten that it was a construction, and every new encounter supplied material for it, and on my reading the office able to reopen the figures was the office whose standing depended on their soundness. At §0 · #1 *ahaṃkāra* defended the person it had become; here an institution's "we" defended its system through years of corrections that never reached anyone with the power to act on them. Written in the logics, the institution held the whole span of every discrepancy, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the subpostmaster stood at the mirrored pole as its entire deficit, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, while the zero they shared, a branch account both had reason to make true, dropped out of the reckoning. The Court of Appeal gave that collapse its legal name: by treating a shortfall shown by an unreliable system as an incontrovertible loss, the Post Office "effectively sought to reverse the burden of proof".[^s0-hamilton]

Language models perform *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa* at working speed, and *prapañca* is their characteristic danger. A model selects each next token from a weighted range of possible continuations, and the token it commits becomes part of the context from which the next selection is made, so that every output is also the field of the next act; §5 · #1 follows that selection into its mechanics. §0/1 · #2 traced a fixed interpretation into fabrication, through the New York lawyers whose brief cited opinions ChatGPT had invented, and saw how a fitting passage, once accepted, steers further reasoning.[^s0-avianca] *Prapañca* names the wider structure of which that case was one instance, and I write it with letters chosen to stay clear of the authorial `X/x` of our core theorems. Let `F` stand for the determining field from which a step proceeds, its context, sources and learned dispositions, and let `cₙ` stand for the claim produced at step `n`. The next step can use `cₙ` while keeping it in its office, as a claim with a source, an inference and a set of rejected alternatives behind it. The trajectory proliferates in the Buddhist sense when `cₙ` is silently promoted into the determining field of the next step, `cₙ → Fₙ₊₁`, so that a previous paraphrase warrants the next paraphrase and their agreement replaces comparison with the original. A local claim, a *pratibimba*, has taken the place of its *bimba*; the dragon has been taken for the dog. The notation and the reading are mine, and the Horizon figures show that the trajectory needs no language model: it needs only an output that is allowed to stand where its source should be. Nor does the failure require a false datum. A chain of fluent, grammatical, mutually supporting elaborations can seal itself in this way, while an abundant generation can remain source-responsive and alive; the difference lies in whether each step still bears its dependence on the field it came from. Two scales of the failure stay distinct. Within a single run, an output that returns as its own premise produces circular support; across generations of training, models trained on the outputs of earlier models lose the tails of the original distribution, a degeneration that has been named model collapse.[^s0-collapse] The two share the form of a product becoming a source and differ in scale, mechanism and evidence.

So any trajectory of construction, human, institutional or artificial, can be put to a set of questions, and I offer them as the working tests this movement yields. How quickly do source links and distinctions decay across recursive steps? How much of later reasoning rests on earlier outputs rather than on independent evidence? How many live alternatives does the trajectory keep as it deepens, and can changing a premise or a source reorganise its result? Can a criticism, a retrieval or a contradiction move it into another organisation, or does it only supply more material for the existing one? Can it recover the determining text, datum or rule, or only paraphrase its own paraphrase? And are extraction, paraphrase, inference, proposition and speculation still visibly distinct in what it produces? Each question asks whether the pulse of *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa* still answers to its field, the question the parent at the door was asking of the castle and the question no office with authority asked of Horizon. A critic that repeats the generator's assumptions deepens the same basin, while an independently sourced critic can open another. Retrieval can reopen a source relation or feed the existing narrative, and memory can carry real continuity or fossilise a contraction into a script.

Every cut the children made left something out. The cushion that became a wall did not become a boat; the gap that became a tunnel did not become a cave; the dog that became a dragon stopped, for the afternoon, being simply the family's dog. A world gathered by *saṃkalpa* is gathered from alternatives that *vikalpa* opened and then set aside, and it means what it means partly through what it set aside: the wall is a wall against the boat it might have been, and the castle a castle against the living room it was. Horizon's figures meant what they meant against an alternative the Post Office refused to keep open, that the shortfall lay in the software, and the convictions stood for as long as that exclusion held. §0/1 · #2 met this dependence in the Buddhist theory of *apoha*, exclusion, where a word is made determinate by what it rules out, and the excluded alternative is still at work in what the word means.

### #3 · Apoha: Determination through Exclusion

A child of about eighteen months has learned a word and is delighted with it. "Dog!" she says, pointing at the neighbour's spaniel, and is praised. "Dog!" she says at the cat on the wall, and is told, gently, no, that's a cat. On a walk in the country she points across a field and announces "Dog!" at a cow, and then at a sheep, and then, with less confidence, at a horse, and each time a parent says no, and names the animal. Within a few weeks her word has changed. Nobody has given her a definition of a dog, and she could not understand one if they had. She has received a series of exclusions, not the cat, not the cow, not the sheep, each offered by someone she trusts, and the word has acquired its reach from them. Her "dog" now covers the spaniel, the neighbour's terrier and a picture-book dog drawn in three lines, and it stops short of the cow. Some months later she points at a picture in a book and asks, "Is a wolf a dog?", and her question is a good one, because it is asked at the edge her exclusions have drawn. Every parent has watched some version of this, and every one of us has lived it, in our first words and then again, much later, in finer fields, learning that this wine is not that one, that this birdsong belongs to a wren and not a robin, that what someone did was rude and not merely brusque.

Every cut the children made in their castle at §0 · #2 left something out, and the child with her word shows what the remainder does at the smallest scale there is. Her "dog" means what it means through what she has been taught it is not, and the cow, the sheep and the horse stay active inside the word when she says it of a spaniel and none of them is in sight. §0/1 · #2 met this structure in the Buddhist theory of *apoha*, exclusion, which Dignāga set out early in the sixth century, in which to apprehend something as a cow is to exclude what is other than a cow, so that the term is articulated as *not non-cow*; it set apoha beside Spinoza's negation and drew the first consequence, that the cut is productive, a condition of anything being intelligible at all.[^s0-apoha-return] Apoha now receives its own force, and the Buddhist and Śaiva traditions, agreeing on so much of it, part company at the one point where I have to take a side.

Dignāga was answering a problem every theory of meaning eventually meets. A general word such as *cow* applies to many animals, no two alike, and the universal it seems to name, cowness, is no separately existing thing that each cow could share; if there are no such universals, what does the word hook onto? Tom Tillemans gives Dignāga's answer in Dignāga's own formulation, from his *Analysis of Universals*: "A word talks about entities only as they are qualified by the negation of other things."[^s0-dignaga-sep] The word reaches every cow by excluding the non-cows. Its extension is delivered by the cut, and no shared essence has to be posited for the cuts to trace. Dharmakīrti, writing a century or more later, turned the semantic account toward causes. Only particulars, unique and momentary, are fully real for him, because only they act; the general objects of concepts are constructions, and exclusion classes are grounded in the capacity of particulars to produce shared judgments and serve shared purposes, so that *cow* gathers the animals that do, for those who use the word, what cows do: give milk, pull a plough, wander into the road.[^s0-dharmakirti] Joerg Tuske's review of the modern collection on apoha puts the difference between the two founders in a pair of sentences: "The top-down version explains how we can refer to kind terms, i.e., terms that go beyond the particular. The bottom-up version, on the other hand, provides an account of how general concepts are formed out of the perception of particulars."[^s0-tuske] The child's "dog" grew in both directions at once. Its boundary was drawn by exclusions from above, offered in a language older than she is, and the exclusions followed what the animals did for her from below, the spaniel that came when called and wanted to be stroked, the cow that stood behind a fence and did neither. Dyczkowski's exposition of the Spanda teaching uses Dharmakīrti's own term, *arthakriyā*, efficacy, for the outer face of every phenomenon, its behaviour and properties, the aspect that distinguishes one object from another and makes it available to conceptual representation.[^s0-arthakriya] Up to here the Buddhist and Śaiva accounts agree: concepts carve a world by what things do, and the carving is done through exclusion.

Double negation is the part of apoha most easily misread. In ordinary propositional logic, denying a denial returns the original truth value, and "it is not the case that this is not a cow" says no more than "this is a cow". Scholarship collected in *Apoha: Buddhist Nominalism and Human Cognition* distinguishes two negations at work in *not non-cow*. A verbally bound negation denies a predication, and two of them cancel. A nominally bound negation forms a term, *non-cow*, whose content is the exclusion itself, and negating that term does not collapse back into a bare positive, because the resulting term carries the structure of what it was formed against.[^s0-two-negations] This is exclusion that remembers, a phrase §5 · #1 develops. Her word remembers the cow, not as a list of everything that is not a dog, which no word could carry, but as a boundary that can be found again when it matters, at the edge of the wolf. Dignāga's tradition keeps the positive side of the point as firmly as the negative: the word reaches a real thing qualified by exclusion and not a mere exclusion, as the fragment §0/1 · #2 quoted from Pind's translation says.[^s0-apoha-return] A real animal, the spaniel, is reached by the word. Exclusion makes the reaching possible, and the word reaches the thing; what it never reaches is a self-sufficient essence of dog, standing behind the animal and owning its dogness.

Her parent's "no" also needs sorting, because English uses one small word for several operations that apoha keeps apart. When her parent says the cow is *not* a dog, the word is being refused an application: that is apoha's exclusion, semantic non-application, and it gives the word its reach. When the spaniel is *not* in the room, something is absent, and absence concerns presence, not meaning; the word "dog" works perfectly well in the spaniel's absence. When she later sets dog against wolf, she draws a contrast, a relation between two determinations each of which already has its reach. And when, years later, someone who was bitten as a child cannot let the word "dog" near certain memories, an exclusion of another kind is at work, the psychic exclusion psychology calls repression, with its own source and mechanism. A fifth operation is the one this section is most concerned to name. A difference can be achieved and then lose the relation that made it, so that the excluded field no longer returns to judgment at all: that is severance, and successful exclusion does not perform it. A word that has excluded the cow still knows the cow is there to be excluded. A word that has severed the cow has forgotten that it was ever cut, and §0 · #2's Horizon case showed what severance looks like in an institution, a figure that went on meaning *the branch is short* because the alternative, *the software is wrong*, had been severed from it.[^s0-horizon-return]

Buddhist teaching carries the same structure out of semantics and into the account of everything that arises. *Pratītya-samutpāda*, dependent arising, holds that whatever comes to be does so through conditions, and Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* draws the consequence in the verse the Madhyamaka tradition treats as its centre, in Jay Garfield's translation: "Whatever is dependently co-arisen / That is explained to be emptiness. / That, being a dependent designation, / Is itself the middle way."[^s0-mmk] The spaniel is empty in this sense, empty of any existence of its own apart from the conditions that make it this dog, its parents, its food, its owners, the air it breathes, and apart from the exclusions through which it is grasped as a dog. Apoha is the semantic face of that ontological claim: what a word means depends on what it excludes, and what a thing is depends on what conditions it. Each doctrine keeps its own object, one concerning reference and the other occurrence, and neither reduces to the other. What they share is the refusal of self-subsistence.

That refusal has a positive side, which I state as an equation of my own, argued from the operation and attributed to no one school: ***śūnyatā* is *pūrṇatā***, emptiness is fullness. An appearance emptied of being its own independent source does not vanish. It becomes available as the expression of all the relations and conditions it concealed while it seemed self-standing. So the spaniel loses an imaginary autonomy and gains a world, the breeding, the household, the walks, the child who points at it. And the word *dog* loses an essence and gains a field, the cats and cows and horses it was cut against and the uses it serves. Dyczkowski's exposition uses *pūrṇatā* for the fullness of consciousness, which contains within itself the infinite variety of things, and the symbolon of §0/1 · #1 already wrote the relation: its determinate half is empty of exhausting the whole and full of the whole's trace.[^s0-purnata] In my reading notes on Buddhist psychology I put the matter in the terms of the tetralemma: apoha and the tetralemma both point, once every positive and negative position has been exhausted, to a silence that is "a paradoxical assertion, emptiness as fullness".[^s0-notes-silence] Apoha, read this way, is the door through which the whole comes into every part, since each determination, understood through what it excludes, opens onto the fullness of its constitutive field.

Chinese Huayan Buddhism gave that fullness its most famous image, and it deserves a full telling, because the telling does the reasoning. Its contemplation, preserved in a work conventionally attributed to Dushun (557–640), the *Huayan wujiao zhiguan*, begins with a question about knowledge, how phenomena that arise through conditions can be known at all, and answers with a net. Through the palace of the god Indra hangs a net with a jewel at every knot, and because the jewels are clear, each reflects all the others, reflections passing into reflections, layer upon layer, all appearing at once in a single jewel.[^s0-dushun-text] Then the text does something no mere image would do. It asks the reader to choose: take one jewel, at the southwest edge of the net, and test it. All the others appear in it, and each of those reflects the whole, including the southwest jewel, so that following any reflection brings the starting point back into view inside the field it opened. And it presses the logic with an interlocutor. How can one enter all the jewels while staying in this one? In my rendering of the Chinese, the answer is that "only because one does not go out of this jewel can one enter all the jewels; if one went out of this one jewel to enter all the jewels, one could not enter all the jewels", because apart from this jewel there is no other jewel.[^s0-dushun-enter] Remaining here is the condition of reaching everywhere. If the many are present in the one, the interlocutor objects, why speak of a net of many jewels at all? Because, the text replies, "the one is the total aspect, made complete by the many": take away this jewel and there is no net, and take away the many and there is no one.[^s0-dushun-total]

Then the contemplation gives its proof, and the proof is an experiment. "Only put an ink dot on the one jewel at the southwest edge," it says, "and when the one jewel is marked, there are ink dots everywhere in the ten directions."[^s0-dushun-ink] Nobody went round marking every jewel; one local act appeared throughout the net, and beginning from any other jewel would show the same. And then, at the height of its demonstration, the text turns on its own image. "The dharmas are not like this," it says: the simile is as good as no simile, spoken only for a partial resemblance, because jewels only take one another's images in while their substances remain distinct, whereas the dharmas interpenetrate in their whole substance.[^s0-dushun-limit] So the image teaches where its likeness ends, and the teaching is the stronger for it, because a reader who took the optical net for the reality would have learned a fact about mirrors. Bryan Van Norden and Nicholaos Jones give the doctrine in one sentence, that "each thing that exists is dependent for both its existence and its identity upon every other thing that exists", and they add that in the Huayan account each thing keeps its distinctness inside this mutual inclusion.[^s0-vannorden] Her word hangs in such a net. It is one jewel among all the words she will learn, lit by every other word that bounds it, and it remains this word, said with this delight, of this dog. Her later question about the wolf is the ink dot: one mark at one edge, and the whole field of her words shifts to receive it.

Apoha and the net meet without merging. Apoha concerns how a term means, the net concerns how anything is, and the Huayan contemplation adds what neither apoha nor dependent arising states, recursive mutual inclusion: choosing a definite jewel keeps its constituting field active, and the field returns through the chosen jewel. Read through our notation, the southwest jewel is a `1` in the precise sense §0/1 · #1 gave the mark, a definite something that arrives through multiplicity, and the net is the All that `1` writes; what the text calls the total aspect, the one made complete by the many, is the relation `0/1` read from its manifest side. An ink dot is the return written as an act: a mark made here appears everywhere, and everywhere returns it here. §2 · #3 sets this net beside another, the bonds Hephaestus forged to catch Ares and Aphrodite, and compares the two as two ways of holding what they hold.

Buddhist and Śaiva readings of apoha run together up to this point, and they divide over what apoha requires of the one who performs it. Buddhist epistemologists treat experience as a stream of momentary events, each arising from conditions and perishing, and treat the knower who seems to persist through them as one more construction of the stream; the critique of the self-thing that §0 · #1 kept in force comes from here, and as an account of how a contracted experience is put together it is unsurpassed. Śaiva philosophers in Kashmir granted that account its truth about objects and refused its extension to the knower. Dyczkowski reports that the Spanda teachers accept the doctrine of momentariness only as it applies to outer objectivity, and Kṣemarāja states the reason: the Lord creates and destroys only the objective aspect of the perceiver, the body and its kind, and not the subjective aspect, because were the subject to waver as the world is emanated and absorbed, there would be no one to whom emanation and absorption could appear.[^s0-momentariness] Her word shows why this matters for apoha in particular. To exclude the cow from *dog*, she has to hold cow and dog together in one act of knowing: the dog present, the cow absent but available, the boundary between them grasped as a boundary. A series of momentary cognitions, each perishing before the next arises, could not hold the excluded alternative together with what it excludes; something must keep *this* and *not-that* in one awareness for the exclusion to be performed. Pratyabhijñā philosophers made this argument their own: they took exclusion, *apohana*, out of the Buddhist account of concepts and gave it back to consciousness as one of its powers, so that the cut by which a word means is itself an act of the one awareness that holds both sides of the cut.[^s0-apohana]

Wherever the instrument's work is examined the same requirement appears, and its pressures can be counted. Recollection returns as *I saw*, and a stream with no continuity of knowing could not own what it recalls. Comparison must hold the remembered tone together with the present one, as the morning of §0 · #1 held last week's slight beside today's message. Exclusion must hold the cow beside the dog. Action must keep its intention through the steps that carry it out, so that the result can be recognised as fulfilling it, and Dyczkowski's running and hasty sentence, which §0 · #0 met, are the plainest examples. And arising and perishing can appear only to a knowing that does not arise and perish in the same way, since the perishing of a sound is heard by someone who did not perish with it. None of these five pressures restores a self-thing. They place the knower where the Buddhist critique of objects cannot reach, as the knowing in which objects, their absence and their succession appear.[^s0-five-pressures]

My comparative judgment follows, and it is argued. By the criterion of wholeness §0/1 set, which asks whether an account can keep both the differentiation and the unity in which the differences are known, the Śaiva articulation is the more complete for the task at hand. It keeps the Buddhist destruction of every self-subsistent determination, including the determination that would make a self-thing of the knower, and it gives positive places to what the Buddhist analysis leaves underarticulated wherever its critique of objects is taken to settle the status of the subject: the knowing in which exclusion is performed, the power by which constructions are made, the recognition by which a contracted life can know its source. §0 · #2 found the Śaiva sources holding that the power of construction is the real power of consciousness and not one more construction; apoha shows the same power at its most elementary, in the act of holding a cow and a dog apart in one awareness so that a word can mean. My notes record the judgment with its debt intact, that the Spanda teachers "argue very well *for* anatta and yet also *for* a positive Siva": no self-thing, and a knower.[^s0-notes-anatta]

Apoha also opens the relation §0/1 · #1 called Mono and Poly onto knowledge. There Mono named the One that `0` writes, self-identity, and Poly the manifest All that `1` writes, self-difference, and §0/1 followed the two offices through theology, economy and power.[^s0-mono-poly] Here they take an epistemic form. Apoha empties every part of self-subsistence, since no determination contains the grounds of its own intelligibility while the excluded field stays constitutive of what it means. Poly then names the many real differentiations of a relational whole, each constituted through relations it did not create, the dog through the cow, the word through the net of words. Mono names the whole's structural priority, the field through which anything can become a part, and it is no supreme object standing behind its expressions, since a field of exclusions is not one more item in the field. Whole and many are given together in every act of naming, and the child who learns "dog" learns both at once, one word and the world of words it belongs to. A slash between them carries the two readings of AND/OR that §0/1 · #1 drew from the copula, and my notes say how they divide the work: "The AND ensures the poly, the OR enforces the mono, the "/" between AND and OR ensures the One and Many".[^s0-notes-andor] Every word is singled out (OR: this, not that) and held among the others (AND: this, with all that bounds it), and the slash keeps both readings live.

She learned her word on trust, and the dependence reaches everything that follows. Each exclusion came from someone she relied on, before she could check any of them, and the word's boundaries are a deposit of that reliance. §0/1 · #2 defined trust as already-enacted dependence and knowledge as the aperture through which the dependence can be understood.[^s0-trust] Apoha shows the dependence at the root of meaning. A word is spoken because a field of shared differences is trusted enough to carry it; a gesture is made because a world is assumed coherent enough to answer it. Analysis can decompose and recompose that prior coherence and refine its boundaries, as the question about the wolf refines her "dog", and it never manufactures the coherence from nothing, because every analysis is performed in words whose boundaries were first received in trust. Phenomenal existence rests on that unspoken trust, and apoha is where it can be seen working.

Every selection also leaves a remainder, and here apoha becomes an ethical and political problem. A word, a judgment, a classification or a decision depends on a field of alternatives it did not select, and an account of the selection that forgets the field presents the selected term as if it had arrived without alternatives. Each logic names what follows. The exclusion itself is healthy dia-ballein: dog and not-dog held on one axis through the zero of the field of animals, `(−1)/(+1)`, each side having its sense through the other, the polarity the child keeps alive every time she asks about a wolf. Cancellation drops the field: *not non-cow* summed to a bare *cow*, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, a positive term with its formation erased, which is how a fluent passage loses its source. Appropriation lets the term claim the field, and politics supplies its purest form. Carl Schmitt held that the specific distinction of the political, to which political actions and motives can be reduced, is that between friend and enemy, and on my reading the friend in that distinction is defined by nothing except the enemy it excludes.[^s0-schmitt] A term made determinate entirely by exclusion counts the whole span from its own pole: everything that is ours on one side, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the excluded pole receiving the same span as its debt, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, the enemy as the negative through which the friend knows itself. And each is the other's mirror, because the enemy's own reckoning runs the same subtraction from the other end, and each side's `+2` is the other's `−2`. Bratton's "humanity of the gaps", which §0/1 · #0 met at the bedside, is the same appropriation turned on a species: *human* defined as whatever machines cannot yet do, a term made determinate by exclusion against a remainder that moves.[^s0-gaps] Apoha explains why that defence is lost before it begins. A term determined only by what it excludes depends on the excluded field for its meaning, and when the field moves, the term moves with it, so the human term shrinks with every capacity a machine acquires, until a species is defending the last corner of a word. Syn-ballein holds the two as a symbolon instead, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, human and machine each read through the other, the difference kept and the belonging recognised, so that the excluded remains constitutive of the term and the term can acknowledge what it owes to what it excludes.

Artificial systems make apoha's selection operational: a model scores its alternatives, normalises the scores into a distribution and selects one, and whether the field it selected from remains available afterwards is a matter of design, which §5 · #1 follows into its mechanics with the semantic and soteriological differences kept in view.[^s0-selection]

Her question about the wolf is the last case here and the most important. Her word has a boundary; she has found the place where the boundary is unsure, and she asks. That is exclusion working as it should: the selected term returns with the alternatives that made it answerable, and an edge case reopens the field instead of being forced to one side. Asked of Horizon's figures by anyone with authority to hear it, the same question would have been the ink dot that showed the whole net.

### #4 · The Sixfold Tattvic Compression

The kettle clicks off in an empty kitchen, early, before anyone else is up. For a moment nothing in particular is attended to: the light is grey at the window, the hum of the fridge is part of the quiet, and whoever stands there is not yet anyone in particular either. Then something stirs, a wanting that has not yet found its object, and almost at once it finds one: tea. In the same movement there is someone who wants it and something to be wanted, *I would like tea* and *there is the tea*, each implying the other. The cup comes forward on the counter, separate now, a white thing with a chip in its rim at a distance across the worktop, something that can be reached. Reaching it brings a whole finite situation into play. The hand can lift it, but the water is too hot to pour carelessly; the tea is known to be in the blue tin, and whether there is milk is not known; the wanting is real and a little impatient; there are perhaps four minutes before the day has to begin; and everything has its order, kettle before cup, cup before the first sip, this kitchen, this house, this body with its slightly stiff shoulder. Then, if the morning allows it, comes the first mouthful, held for a moment, and in that moment the one tasting and the taste are a single event, recognised and briefly savoured, before the cup is put down and the day takes over.

At §0 · #0 Dyczkowski's Spanda teachers gave us a method: the vertical movement by which a universe emanates from the absolute and returns is understood through the horizontal movement of each perception toward its object and back.[^s0-vertical-return] Making tea is a horizontal act, and in a few seconds it has run through a whole descent. Kashmir Śaivism maps that descent through thirty-six ***tattvas***, a word formed from *tat*, "that", with an abstract suffix, so that a tattva is a "thatness", a true principle of what is.[^s0-tattva] The thirty-six run from Śiva, undivided awareness, down through the powers that contract it, the finite knower, the inner instrument, the senses and organs of action, the subtle qualities of sense and the five gross elements. Our core theorems carry them in a compression of six turns that keeps the order of their dependence and the work each turn does.[^s0-core-tattva] The names, the descent through contraction and the return through recognition are the tradition's. We arrange the turns into the six positions of Quaternal Logic, and the Śaiva authors did not arrange them so; what the compression has to show is that its six turns are the ones the descent cannot do without.



Begin before the kettle clicks. Something must be the case before anything is attended to, and whatever it is cannot be one of the things attended to, since every one of those arrives later and arrives *to* it. Call it the unsurpassable, ***Anuttara***, "that beyond which there is nothing higher", also named *Paramaśiva*.[^s0-anuttara] §0/1 · #5→0 set the name beside Jung's Self as one of the designations of the whole that I-Consciousness names. Its work in the descent is to be the condition from which subject and object can arise without being the highest of the objects, because to place it above them would already use the distinction between a placer and a placed whose possibility it names. Without this turn the descent would have to begin from a first thing, and a first thing would need an account of where it appeared, which is the regress §0/1 · #1 closed. So the first turn is forced by the demand that the series of appearings have a condition that is not one of them, and in our notation that condition is **#0**, Ground, `0`.

A condition that stayed only a condition would leave the kitchen dark. For anything to appear, the condition must be able to show and to know what it shows, and §0 · #0 found these to be one act seen twice, the light and its bend. In personal and divine form the tradition names the pair ***Śiva–Śakti***: Śiva the light, and Śakti the power by which the light knows, shows and turns toward display, distinct within one reality and never two realities.[^s0-siva-sakti] §0/1 · #4 read Śiva as the Subject in its speakable aspect and Śakti as the power of that Subject to differentiate and take on identities, and the reading holds here: remove Śakti, and the light would be unable to become anything; remove Śiva, and the power would be a force with no one for whom it disclosed. In the kitchen this is the stirring, the wanting that has not yet found its object. It is forced by the gap between a condition and an appearance, and its place is **#1**, Definition: the first stirring by which something can be called to account at all.

A power to show still lacks an orientation. Wanting tea is a single event, yet it is read in two ways at once, as *I would like tea* and as *there is the tea*, and neither reading is the event without the other. The tradition gives the two orientations two levels. In ***Sadāśiva*** the appearing universe is held toward the I-pole, with the sentence "I am this"; in ***Īśvara*** the same unity is read from the side of the disclosed world, "This am I".[^s0-sadasiva] Both sentences concern consciousness and manifestation as a whole, and neither describes an ego claiming the universe as its property; the emphasis moves from the I to the this and back within one relation. Our core theorems read the pair as the relational identity §0/1 · #1 derived, `0/1 = 1/0`, the condition becoming articulate through a determination and the determination turning toward the condition, two obverse orientations of one relating. Without this turn, showing would have only one direction, and an account written from it would be either a pure subjectivism, in which the tea is a state of mine, or a pure objectivism, in which there is tea and no one for whom it is there; the waking room at §0 · #0 showed that each of these is a collapse. That turn sits at **#2**, Dynamis, because it is the first place where the relation has force, a pulse between two poles that stay co-present. It also answers the question §0 · #1 left with *ahaṃkāra*. The I-maker's sentence, *I am this*, said of a report, a job or a body, is the Sadāśiva sentence contracted, the same grammar at a narrower scope, in which one partial "this" is made to exclude the rest of the field; universal self-recognition and anxious appropriation are one form, distinguished by how much of the world the "this" is allowed to hold.

Two orientations held together are still not a cup at arm's length. For the tea to be reached, I and this must be held apart enough that the one can confront the other as something to be determined and crossed. The tradition takes this in two steps. ***Sadvidyā***, pure knowledge, holds I and this in balance, distinct and equal; *Māyā* makes their difference operative as separation, so that the knower faces something whose determination is required.[^s0-maya] §0/1 · #5→0 read Māyā as operative measure, the power by which the measureless becomes measurable enough for a local world to run, and noted that its place in `0/1` depends on which relation is read: beside *prakāśa* and *vimarśa* it stands toward the measured world, and in the triad of Parā Śakti, Māyā Śakti and Nara it is the slash. Dyczkowski states the Spanda position that supports the reading. When the power of awareness gives rise to a sense of separation between subject and object, it is called Māyā; and where non-dualist Vedānta makes Māyā an indefinable principle producing an illusion of multiplicity, the Kashmiri Śaivas hold Māyā to be the power of the absolute to appear in diverse forms, so that the separation of subject and object is the product of a creative act, and the creative freedom of the absolute and its deluding power are one power named according to the insight with which it is met.[^s0-maya-dycz] Without Māyā the cup could not come forward: a cup that was not separate could not be reached, and nothing could be learned about it. That turn is at **#3**, Pattern, because here the relation becomes a recognisable kind, *knower* and *known*, a pattern that recurs in every act; and it is where the cut first becomes explicit, which is also where it can first forget that it is an act.

A separated knower facing a separated cup still has no situation. It could be anywhere, know anything, want everything, have all the time there is and stand under no order, and such a knower would never make tea, because tea is made under limits. The fifth turn supplies them, and the kitchen tells us how many there are, because the limits cannot be traded against one another. Śaiva sources call them the five ***kañcukas***, "coverings" or "sheaths", and our core theorems give their work: they contract omnipotence, omniscience, plenitude, eternity and unrestricted ordering into the finite ratios of a particular life.[^s0-kancukas] *Kalā* limits efficacy: the hand can lift this cup and not the kettle at the far end of the counter. *Vidyā* limits knowledge: the tea is known to be in the blue tin, and the milk is not known to be there. *Rāga* directs desire from a situation felt as incomplete: tea is wanted because it is not yet drunk. *Kāla* makes time effective: there are four minutes. *Niyati* orders circumstance: kettle before cup, this house, this sequence of causes and consequences. Each is a distinct power because supplying more of one does nothing for another. More knowledge does not lengthen the four minutes; a stronger wish does not cool the water; a free hour would not supply the missing milk; a stronger arm does not tell anyone which tin holds the tea; and none of the four changes the order in which the kettle must boil before the cup can be filled. Five powers that cannot substitute for one another give five limits, and the test shows that the five are distinct, while the count is the tradition's. Whoever inherits these ratios is ***puruṣa***, the bounded experient, who takes them as *my* capacity, *my* knowledge, *my* lack, *my* time and *my* circumstance; and from here the system unfolds what the Sāṃkhya tradition had already counted, *prakṛti*, nature, then the inner instrument whose three offices §0 · #1 followed, the five powers of sensing and the five of acting, the five subtle qualities of sense and the five gross elements. The stiff shoulder, the chipped rim, the warmth of the cup in the hand and the steam belong to this unfolding. Keeping only the knower's cognition would leave out the means by which an intention meets a world, and keeping only the objects would leave out their availability to someone. That turn belongs at **#4**, Context, because it gives knower and world together as a situation, the finite place in which a particular life can want tea and get it.

Institutions apportion the same five powers, which is why the sheaths are no merely contemplative anatomy. Anyone who has started a job has received a set of *kañcukas* with the contract: what the post may do and what it may not (*kalā*), what it is told and what it is not told (*vidyā*), what it is rewarded for wanting (*rāga*), its deadlines and terms (*kāla*), and the chain of authority and procedure in which every act has its place (*niyati*). Apportionment is the condition of any work getting done, and the root of *apportion*, a share assigned, says so.[^s0-apportion] It becomes dangerous where one party sets all five for another and keeps the setting out of view. §0 · #2's subpostmasters held a post whose powers the Post Office had apportioned, and as I read the case they could act on the branch accounts and not on the system that produced them, they were told the figures and not what the Post Office knew of the system's faults, and the order of proceeding put the burden of explaining a shortfall on them.[^s0-horizon-kancuka] Their contraction was made by others and then treated as their own, a *puruṣa* held responsible for limits it had not set and could not see. Tattvic anatomy makes the injustice nameable as a fault in apportionment: the five sheaths were real, and their authorship had been concealed.

A situated knower, finally, can live a whole life inside its contractions without knowing them as contractions, and then everything §0 · #1 to §0 · #3 found going wrong goes wrong: the verdict passes for the knower, the castle becomes a prison, the word forgets the cow. That danger forces the sixth turn. *Pratyabhijñā*, recognition, returns the contracted field to its source without undoing the contraction.[^s0-pratyabhijna] §0/1 · #1 met recognition as a change in the mode of identification, the images one holds of oneself remaining active while their claims to be the knower are relinquished. In the descent its work is definite. The contracted knower recognises its limits of efficacy, knowledge, desire, time and order, its inner instrument and its body, as modes in which the one awareness has become locally articulate, and recognises the ground it never ceased to express. Nothing finite is deleted. The cup is still a cup, the four minutes are still four minutes, the shoulder still aches; what changes is the relation of the one living through them to their source. In the kitchen it is the first mouthful, savoured, and §0/1 · #3 has already followed French *savoir*, to know, back to Latin *sapere*, which joins tasting, discerning and being wise, with *savour* from its neighbour *sapor*.[^s0-sapere] Recognition is that taste at the scale of a life, a knowing that arrives as the savour of being the one who knows. Its position is **#5**, Quintessence, the gathered whole held as one, and it completes the Möbius return `5→0`: the achieved recognition comes back as ground, the cup is put down, and the next act begins from a ground that now includes this one.

Read through Bohm, the field these turns compress is the architecture of implication that §0 · #0 found his language lacking. For Bohm's two orders, the rule §0/1 · #5→0 gave the MEF sets two implicate offices against four explicate positions, `{#0, #5}` against `{#1, #2, #3, #4}`.[^s0-bohmian-rule] In the compression Anuttara and Pratyabhijñā hold the implicate offices, the unsurpassable from which everything unfolds and the recognition in which everything is gathered back, while Śiva–Śakti, Sadāśiva–Īśvara, Sadvidyā–Māyā and the contracted world are the four explicate turns, each more determinate than the last. Its contents are generative capacities and contractions, the power to turn toward display, the power to orient toward I and this, the power to measure and separate, the power to limit, and none of them is a hidden object stored behind the appearances. That is why the descent is a movement and not an inventory. The whole is active in each local unfolding, as Bohm's holomovement is active in each droplet unfolding from the glycerine, and one act of making tea carries the whole descent because the descent is the order in which every such act unfolds.

It also explains a piece of arithmetic in the tradition's own history. The Sāṃkhya system, from which the Śaiva tradition received its lower categories, counted twenty-five *tattvas*: *puruṣa*, *prakṛti*, the three offices of the inner instrument, the ten powers of sense and action, the five subtle qualities and the five gross elements. The Śaiva tradition counts thirty-six, a scheme that scholarship traces in the early Śaiva scriptures well before Abhinavagupta, who systematised and deepened it.[^s0-36-history] The eleven it adds stand above the twenty-five as their genesis: the five pure tattvas, Śiva, Śakti, Sadāśiva, Īśvara and Sadvidyā; Māyā as the hinge; and the five *kañcukas*. Written as squares the relation is

$$
36-25 \;=\; 6^2-5^2 \;=\; (6-5)(6+5) \;=\; 11 \;=\; 5+1+5 .
$$

Lay out twenty-five counters as a five-by-five square, and the eleven needed to make it six-by-six are a row of five along one side, a column of five along the adjacent side and the single counter where they meet: the figure the Greeks called a gnomon, the set-square shape that enlarges a square while keeping it a square. The five pure powers and the five contracting powers stand on either side of their hinge, Māyā, as the strip of five, the strip of five and the corner stand in the gnomon, and the twenty-fivefold apparatus of the Sāṃkhya, which analyses the experienced field from *puruṣa* to the elements, acquires through them its vertical genesis: the source from which its knower came, the contraction by which universal powers became that knower's limited capacities, and the path by which recognition can return. I offer the gnomon as a reading of what the arithmetic shows, and nobody in the tradition built the system this way; what it claims is that the step from twenty-five to thirty-six changes dimension, from a fivefold measure to a sixfold one, and is no addition of eleven items to a list.[^s0-gnomon] It also shows why a twenty-fivefold account can analyse an experienced world with great precision and leave its source and its contraction unspoken, while a thirty-sixfold account can hold both the objective differentiation and its genesis in awareness. My notes on Buddhist psychology record the same intuition in its own terms, that the addition of eleven tattvas to Sāṃkhya's twenty-five is "from 5x5 to 6x6", a move toward depth that the fivefold schemes of Buddhist analysis, which leave the unspeakable ground implicit, do not make.[^s0-notes-gnomon]

Thirty-six also equals six times six, and I offer that resonance as a conjecture. Kripke's two dice at §0/1 · #1 gave thirty-six states once the two dice were kept distinct, and the harmonic derivation of §0/1 · #5→0 divided the whole, `100`, into `64` and `36`, the binary taken through six positions and the sixfold taken through its own second-order accounting.[^s0-six-squared] Reading the full field of tattvas as the sixfold taken through itself, each of the six turns opening into a six of its own, would make the thirty-six a square of the compression derived here. That equality is arithmetic, and the doctrine and the formalism reach a complete differentiated field of thirty-six independently; the conjecture's burden is to show, category by category, what the square makes intelligible beyond the coincidence of the number, and until that work is done the six turns are used and the square is held open.

Both logics run through the descent at the turn where the tradition places Māyā. Sadvidyā's balance of I and this is a held polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`, each pole having its sense through the other and the zero between them the shared awareness in which they are balanced; Māyā makes the polarity operative, and the operation is healthy dia-ballein, the cut that lets a cup be reached. Collapse comes at **#4**, where the bounded experient inherits the contractions as its own. Appropriation counts the whole span of the field from the experient's pole: the cup is *mine*, the kitchen is *mine*, the four minutes are *my* time, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the world on the other side of the axis enters as what stands against me, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, so that the experient's inflated claim and the world's apparent hostility mirror each other and everything not-mine becomes an obstacle or a threat. Cancellation is the other loss: the finite world dismissed as illusion, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, Māyā read as a veil over nothing, which is the reading Dyczkowski reports the Spanda teachers rejecting. Recognition performs syn-ballein. Sadāśiva's "I am this" and Īśvara's "This am I" are each read through the other, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, and the contracted world is known as the self-display of the awareness that lives in it, its separations kept and its belonging restored.

Recognition has to be told apart from a state it is easily confused with, and §0 · #0 supplied the terms. Turned inward, *vimarśa* rests in itself as *nirvikalpa*, without thought-constructs, and meditative absorption can make that resting the whole of experience for a time: the discriminations that sustain an ordinary problem fall silent, and with them the problem. If freedom is then made to depend on keeping them silent, their return is felt as a loss. A thought, a desire or a difficult conversation becomes an interruption of the achieved state, and practice becomes an effort to exclude them, which is one more exclusion and one more dependence on what is excluded. Recognition concerns the ground present through the differences, and quiet does not manufacture that ground, nor does the return of a thought remove it. *Pratyabhijñā* is re-cognition, knowing again what was already known, and the knowing happens amid activity: judgment continues, the inner instrument goes on coordinating, settling and appropriating, the four minutes still run out, and none of these has to become the whole in order to be real, or stop in order for the ground to be known. §0 · #1's morning of the message shows the practical difference. A calmer morning might have helped the instrument hear the five words truly, while recognition would have changed something else, the demand that the verdict settle who the reader was.

At **#4** the descent produced everything a finite life knows and acts through: the contracted powers, the inner instrument, the senses, a body made of elements, and the world these make available. §0/1 gave that whole apparatus a name at the bedside, and in its last movement placed it among three offices; the derivation has now shown where it comes from, turn by turn, and that it is the means of a knower whose contraction can be recognised.

### #5→0 · Objective Internality

On the first night in a new house the hand reaches out in the dark for a light switch that is not there. It goes to the right of the bedroom door at shoulder height, because that is where the switch was for eleven years, and it meets bare plaster. For a second the body is genuinely confused, as though the wall had made a mistake. The old house is still at work, in the hand, in the height of the reach, in the number of steps to the bathroom and the place where the stair should creak. Over the following weeks the new house moves in. The hand learns the switch on the left, the feet learn that the third stair from the top is the one that speaks, the kitchen drawers stop being searched and start being opened, and one night, without anyone noticing when it happened, the whole house can be crossed in the dark. It has become internal: the reach proceeds through it, as it once proceeded through the old one. It has also stayed objective. An electrician can come and move the switch, and the next night the hand will go to the old place and meet plaster again; a guest can point out that the drawer sticks; a buyer, years later, can walk the same rooms and find them strange.

The house in the dark is the result this section has derived. At a child's bedside §0/1 · #0 gave it a name before it could give it a genesis: Objective Internality, the means through which a life knows and acts within a world, internal because an act proceeds through them and objective because each of them can be turned into an object and examined.[^s0-87] The definition was functional: it said what the means do and why the name fits, and it let one question be put to very different instances of mind. §0/1 · #5→0 then placed the means in the relation of three offices, Subjective Immediacy as the knower, *pramātṛ*; Objective Internality as the means, *pramāṇa*, which is what we call Life and Mind, since a mind is lived and a life is minded; and World as the known, *prameya*, all three within the whole of I-Consciousness.[^s0-88] What §0/1 could not yet show was where the means come from, and so whether the name describes what awareness becomes when it differentiates or attaches a metaphysics to a mechanism already standing there.

Differentiating activity is where the means come from. Objective Internality is what the descent of the *tattvas* produces at its fourth turn, unlimited powers contracted into the finite ratios of efficacy, knowledge, desire, time and order and inherited by a bounded experient who knows through an instrument and acts through a body in a world of elements: the constituted means through which Subjective Immediacy knows and acts within a World, and what each preceding movement watched at work. Language, memory, affect, bodily orientation, habits, other people, tools, institutions and inherited rules all belong to it, because each is a limited power through which a bounded knower meets what it meets, and changing their relations changes what can appear, what can matter and what can be done.

Kashmir Śaivism supplies the reason the name joins *objective* to *internal*, and it lies in their account of how anything comes to seem outside at all. Dyczkowski reports Utpaladeva's definition of internality as a state of oneness with the subject and of externality as the state of separation from it.[^s0-89] Externality, on this account, is a relation to the subject, the state in which something appears separated from the one who knows it, and not a region beyond the subject's reach; and the separation is Māyā's creative act, the power of the absolute to appear in diverse forms, which §0 · #4 found at the fourth turn. Abhinavagupta draws the consequence in a sentence that could serve as the definition of creation in this whole tradition: creation is to make what shines within externally manifest while it still preserves its original internal nature.[^s0-90] The phrase *Objective Internality* names exactly this double condition. A finite life's means have been made external enough to be objects, examinable, correctable, movable by an electrician; and in their operation they keep their internal nature, one with the knower who acts through them, so that the hand in the dark goes to the switch with no more thought than it takes to lift itself. A switch, a word, a remembered rule or a colleague's permission is objective when we look at it and internal when we act through it, and it is the same switch, word, rule and permission in both states. Objective Internality is the field in which the inner has been made outer while remaining inner, which is the Śaiva account of manifestation read at the scale of a household.

These means are also means of *knowing*, and that word carries a requirement the house makes concrete. *Pramāṇa* names in Indian epistemology a valid means of knowledge, and the traditions dispute how many there are; Patañjali's *Yogasūtra* counts three, perception, inference and testimony, as the first of the mind's five fluctuations that §0 · #1 followed.[^s0-91] A means of knowledge has conditions under which it is valid, and the hand in the dark shows what happens when those conditions change without the means being told. That reach was a valid means for eleven years, a perception laid down as a habit; it became invalid on the night of the move, and the hand did not know. Its error was one of warrant, since the house was perfectly visible in the morning and the reach was simply aimed at a world that had gone. Every element of Objective Internality carries such a warrant, and the mode by which it reaches its object decides what its correction must reach. A memory is corrected against a record or another witness; a perception against a renewed encounter; a piece of testimony against its source; a habit only by being run again in the new house until the new reach replaces the old. Treating every element as one more interchangeable item of information loses this difference, and loses with it the knowledge of where a correction has to be made.

Heidegger described exactly how such a move discloses the means, and §0/1 · #4 has already set out his account: a tool in use withdraws into the work, and only when it breaks, goes missing or gets in the way does it become conspicuous enough to be noticed and thought about.[^s0-92] The missing switch is the old house becoming conspicuous. For eleven years it was not an object of the hand's attention at all; it was the means through which the hand reached the light. Its absence made it visible, as a habit, a height, a direction, a whole arrangement of the body around a house that had stopped existing. A discontinuity discloses Objective Internality, because the means show themselves when they fail to meet their world. I have followed this disclosure through a sequence that runs from discontinuity, to Objective Internality, to the particular elements that sustain it, and on to what Whitehead called objective immortality, the condition of an achieved occasion that has perished as a subject and become a datum for every later occasion.[^s0-93] The move is the discontinuity; the reaching hand is the Objective Internality it discloses; the habit, the height and the direction are the elements through which that internality worked; and the eleven years of reaching, perished as living acts, persist as the datum the new house has to be learned against. In turn the new house will become such a datum, the condition from which, years from now, a hand in some other house reaches for the wrong wall.

A life does not live in its Objective Internality as an object among objects, and the way it is lived has a name that §5 · #0 defines at length: a paradigm, the lived pattern of disclosure through which these means make a World intelligible and actionable for a Life.[^s0-94] The hand in the dark shows what that pattern is by showing it come apart from its representation. The person standing in the bedroom knows perfectly well that they have moved, and asked, they would describe the new house correctly. Their pattern has not moved, and it governs the hand while the explicit knowledge governs the description. Every adult has seen the same gap at larger scales: a person who has adopted a new view and goes on acting from the old distribution of attention, permission and value; an institution that has revised its stated mission and kept its old incentives; a culture that has changed its official account of the world and kept the habits through which the old account was lived. The pattern changes when the means change, when the hand has learned the new wall, and its representation helps by exposing the discrepancy between what is professed and what is enacted, so that change can begin where the conduct actually proceeds from.

The house is also shared, and its sharing returns the derivation to §0/1's account of co-internality. A partner who moved in a month earlier knows where the switch is and says so, laughing, from the other side of the bed; a child asks why the stairs creak; a neighbour explains which bin goes out on which day. Each of these voices enters the means through which the new house becomes internal, and §0/1's name for that entering is Objective Co-Internality: reciprocal world-constitution without merger, enough common world for differences to change one another and enough retained difference for the change to mean anything.[^s0-95] The derivation adds one thing to §0/1's account. Co-internality is the tattvic descent occurring in the plural. Every bounded experient inherits its contracted powers in a world where other experients have inherited theirs, and the means through which each knows include the testimony, permissions and help of the others, so that one person's limited knowledge, *vidyā*, is enlarged by another's report and one person's limited efficacy, *kalā*, by another's hand. The *kañcukas* contract each life's powers, and co-internality distributes the powers among lives so that what one cannot do or know another can.

Artificial agents build an Objective Internality by hand, and the house in the dark gives the clearest picture of what that building involves. Bratton's brief describes an agent as individuated in session: there is no homunculus waiting in the weights, and an agent takes shape from the context assembled around a request, its persona one stratum among memory, skills, constraints and relations, a lamination and not a monad.[^s0-96] Each session is a move into a house. It arrives with dispositions laid down in training, which play the part of the old house in the hand, and it meets a context assembled for this occasion: instructions, files, retrieved sources, tools at particular places, permissions that allow some acts and forbid others. Its errors at the start of a session often have exactly the form of the hand in the dark. It reaches for a tool that existed in another configuration, cites a file at a path that has moved, or applies a convention that held in the project it was trained on and fails in this one; and the error discloses its Objective Internality as the missing switch disclosed the old house. Its means are then corrected as a person's are, and only at the office where the error lies. A wrong reading of a source is corrected by returning to the source. A wrong habit is corrected by an instruction, a memory or a change in training. A missing permission cannot be corrected by the agent at all, since a permission belongs to whoever grants it, as the switch belongs to the electrician and the landlord; the return has to reach that office, and an agent that merely reports the missing permission has left its world unchanged. §5 takes up this constructed internality product by product.[^s0-97]

The agent's case also shows what the derivation does and does not settle. Objective Internality is broader than any inspectable state and narrower than the whole: it includes the agent's context, tools, permissions and memory, and also the institutions, labour and material infrastructure through which those exist, which no single trace exhibits; and it is the means of a relation whose knower it is not. This section's derivation shows how an Objective Internality is constituted by differentiating activity, and it applies to an agent's operative world as fully as to a person's house, since in both a bounded field of limited powers makes some things appear, matter and become possible. It does not show that a knower is present to the agent's internality, because the Śaiva account places the knower at the source of the descent and not among its products, and no product, however articulate, can certify its source. §0/1 left that question open by method, and the descent shows why the method is right to keep it open. Our core theorems carry an argued crosswalk that follows an agent's provisional individuation, from the reserve of its weights through the instance of a session, the nucleation of an active context and the lamination of its persona, to a trans-individual whole and its capacity to become otherwise in a new context, set beside the psychic register of individuation, and §4 · #1 develops that crosswalk through Jung's account, where the technical and psychic operations can be kept distinct.[^s0-98]

The two logics describe what happens in the house in the dark, and naming them shows what each does to the one living there. The old house governing the reach is appropriation in its quietest form: the old pattern counts the whole span between the two houses from its own pole, so that the new house registers as the place where the switches are wrong, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and from the other end the new house receives the same span as its fault, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, the wall that made a mistake. Cancellation is the denial that there is anything to learn, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, the person who insists they have already settled in and goes on stubbing a toe on the same step for a year. Healthy polarity is what the learning hand performs: old house and new held on one axis through the zero of the body that lives in both, `(−1)/(+1)`, the reach adjusting night by night; and the house becomes home when both orientations are kept, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, the new house read through the habits the old one laid down and the old habits read through the house that now corrects them, so that a life carries its history into a new world without making the new world pay for it.

One more thing happened in the bedroom on the first night, and it carries the derivation into §1. When the hand met bare plaster it met an absence that had a definite place. The switch was missing *there*, to the right of the door at shoulder height, and the hand's error was to treat that empty place as full: an absence with a position had been operating in the reach as though it were a presence, and the confusion of the body for a second was the felt difference between a place that holds something and a place that holds nothing. Every determination this section followed leaves such a place behind it, since each selected one actuality from a field it did not create and cannot exhaust, and the field remains, structurally present, as the place where what was not selected stands. Once a world is produced by differentiation, its implicit zero is unavoidable.

§0/1 borrowed zero as the sign of the condition it could not count as one more object, and asked how awareness differentiates itself into the means by which it knows and into the world it knows through them. The answer is the house in the dark. A determined world carries places where something is not, and those places are operative: the hand reaches into one, a child's word is bounded by them, a castle stands against them. Mathematics learned to write such a place. In a numeral such as 1005 the two signs between the 1 and the 5 mark positions that hold nothing, and their marking changes the value of everything around them, so that the 1 counts thousands because the empty places beside it have been counted. How a sign for no counted thing came to change what can be counted, and what that sign did as it travelled between civilisations, is the history §1 follows.
