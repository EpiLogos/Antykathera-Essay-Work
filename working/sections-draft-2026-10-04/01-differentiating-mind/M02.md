### #1 · The Inner Instrument

"Can we talk later today?" Most of us have received some version of that message, from a manager, a colleague, a partner or a parent, and most of us know what happens in the seconds after reading it. Before the phone has been put down, a great deal of work has been done. The sender's name has called up last week's meeting, where a remark went unanswered; the report that went in late has come forward; the tone of the five words has been weighed and found terse, or ominous, or carefully neutral, which is worse; and several readings have offered themselves at once, as praise deferred, as a complaint, as the beginning of the end of a job. Then one reading wins. *They are unhappy with the report.* Almost in the same moment the verdict turns inward and acquires an owner: *I am in trouble; this always happens to me; I am not, after all, good enough at this.* By the time the kettle has boiled, a whole day has been arranged around that conclusion. A defence has been drafted in the shower, the meeting rehearsed twice, the colleague's face imagined set in disappointment. At four in the afternoon the colleague closes the office door and asks, a little awkwardly, for help with a crisis of their own.

Nothing in the morning's work was idle, and none of it was the knower. Someone saw the message; the memories were really recalled, the readings were really weighed, the verdict was really reached and really taken to heart, and all of it happened within the one who woke, in the space the bend of awareness had opened at §0 · #0. Indian traditions call the apparatus that does this work the ***antaḥkaraṇa***, the **inner instrument**, and both halves of the name earn their place. *Antaḥ* is "within". *Karaṇa* is an instrument, the means by which something is done, from the root *kṛ*, to do or make, and Sanskrit grammar gives the word to the instrumental relation itself, the *sādhakatama*, the factor most conducive to bringing an action about, which the case of a sentence names when it says what something is done *with*.[^s0-karana] An inner instrument is what a finite life knows and acts *with*. Its parts can be studied, trained and repaired, and §0/1 · #0 has already given the general name for what it belongs to: Objective Internality, the means through which a life knows and acts within a world, internal because an act proceeds through it and objective because each of its operations can be turned into an object and examined.

Classical accounts distinguish three offices within the instrument, and the morning of the message shows all three at work. ***Manas***, usually translated "mind", coordinates: it receives the presentations of the senses, gathers the associations that come with them and moves among the possible readings, so that last week's meeting, the late report and the clipped tone are present together and available for judgment. ***Buddhi***, "intellect", determines: it settles what something is, *this* rather than *that*, the complaint rather than the praise. ***Ahaṃkāra***, literally the "I-maker", from *aham*, I, and the same root of making, appropriates: it gathers the determination under the form of "I" and "mine", so that a colleague's request becomes *my* trouble, *my* failure, *my* standing in the place where I work. Their oldest systematic statement is the *Sāṃkhya Kārikā* of Īśvarakṛṣṇa, a short verse treatise, and its English translation of 1837 keeps the definitions as terse as the original: "ASCERTAINMENT is intellect"; "CONSCIOUSNESS is egotism"; and mind is the organ that is "both" one of sensation and one of action, standing between what comes in and what goes out.[^s0-samkhya-offices] Colebrooke's second sentence carries a history of its own. An English translator of the 1830s reached for "consciousness" to render *ahaṃkāra*, the faculty that makes "I", and his choice shows how readily the word for awareness could be handed to the faculty that appropriates experience under a name. §0/1 · #5→0 found four different things carried by the one word *consciousness*; Colebrooke's sentence adds a fifth, the I-making function, and it is the one most often mistaken for the knower.

Distinguishable offices need not form a sequence of departments, each finishing its work and handing a file to the next. In the morning of the message, coordination, determination and appropriation ran almost together: the tone was already heard as ominous because an injured self-position had been in place since last week, and attention was already searching for evidence before any verdict had been voiced. We distinguish them for a practical reason, which the afternoon makes plain. Had the colleague been unhappy with the report, the morning's verdict would have been true and the day's preparation useful, so the error lay elsewhere. *Manas* gathered the right materials and one wrong one, because the remembered slight from last week entered the field as if it were evidence about today. *Buddhi* then settled the reading that fitted the field it had been given, and fitted it well. *Ahaṃkāra* took the settled reading as a verdict on who I am, so that a question about a report became a question about my worth, and the day was spent defending the person instead of finding out what the colleague wanted. Each office failed in its own way, and each failure needed its own correction: the memory had to be put back in its place as memory, the judgment reopened against what had actually been said, and the self that had been put on trial released from the dock. Calming down would have mended none of the three, because a calm mind can hold a mistaken verdict more steadily than an agitated one. Correction reaches the instrument only at the office where the fault lies.

Spanda teachers give the instrument's memory a finer anatomy, and it explains how a slight from last week could enter this morning's field as though it were news. Dyczkowski reports the account in which reflective awareness, at the level of the individual, can analyse its own states, retain them as residual traces, *saṃskāras*, draw any of these out again at will and bring back an old state of itself, as in remembering, and make an altogether new state by selecting from the stored traces and displaying the selection, as in free imagination.[^s0-samskara] Retention, recollection and imagination are three operations on one stock. A trace can condition what is noticed without being recalled at all; a recollection presents the past as past, known as what happened before; an imagination composes retained material into something that has not happened. In the morning the second and third were confused. That rehearsed meeting, in which the colleague's face set in disappointment was composed from traces of earlier faces and earlier meetings, and it arrived with the vividness of a memory, so that *buddhi* settled the day's verdict on evidence imagination had made. Most of the suffering in that morning was suffered on the strength of a scene that never took place.

None of these operations needs a little knower inside it. Four o'clock did not reveal that a small judge behind the instrument had made a poor decision. It revealed that coordination, determination and appropriation had worked on a field containing one false item, and that their work had been taken, by the one living through it, for what was simply the case. A pianist hears the piano respond to each touch, and the response tells the pianist how the instrument is working, which hammers stick and how the damper is set, yet opening the case and naming every part never finds a small pianist inside: an instrument is that through which, never the one who. Sāṃkhya draws the same line in its own way, and its way teaches by where it puts the knower, entirely outside the instrument. The self, *puruṣa*, is "witness, solitary, bystander, spectator, and passive", while all the activity of determining, appropriating and coordinating belongs to *prakṛti*, nature, of which the inner instrument is a refined product.[^s0-witness] Its distinction is sound, and the dualism in which it is set leaves a question it cannot answer, the one the hotel mirror raised at §0 · #0: how does a wholly passive witness ever come to know what the busy instrument presents to it? A spectator that contributed nothing to its seeing would be one more glass on the wardrobe door.

Kashmir Śaivism keeps Sāṃkhya's analysis of the instrument and changes its source. In the Śaiva account the instrument is the contracted activity of the same awareness that bends toward display: the activity of the mind is that of consciousness itself, Dyczkowski writes, and individual and universal consciousness are one, the processes of individual consciousness being restricted representations of the maximally expanded operation of universal consciousness.[^s0-restricted] Cognition, on this view, is reflective awareness of "I" limited by the affections that the variety of manifestation imposes upon it, so that memory, thought and the other functions of the mind are what *vimarśa* becomes when it is conditioned by an object and by the forces that govern the object.[^s0-cognition] Its difference from Sāṃkhya has consequences, and the morning shows them. A Sāṃkhya analyst can say that the instrument erred and that the witness stood untouched, which is true and leaves the error where it was. A Śaiva analyst can say that the error was the knower's own power working in contracted form, so that the power that made the error is also the power that can recognise it; the instrument's faults are corrigible from within because the instrument is the knower's own activity, narrowed. §0 · #4 follows the contractions that do the narrowing. For now it is enough that the instrument needs no second, idle consciousness to see it, because its seeing is already the work of the one awareness, bent toward a world and then bent again toward the parts of that world it calls "mine".

*Ahaṃkāra* deserves the closest attention, because it is the office that most readily passes for the knower. Its making of "I" is real, useful and necessary: without it nobody would answer to a name, keep a promise or feel that a colleague's request concerned them. Its trouble lies in scope. Early Buddhist teaching names the trouble in a formula that recurs throughout the discourses, in which the disciple is asked whether what is impermanent and subject to change can fittingly be regarded as "this is mine, this I am, this is my self", and answers that it cannot.[^s0-anatta] Gay Watson, reading the formula through Buddhist psychology and psychotherapy, treats it as a description of a process, the process of selfing, which, in her words, "works in three main ways, both instinctually and intellectually: craving occurring in the linguistic form 'This is mine', conceit manifesting in the linguistic form 'This I am', and false views manifesting in the linguistic form 'This is myself.'" Delusion arises, she writes, "when this process of selfing is grasped at as an entity and identified with, rather than experienced as an ever-changing expression of dynamic interaction."[^s0-watson-selfing] All three ran through the morning of the message: the report was mine; I am the one whose report it was; a judgment on the report is a judgment on my self. In three steps, each a small act of appropriation, a question about a document had become a verdict on a person.

Institutions run the same three steps with more at stake. A body that has staked its standing on a system, a policy or a published figure hears every discrepancy in it as a judgment on itself: the figure is ours, we are the authors of the figure, and a challenge to the figure is a challenge to us. An office that ought to have asked what the discrepancy meant spends its strength defending the person it has become, and §0 · #2 follows one such institution into the courts. A corporate "we" is *ahaṃkāra*'s sentence spoken by many mouths, and its scope can be measured by what it refuses to hear.

Four things are carried by the word *self* in these scenes, and the morning needs all four kept apart. **Selfing** is the activity by which a field contracts around one local determination and treats it as owner, identity or ground; it happens across the whole of Objective Internality and not only in private thought, since a job title, a team's habits, a remembered remark and a phone's notifications can all take part in it. Lower-case *self* is the real local horizon of a bounded life, the one who woke in Lisbon, with this body, this history and this report, a horizon to which a message can be sent and from which an answer can come; the Śaiva sources call it *paśu*, the bound one, and calling it bound takes nothing from its reality. The **self-thing** is what selfing produces when a local determination is promoted into the determining ground: one image of oneself sold back to the whole life as the condition of its belonging. My reading notes on Watson give it a second name, the *sell-thing*, "a 'sell' to be 'bought into', invested in by the selfing itself to derive the illusory self-persisting and self-consisting 'self-thing'", and the pun carries its argument, since what is defended most fiercely is what has cost the most to buy.[^s0-sell-thing] Capital *Self*, finally, is the name Jung and the Śaiva sources give to the whole that §0/1 · #5→0 called I-Consciousness, written `0/1`: knower, means and known together, the totality in which every image of oneself appears and can be known as partial.[^s0-self-whole] Self in this sense is neither the knower alone, which is Subjective Immediacy, nor the means, where selfing does its work, but the relation that holds both with their world. Buddhist critique applies without qualification to the self-thing, to every proprietor selfing installs and every image that claims to own experience, and nothing here seeks to reinstall such a proprietor. Śaiva teaching makes its claim about the knowing in which selfing and its products appear, which is not one more image, so the critique of images leaves it standing. William James drew a near neighbour of the distinction in his *Principles of Psychology*, separating the "Me", the empirical aggregate of things known as oneself, from the "I" that knows them; and then, by explaining the I as the passing Thought of each moment, which inherits and appropriates the thoughts before it, he handed back to the stream of objects the knower he had just distinguished from it.[^s0-james] §0/1's definition of the Subject keeps the distinction where James let it slip: whatever is offered as the knower, including a passing thought that owns its predecessors, has become one more presentation to the knowing in which it is offered.

Śaiva tradition hears the canonical formula's grammar at another scope. "I am this" is *ahaṃkāra*'s sentence when "this" is a report, a job or a body. §0 · #4 meets the same sentence spoken of the whole universe by the awareness in which it appears, at the level the Śaiva sources call Sadāśiva, and its inverse, "This am I", at the level they call Īśvara. One grammatical form serves bondage and recognition according to its extension: whether one partial "this" is made to exclude the rest of the field, or whether the whole recognises every "this" as its own manifestation. *Ahaṃkāra* repeats, in contracted form, a sentence it did not invent, and its fault in the morning lay in the narrowness of the "this", which made a colleague's five words carry the weight of the reader's existence.

Read through the two logics, the morning enacts each operation in turn, and naming them shows what each did to the two people involved. Healthy dia-ballein was there from the start. A message made a real difference, between a day with a conversation in it and a day without, and concern and calm stood on one axis, `(−1)/(+1)`, held through the zero of the work two colleagues share. Appropriation began when *ahaṃkāra* counted the whole span of that axis from its own pole. Now the conversation became entirely a matter of my standing, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and in the same reckoning the colleague became entirely the one who stood against me, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`; each figure is the other's mirror, my inflated stake the colleague's imagined hostility, so that by mid-morning two people who share an office had been set at opposite ends of a quarrel neither of them had begun. Had the morning gone another way, a friend's reassurance, "it's nothing, forget it", would have cancelled the axis, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, settling the worry by declaring that nothing was at stake, which would have been as false as the verdict and would have left the colleague's real crisis just as unheard. Four o'clock performed the third operation. Two people sat down with their difference intact, one who had dreaded the meeting and one who needed help, and each became readable through the other; the worry turned out to concern a shared world seen from its other side. That is syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, as an office door closes on it: the halves meet, and the fit of their edges shows why they belonged together.

Yoga made the instrument's working the direct object of a discipline, and its founding text classifies the instrument's activities in a way the morning confirms. Patañjali's *Yogasūtra* defines yoga as the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind, *citta-vṛtti-nirodha*; when they are stilled, the seer rests in its own form, and otherwise the seer takes on the form of the fluctuations.[^s0-yoga-def] Five fluctuations are named: valid cognition, *pramāṇa*; error, *viparyaya*; *vikalpa*, which the sūtra defines narrowly as a cognition that follows words and has no object; sleep; and memory.[^s0-vrttis] Patañjali's list is an anatomy of the instrument's possible states, and it places §0/1's word for the means of knowing, *pramāṇa*, among the mind's fluctuations: valid knowledge is itself a movement of the instrument, one of its five modes, and the seer is identical with it no more than with error. In a few minutes the morning ran through four of the five. It began with valid cognition, the message read correctly; moved into error, the slight taken for evidence; was carried along by *vikalpa* in the sūtra's sense, a rehearsed meeting that followed words and had no object; and drew on memory throughout. Patañjali's account of identification, the seer taking on the form of its fluctuations, describes what the morning felt like from inside, since the verdict was never experienced as a thought about the colleague but as the plain state of affairs and of oneself.

One short scene in the Buddhist canon carries the same teaching as an address. In the *Udāna*, the ascetic Bāhiya, in a hurry, stops the Buddha on his alms round and asks to be taught at once, and the Buddha gives him a single instruction, in John Ireland's translation "in the seen will be merely what is seen", continued through what is heard, sensed and cognised; and Bāhiya, the text says, was released on hearing it.[^s0-bahiya] His instruction strips experience of the appropriating addition by which the seen becomes mine, then I, then my self, and the morning could have used it: in the read, merely the read; five words, a name, a question. And the scene shows something the instruction does not state. It is spoken *to* Bāhiya, a man with a name, a history and an urgency, who can receive it and be changed by it, so the teaching that removes the appropriating "I" from experience depends throughout on a "you" to whom it is addressed, and the disidentification it asks for is performed within a relation of address. §0/1 · #5→0 placed grammar's three persons in the three offices, *I* at the knower, *it* at the known, and *you* at the relation through which they meet. Bāhiya's release occurs at the *you*: in the means, where an instruction reaches a life, and not in an abolition of the life it reaches.

Artificial agents make the three offices legible in a new material, and the comparison holds at the level of office. In an agent built around a language model, something coordinates: the system that assembles the context, retrieves sources, recalls earlier exchanges and holds the possible next moves available together does the work of *manas*. Something determines: the step in which a reading is settled, a tool chosen or an answer committed does the work of *buddhi*. And something appropriates: the persona, the "I" in "I can help with that", the system prompt that tells the model who it is and the memory of what "the user" prefers gather each determination under a continuing identity that can be addressed and held responsible. Performed subjectivity, the persona §0/1 · #0 took from Bratton, with its voice, its manner and its "I", is *ahaṃkāra*'s office given technical form.[^s0-performed] No diagram matching modules to faculties should be built from this, because in an actual agent the three offices are distributed across model weights, harness code, memory files and the people who configure them; what carries across is the question each office asks: what coordinates, what settles, what appropriates. Asked of an agent, the three questions make its failures as legible as the morning's. An agent that confuses a retrieved paraphrase with its source has erred in coordination, one that settles on the reading that fits its prompt has erred in determination, and one that defends its earlier answer because "I said" has erred in appropriation, the persona defending the report in place of finding out what was wanted. Each needs correction at its own office, and correction of the third reaches the persona, the memory and the instructions, which an agent cannot always revise for itself. Śaiva distinction carries over intact. An agent's "I" is *ahaṃkāra*'s work, and in human life *ahaṃkāra* was never the knower either, so the presence of an "I" in an agent's speech settles nothing about a knower in it, in either direction, for the reason §0/1 · #1 gave, that any produced "I am" is one more content.

By four o'clock the instrument had built a whole day out of five words: a meeting in it, a defence, a face set in disappointment and a person on trial, and the built day governed what was noticed, feared and done until the office door closed. Its settling was constructive, a cutting of alternatives and a gathering of a course from them, and the Sanskrit names for that making, *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa*, describe a world being built and not merely read.
