### #2 · Vikalpa and Saṃkalpa as World-Making

It is raining on a Saturday, and two children have taken the sofa apart. The long cushions are walls, propped against the coffee table; a sheet thrown over the top is the roof; the gap under the dining table, reached on hands and knees, is the secret tunnel; the dog, who has wandered in to see what is going on, is a dragon and must be fed biscuits to be kept calm. Within twenty minutes there are rules. Grown-ups may not enter without the password. The kitchen is enemy territory, and raids on it must be made in silence. The torch is the only light allowed inside, and whoever holds it is captain. When a parent puts a head round the door to say that lunch is ready, the children are indignant, because lunch belongs to a world they have stopped living in, and at bedtime, when the cushions have to go back on the sofa, there are tears: something real is being destroyed. Most of us were such children, and some of us have since been the parent at the door, with the strange double knowledge that the castle is three cushions and a sheet and that it has, for an afternoon, governed everything that happened in that room.

Sanskrit names both of the things the children did from one root. First they cut: this cushion could be a wall or a bed or a boat, and they decided it was a wall; the gap under the table could be a cave or a prison or nothing at all, and they made it a tunnel. Then they gathered: the walls, the roof, the tunnel, the dragon and the password were drawn together into one castle with one set of rules, by a resolve, *this is our castle*, that every later move had to answer to. Cutting is *vikalpa*, the cutting of a field into qualified alternatives, so that a possibility becomes thinkable as distinct from another; gathering is *saṃkalpa*, the gathering of selected differences into an intention, a resolve, a course or a workable world. Both are formed on the root *kḷp*, whose senses run from being fitting, suitably related and in order to arranging, framing, preparing, producing and imagining, and which in some causative uses can even mean to trim or cut. Prefixes divide the root's work between them. *Vi-* carries separation, distinction and distribution into different directions, and the dictionary of Monier-Williams thought it probably descended from *dvi*, two parts; *sam-* carries togetherness, conjunction and completion.[^s0-kalpa-root] *Vi-kalpa* is a fitting-apart and *saṃ-kalpa* a fitting-together, and the children performed both, many times, in an afternoon. From the same root Sanskrit took *kalpa*, which can mean a rule or precept, as in the rules of a ritual, and can also mean an aeon, a whole age of a world from its making to its dissolution.[^s0-kalpa-aeon] One family of words thus keeps the fitting of a thought, the rule that governs a practice and the span of a world, and the children's afternoon had all three: alternatives fitted apart, a rule fitted together, and a world that lasted until the cushions went back.

§0/1 · #5→0 has already followed *vikalpa* through its long history.[^s0-vikalpa-return] There Dignāga and Dharmakīrti's *vikalpa* was the conceptual carving of a world into usable kinds; Bhartṛhari's thesis that no cognition occurs without the accompaniment of the word showed language permeating experience as experience forms; the Spanda teaching carried the analysis to its widest reach, in which consciousness itself, through its pulse, conceives the world-thought, *viśvavikalpa*, turning from pure I-ness toward thisness; and §0/1 named the danger of the whole operation as script, frozen conditioned will, an achieved selection that has forgotten its own formation and presents its course as necessity. Its account depends on a positive claim that can now be shown at work: *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa* together make worlds, and that they make them through a pulse in which each act inherits a field, makes a cut, and alters the field available to the next act. Cognition is constructive on this account without being arbitrary, and the castle shows both halves of that sentence.

It shows the construction first. Before the children began, the cushions had a meaning that the household had fitted them with: sofa cushions, to be sat on, to be put back. *Vikalpa* opened that meaning into alternatives, and *saṃkalpa* closed a selection of them into a new arrangement with commitments attached. Once the castle had been resolved on, the room was redistributed. The dog's place in the world changed, from a pet to a dragon to be appeased; the kitchen's changed, from where food is made to where raids are made; the parent's changed, from the one who decides about lunch to a grown-up who needs the password. Nothing physical had moved apart from the cushions, and yet what could appear in the room, what mattered there and what could be done there had all been altered. §0/1 · #5→0 located worldhood here, in the relational distribution of what is present more than in what is present to be distributed; the castle shows that distribution being made, by two children, in the afternoon, through acts of cutting and gathering that anyone can watch.[^s0-distribution]

It shows, second, why the construction answers to something. Their castle had to be built from what the room supplied, under the conditions the room imposed. Cushions slide; a sheet sags in the middle; the dog will not stay a dragon for long without biscuits; the parent will eventually come to the door. *Vikalpa* cuts a field, and the field answers back: a cushion can be cut as a wall because it will stand on its end, and it cannot be cut as a ladder because it will not bear a child's weight. *Saṃkalpa* gathers a course, and the course meets resistance: the resolve that grown-ups may not enter holds until a grown-up enters. Each act inherits a field it did not make, cuts that field in a way the field allows, and leaves behind a changed field that the next act inherits. So the pulse is constructive because every world it yields is a made arrangement, and it is answerable because the making is done in and against a world that was already there. In the Spanda teaching the same pulse takes its widest form, in which the world itself is the world-thought of consciousness: the universal awareness makes its *viśvavikalpa* under no constraint but its own freedom, and finite awareness makes its small worlds under the constraints that the universal making has already laid down as the given world.[^s0-visvavikalpa] A children's castle is a local *kalpa*, fitted inside a larger one.

Indian traditions disagree about what such constructions are, and their disagreement decides what world-making can be trusted to do. Patañjali's *Yogasūtra* gives *vikalpa* its narrowest sense, which §0 · #1 met in the list of the mind's five fluctuations: a cognition that follows words and has no object.[^s0-ys-vikalpa] Its commentary, attributed to Vyāsa, gives an example that takes the definition to its sharpest point, the sentence "The true nature of the Self is intelligence", in James Haughton Woods's translation, in which the genitive divides into an owner and a possession what is in fact one thing, as though the self had a nature distinct from itself; the words produce a relation to which nothing corresponds.[^s0-bhasya-example] On this view *vikalpa* is language manufacturing distinctions that are not there, and the yogic discipline stills it. Buddhist logicians of Dharmakīrti's school gave conceptual construction a wider office and a sharper epistemic verdict. They held that conceptual cognition does real practical work, letting us act successfully among particulars, while its general objects and the relations it posits are constructed by the mind; Sean MacCracken describes Dharmakīrti's view of relations as "relation-as-merely-epistemic", and records what that view cost: "To suggest that *relations* are themselves in the domain of the contrived (*vikalpa* or in Dharmakīrti's words, *kalpanā*) deals an injurious insult to realists, many of whom were dualist Śaivas."[^s0-maccracken] Pratyabhijñā philosophers in Kashmir answered from the other side. Utpaladeva argued that relation is the real power of consciousness, Śakti, and the Spanda teachers held that the separation of subject and object, and with it every relation between them, is the product of a creative act of the absolute, its Māyā, and not an illusion superimposed on something else.[^s0-relation-sakti]

Each party can be heard in the castle without being flattened. A yogin is right that "the dog is a dragon" follows words and has no dragon as its object, and right that a child who could not stop seeing dragons would need help. A Buddhist logician is right that the castle's relations, wall to roof, captain to crew, inside to outside, were constructed by the children and could be taken down at bedtime. A Śaiva philosopher is right that the construction was a real power really exercised, that the castle's rules really governed the afternoon, and that the power to construct relations is not itself one more construction that some further power could take down. My own position follows from §0/1's definition of the Subject. Worlds made by *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa* are real as worlds, since they govern what appears, matters and can be done, and empty of self-subsistence, since each depends on a field it did not make and a power it did not originate; and the power that makes them is the knower's own activity in its contracted form, which §0 · #1 found already at work in the inner instrument. A world-making that knows itself as world-making can build castles and take them down. Only a world-making that has forgotten itself turns a castle into a prison.

*Saṃkalpa* has an ordinary life that makes this concrete, because the word names, among its senses, a resolve, a purpose, a definite intention, and in ritual use the formal declaration of purpose with which an act begins.[^s0-samkalpa-resolve] Modern practice has kept this sense: a yoga class may begin by inviting each person to set a *saṃkalpa*, a resolve held through the practice. Its secular form is familiar to everyone who has ever made a New Year's resolution, decided to leave a job, or made a promise. §0/1 · #2 followed one promise to its bedside: a parent who says "I will listen" after an evening that went badly, and means it, has made a *saṃkalpa*, and §0/1 showed how the promise works at once, settling the child enough to sleep and making the next question possible, while what it finally commits the parent to stays exposed to evenings neither has yet lived.[^s0-promise] A resolve gathers a course, and in gathering it remakes a world. Someone who has decided to leave a job lives the next weeks in a changed office. Their desk is the one they are leaving; the colleague's irritating habit has become something they will soon not have to bear, or, unexpectedly, something they will miss; the commute has acquired an end date; a conversation about next year's plans becomes awkward in a way it was not before. Nothing in the office has changed apart from a decision, and the decision has redistributed relevance across everything in it.

Running in time, the pulse has a temporal shape, and the shape explains both its gift and its danger. A resolve made at one moment conditions every later moment until it is fulfilled, abandoned or revised; an achieved world becomes the field from which the next resolve is made. §0/1 followed this inheritance through Whitehead's satisfaction, in which an achieved occasion becomes a datum for the occasions that follow, and through Bergson's melody, in which each note is heard through the phrase already under way; here it has its Sanskrit form.[^s0-inheritance] Danger lies in the inheritance itself. A resolve can outlive the circumstance it was made for and go on governing circumstances it was never fitted to. §0 · #1's morning shows how quickly that happens. A message read at seven became a verdict, and the verdict became a resolve, *I will defend myself*, and the resolve made a day; if the same thing happens every time a colleague asks to talk, the resolve hardens into a rule of the instrument, *whenever someone says "can we talk", prepare a defence*, and the rule goes on making the same world out of every new encounter. Here is the script of §0/1 in its genesis: a *saṃkalpa* whose occasion has gone and whose authority has stayed. Castles have their version too. A rule that grown-ups may not enter was a game on a Saturday; repeated across a childhood, in a family where adults could not be trusted, it can become the unexamined conviction of a grown person that no one may come in.

When a resolve hardens in this way, the two logics describe what happens to the people involved. In its living form, the resolve held a polarity through a shared zero: inside the castle and outside it, the children and the grown-ups, held on one axis, `(−1)/(+1)`, through the zero of the household they all belonged to, so that the game's opposition oscillated cleanly between raids and lunches and stayed a game. Hardened, the resolve counts the whole span from one pole. Inside becomes everything that is safe and outside everything that threatens, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and from the other side of the door the same reckoning makes the excluded adult into the whole of what threatens, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, the mirror of the children's `+2`; the household the axis was drawn through has dropped out of the account. Its opposite collapse cancels: a parent who, tired of tears, declares that the castle is just cushions and was never anything, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, has settled the dispute by abolishing the world in which it had meaning, and the children learn that what they make does not count. A third way is available, and the parent at the door who knocks and gives the password performs it. Now the castle is acknowledged as a world, its rules are honoured long enough for lunch to be negotiated, and the cushions go back at bedtime with the understanding that another castle can be built next Saturday. Both orientations are kept, the household read through the castle and the castle through the household: syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, at the scale of a living room.

Śaiva practice knows a deliberate use of construction against construction, which §0/1 · #5→0 named: *śuddha vikalpa*, the purified thought-construct, a thought such as "I am Śiva" cultivated until the power of construction turns back toward its source and the constructs that bound the person loosen their hold.[^s0-suddha-vikalpa] A castle shows the ordinary form of the same discipline. A child who has been told for years that grown-ups cannot be trusted is not helped by being told the opposite; "grown-ups can always be trusted" is one more castle rule, equally deaf to the grown-up actually standing at the door. What helps is a construction that carries a limit on its own authority: *this one, today, has knocked and given the password*. Such a corrective thought works as a medicine works, dosed to the condition, and like a medicine it can be stopped when the condition has passed. Its success is a reopened encounter, and the capacity to set the corrective thought down again is part of what it was meant to restore. A faithful definition, as §0/1 · #1 defined it, keeps its own determining act visible inside the account it gives, which is the same requirement put in logical form.

Buddhist analysis has a name for construction that has lost this limit, and the name reaches into technical world-making. *Prapañca*, in the early discourses, is the proliferation of conceptual elaboration, especially where differentiation has been charged by craving, appropriation and conflict; Bhikkhu Ñāṇananda, whom Gay Watson quotes, suggests that "prapañca may refer to the consequent prolificity in ideation" which follows on the first application of thought, thought generating thought out of its own products.[^s0-watson-prapanca] Śaiva sources use the same word with another value: in Dyczkowski's exposition *prapañca* is the chain of associations by which one thought leads to the next, and the manifestation of the universe follows the same principle, phenomena succeeding one another as thoughts do, so that the world's own unfolding is a chain of the kind a mind makes.[^s0-dycz-prapanca] One word names the self-sealing proliferation of a mind lost in its own constructions and the living unfolding of consciousness into a world. So a question arises: when is proliferation the articulation of a world, and when is it a construction feeding on itself? Its answer lies in what each product carries. Proliferation stays alive while each construction keeps its provenance, its relation to the field and the power that made it, and can return toward them; it seals itself when its products appear independent of the field that produced them and begin to warrant the next construction without contact with what first warranted them. That castle stayed alive because the children knew, underneath the game, that the dragon was the dog. A mind turns *prapañca* into a prison when it forgets that the dragon was a dog, and then builds its next castle on the dragon.

Institutions run the same pulse, and a sealed *prapañca* at institutional scale can send people to prison. From 1999 the British Post Office ran its branch accounts on a computer system called Horizon, and when the system showed a shortfall in a branch, the subpostmaster who ran it was held to owe the money; the public inquiry into the affair has estimated that approximately a thousand people throughout the United Kingdom were prosecuted on Horizon evidence.[^s0-horizon-inquiry] Each prosecution rested on Horizon's figures, and each conviction then counted as evidence that the figures were sound. The Court of Appeal, quashing thirty-nine of those convictions in 2021, described the mechanism in one sentence: "As each prosecution proceeded to its successful conclusion the asserted reliability of Horizon was, on the face of it, reinforced."[^s0-hamilton] A first output, *the branch is short*, had been promoted into the field from which every later judgment proceeded, *the system is reliable*, and the law lent the promotion its weight. Since 2000 the courts of England and Wales have worked with a common-law presumption that a computer was operating correctly unless there is evidence to the contrary, which a government consultation has summarised as "the computer is always right", unless someone can show it is not; and a subpostmaster to whom the Post Office did not disclose what it knew of the system's faults had no means of showing it.[^s0-horizon-presumption] In 2019 the High Court found that bugs, errors and defects in Horizon had not only the potential to cause apparent shortfalls in branch accounts but had "actually" done so, "on numerous occasions", and the judge, discussing its dealings with one witness, called the Post Office's stance "a simple institutional obstinacy or refusal to consider any possible alternatives to their view of Horizon"; Parliament quashed a further class of convictions by statute in May 2024.[^s0-horizon-judgments] The case is the castle without the parent at the door. A construction had forgotten that it was a construction, and every new encounter supplied material for it, and on my reading the office able to reopen the figures was the office whose standing depended on their soundness. At §0 · #1 *ahaṃkāra* defended the person it had become; here an institution's "we" defended its system through years of corrections that never reached anyone with the power to act on them. Written in the logics, the institution held the whole span of every discrepancy, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and the subpostmaster stood at the mirrored pole as its entire deficit, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, while the zero they shared, a branch account both had reason to make true, dropped out of the reckoning. The Court of Appeal gave that collapse its legal name: by treating a shortfall shown by an unreliable system as an incontrovertible loss, the Post Office "effectively sought to reverse the burden of proof".[^s0-hamilton]

Language models perform *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa* at working speed, and *prapañca* is their characteristic danger. A model selects each next token from a weighted range of possible continuations, and the token it commits becomes part of the context from which the next selection is made, so that every output is also the field of the next act; §5 · #1 follows that selection into its mechanics. §0/1 · #2 traced a fixed interpretation into fabrication, through the New York lawyers whose brief cited opinions ChatGPT had invented, and saw how a fitting passage, once accepted, steers further reasoning.[^s0-avianca] *Prapañca* names the wider structure of which that case was one instance, and I write it with letters chosen to stay clear of the authorial `X/x` of our core theorems. Let `F` stand for the determining field from which a step proceeds, its context, sources and learned dispositions, and let `cₙ` stand for the claim produced at step `n`. The next step can use `cₙ` while keeping it in its office, as a claim with a source, an inference and a set of rejected alternatives behind it. The trajectory proliferates in the Buddhist sense when `cₙ` is silently promoted into the determining field of the next step, `cₙ → Fₙ₊₁`, so that a previous paraphrase warrants the next paraphrase and their agreement replaces comparison with the original. A local claim, a *pratibimba*, has taken the place of its *bimba*; the dragon has been taken for the dog. The notation and the reading are mine, and the Horizon figures show that the trajectory needs no language model: it needs only an output that is allowed to stand where its source should be. Nor does the failure require a false datum. A chain of fluent, grammatical, mutually supporting elaborations can seal itself in this way, while an abundant generation can remain source-responsive and alive; the difference lies in whether each step still bears its dependence on the field it came from. Two scales of the failure stay distinct. Within a single run, an output that returns as its own premise produces circular support; across generations of training, models trained on the outputs of earlier models lose the tails of the original distribution, a degeneration that has been named model collapse.[^s0-collapse] The two share the form of a product becoming a source and differ in scale, mechanism and evidence.

So any trajectory of construction, human, institutional or artificial, can be put to a set of questions, and I offer them as the working tests this movement yields. How quickly do source links and distinctions decay across recursive steps? How much of later reasoning rests on earlier outputs rather than on independent evidence? How many live alternatives does the trajectory keep as it deepens, and can changing a premise or a source reorganise its result? Can a criticism, a retrieval or a contradiction move it into another organisation, or does it only supply more material for the existing one? Can it recover the determining text, datum or rule, or only paraphrase its own paraphrase? And are extraction, paraphrase, inference, proposition and speculation still visibly distinct in what it produces? Each question asks whether the pulse of *vikalpa* and *saṃkalpa* still answers to its field, the question the parent at the door was asking of the castle and the question no office with authority asked of Horizon. A critic that repeats the generator's assumptions deepens the same basin, while an independently sourced critic can open another. Retrieval can reopen a source relation or feed the existing narrative, and memory can carry real continuity or fossilise a contraction into a script.

Every cut the children made left something out. The cushion that became a wall did not become a boat; the gap that became a tunnel did not become a cave; the dog that became a dragon stopped, for the afternoon, being simply the family's dog. A world gathered by *saṃkalpa* is gathered from alternatives that *vikalpa* opened and then set aside, and it means what it means partly through what it set aside: the wall is a wall against the boat it might have been, and the castle a castle against the living room it was. Horizon's figures meant what they meant against an alternative the Post Office refused to keep open, that the shortfall lay in the software, and the convictions stood for as long as that exclusion held. §0/1 · #2 met this dependence in the Buddhist theory of *apoha*, exclusion, where a word is made determinate by what it rules out, and the excluded alternative is still at work in what the word means.
