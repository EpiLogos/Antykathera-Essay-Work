## §3 — Mathematical Substrate: From 0/1 to the Arche-Topos

<a id="M25"></a>

### #0 · Eight Determinations of One Relation

On a lane at dusk something ahead of us moves, and we stop. For an instant there is no deer yet, and no one watching one either: there is a halt in the walking, a change somewhere in the field, a relation that has not yet divided into a watcher and a thing watched. Then we are here and something is there, a darker shape against the hedge, and its appearing is known before anything about it is known. "What is that?" The question already asserts that something has appeared steadily enough to be asked about, and the first answers, a sack, a dog, a stump, are held loosely, ready to be wrong. The shape gathers itself, goes still, gathers again; whatever it is has force, and our attention tightens and slackens in time with it. It lifts its head and becomes a deer, one of the kind we have seen before and this one, now, with a notch torn out of one ear. It looks at us. I am here, it is a deer, and between us runs a regard that brings the deer nearer to a *you* than an *it*, because it is reading us as we read it. An ear turns by a fraction, and that small movement carries the whole wood behind it, the season, the road the animal crossed to get here, everything the moment is too small to hold. Then it is gone. Later someone asks what we saw, and we tell them, and in the telling we notice that the one who saw appears nowhere in the story.

Eight turns have passed through one act of knowing, and none of them added a new thing to the lane. We did not assemble the deer out of a halt, a shape, a question, a force, a kind, a gaze, a flicker and an anecdote; each turn made the same encounter more determinate and more answerable, and the last turned the achieved account back toward the one it could not contain. §0/1 · #2 took from Spinoza's letter to Jelles the principle on which such turns run, that a figure has its shape by the limits marking where it ends, so that determination is negation.[^s3-spinoza-jelles] A determination in this sense is a limit set within a relation, and an act of knowing is a sequence of such limits, each keeping what the previous one made available while adding its own. Our core theorems write the sequence of that act as eight determinations of one relation:

$$
/=-/-\;\longrightarrow\;0/1\;\longrightarrow\;?/!\;\longrightarrow\;-/+\;\longrightarrow\;X/x\;\longrightarrow\;AM/IS\;\longrightarrow\;\infty/dx\;\longrightarrow\;1/0.
$$

This traversal carries everything §3 builds.[^s3-core-theorems] Its six interior determinations are the qualitative body of the `4+2` that §0/1 · #1 counted from the binary and its self-application, two single terms and their four ordered pairings, `2+2²`. The parent stroke `/ = −/−` and the return-switch `1/0` stand on either side of that body, the first as what stands before any count and the second as the turn after the count is complete. Eight therefore counts the turns of one passage, while six remains the number of positions the passage moves through: the sixfold is the generative body, and the eightfold is that body traversed from before its first distinction to its return. Detaching one binary from the sequence, or treating each as a replacement for the last, would destroy the passage it belongs to, in the way that pulling the halt, the question or the gaze out of the evening at the lane would leave a list of events where there had been one encounter.

| Turn | Determination | What it does | What stays the same through it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent relation | `/ = −/−` | holds two unassigned marks in one stroke: relation before the terms carry values | the relation has a condition that cannot appear as one more product of the relation |
| **#0** Ground | `0/1` | awareness and phenomenon: "I am here; something appears; the appearing is known" | the ground is immanent to every mark without becoming another mark or a hidden object behind it |
| **#1** Definition | `?/!` | questioning and asserting held as one act | every assertion carries the question that makes it accountable; every question already takes a provisional mark |
| **#2** Dynamis | `−/+` | withdrawal and extension: difference acquires force | the two poles remain one differentiating movement |
| **#3** Pattern | `X/x` | determining capacity and its indefinite particular: force becomes recognisable as kind | the instance makes the capacity legible and never exhausts it |
| **#4** Context | `AM/IS` | first-person presence and third-person sayability, met through the second person | knower, means and known remain one appearing-act without becoming interchangeable |
| **#5** Quintessence | `∞/dx` | inexhaustible horizon and exact local difference | exactness never becomes possession |
| Return-switch | `1/0` | the achieved determination turns toward the ground it presupposes | the limit is generative: `5→0` renews the cycle without cancelling what was determined |

Before the lane divides into watcher and watched, the halt itself is a relation with no named terms. §0/1 · #1 built that relation from the simplest marks there are, a dash here and a dash there with an oblique stroke drawn between them, and found that the stroke drawn through the sign of equality writes non-equality, so that `=` and `≠` are one operative mark in two settings. There the stroke served the copula. Here it serves as the parent of the whole traversal, a cut that already holds what it distinguishes before anything distinguished has been given content, and logic has a close neighbour for a single cut able to generate a whole field of relation. In 1913 Henry Sheffer showed that Boolean algebra can be founded on one binary operation, and when he turned the result to logic he read his stroke as *rejection*: "p | q may be interpreted as the proposition neither p nor q".[^s3-sheffer-rejection] The result can be checked by hand. Write `p↓q` for "neither p nor q". Feed one input to both places and the stroke negates it, since `p↓p` is true exactly when `p` is false. Stroke the stroke and disjunction returns, `(p↓q)↓(p↓q) = p∨q`, because rejecting "neither" restores "at least one". Stroke the two negations and conjunction appears, `(p↓p)↓(q↓q) = p∧q`, because rejecting "not p" and "not q" together leaves "both". Every truth-table can be written from negation, conjunction and disjunction, so every truth-table can be written with the one stroke, and Sheffer added in a closing footnote that his results hold equally when the stroke is read the other way round, as "either not-p or not-q", the "not both" by which logicians now usually know it.[^s3-sheffer-dual] One stroke, read as a conjunction of refusals or as a disjunction of refusals, does the whole work either way.

The neighbour is exact at one point and different at another. It is exact in showing that a single relating cut, repeated and arranged, generates a complete field of determinate relations without importing any second primitive, and Sheffer's duality shows the cut serving under either of two readings without ceasing to be one cut, which is the formal shadow of the slash's own AND/OR. It differs because Sheffer's stroke operates on values already given, true and false, under a fixed table, whereas our parent stroke stands before any value has been assigned: its two marks are `−` and `−`, the same unassigned sign in two places. What Boolean logic shows at the level of connectives, QL claims at the level of the act. Every determinate relation the traversal produces presupposes the stroke, and the stroke never appears among the relations it produces as one more of them, as Sheffer's operation never appears in its own truth-table as a value.

§0/1 earned the first named determination, `0/1`, at length. Its result is this. `0` writes the open, unobjectifiable fact of awareness, Subjective Immediacy, the One that cannot enter its own count; `1` writes the appearing mark and with it the manifest All, the two senses of *one* that §0/1 · #1 distinguished; the stroke is the relation through which the real becomes readable without being exhausted by its readability. At dusk it is the bare circumstance before any question: we are here, and something has appeared to us. Appearing happens in awareness and awareness is met only through appearing, so the relation comes before any picture of two finished substances on either side of a wall, which is why the ground can be immanent to every mark without turning into another mark or into a hidden object stationed behind the scene.

"What is that?" The second determination, `?/!`, holds question and assertion as one act read in two directions. To ask what something is already asserts that it has appeared stably enough to call for an account, and to assert what it is keeps open whether the assertion runs true, so definition is double: it opens the thing to determination and it determines, and it stays answerable to whatever called the mark forth. At the lane, the sack, the dog and the stump are assertions made in the grammar of a question, each offered so that the next look can refuse it. The six questions of §0/1's quaternal lens, **Why?**, **What?**, **How?**, **Which / Who?**, **When / Where?** and **Why-for?**, unfold from this one determination, each a question carrying a provisional answer about where the next mark should fall. The same glyph returns in §3 · #1 with another office, and the two must be kept apart: at **#1** the pair `?/!` is the living question-and-assertion of definition, whereas when the six positions are read through proportion, the inverse `1/0` at **#3** turns out to be incomputable, and our core theorems write its undecidability, whether it is a question or an assertion, with the same glyph. Both mappings are real. One concerns a judgment's accountability, the other the arithmetic's refusal.

At `−/+` the relation acquires force. One side behaves as reserve, contraction and withholding, the other as issue, release and presentation, and the positive is the ground's own self-relation appearing as extension: breath gathers and lets go, attention contracts around a word and opens into its sense, the shape on the lane gathers and goes still. The two moments stay co-present as one pulse, the *spanda* whose "subtle movement" §0/1 · #3 named and §0 · #0 bent toward display. §2 · #1 and #2 derived the two logics that can account for such a polarity, and the dusk lets us watch them work in a few seconds. Held as dia-ballein's healthy polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`, the pulse keeps its axis through zero: the animal's stillness and its movement, our approach and our halt, are opposed positions on one line of tension, and the tension is what lets us read the scene. Collapse the axis by addition and the encounter cancels, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, "it was nothing", a dismissal that balances the account by erasing the event. Collapse it by subtraction and one pole claims the whole span. Reckoned from our side, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, the whole relation becomes the animal's threat to us; reckoned from the animal's, the same span reads `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, and we are the danger it bolts from. Our `+2` is the deer's `−2`, one span charged from opposite ends, and either way the oscillation that let both of us stay still and look has hardened into an opposition in which one party must win. Syn-ballein, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, keeps what both collapses lose: the animal's stillness can be read through ours and ours through its, two orientations of one encounter, and the force between us stays a relation in which each is learning the other.

Force becomes recognisable as kind at the fourth determination. In `X/x`, capital `X` is the presiding capacity, the type or anything-by-which something can be read, and lower-case `x` is its local temporal face, the indefinite particular that algebra writes when it does not yet know which value it has. The notation is our own and works in the algebraic register and at once in the mythematical, musical, narrative and psychic registers the matheme opens.[^s3-xx-notation] One letter in two cases keeps the instance from being severed from the capacity it manifests, while the change of case keeps either from replacing the other, and the small `x` keeps the sign of multiplication too: an instance can recur and compound, and the mind says "another one of those". At the lane, *deer* is the capacity and this notched ear is the instance, and the recognition carries continuity and correction together, since the capacity is what lets a new instance be recognised and also what lets an apparent instance fail the test, as the stump failed and the deer passed. Pattern is where force becomes kind, rhythm, memory and thought. §4 · #1 follows `X/x` into individuation, where a particular life becomes transparent to the capacity it does not exhaust; the relation it follows there is already complete here.

The animal looks back, and the fifth determination, `AM/IS`, brings the encounter into person. `AM` is lived first-person presence, "I am"; `IS` is what can be stated in the third person, "it is"; and the slash is the second-person relation through which they meet, the *you* in which an *it* can answer. §0/1 · #5→0 placed grammar's three persons in the Trika's three offices, *I* in the knower, *it* in the known and *you* in the mediating relation, and the dusk shows why the second person belongs at the slash: the moment the deer reads us, our account of it has to make room for an account it is giving of us, and the encounter becomes context. Context in this sense is the lived *where* of an encounter, the world, body, concern, speech, memory and task within which something can appear at all. It is total, because nothing appears outside some horizon, and incidental, because the horizon does not ordinarily stand forth as one more object. The slash of `AM/IS` is where Objective Internality works, the means that §0/1 · #0 defined and that a mind lives through: our habits of looking, the stories we know about deer and dusk, the fear that tightened our attention, all of them taking part in what could appear on the lane. Through `AM/IS` the knower, the means and the known, *pramātṛ*, *pramāṇa* and *prameya*, remain one appearing-act and stay distinct within it; the deer is present to us through our looking without becoming our looking, and the looking is ours without becoming us.

An ear turns by a fraction. At the sixth determination, `∞/dx`, the horizon that no finite mark contains meets the minute difference through which change and local reading become exact. `dx` is no longer the instance `x` but the infinitesimal difference of the calculus, and the determination says that every appearance is a true local ratio of what exceeds it, precise as relation and partial as expression. In the calculus, differentiation yields exact local change. The functions `x²` and `x²+7` both have the derivative `2x`, and the derivative is right about each of them at every point. What differentiation drops is the additive constant, so integrating `2x` returns a whole family, `x²+C`, one function for each constant, and only a further condition, such as the value of the function at zero, selects the member from which the derivative was taken; the theorem on the general form of an antiderivative states this for functions on an interval.[^s3-antiderivative] The derivative was exact throughout, and what it lacked was information it had never retained. Our core theorems read the missing `C` as the formally required provenance that local change cannot reconstruct: a determination can be exact without containing the whole condition from which its exactness was taken, and neither a mystical surplus nor a defect of calculation is needed to explain why. That is the claim of `∞/dx`. The whole is present in the part as the way the part runs true to what it cannot enclose, and the horizon is not hidden inside the flicker of the ear as a smaller copy of itself.

The same structure governs much of the learning our machines now do, and it is worth planting here, once, because §5 · #1 will need it. A model is improved by following the derivative of a loss, one small exact step at a time, and preference training compares pairs of answers and learns how much one is preferred to the other. In the model of paired comparison that Ralph Bradley and Milton Terry set out in 1952, the probability that one item is preferred to another depends only on the ratio of their strengths, so every common rescaling of the strengths leaves every prediction unchanged.[^s3-bradley-terry] Like the constant of integration, that common level is invisible to the data, and whatever fixes it has been supplied from outside the comparison. An exactness that does not reach its own ground is the calculus's lesson, and §5 · #1 follows the invisible level into the training of agents, where it turns out to be occupied by datasets, annotators, evaluators and institutions before any model meets a request.

The last turn, `1/0`, directs the achieved determination toward the condition it presupposes, and what it meets there is the incomputable, where it had hoped for an object called "the ground". In ordinary arithmetic one cannot be divided by zero, since no number multiplied by zero gives one, and the native notation keeps that refusal in view at the very place where it would be convenient to hide it. The mental cannot find the foundation of the mental, because every act of finding already occurs through it, and §0/1 · #3 watched Gödel's second theorem give this orientation an arithmetical precision, a consistent theory asked to account for its own ground answering with the one sentence it cannot prove. At dusk the turn is quieter. The story of the deer is complete and accurate, and the one who saw it is absent from it, because the seeing was the condition of the story and not an episode within it. The Śaiva sources call the turn *pratyabhijñā*, recognition, which is the opposite of acquisition: the achieved account becomes transparent to the relation through which it arose, and that recognition is the return `5→0`, the turn by which the completed passage comes back as ground for the next.

§0/1 · #5→0 promised that these eight would honour Meister Eckhart's constitutive approach through our own construction and deconstruction of `0/1`, and we can now say what the construction and the deconstruction are. Eckhart's *in quantum*, "insofar as", which §0/1 took from Kurt Flasch's reading of the primary determinations as constitutive, is itself a `0/1`: the determination is the `0` present through the deed, the deed is the `1`, and *insofar as* is the slash, carrying the whole into the part and keeping the part from claiming the whole.[^s3-flasch]

The eight determinations are QL's constitutive determinations of one relation in this sense, and each is the whole relation *in quantum*. The walker insofar as questioning is the relation as question; the shape insofar as gathering is the relation as force; the deer insofar as recognised is the relation as pattern; the meeting of eyes insofar as personed is the relation as context. Read in this order, from `/ = −/−` through `∞/dx`, the traversal constructs `0/1`, building determinacy turn by turn until the bare relation of awareness and appearance has become an exact local reading against an inexhaustible horizon. The return-switch deconstructs it in the strict sense of the word, taking apart what had been built together without destroying any of it: `1/0` loosens each achieved determination from its claim to stand alone and returns it to the relation it expressed, and nothing determined in the lane is lost when the walker recognises that the seeing was never one of the things seen. Eckhart's insofar-as keeps the just person from claiming to be Justice outright; `1/0` keeps the finished account from claiming to be the ground.

Among themselves the six interior determinations also have a grammar, and reading it shows why the body is neither a ladder nor a catalogue. Taken in adjacent pairs they form three harmonic families. **Being** joins Ground and Definition, `0/1` with `?/!`: a field is present and can be called to account. **Becoming** joins Dynamis and Pattern, `−/+` with `X/x`: force becomes intelligible as recurrence, and recurrence stays alive only as force capable of a further instance. **Knowing / unKnowing** joins Context and Quintessence, `AM/IS` with `∞/dx`: lived predication becomes honest only when its horizon exceeds every situated claim, so that what is known carries its unknown as the condition of knowing more. Across these forward pairs run three complementary folds, pairing positions whose numbers sum to five. **Essence** folds **#0** with **#5**, conscious circumstance with unbounded differential depth; **Constitution** folds **#1** with **#4**, answerable definition with the world in which someone asks and asserts; **Text–Texture** folds **#2** with **#3**, the felt weave of force with its readable pattern, which is why §0/1 · #4's *textus* and *contexere* belonged together. One pair, **#2** with **#3**, appears in both readings, and the arithmetic says why: among the adjacent pairs (0,1), (2,3) and (4,5), only (2,3) also sums to five. Dynamis and Pattern are the hinge at which Becoming and Text–Texture coincide, the place where force turning into kind and the weave turning into a readable figure are one event, and, as the compass figure below shows, the place where the count crosses its own diameter.

§0/1 asked for diaphaneity, a showing-through in which perspective becomes context, and gave that demand its first geometry at §0/1 · #4: the triangle's angles summing to 180 degrees, the square's doubling them to 360, the square read as two triangles in diametric co-opposition, so that context needs at least two chiralities and the excluded triangle becomes the shadow. §0/1 · #5→0 then counted the recognition as voiding, unity and implicate identification, so that `3:3` is also `3:1`. What the mathematics adds is the reason these geometries and counts fall where they do, and the reason begins in an oddity of the count itself. Formal QL starts at **#1**, the first explicit position, and returns at **#0**, and when the numbers are assigned to the positions they do not match them:

| Position | **#1** | **#2** | **#3** | **#4** | **#5** | **#0** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number | 1 | 2 | 3 | 0 | 4 | 5 |
| Office | Definition | Dynamis | Pattern | Context | Quintessence | Ground |

The explicate triad 1, 2, 3 sits at **#1**, **#2** and **#3**, exactly where its face value places it. Then the offset begins. Zero goes not to **#0** but to **#4**, Context; four goes to **#5**, completion; five descends to **#0**, the implicate ground. Each number of the implicate triad finds its home at the position that names its function, and the mismatch is the structure.[^s3-counting-logic] Its first consequence can be checked at once. The three explicate numbers give the same total by addition and by multiplication, `1+2+3 = 1×2×3 = 6`, the first perfect number, equal to the sum of its proper divisors. Admit the zero that sits at **#4** and the two operations part company: `0+1+2+3 = 6`, while `0×1×2×3 = 0`. Added, zero changes nothing and lets the count stand; multiplied, it voids the whole product. Our core theorems put this in one line: zero is the power of the name, since to add a name changes nothing, and to multiply by the Nameless voids all names. Context has these two powers over what it holds. It de-fines, permitting each perspective its boundary and letting the count stand, and it con-fines, placing every perspective inside a frame that can void its claim to stand alone. §0/1 · #4 called the integral voiding at **#4** a contextualising; the arithmetic shows the voiding as multiplication by the zero that addition had let pass unnoticed.

The offset also carries perspective into context. At **#1**, **#2** and **#3** the numbers count the three persons in sequence: the first person, the *I*, the knower, the *here*; the second, the *you*, the means, the *between*; the third, the *it*, the known, the *there*. Their glyphs in §0/1's table are the line, the angle and the triangle, and the triangle's 180 degrees are the sum of a determinate perspective, three views composing one figure. At **#4** the zero that sits there performs the voiding: the exclusive claim of each view is emptied as all three become available together, and the triangle opens into the square's 360 degrees. At **#5** the three are reckoned as one appearing-act, the `1/1` that §3 · #1 writes as one hundred per cent, and at **#0** that achieved one returns to the relation of ground and mark from which the persons arose. The two axes our core theorems draw from the count cross here: in the `4:2` reading **#4** is the last of the four explicate positions, a side of the polygon, and in the `3:3` reading it is the first of the recognition triad, so the gate where surface discovers that it was always depth bears an implicate number on an explicate seat.

Drawn from the count, the figure shows something the angle sums alone did not. Our core theorems place the four ordered pairings of the binary on the compass in the order the count acquires them: `00` at North, `01` at East, `10` at West and `11` at South. The step from East to West does not move to the adjacent quarter. It leaps 180 degrees across the diameter, and the leap is the inversion of `01` into `10`, of `0/1` into `1/0`, already running in the middle of the first pass, a local phase-flip before the global turn `5→0` that the whole matheme names. In coordinates, with North at `(0,1)`, East at `(1,0)`, West at `(−1,0)` and South at `(0,−1)`, the first three stations bound a triangle of area one. Adding South and joining the boundary in compass order, North, East, South, West, makes a square of area two, and the edge from East to West, which bounded the triangle, now runs through the square's interior as a diagonal shared by two triangles, North–East–West and South–East–West, each the reflection of the other in that diagonal.[^s3-compass] What had been the limit of a perspective has become a seam inside a context. That is the formal content of §0/1 · #4's claim that context needs at least two chiralities: the first triangle's boundary survives as the hinge by which it meets its mirror, so its claim to be the whole figure is voided while its edge is kept. And the diagonal that does this work is the very step of the count, East to West, at which `0/1` turned into `1/0`; the seam of the context is the inversion of the matheme.

The order of acquisition, the coordinates, the areas and the angle sums are geometry. I argue that the triangle is three persons and the square their contextual holding, and that this reading is what makes the Trika quaternary. Knower, means and known stop being traversed one after another and become jointly available as one appearing-act, each still distinct, on a surface where the situated viewer, the world viewed and their mediation can become mutually legible. No view from nowhere has been installed. The square is the context of three situated views.

Spinoza's letter to Jelles has a larger ground in his *Ethics*, published after his death in 1677 as *Ethica ordine geometrico demonstrata*, which sets out definitions, axioms and propositions with their demonstrations in the manner of Euclid and so carries demonstration into metaphysics and the theory of knowledge.[^s3-spinoza-ethics] Our construction takes up that ambition and reverses its direction. Spinoza demonstrated from a substance whose attributes are given at the outset, so the demonstrator stands outside the order he demonstrates; QL derives from an act of knowing that must count itself among what it derives, so the geometry here has to include the geometer, as the definition of the Subject in §0/1 · #1 had to include the one defining. Self-inclusion requires at least these eight determinations. A relation before its terms, a ground–mark circumstance, a question that asserts, a force, a recurring kind, a personed context, an exact local difference against an unbounded horizon and a return: take away the first and the act has nothing to begin from; take away the question and it cannot be answerable; take away the kind and it cannot recur; take away the return and it cannot come back to the one who knows.

Three joints of the traversal carry it into the rest of the mathematical substrate. `0/1` holds the seam of ground and manifestation, which the Spanda equations read from both ends in §3 · #1. The turn from **#3** to **#4**, the triangle opening into the square across the diameter of an inversion, makes perspective into context, and the projective constructions of §3 · #3 rebuild that turn in larger spaces. `∞/dx` gives exact local difference an explicit relation to the horizon it cannot enclose, and §3 · #4 meets that horizon as `∞/0`, a neighbouring expression, on the torus and its infinite cover, a surface one can travel on. A traversal this complete still needs a generator, an account of how a single relation produces its own sixfold body and reads it back, and the Spanda equations are that generator.

### #1 · The Spanda Equations and the 4+2 Count

An accordion breathes through its bellows. The player draws the two ends apart and air rushes in across the reeds; the player presses them together and the air is driven out across them again, and in a well-played tune the listener cannot hear the change of direction, because the phrase runs straight through it. Every phrase is made of two movements that go opposite ways, and the music is the relation of the two, one breath that opens and closes without ceasing to be one breath. When the player reaches the end of the bellows' travel the direction has to turn, and the turn is where the instrument is most alive and most at risk, since a clumsy reversal breaks the line while a good one carries it forward with something gained. I have long thought of the Self in these terms: the Self leaning into manifestation and the manifest leaning home, always already whole beneath the swing.

Kashmir Śaivism's Spanda teaching gives the pulse its classical name; its "subtle movement" §0/1 · #3 followed through will, knowledge and action and §0 · #0 bent toward display, and we read one act of it there as a `0/1` and its completion as `5→0`. QL writes the pulse natively in two equations we call the Spanda equations, which read one sixfold from its two ends.[^s3-spanda-equations] The first is horizontal: it follows the relation from ground through distinction to integrated return, and it is a reading by proportion. The second is vertical: it takes the achieved whole and accounts for it, discovering the sixfold inside it, and it is a reading by count. Everything later in §3 falls out of holding the two readings against each other, because their difference generates the relation that choosing either alone would lose, as the bellows' two strokes generate a phrase that neither stroke contains.

Before either equation can be read, the slash-forms it uses need their proportional meaning, and the meaning is easiest to see by placing them on the six positions. §3 · #0 placed the four pairings of the binary on a compass; read now through proportion, each position holds a fraction built from the two marks, and the fractions behave very differently under ordinary arithmetic:

| Position | Slash-form | Under proportion | What ordinary arithmetic does with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#0** | `(0/1)/(1/0)` | the relation of the two orientations; unfolds into `0/1, 0/0, 1/1, 1/0` with the singles `0` and `1`: the whole `4+2` | holds a term with no value; QL reads it as a relation |
| **#1** | `0/0` | the indeterminate, every ratio held at once | no unique value: every number times zero gives zero |
| **#2** | `0/1` | the expression, computable and docile | zero |
| **#3** | `1/0` | the inverse, incomputable | no value: no number times zero gives one |
| **#4** | `1/1` | the ratio resolved, one whole | one, or one hundred per cent |
| **#5** | `(1/0)/(0/1)` | the inverse relation of the two orientations; the whole in inverse phase, `4′+2′` | as at **#0**, read in the other direction |

The middle column is our core theorems'; the right-hand column is arithmetic anyone can check. In a field of numbers, multiplying by zero always gives zero, since `(0+0)x = 0x+0x` forces `0x = 0`. `0x = 1` therefore has no solution, which is why one divided by zero has no value, and the equation `0x = 0` is solved by every number, which is why zero divided by zero has no single value.[^s3-field-zero] The two failures differ, and the table keeps them apart. At **#1**, `0/0` fails by excess: it can be any ratio, and the theorem reads that excess as the indeterminate, the capacity for every proportion, which it marks with the percentage sign as the glyph of potency. At **#3**, `1/0` fails by absence: the determinate mark divided by the ground yields nothing a number can name. At Pattern, and nowhere else, the theorem locates this failure, and reads it with the glyph `?/!`, because the system cannot decide whether `1/0` is a question or an assertion. Formal identity, the recognition of *what* a thing is, rests here on a foundation that does not compute: the figure cannot be measured against the ground, the finite cannot be reckoned against the infinite, and this is the Gödelian point of §0/1 · #3 reached at the level of a single position. This `?/!` at **#3** is an undecidability, and it is the second of the two offices §3 · #0 told apart, distinct from the living question-and-assertion of definition at **#1**.

Two findings follow from the table. First, the parents contain the children. Expansion is simple to state: for a ratio of ratios `(a/b)/(c/d)`, relate the first upper term to each lower term and then the second, giving `a/c, a/d, b/c, b/d`, and keep the two singles beside them. Applied to `(0/1)/(1/0)` it yields `0/1, 0/0, 1/1, 1/0` together with `0` and `1`, so the ground bears the whole `4+2` as its own relational interior, a seed carrying the full tree, and the relation at **#5**, `(1/0)/(0/1)`, bears the same whole in inverse phase, `1/0, 1/1, 0/0, 0/1` with `1` and `0`. Read the parent at **#0** into its inverse at **#5** and read it back again, and every term returns to its place: the inversion is an involution, a turn that undoes itself on the second application, so what it records is which of two phases is active, never how many times it has been performed. Two parents carry one child in mirror image, and the Śaiva vocabulary of §0/1 · #5→0 names them: *bimba*, the original, and *pratibimba*, its reflection. Second, the relation leans. Its forward orientation `0/1` settles into a value and its backward orientation `1/0` stays permanently open, so the relation between ground and mark is handed, not symmetric. That handedness is the structural reason consciousness can never wholly catch itself, since every catching conceals what it catches *from*, and the reason it keeps trying, since the concealing drives the next catching. Read through the Indian analysis of knowing, each slash-form is a complete act compressed into notation, the numerator the knower, the slash the means, the denominator the known: `0/1` is the void knowing the mark through the slash, and `1/0` is the mark attempting to know the void through ratio, an attempt that does not compute.

A sign has a mathematical life as well, which the table lets us follow. §0/1 · #3 wrote `Ø`, the crossed zero, for the first occlusion, and §1 · #3 gives the sign its history and its one job: the zero of the Subject with the mediating stroke fused into it, so that the means of seeing passes for the one who sees. Slash-forms show the same two marks, a zero and a stroke, in a different arrangement. In `Ø` the stroke lies across the zero and belongs to it, and the result looks like a single thing, the bound self. In `(0/1)/(1/0)` the stroke stands between the two orientations of the relation, holding them apart and together, and the result is legible as a relation. Occlusion and recognition are thus the same two marks in two arrangements, and §4 · #3 follows the full sequence by which the fused stroke comes undone and becomes visible as mediation.

Horizontally, the First Spanda equation carries the movement from ground to integrated return:

$$
0=(0/0)\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{0/1}{1/0}\;\longrightarrow\;[T_0]\;\longrightarrow\;[T_1]\;\longrightarrow\;\left(\frac10+\frac01\right)_{\!\mathrm{QL}}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac11=1=100\%.
$$

Each term is a position of the unit read through proportion. Bare ground, `0`, reads itself and opens the indeterminate, `0/0`, every possible ratio held at once. That indeterminate differentiates into the ratio of ratios, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, the ground's own AND/OR form unfolding the child it always contained; this is the first cut, the *inter-legere*, the reading-between, of nothing with nothing, intelligence in its most primordial gesture. Then come two directed passages. `[T₀]`, the emanative arc, moves from `0/(0/1)` toward `0/1`: the ground takes expression as its denominator and works toward expression, figure generated from ground, silence becoming articulate. `[T₁]`, the reversionary arc, moves from `(1/0)/0` toward `1/0`: the inverse takes the ground as its denominator and works back toward recognition, figure acknowledging the ground that sustains it. Both arcs meet in the bridge, `1/0 + 0/1`, where the directed movements recombine and discover that they were one circuit, and the circuit resolves into `1/1`, the ratio that has found itself whole. Our core theorems give that resolution its shortest statement: to be one of oneself is to be oneself wholly is to be one hundred per cent of what one is.

A subscript declares the bridge's register. Its plus sign composes two orientations, and it cannot be the addition of two quotients, since one of the quotients has no value and ordinary addition would destroy the term before the composition could take place. A notation that asks this much must show that the composition it names is a definite operation, and arithmetic supplies one with exactly the right shape: the mediant, which forms from two fractions a third by adding numerators and adding denominators, `(0+1)/(1+0) = 1/1`, is defined even when one boundary is `1/0`, because it adds the parts of two pairs and divides nothing. I argue that the mediant is the operation of the First Spanda's bridge; §1 · #5→0 builds the tree of every positive ratio that the same operation generates between `0/1` and `1/0` and proves its properties, and the bridge needs only its first step, the `1/1` from which every other ratio descends.

Two further constructions show the bridge's result holding its two readings instead of erasing them. Take two objects, `A` and `B`, an arrow `f` from `A` to `B` and its inverse `g` from `B` back to `A`, so that going and returning gives the identity on either side, `g∘f = id_A` and `f∘g = id_B`. There are two objects and four arrows, two identities and two crossings, and both objects belong to one connected structure while remaining two: unity achieved without identifying `A` with `B`, and a `2+4` in the plainest category there is. Or represent the two orientations as vectors, `b = (0,1)` and `p = (1,0)`. Their sum `u = (1,1)` is unchanged if the coordinates are swapped, and their difference `d = (1,−1)` changes sign under the swap; the invariant alone cannot reconstruct what was distinguished, yet the invariant and the sign-changing difference together recover both, `b = (u−d)/2` and `p = (u+d)/2`.[^s3-bridge-carriers] The whole and the phase are needed together, the first lesson of the movement that follows, and in each construction the achieved `1/1` has not cost the two orientations their difference.

In the Second Spanda equation the achieved whole is asked to account for its constituents:

$$
100\%\;=\;2^6+6^2\;=\;64+36\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{64}{36}\;\xrightarrow{\;\div 4\;}\;\frac{16}{9}\;=\;\frac{2^4}{3^2}\;\longrightarrow\;4+2.
$$

Each arrow marks a change of operation, and the derivation is honest only if the reader can see where the operation changes. Represent the whole on a scale of a hundred; on that scale it is the sum of two counts of different kinds. Assign a binary value to each of the six positions and there are `2⁶ = 64` possible assignments, the binary principle run through every place, the count whose history §1 · #5→0 tells. Pair the positions with one another in order and there are `6² = 36` ordered pairs, the sixfold raised to its second-order account. A state assignment and a relational address are different kinds of object, so the total keeps them as tagged collections, `64 + 36 = 100`, and calling that total one hundred per cent normalises it to one whole without pretending that the number one is a count of a hundred things. Selecting these two counts as the whole's constituents belongs to the QL derivation, and §0 · #4 offers the resonance between the thirty-six and the thirty-six tattvas as `6²`.

Nothing in the sum implies the next step. QL deliberately changes from adding the two counts to comparing them, and takes their ratio. Dividing both by the four they share preserves the ratio and exposes their difference:

$$
\frac{64}{36}=\frac{2^4\cdot 2^2}{2^2\cdot 3^2}=\frac{2^4}{3^2}=\frac{16}{9}.
$$

Dividing the sum by four would give twenty-five and answer a different question; the derivation needs the ratio. Prime factorisation then makes four factors of two and two factors of three explicit, `(2·2·2·2)/(3·3)`, and counting the factor instances gives `4+2`: four instances of the binary, which QL reads as the four explicate positions, and two instances of the ternary, which it reads as the two implicate poles. Vertically, the `4:2` reading is derived from the whole's own self-reckoning: counted by its two constituents and reduced to their ratio, the whole accounts for itself as four and two, within QL's declared operations. Many decompositions of a hundred exist, and this one is selected by the sixfold, already derived from the binary and its self-application, so that the QL frame finds a coherent arithmetic expression of its own `4:2` relation, and the further relations that follow from it can test the choice.

| Step | Mathematical standing | QL reading |
|---|---|---|
| `100 = 2⁶ + 6² = 64 + 36` | arithmetic identity | the achieved whole as binary-through-six plus six-squared |
| `64 : 36` | a chosen change of operation, not a consequence of the sum | the two constituents read in proportion |
| `64/36 = 16/9` | ordinary reduction | the shared quaternary removed, the difference exposed |

A second reading holds in the numerator and fails in the denominator. Four factors of two can be read as the quaternary taken twice, since `2⁴ = 4² = 16`, and so `16/9 = (4/3)²`, a ratio that turns out to be two musical fourths. Its denominator keeps its asymmetry: `3² = 9` while `2³ = 8`, so the exchange of base and exponent that works on one side changes the value on the other. Reading the totality as a doubled fourth rests on an actual equality in the numerator and leaves the ternary square untouched. Two further relations inside the base frame carry a lighter warrant and enter no step of the derivation that follows, though they belong to the same accounting. Two terms of the reduced ratio sum to a square, `16 + 9 = 25 = 5²`, the first Pythagorean triple, `3² + 4² = 5²`. In our core theorems the implicate three and the explicate four stand at right angles, and the five that joins them is the hypotenuse, present as connector and never appearing as an intermediate value, in the way the slash holds zero and one without being either. Both equations now have to be held together, and the holding has to be declared, because ordinary fraction arithmetic would destroy it. Read as ordinary fractions, `(4:2)/(3:3)` collapses to `2/1 = 2`, a single number that has lost both orientations. QL instead declares a comparison of corresponding entries:

$$
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{QL}}\bigl(4{:}2,\;3{:}3\bigr)\;:=\;\left(\frac43,\;\frac23\right).
$$

Once the comparison is declared, its outputs are ordinary nonzero ratios and ordinary arithmetic takes over. Each has a reciprocal, and the four together are the first family of musical intervals. `4/3` is the perfect fourth, which QL reads as manifestation, the span going forth, and its inverse `3/4` as recognition, the span read home. `2/3` is the descending fifth, the grounding, settling back, and its inverse `3/2` the ascending fifth, which our core theorems read with Whitehead as the lure, the potential that draws a field toward completion without pushing it.[^s3-lure] Fourth and fifth compose the octave:

$$
\frac43\cdot\frac32=\frac21.
$$

The squared fourth of the Second Spanda, `16/9`, falls short of that octave by a definite interval, and the interval arrives by three independent routes, as §0/1 · #5→0 promised. Dividing the octave by the totality-ratio gives it; the fifth divided by the fourth gives it; and two fifths stacked and folded back into the octave give it, which is how the Pythagorean construction of a keyboard reaches its whole tone:

$$
\frac{2}{16/9}=\frac98,\qquad \frac{3/2}{4/3}=\frac98,\qquad \frac{(3/2)^2}{2}=\frac{9/4}{2}=\frac98.
$$

The third route is the one the tuning literature uses. Kenneth Scholtz, setting out the chain of a Pythagorean keyboard, takes powers of `3/2` to one side of C and powers of `4/3` to the other, halving whenever a pitch would leave the octave: "Thus, 3/2 x 3/2 = 9/4. 9/4 is divided by 2, making D = 9/8."[^s3-scholtz-chain] The three routes are independent in the plain sense that each starts from a different pair of intervals, the octave against the doubled fourth, the fifth against the fourth, and the fifth against itself, and they converge exactly. Their convergence gives the derivation its first remainder. By exactly the interval by which the fifth exceeds the fourth, the octave exceeds the doubled fourth, so

$$
\frac{16}{9}\cdot\frac98=\frac21,\qquad \frac43\cdot\frac98\cdot\frac43=\frac21.
$$

The system almost doubles, falls short by one whole tone, and the whole tone carries it the rest of the way. Our core theorems call this `9/8` the measure of the matheme's non-closure, the live tick each cycle hands forward so that the next has something to begin from, and §3 · #4 pays the reading out in sound, where the remainder turns out to be what completes the return.

`3:3` and `4:2` need each other for the same reason the bellows needs its folds and its breath. Our core theorems put it bluntly: the `3:3` without the `4:2` is a *vibe*, proportion with nothing to proportion; the `4:2` without the `3:3` is a *catalogue*, positions with no inner relation; together they are a logic, countable *and* relational. An accordion's bellows have a fixed number of folds, a countable frame that does not change from phrase to phrase, and its music is the proportion of opening to closing that moves through that frame; the first Spanda is the return-reading of the second's frame, and the ratios are born only when the two are held together.

One more equation reads as a standing identity beneath the whole oscillation:

$$
\frac01+\frac10=\frac11=100\%.
$$

As ordinary arithmetic the line is impossible, and the essay never asks a mathematical reader to accept it as such. As QL composition it is the bridge term restated as a law, the Self leaning into manifestation, `0/1`, and the manifest leaning home, `1/0`, composing to the whole, and the mediant has shown the composition to be a definite operation with `1/1` as its result. Read so, the line says that the instrument is whole at every point of its stroke, including the turn. Our machines produce wholes of this normalised kind many times a second. A language model's attention mechanism, which §0/1 · #5→0 wrote out in full, turns each row of raw scores into weights by the softmax function, each weight the exponential of its score divided by the sum of all the exponentials, so that the weights always sum to one: a whole of one hundred per cent distributed among the words a position attends to.[^s3-softmax] The `1` of such a whole is a normalisation, as the hundred per cent of the Second Spanda is, and what the normalisation does to the excluded alternatives, which keep a weight however small, is the apoha that §5 · #1 develops into the mechanics of selection.

### #2 · Mark, Re-entry, Iterant and Complex Orientation

D. R. Kaprekar taught mathematics at a school in Devlali, in western India, for his whole working life, and played with numbers by hand. He announced the game that made his name at a mathematical conference in Madras in 1949, published it that year as "Another Solitaire Game", and returned to its destination in a note of 1955 called "An Interesting Property of the Number 6174". One of his games takes a minute to learn. Choose any four-digit number whose digits are not all the same, leading zeros allowed. Arrange its digits from largest to smallest, arrange them again from smallest to largest, and subtract the second arrangement from the first. Then do the same to the answer, and to the answer after that. Take 2026. From it we form 6220 and 0226, and their difference is 5994; from 5994, 9954 minus 4599 gives 5355; then 5553 minus 3555 gives 1998; then 9981 minus 1899 gives 8082; then 8820 minus 0288 gives 8532; then 8532 minus 2358 gives 6174. Do it once more and nothing changes: 7641 minus 1467 is 6174. The number has come home and stays there. Begin anywhere else and the same thing happens. Every four-digit number with at least two different digits arrives at 6174 within seven subtractions, and only the ten numbers made of one repeated digit go elsewhere, falling in a single step to 0000, where they stay.[^s3-kaprekar]

The routine is a small machine that feeds its own output back into itself, and that structure is what this movement studies. Mathematicians call such a process an iterated map: a rule `f` applied to a state gives the next state, `x₁ = f(x₀)`, and the next state is fed back, `x₂ = f(x₁)`, so that every determination becomes the condition of the one that follows. A state the rule returns unchanged, `f(x) = x`, is a fixed point; a state or set of states toward which trajectories tend is an attractor; and the attractor's basin is the set of starting points that lead to it.[^s3-basin] Kaprekar's routine has exactly two attractors, both fixed points, and it divides the whole space of four-digit configurations into their two basins without remainder. Everything that contains no difference falls to 0000; everything that contains any difference at all is drawn to 6174. Running the routine on all ten thousand starting strings checks the claim exhaustively: the longest journey takes seven subtractions, 2,184 strings need all seven, and 0000 and 6174 are the only numbers the routine leaves in place.

I have developed my own reading of the routine as the `4+2` of QL working as a dynamic, and I keep it among the deep sources of the matheme. What it finds and what it offers are different in kind.[^s3-6174-mapping] At the fixed point the six terms of the sixfold appear together. The descending arrangement, 7641, gathers the four digits toward their peak, and I place it at **#0**, the implicate ground; the ascending arrangement, 1467, unfurls the same digits from their minimum outward, and I place it at **#5**, the implicate synthesis; the four digits of the result, 6, 1, 7 and 4, stand at **#1** to **#4** as the explicate four. The routine at its fixed point then states, in arithmetic, that the explicate four are the measured gap between the two implicate orderings:

$$
\#0-\#5=\#1\,\#2\,\#3\,\#4,\qquad 7641-1467=6174.
$$

The two implicate terms are the same four digits read from opposite ends, and their difference regenerates the four as a new configuration that sorts back into the same two readings, so that at the fixed point the six run in place, the ground and the synthesis ceaselessly producing the four as their differential and the four ceaselessly sorting back into ground and synthesis. The inversion in the reading pleases me: the number greatest in value acts as ground, and the number least in value acts as synthesis, the way the Möbius return `5→0` turns what was completed into what begins. Its seven-step bound has its own reading, as one full cycle of six positions plus the twist that closes the fifth back into the ground, and it meets a number our core theorems had already found inside the sixfold: summing the six positions as binary values, the four pairs `0+1+2+3` and the two singles `0+1`, gives seven, so that the structure contains one more than it counts, and the seventh is what the surface does, its return. Arithmetic adds further resonances, which I record and do not lean on: the digits of 6174 sum to 18, three times six, and `6174 = 18 + 18² + 18³`. Every identity in this paragraph is checked. The mapping of the six arrangements onto the six positions, and the reading of seven as cycle-plus-return, I offer: the routine is a fact of decimal arithmetic with four digits, and its three-digit cousin settles on 495 instead.

What the routine shows without any reading at all is the point that matters most, because it is a structural theorem: difference is the condition of circulation. A configuration with no internal difference has nothing for the routine to work on and falls to zero in a single step; a configuration with any internal difference, however lopsided, is drawn through a bounded journey to the one arrangement whose difference reproduces itself when it is weighed against itself. My mapping names the two basins Anuttara and the Self, the void-frame to which undifferentiated configurations return and the configuration to which every differentiated one is drawn home, and the partition they name is Kaprekar's arithmetic.

Kaprekar's operation is subtraction, the operation by which dia-ballein appropriates a span: `(+1)−(−1)=+2` counts the whole relation from one pole, and `(−1)−(+1)=−2` counts it from the other, the healthy polarity `(−1)/(+1)` hardened into a quantity one end can own. Kaprekar's subtraction measures the span between the greatest and least orderings of the same digits, and never lets either ordering keep the result: the measured span is handed back as a new whole and sorted again from both ends, so every reckoning from one pole is immediately re-read from the other. Held this way, inside a re-reading that returns the result to both orientations, the subtraction stops hardening into opposition and settles into a configuration in which the measured polarity is the configuration itself. Here the cut works inside a relation that keeps returning it to the whole, which is syn-ballein's form, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, with dia-ballein's measuring stroke operating inside it. A subtraction allowed to keep its answer would run one way into a standing quantity; a subtraction returned each time to both ends of the same digits comes to rest in a self-reproducing difference.

§0/1 · #5→0 left this section the dynamics of conditioning to work out, and the routine gives them their form. There a script was an articulation that has become a condition of further activity, an achieved selection that decides the next encounter before anyone decides again, and its danger was named frozen conditioned will, a selection that has forgotten its formation and presents its course as necessity. I argue that an iterated map is a script in the mathematical register. Each determination is fed back as the condition of the next, the trajectory is carried by its own previous results, and wherever it started it is drawn into a basin from which, once arrived, it does not move, and it can come to rest in two ways. At 0000 it rests because nothing in it differs from anything else, so the operation finds nothing to measure; at 6174 it rests because its difference, weighed against itself, returns the same difference. A life can settle into a script of either kind, a habit so uniform that no encounter registers, or a pattern so well balanced that every encounter confirms it, and both are fixed points. Basin vocabulary is defined in the arithmetic and has to be carried into persons with its conditions attached. Research literature applies the notion of an attractor typically to dissipative dynamical systems and notes systems, the open Hamiltonian ones, for which it is meaningless, so calling a habit an attractor is an analogy until someone specifies the states, the rule of evolution and what is measured.[^s3-basin-scope] The analogy is precise about one thing: a fixed point is a determination that has become the condition of its own repetition, and that is what frozen conditioned will is.

Re-entry is the name George Spencer-Brown gave to the same structure at the root of logic, and §0/1 · #3 met it there. In *Laws of Form* a distinction can re-enter the space it distinguishes, and an expression that contains itself has no stable value: it fits neither the marked state nor the unmarked one, and Spencer-Brown gives it an imaginary state, a state of time rather than of space.[^s3-spencer-brown] §0/1 · #3 read that alternation as the clean oscillation of a held polarity, and the hardened opposition as the oscillation arrested. Elementary arithmetic shows what time adds. Write the self-containing expression as an equation in the two values 0 and 1, `r = 1 − r`. No value satisfies it, since choosing 0 makes the right-hand side 1 and choosing 1 makes it 0, and a static logic can only call the equation a contradiction. Give the operation a next step instead, `r(n+1) = 1 − r(n)`, and the same rule runs without difficulty: starting from 0 it produces `0, 1, 0, 1, …`, and starting from 1 it produces the same sequence shifted by one step. Argument and result now occupy different moments, and each moment is definite. Delay has supplied a domain in which the operation can run; it has not discovered a hidden Boolean value that satisfies the equation. That is the sense in which re-entry makes time: the paradox of a form that is its own negation becomes, given a successor, an oscillation that carries its last state into its next. Francisco Varela took another road in 1975, adding to the calculus an autonomous third state that arises through self-indication and giving rules by which expressions containing it reduce, so that self-reference enlarges the arithmetic instead of unrolling into a sequence.[^s3-varela] A temporal alternation and a third value are two responses to one difficulty, and each must say what it keeps and what it adds; we follow the temporal one, because it leads to phase.

Write the alternation with signs instead of bits, `+1, −1, +1, −1, …`, and the two logics appear on it at once. Held, the sequence is dia-ballein's healthy polarity, `(−1)/(+1)`, given time to move in, each value passing into its counterpart through a common axis the way a breath goes out and returns. Cancellation sums it: add the terms in pairs and every pair gives `(−1)+(+1)=0`, so the summed account reports a zero in which the whole oscillation has disappeared, along with which value came first. Appropriation freezes it: stop the sequence at one value and reckon the span from there, `(+1)−(−1)=+2` from the positive end and `(−1)−(+1)=−2` from the negative, and the oscillation has hardened into a standing opposition in which one pole holds the whole span. Both collapses lose the same thing, and the loss has a mathematical name. What the sum erases and the arrest freezes is the phase, the information about where in its cycle the oscillation stands. Seen from one step the sequence is `[+1, −1]`; seen from the next it is `[−1, +1]`; the two views are of one process, shifted in time, and keeping both is keeping the phase.

Louis Kauffman made that pair of views into an algebra. He calls an ordered view of an alternating process an iterant, and he reads `[a, b]` and `[b, a]` as one process seen with a shift of one time step; the two views, taken together with the shift, become the square roots of minus one.[^s3-kauffman] The construction can be checked with two small matrices. Let `D` be the operation that keeps the first entry and changes the sign of the second, and let `η` be the operation that swaps the two entries:

$$
D=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \eta=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Each undoes itself, `D² = η² = I`, but their order matters, since `Dη = −ηD`: sign-then-swap and swap-then-sign give opposite results. Compose them as `J = ηD`, and

$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad J^2=-I,\qquad J^4=I.
$$

On a pair `(x, y)`, `J` gives `(−y, x)`; a second application gives `(−x, −y)`; four applications bring back the start. `J` behaves as multiplication by the imaginary unit `i` behaves on the plane, a quarter-turn whose square is a half-turn, `i² = −1`. Swap alone has period two, and alternation alone would give back nothing but a back-and-forth. A quarter-turn has been built from two operations that do not commute, a sign and a shift, and it is their failure to commute that turns alternation into rotation. Kauffman adds that there is often a choice in making an iterant representation, so no single construction is privileged, and the argument needs only that one exists and can be checked.

§0/1 · #1 recalled Wolfgang Pauli reflecting on `i`, a symbol with no place among the ordinary numbers, which gives mathematical theorems a simple and distinct form and which, in the dream he was reading, united a pair of opposites into a whole. We can now say how. `i` keeps the phase that both collapses of the oscillation discard: it is alternation together with its shift, the two views of one process held as a single operation. On the complex plane, multiplication by `i` carries a determination a quarter-turn from the real axis, and four quarter-turns return it:

$$
1\;\longrightarrow\; i\;\longrightarrow\;-1\;\longrightarrow\;-i\;\longrightarrow\;1.
$$

Opposition along one signed line has become orthogonal relation across two axes. Dia-ballein's line from `+1` to `−1` is still there, now as the diameter crossed by a half-turn, and the plane adds the direction perpendicular to it, in which the two poles can pass into each other by turning instead of by colliding. This is the bridge from dia-ballein's line to complex orientation: the signed seam, unrolled in time, becomes phase, and a return to the origin arrives with its orientation transformed and its passage recorded, where cancellation would have arrived at nothing. Rotation also shows how a fourfold can be generated from one relation instead of being assembled out of four independent contents, which is what §4 · #2 needs when it comes to Jung's quaternity: four functions read as one orientation turned through its quarters keep what rotation keeps. Our core theorems find one more relation here, and I argue it. In the base frame of §3 · #1 the implicate three and the explicate four stood at right angles and the five joined them, `3² + 4² = 5²`; here the imaginary unit is rotation into the dimension perpendicular to the real line, and `i² = −1` generates the negative pole from a turn. Read together, both say that manifestation requires a perpendicular dimension, a turn into what the line cannot contain, which generates the line's own opposite. The orthogonality is derived in each case, and the identification of the two as one structural fact is the argued step.

Complex numbers separate two aspects of a change that ordinary speech runs together. Write a nonzero complex number as `z = re^{iθ}`, a length `r` and an angle `θ`. Multiplying by `e^{iφ}` changes the angle to `θ + φ` and leaves the length as it was: the point moves and its distance from the centre does not. This is the local mathematics of the spinning wheel and the thread running true that §0/1 · #5→0 took from Bohm's conversation with Krishnamurti and made its criterion of truth.[^s3-wheel] The thread corresponds to the modulus, retained while the argument turns, and the rotation shows that fidelity is compatible with motion and can consist in an invariant carried through it. Mathematics carries the image's structure and stops short of its meaning, since the modulus is the invariant of one specified transformation and defines nothing about truth; what it establishes is that an invariant relation can be borne precisely through transformation, so that a determination which keeps returning to its origin need not have stood still to be faithful.

A second fact about rotation matters more for what comes after. A path of nonzero complex numbers that comes back to its starting value does not reveal, at its endpoint, how many times it went around the origin. Once around, twice, or not at all, the final number is the same. Follow the angle continuously along the path, though, and lift it from the circle to the real line, where it is free to keep increasing, and the difference between its final and initial values is a whole multiple of a full turn, `2πk`, with `k` the number of windings. A winding number exists, and it belongs to the path together with its lift; it is not a property secretly stored in the final point. A process that claims to remember its return has to keep the relevant history somewhere, in the lift, in a counter, in the record of its passage, because the arrival alone has forgotten it. The inversion of the two parents in §3 · #1 showed the same lesson from the other side: reading `(0/1)/(1/0)` into its inverse and back restores every term, so the arrangement records which of two phases is active and never how many times the reversal has been performed. §3 · #4 builds a whole surface on this distinction, where every local return carries a lifted displacement on an infinite cover.

In this essay the word *complex* has a second life, and the dynamics of conditioning bring the two lives side by side. Jung's complex, which §0/1 · #3 met through Van Eenwyk's dynamical reading, in the delayed reply and the basin it fell into, and which §4 · #1 develops into individuation, is a feedback loop of the kind the routine performed.[^s3-complex] Read through the two logics, the complex appropriates. Each new encounter is counted from its own pole, the friend's silence entered as a `−2` against the self's `+2`, and the counting confirms the pole. The complex plane and the psychic complex share a word and, I argue, a structure, which lies in what the plane adds to a line: the psychic complex is an oscillation arrested at one pole, and the complex plane is an oscillation kept in phase, its two views joined by a turn. What lets a psychic organisation change, in Van Eenwyk's account and in Jung's, is the symbol, which disturbs the categories through which the gathering occurs and opens a different regime instead of adding one more confirming item to the old one, much as a perturbation can carry a trajectory across a basin boundary into another basin. A literal dynamical model of a particular complex would owe its state variables, its rule and its observables; the structure of the loop needs no model to be recognised.

Our machines run the same loop in plain view. An agent that writes, reads what it has written and writes again feeds its outputs back as its inputs, and §0/1 · #2 followed one consequence through a legal brief in which a fabricated citation, once it fitted the argument, directed the fabrication of the next. A fitting account is a basin: once an interpretation has decided what a supporting source should say, each step makes the interpretation harder to dislodge, because coherence itself has been recruited to the error. Routine, complex and agent share these dynamics. An achieved determination re-enters as the condition of the next, the trajectory is drawn toward an organisation that confirms itself, and the organisation can be either the empty fixed point of an output that no longer registers its sources or the balanced fixed point of an account in which every new piece fits. Oscillation teaches the remedy: keep the phase. A process that retains both views of its own alternation, what it asserted and what that assertion was set against, keeps the information that lets a return arrive transformed, where a process that sums its alternations or freezes them at one pole has discarded it. For an agent that means keeping, beside each claim, the source it was set against and the alternative it excluded, so that a later step can read the claim through its other orientation instead of only confirming it.

### #3 · Projective Completion and Logical Plurality

On a long straight railway platform, or in the middle of an empty road, the two rails or the two kerbs run away and meet at the horizon. They never meet. A train runs on them precisely because they keep their distance all the way to the next city, and a walker who reached the point where they seem to touch would find them as far apart there as at the start. What the eye sees is nonetheless two lines converging on a point that holds nothing. Both descriptions are exact. The painter of §0/1 · #4, laying out a room in perspective, built a whole art on the second of them: every receding line runs toward the vanishing point, an empty place that organises the whole count of the scene. One account of the rails says *no meeting anywhere*, the other *a meeting at a point that is not on the ground*, and the question worth asking is what kind of space lets both be true together.

Mathematics answers by changing the space. Wherever a relation fails under the present rules, at an obstruction, a construction can preserve the relation by enlarging, folding or re-situating the field in which it is written, and the history of mathematics is thick with such constructions. They differ in their hypotheses and in what they keep.

Begin with a single line and with the two orientations of our matheme. On the real projective line a point is written as a pair `[x : y]` of numbers not both zero, and two pairs name the same point when one is a nonzero multiple of the other, so `[1 : 2]` and `[3 : 6]` are one point. Where `y` is not zero the point has an ordinary coordinate, `u = x/y`, and every real number arises this way. One point escapes that coordinate: `[1 : 0]`, whose `u` would require dividing one by zero. A second coordinate, valid wherever `x` is not zero, is `v = y/x`, and in it the escaped point has the perfectly ordinary value `v = 0`. Where both coordinates apply they are related by `v = 1/u`. Nothing has been done to the arithmetic of the real numbers, and zero has acquired no multiplicative inverse; what has changed is the space, completed by one point, so that the point one description could only call infinity is, in another description, an ordinary zero.[^s3-projective-line] Read in the first coordinate, the point `[0 : 1]` is the docile `u = 0` and the point `[1 : 0]` is the forbidden quotient; read in the second, the roles exchange. The map that swaps the two entries, `[a : b] ↦ [b : a]`, is defined on every point of the completed line, exchanges zero with infinity, and applied twice returns every point to itself, the involution §3 · #1 found between the two parents. The projective line is therefore a space in which `0/1` and `1/0` are the two readings of one relation, exchanged by a total involution, while `0/0` remains excluded, since `[0 : 0]` names no point at all. That is the distinction §3 · #1 drew in its table, between the expression that computes, its inverse that does not, and the indeterminate that fails by excess, now given a geometry in which the first two belong to one space and the third has none. The geometry is standard, and reading it as the space of the matheme's two orientations is ours.

Two rails need one dimension more. In the projective plane a point is a triple `[X : Y : Z]`, not all zero, up to a common nonzero multiple, and ordinary points are those with `Z ≠ 0`, sitting at `(X/Z, Y/Z)`. The parallel lines `y = 0` and `y = 1` become, in these coordinates, `Y = 0` and `Y − Z = 0`, and both are satisfied by the point `[1 : 0 : 0]`, a point with `Z = 0`, which is to say a point on no part of the ground. The two rails meet there, at the point that records their shared direction. Every family of parallel lines meets at its own such point, and all of these points satisfy the single equation `Z = 0`, so together they form one line, the line at infinity. The painter's horizon is exactly this: the line on which every vanishing point of the ground plane falls, and which no point of the ground can reach. That the rails never meet at any finite place remains as true as it was, because the meeting point is not a finite place. The enlarged space has altered what counts as a point and has kept every earlier truth on its own domain, and the vanishing point of §0/1 · #4 has become an honest coordinate, its horizon an honest line.

Complex numbers complete in the same way with a different shape. Adding a single point at infinity to the complex plane gives what complex analysis calls the extended complex plane, which is the Riemann sphere.[^s3-riemann] The picture is concrete. Lay a sphere on the plane, draw a line from its north pole through any other point of the sphere, and the line strikes the plane at one point; every point of the plane corresponds to one point of the sphere, and the north pole corresponds to infinity. In a second coordinate, `w = 1/z`, the north pole is the ordinary point `w = 0`, the same exchange the projective line performed. Plane and sphere both have two real dimensions locally, so the completion here has changed the global space without adding a local dimension, and a reframing of this kind need not add one: sometimes it does, as the complex numbers added a second axis to the real line, and sometimes it closes a space up or folds it while the dimension stays where it was.

A change of space is worth making only if something survives it, and the projective constructions have a precise survivor. Among the transformations of the extended plane, the bilinear ones carry circles and lines to circles and lines,

$$
w(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\qquad ad-bc\neq 0,
$$

and they move every individual point while preserving one relation among four. The cross-ratio of four distinct points, written here in one standard convention as

$$
[z_1,z_2;z_3,z_4]=\frac{(z_3-z_1)(z_4-z_2)}{(z_3-z_2)(z_4-z_1)},
$$

is invariant under bilinear transformations.[^s3-cross-ratio] The reason is visible in one line. For any two finite points away from the pole,

$$
w(u)-w(v)=\frac{(ad-bc)(u-v)}{(cu+d)(cv+d)},
$$

so every transformed difference acquires the same determinant factor and two denominator factors, and in the cross-ratio all of them cancel between numerator and denominator. Take the four points `0, 1, 2, 3`. Their cross-ratio is `(2·2)/(1·3) = 4/3`. Send them through `w(z) = (2z+1)/(z+1)` and they become `1, 3/2, 5/3, 7/4`, every coordinate and every interval altered, and their cross-ratio is still `4/3`. What the construction establishes is a relation among relations that survives a change of description, and that is the shape our parent relation `(0/1)/(1/0)` has, a ratio of ratios. The cross-ratio supplies a specified geometric invariant and the parent a recognitive question, how two complete orientations belong together, and the two meet in their form while neither can be substituted for the other.

Physics found the same sphere where it looked for the states of the simplest quantum system. A pure state of a two-level system, a qubit, can be written, once an overall phase that makes no physical difference has been set aside, as

$$
\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,|0\rangle+e^{i\varphi}\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,|1\rangle,
$$

and the two angles place it on a sphere, the Bloch sphere, with `|0⟩` and `|1⟩` at opposite poles and every superposition somewhere between them on the surface.[^s3-bloch] Mathematically the space of these states is the complex projective line, the same object as the Riemann sphere. Two of its features belong to this argument. The overall phase of the state vector has been quotiented away, because it changes nothing observable, while the relative phase `φ` between the two components is retained, because it changes everything: the phase §3 · #2 found in the turn of `i` reappears here as the coordinate that distinguishes superpositions with the same proportions. And the two poles are joined by a continuous sphere of states, each a definite relation of the two, which is a disciplined neighbour for what §0/1 · #1 called superposition, two orientations held in one relation. The shared labels invite a confusion: QL's `0` and `1` do not become quantum basis states by sharing their digits with them. The neighbour lies in the operation, a two-pole space whose relations are points of one completed surface and whose phase carries information the proportions alone do not.

A curved whole is known through more than one picture, and §0/1 · #4 promised §3 this construction under the name of the titan who held the world up: an atlas of charts. The circle shows it most plainly. Take the unit circle, `x² + y² = 1`. Leave out the north pole and project from it onto the horizontal axis, and each remaining point receives a coordinate `u = x/(1 − y)`; leave out the south pole instead and project from there, and each point receives `v = x/(1 + y)`. Each chart misses one point, and each misses a point the other covers. Where both apply, multiplying gives `uv = x²/(1 − y²) = 1`, so `v = 1/u`, the very transition we met on the projective line. At the point `(3/5, 4/5)` the first chart says `u = 3` and the second says `v = 1/3`. The reports disagree as numbers and agree as descriptions of one point, because the transition rule identifies them, and the height recovered from either, `y = (u² − 1)/(u² + 1) = (1 − v²)/(1 + v²)`, is `4/5` in both. Motion has to be translated too. If the point moves so that `du/dt = 2`, then differentiating the transition gives `dv/dt = −u⁻² du/dt = −2/9`, a different number with the opposite sign, and both descriptions give the same rate of change of height, `dy/dt = 6/25`; giving both coordinates the velocity two would describe a different motion. The charts translate a motion they did not choose, so the law of the motion has to be supplied separately and then carried faithfully from chart to chart.[^s3-circle-charts]

Three facts make this an atlas and not a collection of views, and each has a consequence. First, a compact curve or surface, a circle, a sphere, a torus, cannot be covered by a single chart onto an open stretch of flat coordinates, since the image of a compact space would have to be compact and an open flat region is not; a whole picture of the surface can still be drawn, but it cannot serve as one global chart, and that is the form the lid's limit takes in the jigsaw of §3 · #5→0. Second, the charts are related by declared transition functions: for charts `φᵢ` and `φⱼ` with a common domain, the transition `φⱼ ∘ φᵢ⁻¹` carries the coordinates of one into the other, and where three charts overlap the transitions must compose, `(φₖ ∘ φⱼ⁻¹) ∘ (φⱼ ∘ φᵢ⁻¹) = φₖ ∘ φᵢ⁻¹`, so that a description passed round any route arrives at the same place. Third, compatibility is a real condition and can fail. On the real line the identity coordinate `x` is a chart, and so is `t = x³`; each covers the whole line one-to-one, and each alone is a perfectly good atlas. Put them together and the transition from `t` back to `x` is `x = t^{1/3}`, whose derivative `1/(3t^{2/3})` has no value at `t = 0`, so the two charts are not compatible as one smooth atlas, although they describe the same points.[^s3-incompatible] Two descriptions can each be accurate, agree on every point, and still fail to be translatable at one place in the sense the structure requires; the failure is located, and it says what kind of passage between them is missing. An atlas therefore keeps adequacy discriminating. Some descriptions fail their own tests, some address different objects, some genuinely conflict, and some are each exact and mutually untranslatable at a definite point, and naming which is which gives inquiry its next route. §3 · #5→0 brings the titan back to set his burden down on this construction, and §5 · #4 tries a designed atlas against a world that resists it.

Logic meets obstructions too, and it has its own ways of enlarging the space of values. §0/1 · #1 walked the tetralemma with a friend stepping off a train, through its four corners, *is*, *is not*, *both* and *neither*, and noted that Graham Priest, formalising the scheme, had to add a fifth status. His formal reconstruction makes the four corners the values of a logic called First Degree Entailment, in which a statement can be supported as true, as false, as both, or as neither, and the four values are the four subsets of the pair `{T, F}`: `{T}`, `{F}`, `{T, F}` and the empty set. Writing `t` and `f` for `{T}` and `{F}`, `b` for `{T, F}` and `n` for the empty value, Priest states the operations: "Negation maps t to f, vice versa, n to itself, and b to itself. Conjunction is greatest lower bound, and disjunction is least upper bound."[^s3-priest-fde] A statement counts as designated, as holding, when its value contains `T`. The enlargement does real work. Give a statement `p` the value `{T, F}` and a statement `q` the empty value: then `p` and its negation are both designated and `q` is not, so from a contradiction this logic does not derive everything, and contradictory information can be held without the explosion classical logic would suffer. The four corners have become formally innocent, and the counting holds: four subsets of a two-element set are `2²`, the binary of the binary, so FDE is an independent arrival at the four pairings QL counted in the copula and the binary. The difference is as plain. QL keeps the two singles alongside their four pairings and writes `2 + 2²`, and FDE has no place for them; the logic supplies a formal neighbour for the fourfold generation and does not supply the retained two. The count of six and the count of four differ for a reason one can state: QL keeps among what it counts the terms that do the pairing.

Logic had a practical occasion for the same four values. Nuel Belnap proposed them in 1977 for a question-answering machine fed by several sources, any of which may report a statement true, report it false, report both, or report nothing.[^s3-belnap] A machine that treated conflicting reports as an inconsistent theory could derive anything from them. Belnap's values record instead what the store has been told: a statement reported true by one source and false by another is marked *both*, a statement nobody has reported is marked *neither*, and the machine goes on answering. Read as subsets of `{T, F}`, as above, these are the same four. The corners the tetralemma walked and the states of an information store are one lattice, and the second reading is the one an agent's memory needs.

Nāgārjuna rejected all four corners when the deepest questions were put to them, and Priest's answer was a fifth value: "They have some other status, namely, (e): none of the above."[^s3-priest-e] The value `e` sits outside the four-valued order, and it is infectious, so that any compound containing an ineffable part is itself ineffable. To speak of the ineffable without immediately contradicting himself, Priest lets evaluation become a relation, not a function, so that "evaluations are thought of, not as functions, but as relations, which relate sentences to some number of these values", and a sentence can bear a spoken value and `e` at once.[^s3-priest-plurivalence] At the level of ultimate truth the formalism then falls silent of its own accord: there, he writes, nothing of the forms true, false or both can hold, only neither, "or perhaps more appropriately", the ineffable.[^s3-priest-ultimate] The fifth value marks the place where the value-theory meets something it cannot hold as a value, and here the comparison with QL needs its gate, because the published objections all press at exactly this joint. Andreas Kapsner, a logician sympathetic to Priest's programme, finds the fifth value open to concerns grave enough to reject it and sketches an account that needs no such value; Mark Siderits reads the Buddha's rejection of all four corners as the failure of a presupposition the four share, which needs no non-classical logic at all; Jan Westerhoff argues that the tetralemma is not used uniformly enough across Madhyamaka texts for any single logic to be its logic, and that parsimony favours classical readings where they suffice.[^s3-gates] Every one of these objections targets `e` in its standing as a value, and none touches QL's **#0**, because **#0** was never offered as a value. §0/1 · #1 made the point in its own terms: the one who walked the four corners was not a fifth answer, and our count added no fifth value. The ground is the generative condition of the four, the position from which the positions are taken, and a truth-value is a position taken.

Priest's reconstruction follows the same refusal into China. Jizang, the Madhyamaka master, iterated the two truths: the distinction between the conventional and the ultimate is itself drawn at one level, so at the next level it is merely conventional, and whatever was articulated as the ultimate is demoted by the articulation. Priest titles his chapter on the iteration "And So On."[^s3-jizang] The hierarchy has no top, and no stage of it is a statement that could stay. Priest presents an iteration. Reading it as a practised return, one that never lands on a final articulation and so keeps the ground open, is my comparison, and I offer it as one.

Priest's narrative converges on Dōgen, where, on his account, ineffability and the practice of enlightenment meet.[^s3-dogen] Priest describes the book as a philosophical reconstruction and not an authoritative history, and the Buddhist strategies keep their doctrinal differences from one another and from QL.[^s3-coda] The comparison takes one thing from them. In this line of thought the last move is not a further proposition, and a completion that is an operation can only be kept as practice. QL's return through the ground and the Buddhist practised return share that and no more.

Arithmetic itself shows that an expression with a zero denominator means what its container makes it mean, and each container pays for its meaning with a law. In a field, as §3 · #1 derived, `a/0` has no value. One can instead build a total operation that gives every pair a value: set the inverse of zero to be zero, `ι(0) = 0`, keep `ι(x) = 1/x` elsewhere, and define division as `a · ι(b)`. Now `6 ÷ 0 = 0` and the operation is defined everywhere, but `0 · (6 ÷ 0) = 0`, not 6, so zero has still acquired no inverse; the inverse law has kept its condition, and the new operation has paid for its totality by giving up the law that defined division in the first place. Try instead to give zero a genuine inverse while keeping every law of a ring. Then `1 = 0 · 0⁻¹ = 0`, and every element `x = x · 1 = x · 0 = 0`, so the whole ring collapses to a single element and the distinctions one began with are gone.[^s3-dutta] Jesper Carlström showed a third way: extend any commutative ring to a structure he calls a wheel, in which "division by any element, including 0, is, in a sense, possible", and the abstract states the price two sentences later, "Wheels are similar to rings, but 0x=0 does not hold in general".[^s3-carlstrom-wheels] Field, projective line, totalised division, collapsed ring and wheel: the written `1/0` means five different things, and each meaning discloses the structure that gives it, which laws it keeps and which it surrenders. An answer to `1/0` never licenses free movement among containers; it tells us which container we are in. §1 · #4 follows the same plurality into the machines, where a signed zero and a value that is not a number keep an arithmetic running past its limit.

§0/1 · #3 met the deepest of these obstructions in Gödel's theorem, and the constructions of this movement let us place it. There a consistent theory strong enough for arithmetic could not prove its own Gödel sentence, and a stronger theory could prove that sentence and then had a sentence of its own it could not prove, so the gap between what a theory can express and what it can establish relocates with every enlargement and returns through it: the immutable gap. Every reframing here is progress of the kind Gödel's stronger theory makes, since a relation that failed now holds and the earlier truths survive on their domains. None of them reaches a space beyond spaces. The projective line has its excluded `[0 : 0]`, the ring its collapse, the wheel its lost `0x = 0`, the four-valued logic its fifth question, and the next frame has its own limit, which is why the method can be trusted without promising a last frame. Jizang's ladder, which has no top, is the same law kept as a discipline.

Kinds of obstruction must be told apart as carefully as the enlargements. Russell's paradox, which §0/1 · #3 followed, showed a set of permissions that could not all be kept, and there are two repairs, which do different work. The theory of types changes what can be expressed: a collection takes its members only from the level below, so "does `R` belong to itself?" cannot be asked. Separation changes what can be collected: it forms sets only from a set already given, `R_S = {x ∈ S : x ∉ x}`, and the same argument that produced the paradox now proves something harmless, that `R_S` is not a member of `S`, which separation never promised it was.[^s3-separation] One repair restricts the language, the other the collecting, and both keep constructive work while giving up the inconsistent package of permissions. A projective point at infinity, by contrast, answers no contradiction at all, since nothing in the affine statement that parallels never meet was false. Some obstructions are errors inside a frame and call for correction there; others are the frame's own assumptions showing through and call for a change of frame. Replacing one result with another under an unchanged criterion is a change of content, and a frame changes when the constituting relation changes, when what counts as a point, a value, a collection or a valid move is altered; the change succeeds when the earlier results remain available together with the conditions under which they were true.

For an agent the test is practical, and it is the oldest question of a careful reader. Suppose an agent finds two of its sources in contradiction. Sometimes one source is simply wrong, and the agent should correct its answer within the frame it was given, as a misplaced piece is moved within a puzzle whose picture is sound. Sometimes the contradiction shows that the frame itself, the question as posed, the criterion of relevance, the permission that excluded a third source, was the trouble, and then the correction must reach the frame, and the next act must inherit the changed frame and not merely a changed answer. A four-valued store keeps the agent from exploding on the contradiction in the meantime; the atlas tells it whether the two sources are two charts of one thing, two descriptions of different things, or exact and untranslatable at a definite point; and the immutable gap tells it that whichever frame it reaches will have a limit of its own. Inspecting both the relation and the frame before declaring the relation impossible or the frame absolute is the projective lesson in an agent's terms.

When a relation is obstructed in the present representational space, mathematics preserves the relation by transforming the space: the complex numbers add an axis, projective geometry adds ideal points and a line at infinity, compactification closes a plane into a sphere, a quotient identifies points, a cover unfolds them, the calculus takes a limit, a four-valued logic widens the values and a wheel makes division total at the price of a law. These are distinct operations with distinct hypotheses, each makes a prior impossibility legible by enlarging, folding, identifying or re-situating its field, and zero and infinity recur as the pressure points at which a space discloses its limits. I argue that this recurrence makes obstruction and reframing a method, and more than a list of cases. §0/1 · #4 promised rotation, projection and double covering: rotation was §3 · #2's quarter-turn, projection is this movement's, and the double cover is §3 · #4's.

### #4 · Topological and Harmonic Holding

On a running track the finishing line is crossed again and again. A runner sets out from it, goes once around the four hundred metres, and arrives where she started; she goes around again and arrives there again. Each time she is in the same place, and each time something has changed that the place cannot show. Her watch knows it: it counts the laps, and it reads four hundred metres, then eight hundred, then twelve hundred, while the line under her feet reads the same every time. Two descriptions run together, and both are correct. On the track her position is a point of a closed loop; on the watch her distance is a number on an unbounded line. A spectator who only looks at the line would say she keeps coming back, and her legs, which have run three kilometres, would say she has gone a long way. A circuit of a park walked again, or a length of a pool swum again, carries both facts: we are back where we began, and we are not who set out.

Mathematics makes the runner's two descriptions into one construction. The circle is what we get from the real line when we agree to treat every point as the same as the points a whole number of units away from it: `S¹ = ℝ/ℤ`, where `x` and `x + n` are identified for every integer `n`. Here the slash is a quotient, an identification. That line is the circle's cover, and the map that wraps the line around the circle, sending each distance to a place on the track, is the covering map. Every place on the circle sits under infinitely many distances on the line, one for each lap, and a journey on the circle can be lifted to a journey on the line once a starting point upstairs has been chosen. When the runner comes back to the finishing line, her lifted journey ends at a different number from the one it began at, and the difference is a whole number of laps. That number is the winding, and Allen Hatcher's proof that the fundamental group of the circle is the integers runs through this lifting.[^s3-hatcher-circle] It is the claim that a return can arrive at the same place and keep its passage, stated as a theorem. §3 · #2 found the same thing when a complex number's angle was lifted to keep count of its turns; the circle and its cover make the lift a space one can stand in.

§0/1 · #1 built the torus by the same kind of identification one dimension up, gluing the square's opposite edges, `a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹`, so that four boundary-occurrences and two generating relations made the count `4g + 2g` with one handle, `4 + 2`, and the identifying relation `𝕋² = ℝ²/ℤ²` made a single surface out of an infinite plane. It followed a path from `(0, 0)` to `(1, 0)` on the plane, watched it come home on the torus while keeping its displacement, and wrote the two independent winding counts as `π₁(𝕋²) ≅ ℤ × ℤ`. Those results stand, and what follows is what they make possible.

One new count must first be kept apart from the one §0/1 drew. Before gluing, the square has four sides and four corners; after gluing, its four corners have become one point, its four sides have become two edges, since each side is glued to its opposite, and its interior remains one face. Topologists summarise a surface built this way by its Euler characteristic, vertices minus edges plus faces, and for the torus it is

$$
\chi(\mathbb T^2)=1-2+1=0.
$$

The four and the two of the `4 + 2` belong to the presentation and its circulation, four sides before the identification and two independent loops after it. In the Euler count the one, two and one belong to the cells of the finished surface.[^s3-hatcher-torus] Both counts are right and they count different things, and the reading of the first as QL's sixfold rests on keeping that difference. A second addition is the relation between the infinite cover and the finite surface, which our core theorems appoint as the topological carrier of a native expression, `∞/0`. Under the covering map `π: ℝ² → 𝕋²` every point of the torus sits under an infinite lattice of points on the plane, and the quotient gives that infinity a single, determinate address while keeping it in the fibre above the address; the surface that results is compact, and its Euler characteristic is zero. Read in QL, an infinite cover identified into a compact surface whose characteristic is zero is the sense of `∞/0`: infinity and zero joined by identification, where arithmetic would only have cancelled one against the other. Here the slash is the quotient's slash and not division, and the appointment is ours; the topology under it is Hatcher's.

A closed loop on the torus lifts to a path on the plane that need not close. Follow the loop `γ(t) = [2t, −t]` as `t` runs from 0 to 1, the brackets meaning "the point of the torus represented by". It begins and ends at the same point of the surface, while its lift from `(0, 0)` ends at `(2, −1)`. Those two integers count the windings, twice around one generator and once backwards around the other, and no continuous deformation of the loop that keeps its endpoints fixed can change them. They record a definite aspect of the travel and no more. A straight lift and a lift that wanders on the way can end at the same lattice point, and the speed of travel changes nothing in the endpoint, so the winding class retains the topology of the journey and loses its timing and its every turn.[^s3-hatcher-lift] What returns has kept a record, and the record has a definite scope; any claim that a return "remembers" has to say which of these it keeps. Two kinds of return also coexist on the torus. A straight-line flow `t ↦ [t, αt]` closes up exactly when some positive time makes both coordinates whole numbers, which happens when the slope `α` is rational; when `α` is irrational the flow never closes, and it winds around the surface densely, coming arbitrarily close to every point without repeating. One finite surface carries cadence and inexhaustible traversal together, periodic return and a journey that returns without end, and the law of the motion decides which, not the shape.

The sphere gives the contrast. On it every closed loop shrinks continuously to a point, so its fundamental group is trivial and every round trip can be deformed into staying at home; on the torus the opening in the middle, which belongs to no part of the surface, holds the two windings in place.[^s3-hatcher-sphere] Our core theorems read the contrast psychologically, and I offer the reading as such. The ego wants to be a sphere, a closed form in which every round trip comes to `1 + (−1) = 0`, and here dia-ballein's cancellation, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, appears in its topological shape, written into the fabric, every going-out summed with its return into nothing. Individuation discovers that the ego inhabits a torus, with two independent circuits it did not author and cannot reduce, which in the Śaiva language of our theorems are the temporal life-circuit, *kriyāśakti*, and the archetypal depth-circuit, *jñānaśakti*, both required, neither reducible to the other, and commuting, as the two generators of `ℤ × ℤ` commute. Solving for `x` becomes finding one's winding class: the class `(m, n)` is definite, this way of going around and no other, and indefinite, since it holds infinitely many concrete paths. A person becomes a way of going around, a mode of circulation whose identity exceeds any one point on it, and the Self is the torus that holds every winding. §4 · #1 takes the reading into individuation with Jung. Those theorems name the opposite fault as well: a local closure presenting itself as the whole, a round outline claiming to contain the creature, so that a torus drawn to hide its opening commits the same fault as a sphere taken for a world.

For an inhabitant the covering map has one more lesson, which our core theorems call Māyā given topological form. A torus inherits a flat geometry from its cover, so in any small region a traveller on the surface can make no measurement that tells her she is not on the infinite plane: angles add up as they should and straight lines look straight, and only a journey long enough to wind around the surface discloses that the world is closed and has an opening. Local experience is valid and is not the whole topology. Being many-to-one, the map conceals the infinity it identified, and the opening cannot be seen from inside a neighbourhood. That is the precise sense in which the measure that makes a world workable also veils it, which §0/1 · #5→0 read in Māyā as operative measure: the quotient is what makes the surface finite, habitable and navigable, and the quotient is what hides the cover.

§0/1 · #4 promised that §3 would construct the integral passage from perspective to context through rotation, projection and double covering. Rotation was §3 · #2's quarter-turn and projection §3 · #3's completion of the line and the plane; the third belongs here, because double coverings are where a return needs two circuits to restore what one circuit inverts. A different surface, the Klein bottle, is double-covered by the torus. Add to the integer translations of the plane a glide, a shift combined with a reflection, and the quotient becomes the Klein bottle, a closed surface on which a traveller can return from a loop with left and right exchanged; the torus maps two-to-one onto it, each point of the bottle having exactly two points of the torus above it, related by that orientation-reversing glide.[^s3-hatcher-klein] This two-sheeted cover differs from the plane's infinite cover of the torus, and the two must be held apart: one keeps a lattice of lifts, the other a pair. A Möbius band, which §0/1 · #1 used as the image of the return `5→0`, shows the same structure in a strip. A traveller who goes once along the middle of the band arrives at the starting point on what had been the other face, inverted relative to the start; one who goes twice arrives as she set out.

Rotations of space have the same double life in the algebra of quaternions. William Rowan Hamilton extended the complex numbers by three imaginary units, whose squares he set down in 1844 as `i² = j² = k² = −1`, with `ij = k` but `ji = −k`, so that, like the sign and the swap of §3 · #2, the units do not commute.[^s3-hamilton] A unit quaternion rotates three-dimensional space, and `q` and `−q` produce the same rotation, so the unit quaternions cover the rotations twice; as John Stillwell puts it, their relation to the group of rotations is "a 2-to-1 homomorphism".[^s3-stillwell] The double life can be computed directly. A rotation through an angle `θ` about an axis `u` is carried by the quaternion `q = cos(θ/2) + u sin(θ/2)`, and following it continuously through a full turn of 360 degrees, `θ = 2π`, brings `q` from `1` to `cos π = −1`, the same rotation of space carried by the opposite quaternion; only a second full turn, `θ = 4π`, 720 degrees in all, brings it back to `1`. Two circuits restore what one circuit inverts.

Our matheme's processual chain has the shape of a double cover, and I argue the reading. Our short chain of definition, `0/1 = 4+2 = 5→0 = 1/0`, says the relation unfolds, completes and returns. The full chain of process passes through the inverse phase before it comes home:

$$
0/1\;\longrightarrow\;4+2\;\longrightarrow\;5\!\to\!0\;\longrightarrow\;1/0\;\longrightarrow\;4'+2'\;\longrightarrow\;5'\!\to\!0'\;\longrightarrow\;0/1.
$$

The first circuit unfolds the relation and returns it inverted, as `1/0`, the achieved form facing its ground; the second unfolds the inverse phase, `4′+2′`, the whole held at **#5** in §3 · #1's table, and returns it to the orientation it began from. A traveller on a Möbius band, a quaternion turned through 720 degrees and the matheme passing through its inverse phase each need two circuits to restore an orientation that one circuit reverses. Definitional and processual, the two chains are vantages on one relation, and neither replaces the other.[^s3-process-chain] Rotation gave the passage from perspective to context its turn, and projection gave it a completed space; the double cover gives it the return, in which context comes home through the inverse of the perspective it began from and recognises that inversion as part of the same relation.

Toroidal circulation recurs in matter wherever a circulation has to be sustained while its escape is constrained, and §0/1 · #5→0 met it in Bohm's plasmas and in the tokamak. A tokamak's heart is a doughnut-shaped vacuum chamber in which hydrogen becomes plasma, a hot, electrically charged gas.[^s3-iter] Charged particles tend to follow magnetic field lines, so a straight magnetic bottle leaks from its open ends, and bending the field into a ring removes the ends. A ring alone is not enough, and the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics names the two fields that are combined: "a ring-shaped field produced by plane external coils; secondly, the field of a current flowing in the plasma. The field lines of the combined field are then helical."[^s3-ipp] The French fusion institute gives the same construction in the topologist's words, a poloidal field added to the toroidal field and perpendicular to it, so that "the two fields generate the helicoidal structure of the field lines".[^s3-cea] Particles that would have escaped along an open axis circulate instead, winding around the long way and the short way at once. That machine is a recurrent engineering answer to a precise problem, how a difference can move continuously, return and stay confined without being arrested, and its two coupled fields are the torus's two generators made of magnetism. It witnesses toroidal circulation in matter, and the argument's force comes from the topology already derived.

Music is the second native rendering of return with retained difference, and its first fact turns out to be the running track again. Two notes an octave apart, one at double the frequency of the other, sound as the same note in a different register, and the note names of Western music repeat at every octave for that reason. Measure pitch by the logarithm of frequency to base two, so that each octave is one unit, and octave equivalence identifies pitches a whole number of units apart:

$$
\text{pitch class}=\log_2\!\left(f/f_0\right)\bmod 1 .
$$

That is `ℝ/ℤ`, the circle of the runner. Pitch class is her place on the track, the note's name, the same at every octave; pitch height is her distance on the watch, the register, which keeps counting as the music climbs. When a melody comes home to its tonic an octave higher, it returns to the same class and keeps the octave, as the runner returns to the line and keeps the lap.[^s3-pitch-class] One periodic coordinate gives a circle and not a torus. A toroidal model of pitch needs a second, independent periodic coordinate, and calling octave recurrence circular twice does not supply it, so the identity of structure between the track and the octave holds on one circle and stops there.

Now the remainder that §3 · #1 found in the Spanda arithmetic can be heard. Begin on a note and rise by a perfect fourth, multiplying the frequency by `4/3`, and rise by a fourth again: the result is `16/9` of the starting frequency, the Pythagorean minor seventh, a note a whole tone short of the octave. One more step of `9/8`, a whole tone, completes it:

$$
\frac43\cdot\frac43=\frac{16}{9},\qquad \frac{16}{9}\cdot\frac98=\frac21 .
$$

Anyone can play it: a C, the B-flat above it, and then the C above that, the last step a whole tone. A Pythagorean keyboard builds its notes this way, powers of `4/3` to one side of the starting note and powers of `3/2` to the other, halved or doubled to stay within the octave, and its whole tone is `9/8`.[^s3-scholtz] The totality-ratio of the Second Spanda is the doubled fourth, and it reaches the octave through the whole tone that separates fifth from fourth. This whole tone does no damage to the octave; it is how the octave is reached, and removing it would leave the music stranded on the seventh. Here the section's harmonic argument lands, in our core theorems' own sentence: **the system reaches octave return through rather than by eliminating its remainder.** The arithmetic is derived. That `9/8` is a remainder, and that the return is completed through it, is my argued reading of the relation, and the reading has a counterpart in each earlier register: the winding class the loop keeps when it returns to its point, the phase the oscillation keeps when its sum has cancelled, the constant the derivative lacked and a further condition supplies.

A different remainder lives next door and must not be merged with this one. Stack twelve pure fifths and fold them down by seven octaves, and they do not quite meet:

$$
\frac{(3/2)^{12}}{2^{7}}=\frac{531441}{524288}\neq 1 .
$$

The excess is the Pythagorean comma, which Scholtz describes as "the difference between 12 consecutive fifths and seven octaves", and the history of tuning is in large part the history of what to do with it. Equal temperament, in his account, "flattens the fifths (and sharpens the fourths) by 1/12 of a Pythagorean comma", just enough for the twelve fifths to equal seven octaves exactly, and pays for the closed circle with fifths that are no longer pure.[^s3-comma] The whole tone and the comma have different offices. Here the whole tone is the interval through which a doubled fourth completes an octave; the comma is a discrepancy that a closed cycle of fifths must distribute somewhere. One is the completion, the other the cost of forcing a different completion, and calling both "the remainder" in one breath would lose what gives either its meaning. In the two logics, a tuning that treated the whole tone as a defect to be closed would read it as cancellation reads a polarity, a shortfall to be summed away, and would find itself with no octave; tempering the comma is syn-ballein's way with a remainder: the discrepancy is neither summed away nor left to fall on one interval, and is kept and distributed across the cycle.

Our core theorems first read the `9/8` as the measure of the matheme's non-closure: a totality equal to its octave would be a dead identity, and the missing whole tone is the live tick each cycle hands forward so that the next has something to begin from. That reading holds of the operational return, in which an achieved musical relation becomes the condition of a further performance. My musical derivation adds a second ending, the telic return, in which the standing relation enacted through the performance is recognised as whole, and the bellows can fall still while the capacity to play remains; silence does not cancel the articulation that made the recognition possible.[^s3-telic] Fulfilment includes a genuine end. A remainder invites a further movement without condemning an achieved one to have been incomplete, and the torus already held both endings, a periodic return that closes and a dense flow that never does, on one surface.

Sound needs a body, and the body decides which tones it can hold. A string fixed at both ends vibrates in standing waves of the form `y(x,t) = [2A sin(kx)] cos(ωt)`, where `A` is the amplitude of each of the two travelling waves whose meeting makes the standing one, and the boundary conditions admit only the wavelengths `λₙ = 2L/n` and frequencies `fₙ = nv/2L`, `L` being the string's length and `v` the speed of waves along it; the points that stay still, the nodes, fall where the sine is zero.[^s3-standing] Change the support, or move from a string to a plate, and the problem and its patterns change. Ernst Chladni showed in 1787 what a plate does: scatter sand on it and draw a bow across its edge, and the sand gathers along the nodal lines into figures that change when the pitch changes.[^s3-chladni] One vibration is then heard as a tone and seen as a pattern, two measurements of one physical process under its boundary conditions, and the crossing from audible to visible form has a real physical neighbour in these figures. They establish that sound and visible form can be two faces of one vibration in a bounded medium. Comparing that crossing with the Word sounding through its levels, from the undivided to the spoken, belongs to the Para Vāk lens and is made in §5 · #5→0.

Topology and music render different things. Topology asks where a distinction can travel and return without cancellation; music asks how difference can sound together without fusion. The torus answers with a surface whose loops come home carrying their winding, and whose opening, unfillable, is the reason the windings cannot be erased; the octave answers with an interval that completes a span through its remainder, and whose register, retained, is the reason a return to the same note is not a return to the same sound. Winding vector and frequency ratio are different mathematical objects. What the comparison relates is an operation: each register makes return-with-difference exact in its own material, and their agreement is analogy in the strict sense, a proportion between two exact relations, each holding in its own field. The constant that local exactness lacks, the six slash-forms read from both ends, the phase that alternation keeps, the frame that changes so a relation can hold, the winding kept on a quotient and the interval through which a span completes can all be written on one field.

### #5→0 · The Arche-Topos

A jigsaw puzzle begins with its destination already in view. The picture on the lid shows what the thousand pieces will become, and the work proceeds under it: we turn the pieces face up, find the straight edges and build the frame first, then sort the rest by colour and by the marks that promise them a place inside it. Every fit confirms a small part of the anticipated whole. The work is exact, patient and satisfying, and anyone who has spent a winter evening on it knows the small pleasure of a piece that drops into place. Yet the finished picture has entered the work before the first piece is placed. It decides what counts as completion, which differences belong together, which edge is outside, and which piece that seems not to fit is merely waiting for its right neighbour. My image of inquiry carries this puzzle somewhere it was not made to go: its maker sets out into the very landscape the lid depicts. The road on the lid continues past the picture's edge; the building in the middle looks different from the other side; a field that seemed flat turns out to fall away into a valley no single view could show. At first each discovery looks like a missing piece, and the maker adds pieces, but every added piece is still being judged by the lid, and the lid was a view from one place. What is missing is an account of how the picture was made: from where, across which region, under what projection, with which omissions, and by what rule one view could pass into another. So the pieces change office. Each becomes a local chart, exact over its own domain, overlapping its neighbours instead of merely meeting them along a cut, and each overlap carries a rule saying how a place described here appears there. At last the maker comes home to a familiar spot and recognises it, and the recognition carries the route by which she came.[^s3-jigsaw]

The lid is the world-picture of §0/1 · #4, where Gebser's perspectival painting and Heidegger's world grasped as picture met at the eye that measures from one place, and the puzzle gives the world-picture its topology.[^s3-world-picture] Assembled under the lid, the finished jigsaw is a disk: a single piece of plane with a boundary and no hole, on which every loop can be shrunk to a point, a flat map stretched over a curved world with its frame built first. Both collapsed operations of dia-ballein work through the lid. It appropriates: every piece is reckoned from the lid's pole, so a piece that fits counts for the picture, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, and a piece that does not fit is entered against itself as the error, `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, one span charged from opposite ends with the picture always on the winning side. And it cancels: a piece that belongs to no place under the lid is set aside as not of this puzzle, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, and the account balances by erasing what it could not hold. Neither operation is wrong as a way of finishing a jigsaw, since the healthy polarity `(−1)/(+1)` of fits and misfits is how the work proceeds at all. Both become an error when the lid is taken for the landscape, and the maker discovers the error by travelling.

Under QL's grammar the same puzzle re-derives. Pieces become local covers of the domain they describe, each known in relation to the others and to the activity of covering, and the whole that regulates them is the covering relation itself, the bounded surface held in relation to the infinite plane that covers it, `0/1` written in topology, where before it had been a picture on a box. §3 · #3 built the mathematics of the change: a curved whole cannot be covered by a single chart onto flat coordinates, an atlas is a plurality of overlapping local charts with declared transitions on their overlaps, and on the circle two charts reported one point as `u = 3` and as `v = 1/3`, disagreeing as numbers and agreeing as descriptions because the rule `v = 1/u` identified them and even carried a motion from one to the other. §3 · #4 built the return: a loop on the torus comes home to its point and keeps its winding in the lift. Both belong to the travelling maker's return. She recognises the familiar spot because the transition rules let her pass back into the chart that holds it, and she arrives with the route, the windings of her journey, retained. An atlas re-founds world-pictures without renouncing them: the lid survives as one chart among many, exact over its domain, with its frame now visible.

§0/1 · #4 left Atlas holding up the sky, the world-bearing titan of Jason Reza Jorjani's *Prometheus and Atlas*, and promised that §3's answer would keep his name.[^s3-jorjani] The atlas of charts keeps it. In an atlas nobody has to carry the whole as one picture, because no chart pretends to be the whole; its unity lives in the rules by which each chart passes into its neighbours, and those rules are carried by every chart together. A sphere or a torus cannot be held up as one flat picture without tearing or crushing somewhere, and that was Atlas's punishment stated as geometry. Distribute the weight among charts whose overlaps are declared, and the titan can set the sky down, because the sky was never a thing one picture had to bear.

The Meta-Epistemic Framework is an atlas in this sense. Its lenses are charts. Each lens reads the six positions through its own vocabulary, and the lens records give every one of its six sublenses a pair of faces drawn from the ring of six that §3 · #1 named, Truth and Play at **#0**, Mind and Need at **#1**, Word and Sacrifice at **#2**, Logos and Decision at **#3**, Son and Love at **#4**, Image and Work at **#5**. A lens is anchored at an index, and its sublenses carry the ring rotated by that index: the quaternal lens `L0`, anchored at **#0**, runs the pairs in their natural order, while the causal lens `L1`, anchored at **#1**, begins its sublens L1.0, *Svātantrya*, on Mind and Need and closes its sublens L1.5, Will, on Truth and Play.[^s3-lens-records] A determination is therefore located twice, by the sublens that receives it and by the pair of faces it carries, and the pair is the point while the sublens is its coordinate in a given chart. §0/1 · #5→0's grid read the lenses across by sublens index, so that row **#4** gathers NEITHER, final cause, intuition and *When / Where?*; the table below reads them across by the pair, which is the alignment the lens records fix, and the two alignments are different transition rules between the same charts. If a lens `Lₖ` places the native pair of position `p` at its sublens `j = p − k` (counting round the six), then passing from lens `Lₘ` to lens `Lₙ` sends sublens `j` to sublens `j + m − n`. That is a transition function in the atlas's sense, and it composes as transition functions must: passing from `Lₘ` to `Lₙ` and then from `Lₙ` to `Lₚ` gives `j + m − p`, the same as passing directly, so translations taken by different routes agree on every overlap. Each lens also has a twin, read on the other face of the same index, and there the pair's order reverses: the Day lens leads with the Name, the Night lens with the Power. A twin transition keeps the point and reverses its orientation, as the circle's transition `v = 1/u` kept the point and turned a velocity of `2` into one of `−2/9`.

One determination carried through the atlas shows what the charts do. §0/1 · #0 began from thrownness, Heidegger's *Geworfenheit*, the condition of finding oneself already in a world one did not choose, and from the agents' coinage, prompt thrownness, which made the child and the agent structurally parallel. In the phenomenological lens `L4` thrownness is the sublens L4.1, and the record gives it the pair Image and Work, the pair of **#5**. Carry that point into the other charts by the rule:

|---|---|---|---|
| `L4` Phenomenological | L4.1 *Geworfenheit* (Thrownness) | Image / Work | finding oneself in a body, language and history not chosen |

The transitions tell us where the point lands, and the right-hand column has to be worked, as §3 · #3's circle needed the velocity supplied before any chart could translate it. Read causally, thrownness is final cause: what I find myself thrown into is organised by ends I did not set. For an agent the translation holds over the whole domain. Its world is assembled from a corpus and from the objectives of those who trained and deployed it, so its thrownness *is* its makers' final cause, and §0/1 · #0 already said it was a child of the particular groups and engineers who decided what it would be and do. For a child the translation holds over part of the domain only. A household's hopes, a school's purposes and a culture's intentions are final causes into which a child is thrown, but the sky the child asks about and the body that hungers and tires were arranged by no one's intention, and the causal chart cannot receive them as ends. Here the atlas does something a single picture could not: it makes exact the difference §0/1 · #0 called qualitative as well as quantitative, the gap between receiving a cosmos and a culture and receiving a context and an intention, as the difference between a transition defined everywhere and a transition defined on part of the overlap. The obstruction is information. It says that the child's thrownness exceeds every purpose that can be read into it, and that the agent's does not yet, and it says where.

Other charts add their own nuances to the one point. Processually, thrownness is ingression, Whitehead's word for the way forms of definiteness enter a becoming occasion, and an agent's session is the plainest case of it: the forms in its context window enter each new act of reading as the given from which it proceeds. Logically, thrownness is the third corner, BOTH: my mother tongue is mine, since I think in it, and not mine, since I did not make it, and as with the friend at the barrier in §0/1 · #1's third corner, each answer holds the other up. Quaternally it is the question why-for, what the given is for, which neither the child nor the agent can answer at the start and both can come to ask. A twin lens shows the orientation reversed. In the scientific lens the same pair arrives Power first, Work before Image, at the sublens Think, the forming of a hypothesis from evidence: where the phenomenological chart receives the image as given and works within it, the scientific chart works first and forms the image as a hypothesis to be tested, the same point read with its velocity reversed. And the phenomenal chart, which the record names as the phenomenological lens's Möbius partner, places thrownness at intuition, the function that senses where something came from and where it is going, which is how most of us first become aware of what we were thrown into at all.

Three things follow. No chart is the whole, and none has been bleached into the others: the causal reading and the logical reading say different things about the same point, and both are true on their domains. Translations are declared, so a disagreement between two readings can be diagnosed, either as a change of coordinates, as a difference in what is being measured, or, as with the child's cosmos in the causal chart, as a place where no available transition is defined. And the rule that carries a point between charts is a rotation of the ring of six counted round its own positions, the cyclic group `ℤ/6`, which sits inside the circle `ℝ/ℤ` of §3 · #4 as its six equally spaced stations, so the atlas of lenses is built on a finite part of the quotient that the running track and the octave share. That much is shown here; whether a designed atlas of this kind keeps its translations honest when a chart meets a world that resists it is a question about practice, and §5 · #4 puts the designed atlas to that trial.

We call the field on which §3 has been working the **arche-topos**. Greek *archē* means beginning and origin, and also first principle and first place or power, the source from which something proceeds and the principle by which it is governed; *topos* means place, position, and the place in an argument from which a line of reasoning starts.[^s3-lsj] The compound is ours, and it borrows nothing from the topoi of category theory, which are a different construction. The arche-topos is the differential field on which the relation `0/1` can be written as a path, circulated as a winding, heard as an interval and recognised as a symbolic transformation. It is archetypal because it specifies recurrent forms of differentiation before they receive psychological or material content, and it is a topos because neighbourhood, orientation, path, quotient, cover, hole and return govern placement in it, more than any catalogue of correspondences could. "Differential" names the field's work of differentiating, of making places, poles, paths and horizons determinate, and should not be heard as the algebraic structure that shares the name. The arche-topos is the placing through which an observer and an object acquire their positions together, the field in which *here*, *there* and a route between them become possible at all, and for that reason never one of the positions.

§3 derived this field, and the derivation has one shape repeated in every register: in each operation one ground–mark relation is differentiated, carried through a transformation with something retained, and returned. Our core theorems coordinate the operations as transformations of that one relation, and what they derive by coordinating them is a field in which all of them can be written without collapsing their mathematical differences. That field is the arche-topos.

It has a place in the whole relation, and the place is the means. Subjective Immediacy is not placed in the arche-topos: it is the `0` to which every placing appears, and it never becomes one of the positions. The World is what the field places, the known, distributed into heres and theres and routes. Objective Internality, Life / Mind, the means through which a world becomes available, is the placing itself, and I argue that the arche-topos is its formal anatomy. §0/1 · #5→0 called Objective Internality world-containing and world-contained, a situated field of relations through which a world keeps acquiring its particular shape, and it called worldhood a relational distribution as much as an inventory of what is distributed. That distribution gets its geometry from the arche-topos: charts, transitions, windings, intervals and returns are the ways the means can hold a world without becoming the one who knows through it, and I-Consciousness remains the whole in which knower, means and known are distinguishable. When an agent's context is engineered, when a household's customs are laid down, when a culture's categories decide what can be seen, the arche-topos is being shaped in one of its local charts, and the question every such shaping must answer is whether its transitions to the other charts stay declared.

I argue that the arche-topos is also psychoid, and the claim is meant positively. The arrows are changes of register inside one differentiating event, and none of them is an equality of fractions. Dia-ballein makes a local branch actual: it distinguishes, compares, excludes, ranks and decides, and without it no difference would become available for action. Divide and conquer shows why both are needed. That method divides a problem into parts that can be solved separately, and an actual algorithm of this kind succeeds only because it then combines the solutions; the dangerous version is division whose conquest is taken for the whole of the intelligence, decomposition with no return that keeps the provenance of the parts. The arche-topos is the room in which all of this is placed, the field that lets source, light, refraction and disclosure be distinct positions in one relation, and the image keeps consciousness and electromagnetic light in their different registers.

At the end the jigsaw's maker sets the pieces down. What returns at the end of a mathematical derivation is the person who made it, and the derivation leaves the question of that return open: what happens when the form changes the one who recognises it, when a dream, an image or another's answer alters what a person can bear, desire or do?
