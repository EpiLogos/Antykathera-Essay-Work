### #1 · The Spanda Equations and the 4+2 Count

An accordion breathes through its bellows. The player draws the two ends apart and air rushes in across the reeds; the player presses them together and the air is driven out across them again, and in a well-played tune the listener cannot hear the change of direction, because the phrase runs straight through it. Every phrase is made of two movements that go opposite ways, and the music is the relation of the two, one breath that opens and closes without ceasing to be one breath. When the player reaches the end of the bellows' travel the direction has to turn, and the turn is where the instrument is most alive and most at risk, since a clumsy reversal breaks the line while a good one carries it forward with something gained. I have long thought of the Self in these terms: the Self leaning into manifestation and the manifest leaning home, always already whole beneath the swing.

The pulse has its classical name in the Spanda teaching of Kashmir Śaivism, whose "subtle movement" §0/1 · #3 followed through will, knowledge and action and §0 · #0 bent toward display, and we read one act of it there as a `0/1` and its completion as `5→0`. QL writes the pulse natively in two equations we call the Spanda equations, which read one sixfold from its two ends.[^s3-spanda-equations] The first is horizontal: it follows the relation from ground through distinction to integrated return, and it is a reading by proportion. The second is vertical: it takes the achieved whole and accounts for it, discovering the sixfold inside it, and it is a reading by count. Everything later in §3 falls out of holding the two readings against each other, because their difference generates the relation that choosing either alone would lose, as the bellows' two strokes generate a phrase that neither stroke contains.

Before either equation can be read, the slash-forms it uses need their proportional meaning, and the meaning is easiest to see by placing them on the six positions. §3 · #0 placed the four pairings of the binary on a compass; read now through proportion, each position holds a fraction built from the two marks, and the fractions behave very differently under ordinary arithmetic:

| Position | Slash-form | Under proportion | What ordinary arithmetic does with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#0** | `(0/1)/(1/0)` | the relation of the two orientations; unfolds into `0/1, 0/0, 1/1, 1/0` with the singles `0` and `1`: the whole `4+2` | holds a term with no value; the native reading treats it as a relation |
| **#1** | `0/0` | the indeterminate, every ratio held at once | no unique value: every number times zero gives zero |
| **#2** | `0/1` | the expression, computable and docile | zero |
| **#3** | `1/0` | the inverse, incomputable | no value: no number times zero gives one |
| **#4** | `1/1` | the ratio resolved, one whole | one, or one hundred per cent |
| **#5** | `(1/0)/(0/1)` | the inverse relation of the two orientations; the whole in inverse phase, `4′+2′` | as at **#0**, read in the other direction |

The middle column is our core theorems'; the right-hand column is arithmetic anyone can check. In a field of numbers, multiplying by zero always gives zero, since `(0+0)x = 0x+0x` forces `0x = 0`. The equation `0x = 1` therefore has no solution, which is why one divided by zero has no value, and the equation `0x = 0` is solved by every number, which is why zero divided by zero has no single value.[^s3-field-zero] The two failures differ, and the table keeps them apart. At **#1**, `0/0` fails by excess: it can be any ratio, and the theorem reads that excess as the indeterminate, the capacity for every proportion, which it marks with the percentage sign as the glyph of potency. At **#3**, `1/0` fails by absence: the determinate mark divided by the ground yields nothing a number can name. The theorem locates this failure at Pattern and nowhere else, and reads it with the glyph `?/!`, because the system cannot decide whether `1/0` is a question or an assertion. Formal identity, the recognition of *what* a thing is, rests here on a foundation that does not compute: the figure cannot be measured against the ground, the finite cannot be reckoned against the infinite, and this is the Gödelian point of §0/1 · #3 reached at the level of a single position. This `?/!` at **#3** is an undecidability, and it is the second of the two offices §3 · #0 told apart, distinct from the living question-and-assertion of definition at **#1**.

Two findings follow from the table, and both carry weight later. The first is that the parents contain the children. The expansion rule is simple to state: for a ratio of ratios `(a/b)/(c/d)`, relate the first upper term to each lower term and then the second, giving `a/c, a/d, b/c, b/d`, and keep the two singles beside them. Applied to `(0/1)/(1/0)` it yields `0/1, 0/0, 1/1, 1/0` together with `0` and `1`, so the ground bears the whole `4+2` as its own relational interior, a seed carrying the full tree, and the relation at **#5**, `(1/0)/(0/1)`, bears the same whole in inverse phase, `1/0, 1/1, 0/0, 0/1` with `1` and `0`. Read the parent at **#0** into its inverse at **#5** and read it back again, and every term returns to its place: the inversion is an involution, a turn that undoes itself on the second application, so what it records is which of two phases is active, never how many times it has been performed. Two parents carry one child in mirror image, and the Śaiva vocabulary of §0/1 · #5→0 names them exactly: *bimba*, the original, and *pratibimba*, its reflection. The second finding is that the relation leans. The forward orientation `0/1` settles into a value and the backward orientation `1/0` stays permanently open, so the relation between ground and mark is handed, not symmetric. That handedness is the structural reason consciousness can never wholly catch itself, since every catching conceals what it catches *from*, and the reason it keeps trying, since the concealing drives the next catching. Read through the Indian analysis of knowing, each slash-form is a complete act compressed into notation, the numerator the knower, the slash the means, the denominator the known: `0/1` is the void knowing the mark through the slash, and `1/0` is the mark attempting to know the void through ratio, an attempt that does not compute.

The table also lets us follow the mathematical life of a sign. §0/1 · #3 wrote `Ø`, the crossed zero, for the first occlusion, and §1 · #3 gives the sign its history and its one job: the zero of the Subject with the mediating stroke fused into it, so that the means of seeing passes for the one who sees. The slash-forms show the same two marks, a zero and a stroke, in a different arrangement. In `Ø` the stroke lies across the zero and belongs to it, and the result looks like a single thing, the bound self. In `(0/1)/(1/0)` the stroke stands between the two orientations of the relation, holding them apart and together, and the result is legible as a relation. Occlusion and recognition are thus the same two marks in two arrangements, and §4 · #3 follows the full sequence by which the fused stroke comes undone and becomes visible as mediation.

The First Spanda equation carries the horizontal movement from ground to integrated return:

$$
0=(0/0)\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{0/1}{1/0}\;\longrightarrow\;[T_0]\;\longrightarrow\;[T_1]\;\longrightarrow\;\left(\frac10+\frac01\right)_{\!\mathrm{QL}}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac11=1=100\%.
$$

Each term is a position of the unit read through proportion. The bare ground `0` reads itself and opens the indeterminate, `0/0`, every possible ratio held at once. The indeterminate differentiates into the ratio of ratios, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, the ground's own AND/OR form unfolding the child it always contained; this is the first cut, the *inter-legere*, the reading-between, of nothing with nothing, intelligence in its most primordial gesture. Then come two directed passages. `[T₀]`, the emanative arc, moves from `0/(0/1)` toward `0/1`: the ground takes expression as its denominator and works toward expression, figure generated from ground, silence becoming articulate. `[T₁]`, the reversionary arc, moves from `(1/0)/0` toward `1/0`: the inverse takes the ground as its denominator and works back toward recognition, figure acknowledging the ground that sustains it. The two arcs meet in the bridge, `1/0 + 0/1`, where the directed movements recombine and discover that they were one circuit, and the circuit resolves into `1/1`, the ratio that has found itself whole. Our core theorems give that resolution its shortest statement: to be one of oneself is to be oneself wholly is to be one hundred per cent of what one is.

The subscript declares the bridge's register. Its plus sign composes two orientations, and it cannot be the addition of two quotients, since one of the quotients has no value and ordinary addition would destroy the term before the composition could take place. A notation that asks this much must show that the composition it names is a definite operation, and arithmetic supplies one with exactly the right shape: the mediant, which forms from two fractions a third by adding numerators and adding denominators, `(0+1)/(1+0) = 1/1`, is defined even when one boundary is `1/0`, because it adds the parts of two pairs and divides nothing. The mediant is the operation of the First Spanda's bridge; §1 · #5→0 builds the tree of every positive ratio that the same operation generates between `0/1` and `1/0` and proves its properties, and the bridge needs only its first step, the `1/1` from which every other ratio descends.

Two further constructions show the bridge's result holding its two readings instead of erasing them. Take two objects, `A` and `B`, an arrow `f` from `A` to `B` and its inverse `g` from `B` back to `A`, so that going and returning gives the identity on either side, `g∘f = id_A` and `f∘g = id_B`. There are two objects and four arrows, two identities and two crossings, and both objects belong to one connected structure while remaining two: unity achieved without identifying `A` with `B`, and a `2+4` in the plainest category there is. Or represent the two orientations as vectors, `b = (0,1)` and `p = (1,0)`. Their sum `u = (1,1)` is unchanged if the coordinates are swapped, and their difference `d = (1,−1)` changes sign under the swap; the invariant alone cannot reconstruct what was distinguished, yet the invariant and the sign-changing difference together recover both, `b = (u−d)/2` and `p = (u+d)/2`.[^s3-bridge-carriers] The whole and the phase are needed together, the first lesson of the movement that follows, and in each construction the achieved `1/1` has not cost the two orientations their difference.

Beyond its sequence, the equation carries a partition: its six articulated stages after the bare ground fall into two triads.

| Emanative triad: source toward expression, *bimba*, *prakāśa* | Reversionary triad: return toward recognition, *pratibimba*, *vimarśa* |
|---|---|
| 1. `(0/0)`, the indeterminate | 4. `[T₁]`, the reversionary arc toward `1/0` |
| 2. `(0/1)/(1/0)`, the slash differentiating itself | 5. `1/0 + 0/1`, the bridge |
| 3. `[T₀]`, the emanative arc toward `0/1` | 6. `1/1`, achieved unity, one hundred per cent |

The partition is the one §3 · #0 drew from the counting logic, `1-2-3 / 4-5-0`, and its primary sense comes from the definition of the Subject. The first three unfold perspective in sequence, first, second and third person; the second three void the isolation of the three views, recognise their unity and return that unity as `0/1`. So the horizontal reading is `3:3`, and because the second triad is one recognition in three operations, it is also `3:1`. Emanation and reversion, *bimba* and *pratibimba*, *prakāśa* and *vimarśa*, the light and its reflexive return upon itself, are further readings of this subject-structure; they name what the partition does from the side of the Śaiva sources, and none of them is the source of the arithmetic. The two triads remain complementary aspects of one operation, emanation from the ground and return to unity, and nothing in the equation makes them two substances.

The same doubled reading runs through each of the six positions as a pair of faces, and naming them shows what the two triads feel like from inside a life. At **#0** Truth faces Play; at **#1** Mind faces Need; at **#2** Word faces Sacrifice; at **#3** Logos faces Decision; at **#4** Son faces Love; at **#5** Image faces Work. The first series is the emanative Name, the articulated determination as it appears from outside, and the second is the reversionary Power, the same determination felt as a demand from within.[^s3-name-power] Truth precedes the saying that will reflect it, and Play is the freedom of that beginning, before what is has been compelled into one expression. Mind grasps something first, and Need is the lack that made the grasping urgent. Word makes the first articulate cut, and Sacrifice is what speaking costs, the silence given up and the possibilities left unspoken. Logos gathers terms in proportion, and Decision commits to the proportion and closes the alternatives for this course. Son is the universal become someone's, and Love the holding concern without which nobody stays someone. Image is the form achieved, and Work is the labour by which an image becomes a world. At every position the articulated form and its effective demand are co-present, which is what the two arcs of the equation require: an emanation that never returns would be Name without Power, and a return that never went out would be Power with nothing to bear.

The Second Spanda equation takes the achieved whole and asks it to account for its constituents:

$$
100\%\;=\;2^6+6^2\;=\;64+36\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{64}{36}\;\xrightarrow{\;\div 4\;}\;\frac{16}{9}\;=\;\frac{2^4}{3^2}\;\longrightarrow\;4+2.
$$

Each arrow marks a change of operation, and the derivation is honest only if the reader can see where the operation changes. Represent the whole on a scale of a hundred; on that scale it is the sum of two counts of different kinds. Assign a binary value to each of the six positions and there are `2⁶ = 64` possible assignments, the binary principle run through every place, the count whose history §1 · #5→0 tells. Pair the positions with one another in order and there are `6² = 36` ordered pairs, the sixfold raised to its second-order account. The state assignment and the relational address are different kinds of object, so the total keeps them as tagged collections, `64 + 36 = 100`, and calling that total one hundred per cent normalises it to one whole without pretending that the number one is a count of a hundred things. The selection of these two counts as the whole's constituents belongs to the QL derivation, and §0 · #4 offers the resonance between the thirty-six and the thirty-six tattvas as `6²`.

Nothing in the sum implies the next step. QL deliberately changes from adding the two counts to comparing them, and takes their ratio. Dividing both by the four they share preserves the ratio and exposes their difference:

$$
\frac{64}{36}=\frac{2^4\cdot 2^2}{2^2\cdot 3^2}=\frac{2^4}{3^2}=\frac{16}{9}.
$$

Dividing the sum by four would give twenty-five and answer a different question; the derivation needs the ratio. Prime factorisation then makes four factors of two and two factors of three explicit, `(2·2·2·2)/(3·3)`, and counting the factor instances gives `4+2`: four instances of the binary, which QL reads as the four explicate positions, and two instances of the ternary, which it reads as the two implicate poles. The vertical reading `4:2` has come out of the whole's own self-reckoning. Its value is reflexive, and its claim has exactly that scope: many decompositions of a hundred exist, and this one matters because the native sixfold, already derived from the binary and its self-application, selects it, so that the QL frame finds a coherent arithmetic expression of its own `4:2` relation and can then be tested by the further relations that choice produces.

| Step | Mathematical standing | QL reading |
|---|---|---|
| `100 = 2⁶ + 6² = 64 + 36` | arithmetic identity | the achieved whole as binary-through-six plus six-squared |
| `64 : 36` | a chosen change of operation, not a consequence of the sum | the two constituents read in proportion |
| `64/36 = 16/9` | ordinary reduction | the shared quaternary removed, the difference exposed |
| `16/9 = 2⁴/3²` | prime factorisation | four binary factors and two ternary factors: the vertical `4:2` |

A second exact reading holds in the numerator and fails in the denominator. Four factors of two can be read as the quaternary taken twice, since `2⁴ = 4² = 16`, and so `16/9 = (4/3)²`, a ratio that turns out to be two musical fourths. The denominator keeps its asymmetry: `3² = 9` while `2³ = 8`, so the exchange of base and exponent that works on one side changes the value on the other. Reading the totality as a doubled fourth rests on an actual equality in the numerator and leaves the ternary square untouched. Two further relations inside the base frame carry a lighter warrant and enter no step of the derivation that follows, though they belong to the same accounting. The two terms of the reduced ratio sum to a square, `16 + 9 = 25 = 5²`, the first Pythagorean triple, `3² + 4² = 5²`. In our core theorems the implicate three and the explicate four stand at right angles, and the five that joins them is the hypotenuse, present as connector and never appearing as an intermediate value, in the way the slash holds zero and one without being either. The triangle so formed has area `(3×4)/2 = 6`, the sixfold again, now as the area spanned by relation and structure at right angles, and perimeter `3+4+5 = 12`, the sixfold doubled; the whole Second Spanda can be written `100 = 4 × 5²`, four orientations times the square of the transcendent. These identities are exact and the readings attached to them are offered as resonance.

The two equations now have to be held together, and the holding has to be declared, because ordinary fraction arithmetic would destroy it. Read as ordinary fractions, `(4:2)/(3:3)` collapses to `2/1 = 2`, a single number that has lost both orientations. QL instead declares a comparison of corresponding entries:

$$
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{QL}}\bigl(4{:}2,\;3{:}3\bigr)\;:=\;\left(\frac43,\;\frac23\right).
$$

Once the comparison is declared, its outputs are ordinary nonzero ratios and ordinary arithmetic takes over. Each has a reciprocal, and the four together are the first family of musical intervals. `4/3` is the perfect fourth, which QL reads as manifestation, the span going forth, and its inverse `3/4` as recognition, the span read home. `2/3` is the descending fifth, the grounding, settling back, and its inverse `3/2` the ascending fifth, which our core theorems read with Whitehead as the lure, the potential that draws a field toward completion without pushing it.[^s3-lure] Fourth and fifth compose the octave:

$$
\frac43\cdot\frac32=\frac21.
$$

The squared fourth of the Second Spanda, `16/9`, falls short of that octave by a definite interval, and the interval arrives by three independent routes, as §0/1 · #5→0 promised. The octave divided by the totality-ratio gives it; the fifth divided by the fourth gives it; and two fifths stacked and folded back into the octave give it, which is how the Pythagorean construction of a keyboard reaches its whole tone:

$$
\frac{2}{16/9}=\frac98,\qquad \frac{3/2}{4/3}=\frac98,\qquad \frac{(3/2)^2}{2}=\frac{9/4}{2}=\frac98.
$$

The third route is the one the tuning literature uses. Kenneth Scholtz, setting out the chain of a Pythagorean keyboard, takes powers of `3/2` to one side of C and powers of `4/3` to the other, halving whenever a pitch would leave the octave: "Thus, 3/2 x 3/2 = 9/4. 9/4 is divided by 2, making D = 9/8."[^s3-scholtz-chain] The three routes are independent in the plain sense that each starts from a different pair of intervals, the octave against the doubled fourth, the fifth against the fourth, and the fifth against itself, and they converge exactly. The convergence gives the derivation its first remainder. The octave exceeds the doubled fourth by exactly the interval by which the fifth exceeds the fourth, so

$$
\frac{16}{9}\cdot\frac98=\frac21,\qquad \frac43\cdot\frac98\cdot\frac43=\frac21.
$$

The system almost doubles, falls short by one whole tone, and the whole tone carries it the rest of the way. Our core theorems call this `9/8` the measure of the matheme's non-closure, the live tick each cycle hands forward so that the next has something to begin from, and §3 · #4 pays the reading out in sound, where the remainder turns out to be what completes the return.

The `3:3` and the `4:2` need each other for the same reason the bellows needs its folds and its breath. Our core theorems put it bluntly: the `3:3` without the `4:2` is a *vibe*, proportion with nothing to proportion; the `4:2` without the `3:3` is a *catalogue*, positions with no inner relation; together they are a logic, countable *and* relational. An accordion's bellows have a fixed number of folds, a countable frame that does not change from phrase to phrase, and its music is the proportion of opening to closing that moves through that frame; the first Spanda is the return-reading of the second's frame, and the ratios are born only when the two are held together.

One equation in the native set reads as a standing identity beneath the whole oscillation:

$$
\frac01+\frac10=\frac11=100\%.
$$

As ordinary arithmetic the line is impossible, and the essay never asks a mathematical reader to accept it as such. As QL composition it is the bridge term restated as a law, the Self leaning into manifestation, `0/1`, and the manifest leaning home, `1/0`, composing to the whole, and the mediant has shown the composition to be a definite operation with `1/1` as its result. Read so, the line says that the instrument is whole at every point of its stroke, including the turn. Our machines produce wholes of this normalised kind many times a second. The attention mechanism of a language model, which §0/1 · #5→0 wrote out in full, turns each row of raw scores into weights by the softmax function, each weight the exponential of its score divided by the sum of all the exponentials, so that the weights always sum to one: a whole of one hundred per cent distributed among the words a position attends to.[^s3-softmax] The `1` of such a whole is a normalisation, exactly as the hundred per cent of the Second Spanda is, and what the normalisation does to the excluded alternatives, which keep a weight however small, is the apoha that §5 · #1 develops into the mechanics of selection.

Here the derivation ends and the reading begins, and the line between them runs through every display above. Ordinary arithmetic derives the field facts about `0/0` and `1/0`, the mediant's first step, the categorical and vector carriers of the bridge, `2 + 2² = 6`, `100 = 64 + 36`, `64/36 = 16/9 = 2⁴/3²`, and every identity among `4/3`, `3/2`, `9/8`, `16/9` and `2/1`. Our core theorems derive, within their declared operations, the proportional reading of the six slash-forms, the ordered First Spanda, and the `3:3` and `4:2` organisation of the sixfold. The mediant as the bridge's operation, the change from sum to ratio, the declared cross-comparison and its reading as the harmonic body of the sixfold, and `9/8` as the remainder that completes are argued; the right triangle in the base frame and the Śaiva and Whiteheadian names are offered. A pulse so derived still has to meet the formal neighbours that have studied self-reference on their own terms, and the first of them is a schoolteacher's subtraction, done by hand, that kept returning a number to itself.
