## The balance being examined

Read three sides together: **the achieved prose; the mature canonical argument/concept/etymological field; the author's original insight and its subsequent development.** Reconstruct the reasons joining them. The task is fidelity to a living argument, not restoration of the earliest wording or stylistic imitation of fragmentary notes.

The prose may preserve a later correction that an early notebook could not yet contain. It may also turn a first-person insight into a distant attribution, suppress an explicit claim, retain a slogan while dropping its inferential body, or lose an essential difference during compression. Establish which happened. An old phrase's intensity is not evidence that the later version is wrong; a polished formulation is not evidence that its source has survived. Record genuine tensions for authorial decision instead of quietly choosing a side.

## What to trace at each important passage

Locate the current assertion, the inference it performs, the mature owning proposition, the original authorial occurrence and relevant later correction. Preserve the difference between the author's words, a copied external quotation, assistant-generated development, and an explicitly adopted refinement. A filename containing the author's name, or text appearing in NOTES, does not establish authorship of every sentence. In mixed transcripts use speaker markers and surrounding turns; an initial assistant answer does not outrank the user's correction.

Give the actual connective reasoning. Removing A/C identifiers and hyperlinks must leave an intelligible argument. Explain how an etymological operation changes the claim here. Keep lexical attestation, authorial semantic construction and philosophical inference distinct while letting each carry its full force. Whole Mythemes need their figures, actions, transformations and aftermath; a decorative motif is not the whole's reasoning.

Review paradigm, identity, (no)Name, Power, limit, lived mediation and source-return through their actual local arguments. Their recurrence should accumulate meaning. Where a phrase such as “return to source” or “the means become visible” has lost the conditions that make it true, recover those conditions rather than merely intensifying the phrase.

## Voice and position of speaking

Examine “the essay”, “the author argues”, “the present work proposes”, “we can now see”, and analogous constructions contextually. Ask who speaks, what act the sentence performs, and whether it replaces an enacted argument with commentary upon that argument. A note distinguishing historical attribution from the author's inference can legitimately refer to the work. Do not ban a string, replace every occurrence with “we”, or invent autobiographical first-person experience. Proposed authorial excerpts must be exact and traceable; any proposed adaptation must be visibly separate from the original.

The author's request to recover his own voice authorises **reading and proposing excerpts**, not writing new material and passing it off as his pen-to-paper words. Keep spellings/lineation in the evidence quotation, with a separate suggested edited form only where useful. Extract complete enough passages to retain their movement. Poetry, polemic, intimate fragments and external quotations have distinct placement/permission questions. Public use awaits authorial approval and appropriate source standing.

## Findings and report in chat

Return a substantial, readable section report in chat. Start with the argument the current section successfully makes and the passages which should survive unchanged. Then prioritise consequential findings, not counts. Each finding must include:

- a stable finding ID (`s3-F01`, etc.), current manuscript location/short exact quotation, and the real claim being assessed;
- the mature canonical owner and operative proposition;
- the exact authorial-root passage, its path, heading/page/line locator, speaker and recoverable development stage; dates only where evidenced;
- the later refinement/ratification where relevant, or an explicit gap in that chain;
- the semantic difference and its consequences for reasoning, register, claim strength, nuance, voice or section placement;
- a bounded proposed repair direction, what existing achievement must be preserved, and any cross-section effects.

Use meaningful dispositions: **preserve/no fault; legitimate refinement; deliberate withholding/radial placement; lost inference; softened claim; slogan substitution; register or distinction drift; outside-narrator posture; excerpt opportunity; genuine unresolved authorial decision**. An overclaim allegation must identify the asserted proposition and the warrant exceeded; an underclaim allegation must identify the warranted proposition lost. More force is not automatically more fidelity.

Add a small, considered selection of **authorial passage opportunities**. For each, give the exact excerpt, source and context; distinguish authorial prose from an external quotation; explain what it contributes and its possible role (argument, transition, image, epigraph, note, or refrain). Preserve the connection to the mature field. There is no excerpt quota and no obligation to decorate every movement.

Finish with a coverage ledger: each of six quilt files and actual blocks read; relevant source-house pairs; Paper/Phone entries and their dispositions; canonical owners; receipts and later corrections; unread or inaccessible material. Report source gaps and review limits without projecting weakness onto arguments not implicated by them. Include a short cross-section pattern/seam return. Large fields may require continuation, but return actual supported findings and the exact unread frontier rather than claiming completion from an index.
