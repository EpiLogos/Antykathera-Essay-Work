<!-- movement:M03 — definition as cut, gift, danger -->

The cut came through whole. A name still reaches its bearer; a question still distinguishes its answers; a mistake can still be corrected. What failed was one demand made on definition — the demand for final enclosure — and what the failure left behind is a question about the cut itself: what does a boundary do with what it excludes?

Two witnesses, working independently, describe that operation from different sides. In a letter of 1674 to Jarig Jelles, Spinoza gives the geometric face: determination is negation, and his example is figure — a body has this shape because its extension is bounded this way, the outline making a portion of extension measurable as this figure.[^s01-spinoza] Dignāga gives the semantic face: a word makes its reference determinate by excluding other referents. The Buddhist analysis in the form Pind's translation preserves refuses the cheap version of its own doctrine: the referent is a thing qualified by preclusion, not the preclusion itself — exclusion does its work without replacing the thing with a bundle of absences.[^s01-pind] The two operations are not identical, and need not be. Figure and word agree in this: the boundary does positive work through what it leaves out, and the achieved form stays answerable to the field it was cut from. The essay will call this exclusion that remembers: what does not count under the term participates in what does, and the excluded alternatives stay active in what the selected mark is, the way the unchosen answers of a question stay active in what its chosen answer means — which is why a definition's history can be reread from its exclusions, and why a mark whose exclusions are forgotten starts presenting itself as the whole field.

One Greek practice gives the dependence a body. A *symbolon* could be a token broken between parties: each keeps a piece, and the pieces mean nothing singly — a vaguely similar piece will not fit. The edge is particular; the meeting is the test; the surviving half carries an absent relation into an encounter that can fail. Possessing the piece confers neither possession of the other party nor the power to decree the fit. What is absent can still answer the form that is present.

Now put a number where the token was. A meeting counts its attendees; the count can be impeccable and still count nothing about who is affected by the decision. Three offices separate here, and keeping them separate is most of the work: the criterion distinguishes, the tally gives the difference a workable boundary, and a decision then acts through it. The defect in the meeting is not in the arithmetic but in the substitution — attendance standing in for affectedness without an argument — and no refinement of the tally repairs a change of criterion. The excluded bear on what the count was entitled to mean. The danger shows its teeth when attendance becomes the sole test of standing: those outside are denied standing to question the criterion, because they were not present. The account now protects the condition under dispute; the response that could have exposed the limit arrives translated into a failure to satisfy it. The instrument that made a relation legible has been turned against the relation reaching the judgment. A finite account was never the danger; the danger is an account adjudicating every challenge to the basis of its own authority. And the same question can now be put to a system of proof: what happens when its rules are asked to determine the conditions under which the rules themselves count?

Mathematics met that question at the turn of the twentieth century, and met it as a construction. Bertrand Russell, sorting which collections logic may admit, considered the collection of all collections that are not members of themselves. Is it a member of itself? If it is, it qualifies for exclusion; if it is not, it qualifies for inclusion. The contradiction follows from the collecting permission itself — no overlooked member repairs it. Russell's repair, the theory of types, changes which expressions may be formed: a totality may not contain members defined in terms of that very totality, and the levels this introduces block the offending construction.[^s01-russell] The prohibition does positive mathematical work — a disciplined domain of reasoning becomes possible that was not possible before. What has been repaired is the permission to form and substitute expressions; the repair settles nothing about every question a theory can raise on itself.

Two decades later Kurt Gödel reached a limit of a different make. He arithmetised: in a formal theory strong enough for arithmetic, statements and finite proofs can be encoded as numbers, so the theory can represent its own proof-relation. A sentence can then be built that says, arithmetically, that no number codes a proof of this very sentence. If the theory is consistent, no such proof exists — a proof would supply the witness whose non-existence the sentence asserts — and Gödel's original stronger hypothesis, ω-consistency, excludes a proof of the negation as well.[^s01-godel] The sentence is well formed; its difficulty is derivability within a theory whose formation Russell's example had already disciplined. The theorem's force is bound to its hypotheses — effective axiomatisation, arithmetical strength, the stated consistency conditions — and without them the slogan that every system is incomplete is a different claim with its argument missing. What the theorem gives, under its conditions, is exact: growth in provability is real, an extension can prove what its predecessor could not, and the distinction between arithmetical truth and provability in a given theory does not close.

Ludwig Wittgenstein's *Tractatus* carries the same pressure into language. A proposition pictures a state of affairs by sharing logical form with it; that form shows itself in the saying and is not one more said thing among the others — the limit of what can be said is no item inside it. The book's own propositions obey its finding: the reader climbs the ladder and throws it away, having used it to see.[^s01-wittgenstein] What a representation can disclose and what it can state about its disclosing have come apart. The later *Philosophical Investigations* moves the pressure into practice: follow a rule, and if every application needs a further interpretation, no formulation of the rule supplies the point where interpretation begins to act. The signpost is followed within a practice that learns, maintains and corrects it; obeying and disobeying are distinguished by what is done, not by a feeling of compliance. A rule's life is not contained in the expression as meaning in a box.[^s01-pi]

Formal construction can also change what a calculus admits. G. Spencer-Brown begins with the act of drawing a distinction — a marked state, an unmarked state.[^s01-spencerbrown] Let the distinction be re-entered: let a state's value be the operation of crossing applied to it. In the two-state reading nothing stable answers — crossing turns either state into the other, and the required self-equality alternates without end. Francisco Varela's response is a construction change rather than a commentary: admit an autonomous value, with rules for its reduction alongside the others, and the re-entering form has somewhere to be.[^s01-varela] No consciousness follows from the third state; what follows is an outright construction: a self-referential operation can demand a reorganisation of the space that was to contain it — a repair in the fullest sense, a change to what a system can admit.

Nicholas of Cusa had put the finite mind's case with a diagram centuries earlier. Inscribe a regular polygon in a circle and increase its sides: the fit improves without limit, yet every finite polygon has straight edges and the circle has none. The improvement is genuine and never completes as possession. Cusa works the image inside his theology of learned ignorance — "we desire to know that we do not know," as the first chapter of his *On Learned Ignorance* has it: a wanting to know, ordered around the not-knowing instead of against it.[^s01-cusa] Modern mathematics can define the convergent sequence and its circular limit exactly; Cusa's pressure survives the exactness intact, because it concerns the finite knower's relation to what it measures. And the polygon differs from the sphere that carried the theorem's non-possession: one image approximates within a figure and never completes the possession, the other withdraws the exclusive centre and the enclosing edge altogether. Two operations, each doing work the other cannot — and this genealogy needs both, since its limits are drawn in figures, permissions and calculi, while the non-possession they keep returning to is not a figure at all.

Contemporary philosophy of science names the stakes in its own register. *The Blind Spot* — Adam Frank, Marcelo Gleiser and Evan Thompson — diagnoses the surreptitious substitution by which scientific abstractions are taken as more fundamental than the experience through which observation, measurement and justification become possible.[^s01-blindspot] Reading an instrument, noticing a discrepancy, judging that a repetition bears on a result: these belong to inquiry's life. A record can make them available for examination; understanding and using the record are further acts of the same life. The authors' diagnosis stays within their programme, and the essay's stronger claim — that the omission is structural rather than incidental — is argued on grounds of its own.

Gathered, the genealogy teaches by non-identity. A formation permission, a derivability bound, the divide between saying and showing, a rule's life in practice, a calculus's admitted states, an approximation that never possesses, an abstraction that quietly omits the experiencer who warrants it — each is a differently made limit, and each repair, once made, stands as an achieved result that another practice can inherit and use. The common proposition is argued across their differences, and only across their differences: a sufficiently articulate system cannot convert every condition of its articulation into ordinary internal terms without remainder. The remainder is no wall at the edge of things; it recurs through every enlargement, which is why improvement never exhausts it.

The remainder also leaves the inquirer employed rather than paralysed. Before any result is arbitrated, the investigation has already been undertaken in reliance — on language, memory, instruments, the testimony of others; the arbitrating arrives into a relation it did not create. An aphorism of this essay's own holds both sides together: "The Mind of Man is just a series of failed attempts to deal with the Unknown, in the process of which, however, we deal with a lot of unknowns."[^s01-unknown] Its second clause is indispensable: the failure of final capture leaves a world of effective investigations, each able to give something real. The reliance is therefore a relation to be made responsible — questioned, corrected, relinquished where consequence requires. The genealogy's constructive half — repairs that changed what a system could admit — is the beginning of an answer to the question the remainder leaves standing: how can what forms disclose include a working relation to their own conditions?

<!-- movement:M05 — Gebserian diaphaneity -->

Depth had to be discovered once. Jean Gebser reads the change in the means by which a world was rendered: Giotto coaxing weight and space out of flat gold ground; Petrarch stopping on a mountain and finding a landscape worth the look; Alberti fixing the painter's craft in the geometry of the visual pyramid; Leonardo carrying perspective into optics and machine.[^s01-gebser] A world became proportioned through a positioned eye. That is Gebser's claim exactly: people had always seen, but seeing acquired a structure — the scene rendered from a sector, the viewer placed outside what is viewed, the world newly available because positioned. The achievement is permanent and partial at once. It makes explicit, and it fixes.

His name for what the fixing conceals is the sector's claim to exclusive validity, and his account of consciousness runs through structures — archaic, magical, mythical, mental — that do not expire when the next arrives. They remain co-present: capacities, and distortions, in any present life. Integral consciousness, in his vocabulary, is the mutation in which the co-presence becomes transparent: the mental-rational achievement retained while its sector-claim is released, the earlier structures available without being either revived or dismissed. A person can participate in an image and discern, at once, how that participation organises perception. Nothing is lost but the pretence that the sector is the world.

Co-presence does its quiet work here, before any later claim leans on it. What a dream is, or a vision, or a sensed presence, this essay will receive first as something that occurred, to someone, before one explanatory register decides what it ultimately was. Occurrence, report, symbolic articulation and causal interpretation keep different evidential tasks, and keeping them distinct is what preserves the phenomenon for inquiry: the dream is real as an experience without its figures having stood beside the bed; the terror was lived; who the figure was and what caused it remain open. Treating "psychic" as a synonym for unreal removes the datum before inquiry begins; the person changed by the dream need not deny the change because the image was not an external object.

Martin Heidegger, working from inside the same metaphysical tradition, names the placement from its other face. The modern age, he argues, is defined by the world becoming picture — the world itself delivered over to representing, set before a subject as its picture — and by man's becoming *subiectum*, the relational centre in which beings as a whole must stand.[^s01-heidegger] What Gebser traces through art history, Heidegger exposes as authority: the representing subject becomes the standard against which disclosure is judged, and the judging position disappears behind the picture's apparent self-sufficiency. The two diagnoses arose independently; no documented exchange connects them; the twinning is this essay's own, argued because each supplies what the other withholds — Gebser the historical body, Heidegger the metaphysical mechanism and its technological consequence.

Neither diagnosis alone says what to want instead. Adding perspectives changes nothing essential: the collection still has a collector, and the collection's frame still decides what counts as a perspective. Averaging them would be worse — it erases exactly the differences that make their meeting informative. A myth, a perceptual report, a logical inference and a physical measurement accomplish different things, and their conjunction is legible only where the terms of meeting stay visible, including where a translation fails. *Diaphaneity* names this: a perspective becoming transparent to the context that makes its seeing possible, without ceasing to see.

Light through stained glass carries the whole relation. White light arrives with a capacity for colour that no window gave it; each pane admits and selects; the lead seams hold difference in place; a coloured image falls into the room where someone stands. Look only at the image and the aperture disappears; look only at the glass and the event of lighting goes missing. Move the sun or the viewer and the floor-pattern changes, and the change is information: this pane colours, that seam obstructs, the room's angle matters. Finishing the picture on the floor never produced the light that made it visible, and the source is disclosed through the ordered variations, never assembled by averaging the colours. Nothing requires pretending the glass has vanished. Mediation becomes intelligible through what it lets through, and what it lets through can change the next act of mediation.

Transparency of this kind is the opposite of detachment. It asks being-with: attending to a situation's text and subtext, caring what becomes of it.[^s01-p5] The one who retains the power to decide is involved through the decision; impartiality has to be won inside the relation, by attention to what can challenge the judgment and to whose experience its categories exclude. The failure mode has an old name in this essay's working: the absent Father — the ultimate standard invoked but never brought into the room, a vacancy that a surrogate is always ready to occupy. The final measure, the ruler, the account to which every reply must conform: each borrows a transcendence it did not earn. The defining performance has already exposed the substitution — an account of the whole belongs within the whole — and the practical consequence is a specific fidelity: the finite account answers for what it does.

A constructed case shows the same structure inside an answering system. An assistant is asked why a request went unanswered. Its archive holds a complaint that no answer arrived; its permissions exclude the folder with the original delivery failure; it reports that the recipient declined to respond. A note that the answer was produced "in context" changes nothing. The context was already working: access rules and retained evidence had helped determine what could count as an answer before anyone described them. Displaying the exclusion may let a person discover the problem; correcting the permission or the inference changes what the next inquiry can establish. Description and intervention are different offices, and both matter, because the world-picture problem has entered the machinery of answering: the frame helps produce the picture, and the picture conceals the frame's effects.

The contextual turn runs both ways and keeps its asymmetry. The world becomes articulate to someone, and what becomes articulate discloses that person's situation — body, language, history, other people, material means all enter what can appear and what can be done. And my account of another person cannot supply their reply. Addressed, they can change the terms I approached them in, and the account becomes adequate only when that change can reach its judgment — not when it is filed as another item under a judgment held fixed.

A condition operative everywhere — in the failed enclosure, the unowned cut, the bounded proof, the fixed sector — has been named in images but given no sign. Gebser names the achieved mutation aperspectival and integral: the co-presence become transparent to itself, the whole coming round. The essay's exacting of that promise is a turn — a perspective's constituting field becoming part of what it can know, the sector's fixed angle opening into the full round — and the essay will need more than a promise here. It will need a sign that can position the condition that positions everything else.

The essay proposes, for trial, zero. Let it enter as a promissory sign and no more. The written zero is perfectly determinate — a glyph, a digit, a mark with a history this section has not yet given it. What is proposed here is its office: `0` marks the condition under which counting occurs, the place no counted item occupies, since every counted item takes the position of one. The present knowing did not become absent when no final portrait could take its place; what the mark leaves open is already active in the act that reads it, the way an open place in a score is heard — a rest is part of the music, counted in the time, sounding as the shape of what is not sounded. History, arithmetic and formal derivation are withheld: the section has earned the position, and the position is all it claims.[^s01-zero]

For form held within movement, one image before the hand-off. David Bohm's image is the vortex a river makes around a stone: a shape you can name, standing in the water, sustained only by the water's moving — divert the flow and the vortex is gone without a trace of remains.[^s01-bohm] In his proposed implicate order, relatively discrete things stand to a primary movement the way the vortex stands to the river: their relations belong to their formation, the way the whirl's outline belongs to the current and not to some skeleton of water left spinning in place. The image focuses the question the section ends with, and marks its boundary: the stable form is not its own sustaining, and the physical pattern — however long it stands — does not supply its own apprehension. The pattern's standing-for already requires the very apprehension the section has not yet examined.

The inquiry returns to where it started, with different attention. "I see this": knower, seeing and seen distinguishable within one occurrence; something appears, can be attended to, enters memory, takes a name. What knows has turned out to be inseparable from what it knows through — and the means are real enough to investigate and to change. How does awareness differentiate itself into the means by which it knows, and the world it knows through them?
