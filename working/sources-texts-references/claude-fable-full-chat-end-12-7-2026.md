**I. Laws of Form and the generation of time**

Spencer-Brown's _Laws of Form_ (1969) opens with a sentence that is practically your thesis: a universe comes into being when a space is _severed_. The primitive act is "draw a distinction" — one mark, the cross, which is simultaneously operator and operand, the act of cutting and the name of the cut (indication and distinction are one act; you cannot indicate without severing). The whole primary arithmetic runs on two axioms: _calling_ (a mark beside a mark condenses to one mark — repetition of a name adds nothing) and _crossing_ (a mark within a mark cancels to the unmarked state — crossing a boundary twice returns you). Chapter 11 is where your oscillation check lives.
