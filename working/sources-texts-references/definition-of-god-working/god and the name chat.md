This is exactly the right place to bring in the amplificatory field — because the phenomenon you're describing (fascination as flight-from-awareness-into-object) has been independently triangulated by several traditions, and lining them up gives you both terminological cover and confirms the pattern isn't an artifact of your own system.

**The mechanism itself: Otto and Jung.** Rudolf Otto's _mysterium tremendum et fascinans_ is the classical starting point — the numinous grips as _simultaneously_ dread and allure, and crucially, it grips; it isn't chosen. Jung took this directly into his definition of the numinosum: an autonomous psychic content that seizes the subject rather than being surveyed by it. This answers your "why does attention depart from awareness in flight toward its object" question at the mechanical level — attention, in the Husserlian sense, is _structurally_ intentional, always consciousness-_of_ something; it cannot rest without an object, the way an eye cannot see without focusing. Awareness, by contrast, is what doesn't need an object — it's the field within which objects and their pursuit occur. Fascination is what happens when attention's ordinary outbound motion meets a content charged enough to _hold_ it there rather than let it cycle on to the next object. The imago dei, in whatever guise, is by definition the _maximum_ available charge — so it's the paradigm case of fascination, not an exception to it.

**Why this serves adaptation even at awareness's expense.** You're pointing at something real: fascination is the mind's tool for allocating trust economically. An organism can't deliberate every stimulus; it needs some contents pre-marked as "attend here, this matters," and that marking _is_ a proto-trust-verdict issued beneath cognition. The god-image, historically, is the most efficient possible carrier of that marking — it says "ultimate concern goes here" and the whole nervous system organizes around the claim. That's the adaptive gain. The cost — and this is your "against the one for whom fascination is fixating" — is that awareness, which was whole and objectless, gets progressively bent into a permanent outbound posture chasing its own most charged productions. Individuation is real and worth having, but it's purchased with exactly this coin: awareness curving into attention, ground curving into figure.

**The imago dei's flowering as splintering — Shaivism has a precise term for this.** What you're calling "the slice that is a pie" has a name in Kashmir Shaivism: _kalā_, one of the tattvas in the descent from Śiva through Māyā-śakti — literally "a fragment/portion," the limited capacity to act that results when Śiva's total power (_sarva-kartṛtva_) contracts into a finite agent. It's not corruption, it's the technical mechanism by which the infinite becomes experienceable at all — totality can only _appear_ as a series of partial disclosures, never as a lump. Your yogic-soteriological point stands exactly here: full access is _tacitly_ always retained (that's why liberation is recognition, not acquisition), but ordinary experience is necessarily rationed into kalā-sized portions, sacredly, because unrationed totality isn't an experience anyone finite could have and remain finite.

**Suddha vikalpa, and why "all ideas are words for God" is a technical claim, not a poetic one.** In Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta's Pratyabhijñā school, every _vikalpa_ — every determinate thought, every act of naming something as something — is, at bottom, nothing but Śiva's own self-referential vibration (_spanda_) touching itself. Ordinary (_aśuddha_, impure) vikalpa forgets this and treats the naming as closure onto a separate object. _Śuddha_ vikalpa is the same act of thought, recognized (_pratyabhijñā_) as Śiva recognizing himself — "this is Śiva." This gives you the missing philosophical hinge back to the diabolical turn we mapped earlier: the diabolical is precisely _aśuddha_ vikalpa functioning at scale — naming that has forgotten its own ground and claims itself as terminal rather than as a moment of Śiva's self-touch. The cure isn't fewer names, it's names that know what they're doing.

**The overlap of archetypal and actual reality — Māṇḍūkya gives you the map you're reaching for.** The four _padas_ of Ātman in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad are exactly the architecture you're gesturing at: _Vaiśvānara_ (waking, gross, outward cognition — your "actual"/empirical axis), _Taijasa_ (dream, subtle, inward cognition), _Prājña_ (deep sleep, causal, undifferentiated seed-mass where all distinctions are enfolded but unmanifest), and _Turīya_, the fourth, which is not a state alongside the others but the witnessing ground pervading all three. _Taijasa_ is functionally identical to what Henry Corbin called the _mundus imaginalis_ (_ʿālam al-mithāl_) — an intermediate ontological register, neither merely mental nor merely physical, where archetypal meaning takes on quasi-perceptible form. This is the plane where "archetypal reality and actual reality overlap and co-express," because it isn't actually two realities meeting — it's the middle term (Taijasa/imaginal) mediating between the causal seed-totality (Prājña, your "pie" whole) and the sliced, gross instantiation (Vaiśvanara, empirical history). Turīya is the awareness that attention departs _from_ in the first place — so structurally, your whole question resolves into: fascination is Turīya's motion into Taijasa's imaginal charge, seeking closure in Vaiśvānara's actuality, forgetting Prājña's undifferentiated wholeness along the way, and calling the forgetting "waking up."

For the series, this suggests you don't need a new lexicon here at all — you can use the Māṇḍūkya's four states almost visually (they're already a near-perfect diagram: seed / imaginal / actual / witness) as the episode's spine, with Otto and Jung supplying the felt phenomenology of fascination, Corbin supplying the Western-esoteric bridge so it doesn't read as imported Vedanta, and _kalā_/_śuddha vikalpa_ supplying the precise technical language for "splintering as sacred, not just as loss."
