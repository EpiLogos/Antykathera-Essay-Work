There are, of course, several consequences of
the abandonment of such a classical principle, but
these are not so serious as one might expect.
Ackerman and Fitch'?:'* have presented consistent
contradiction-free logical systems leaving out
tertium non datur, and have been able to show that

the richness of such logic is ample enough to
permit the construction of most of the classical
mathematics. In this sense, a three-valued logic,
although it forces us to abandon logical principles
which appear so basic to our common discourse,
can nevertheless be reconstructed so as to yield
enough richness to deal in some other way with
the common forms of discourse (and thus with
basic mathematics), For the extended algebra,
being interpretable as one of these logics, similar
conclusions are valid.
