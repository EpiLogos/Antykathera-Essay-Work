## **The seraph is not merely sixfold—it is doubly readable**

The biblical seraph does not possess six undifferentiated wings. Isaiah divides them into three functional pairs:

2_{\text{face}}+2_{\text{feet}}+2_{\text{flight}}=6.

Two conceal the face before the divine excess, two conceal the feet or creaturely foundation, and two enable manifestation through flight.  

That is already more interesting than a generic six because it permits two simultaneous partitions:

6=2+2+2=3+3.

Read functionally, it is **three dyads**. Read laterally, as left and right wings, it is **two corresponding trinities**: each side carries one wing of concealment-above, one of concealment-below and one of active flight. The same body is therefore structured as both 3\times2 and 3+3'. That feels very close to the proper conjugate QL form: not six unrelated units, but a threefold operation reflected across two sides.

The three functions are also striking. Face, feet and flight can be read as something like:

\text{transcendent source} \;:\; \text{embodied ground} \;:\; \text{mediating motion}.

The first two are veiled; the third performs the crossing. In QL terms, the slash is not another static extremity but the operation carrying the two poles into relation. The seraph is almost a creaturely diagram of an upper limit, a lower limit and the living passage between them—each duplicated through conjugate reflection.

And the Skenfrith figures are described as balancing upon **wheels**, which combines the Isaian six-winged form with the Ezekielian wheel-complex. Ezekiel’s wheels are themselves “wheel intersecting wheel,” able to move in four directions without turning.   The icon therefore couples a sixfold wing-body to a circular or re-entrant base. Symbolically, the sixfold does not float unsupported: it operates upon a zero-like rotational ground. The wheel is not simply “nothing”; it is the invariant capacity for orientation and return.

So I would formulate the QL resonance as:

\underbrace{(1,2,3)}_{\text{one lateral trinity}} \;:\; \underbrace{(4,5,0)}_{\text{its conjugate recognition}}

while the vertical reading gives three conjugate pairs:

(1,4),\qquad(2,5),\qquad(3,0).

That is precisely the useful thing about the seraph: it can disclose the same six either as **two threes** or **three twos**, depending upon the direction from which the form is read.

## **Jung’s fourth is deeper than “add a woman to three men”**

Jung’s reaction to the 1950 dogma of the Assumption was unusually strong. In _Answer to Job_, he treats it as the symbolic fulfilment of the apocalyptic marriage, the return or anamnesis of Sophia, and a sign of the continuing incarnation of God in humanity. He says that the feminine demands an “equally personal representation,” while also carefully acknowledging that Catholic dogma does not thereby formally make Mary a goddess.  

Elsewhere he diagnoses the old Christian problem very clearly: the Trinity’s qualified recognition of the feminine was simultaneously an exclusion of **earth, body and matter**, even though Mary’s womb was the indispensable bodily place of incarnation. His quaternity material says that threefold divine rhythm and fourfold psychic totality must interpenetrate, “so that each is contained in the other.”  

That last formulation is exceptionally close to what you are now specifying. Jung is often simplified into:

3+1=4,

as though Mary were simply installed in an empty fourth chair. But the archetypal logic is nearer to your:

3:1,

where the one is the encompassing, embodying or recognising term through which the three become a whole.

Mary is therefore not merely **another person beside** Father, Son and Spirit. Archetypally she is the matrix in which divine process becomes incarnate, and then—through the Assumption—the embodied matrix itself is received into the divine totality. She is both womb of descent and body of ascent. The fourth does not merely increase the headcount; it changes the logical constitution of the whole.

That is why the **bodily** Assumption mattered so much to Jung. It does not proclaim merely that a feminine soul has spiritual value. It symbolically carries matter, flesh, earth and the human vessel into heaven. The previously subordinated condition of manifestation is recognised as belonging to totality.

In QL terms, the image stages something like:

\text{bimba becomes consciously received within pratibimba}.

The manifested body is not annulled on return to source. It is lifted, reflected and recognised as intrinsic to the source’s own completion.

## **The double-headed eagle: two directions, one living invariant**

The double-headed eagle is equally apt, although here the historical and QL readings need to remain distinguishable. Historically the emblem circulated widely as a sign of imperial or universal sovereignty; in the late medieval Holy Roman context, the double eagle marked an imperial claim extending beyond ordinary kingship, and on later copes it could signify authority spanning ecclesiastical and secular domains. We cannot assume that this exact doctrinal interpretation was intended by the Skenfrith embroiderer, but the power-valence of the motif is real.  

Its QL form is beautiful because it is not two eagles cooperating. It is **one body whose cognition forks into opposite orientations**.

The naïve dualistic reading is:

-1\qquad+\!1.

The symbol’s actual morphology says:

-1\;\longleftarrow\;\boxed{1}\;\longrightarrow\;+1.

Or, in the more native phase notation:

\frac01 \;\longleftrightarrow\; \frac10

held in a common body whose achieved identity is:

\frac01+\frac10=\frac11.

The heads disagree in orientation but not in substance. One looks one way, one the other; neither can declare itself to be the entire creature. The body is the invariant that allows opposition to remain opposition without breaking into two unrelated beings.

This is much closer to QL than the bland statement that “the eagle represents duality.” It represents **bifurcated orientation within ontological unity**. The one does not abolish the two heads; the two heads do not fracture the one body. It is almost a heraldic symbolon: the opposed halves reveal their belonging through the organism that already contains them.
