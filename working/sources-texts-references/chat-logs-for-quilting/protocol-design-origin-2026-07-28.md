## Obsidian Publish is suitable—with one important limit

Obsidian Publish natively gives us search, navigation, hover previews, backlinks, table of contents, stacked pages, and graph view. It also allows a curated navigation order. That is an excellent base for the work’s relational reading experience. [Obsidian’s current Publish settings document those components](https://obsidian.md/help/publish/manage-sites).

It also supports `publish.css`; with a custom domain it supports `publish.js`, so the constellation plates and richer ontology navigator can be progressively added there. [Official customization documentation](https://obsidian.md/help/publish/customize).

But the built-in graph is a link graph, not a semantic ontology renderer. It cannot by itself show that one edge is a warrant, another a transition, another a resonance, and another `Argued-from`. Therefore:

- ordinary Markdown links must remain the durable graph edges;
- typed relationships must also exist in machine-readable data;
- the build should generate visible relation rails from those typed edges;
- a custom constellation navigator can read a generated ontology manifest;
- the whole vault must remain intelligible when that JavaScript is unavailable.

Community plugins should not be runtime dependencies of the public work. Publish does not generally execute Dataview or other plugin-rendered queries. Bases and Canvas may be useful authoring tools, but every public view should be precompiled into Markdown, SVG, or the controlled `publish.js` layer.

## The eventual form

The publication has three inseparable dimensions:

1. **The essay** — Frank’s lean, continuous argumentative line.
2. **The constellation** — the same work opened spatially through sections, ideas, sources, diagrams, and transverse paths.
3. **The companion skill** — an intelligent way of walking and questioning the work through unified QL/MEF operations.

The essay remains sovereign as the primary reading experience. Someone should be able to read it from beginning to end, follow the argument, and never need to open another note. Links appear where the prose genuinely opens onto further depth—not as administrative annotations on every sentence.

The vault then permits another kind of reading: entering outward from a passage, following an idea across several sections, dwelling in a source encounter, viewing a diagram, or tracing one of the work’s recurring transformations.

That is the holographic form: the whole remains perceptible from several local entrances.

## The essay and its constellations

At the scale of the complete essay, the movement is:

`0/1 → 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 5→0`

That same form recurs within each major section:

- **0/1 — Arrival.** The section’s threshold: what has arrived here, what pressure or question is now live, and what the section inherits from elsewhere.
- **Sixfold movement.** The actual philosophical development of the section.
- **5→0 — Return.** What the movement has transformed, what follows from it, and where it now passes within or beyond the section.

The two additional layers are primarily functional, but they can absolutely contain prose. Indeed, they probably should.

The `0/1` might be a compact threshold passage, a question, an inherited image, a diagram, a source encounter, or a statement of the tension entering the section. Its function is to gather the reader into the local movement.

The `5→0` should not merely summarize. It can be a written return: consequence, inversion, implication, release, or passage into another constellation. Its function is to show what the section now makes possible.

So each section becomes both:

- a continuous piece of essay prose; and
- a navigable constellation with an arrival, sixfold development, return, diagrammatic form, and outward pathways.

The form is a rhythm, not a form to be mechanically filled. Different sections can weight those elements differently.

## What the published vault should feel like

The front page should be spare and invitational:

- **Read *The Return of Zero***
- **Enter the constellation**
- **Follow an idea**
- **Walk a path through the work**

The main essay should look like an essay: generous typography, minimal interface noise, and restrained linking. At section transitions, a small constellation diagram can offer a spatial view without interrupting the reading.

Each section’s constellation page can hold:

- threshold prose for `0/1`;
- the sixfold movement;
- the relevant diagram extracted from the existing HTML;
- selected links into ideas, sources, other sections, and paths;
- return prose for `5→0`;
- several genuine ways onward.

The deeper notes are not merely support records. They are chambers in which an idea can be developed beyond what the essay’s line can bear. Some may be predominantly Frank’s writing, some genuinely co-developed, and some generated as exploratory elucidations. That difference need not become the publication’s subject. The test is whether the note deepens the work, remains faithful to its movement, and helps the reader think.

The visible vault can therefore remain simple:

```text
Start Here
The Return of Zero
The Constellation

Sections/
Ideas/
Sources/
Paths/
Diagrams/
```

Folders are backstage conveniences. The reader encounters meaningful pages and pathways, not the taxonomy.

## Linking and the graph

Ordinary Obsidian links are sufficient because the prose supplies their meaning.

A passage may say that a claim is developed elsewhere, depends upon a particular source encounter, undergoes an inversion in another section, or appears differently under another determination. The link does not need to encode that relation separately: the sentence already does it.

The graph then becomes the visible residue of actual intellectual relations. It need not pretend to be a formal ontology engine. Backlinks, local graphs, previews, search, and the global graph allow the reader to feel the work’s density and discover unexpected routes.

The graph is not the argument and should not replace navigation. It is the work’s navigable aura.

The HTML symbol material can be extracted into static diagrams for the Markdown pages. Those diagrams should function as philosophical plates within the publication—not merely illustrations and not a new software subsystem.

## QL/MEF as one instrument

QL and MEF should never be presented as two independent systems.

They form one compound practice:

- QL carries the positional and transformational movement.
- MEF is the operation by which that movement is encountered through a particular lens, field, or register.
- Tetralemma is one MEF lens.
- The other lenses and operations belong to the same instrumentarium.

The reader should not have to decide, “Do I use QL or MEF?” The companion should determine how the unified instrument meets the question.

Likewise, the public skill package should not expose a pile of specialist skills. It should have one front door—something like **Enter *The Return of Zero***—with three natural forms of engagement:

- **Walk the work:** read linearly, by section, or through a selected path.
- **Follow an idea:** trace one idea through prose, diagrams, sources, and transformations.
- **Investigate a question:** bring a live question into the unified QL/MEF field and allow the appropriate lenses to operate.

Tetralemma, CMEA, encounter-axis operations, topology, and related procedures can remain internal capacities of that companion. They appear when they genuinely illuminate the question, not as a menu of frameworks.

The companion should begin with the reader’s pressure: *What are you trying to understand? What passage has caught you? What contradiction or intuition are you carrying?* It then finds a living entrance into the work. It should not begin by dumping coordinates or explaining the architecture.

Kepano-style Obsidian handling belongs beneath this layer. It gives the agent reliable command of notes, links, properties, embeds, and navigation. It is craft infrastructure, not part of the intellectual spectacle.
