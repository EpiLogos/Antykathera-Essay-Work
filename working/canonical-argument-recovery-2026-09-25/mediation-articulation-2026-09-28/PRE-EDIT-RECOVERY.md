## Recovered generating relation

The whole is Life / Mind, differentiated as Subjective Immediacy / knower, Objective Internality / means, and World / known. The middle is constitutive: a knower does not first stand opposite a completed known and subsequently select a neutral bridge. The mode of disclosure helps determine how something can be encountered, discriminated, remembered, addressed and acted upon. This is a positive appointment of pramāṇa, not an inventory of functions from which the knower is later manufactured.

Dyczkowski's available transcript gives the immediate derivation. At pp.63–64 knower, means and known attend one cognitive act together. Fire assimilates, Sun illuminates, Moon appears by reflected light: all three belong to the image. At pp.65–66, blue and yellow appear distinctly in one cognition. Absolute homogeneity could not distinguish them; unrelated cognitions could not hold their difference in one act. Formlessness and omniformity belong together. The source's account of self-illumination meets the regress of a second awareness illuminating the first: adding another observer simply renews the question.

The mirror has **two distinct limits**. An ordinary mirror requires an exterior original; the Śaiva mirror generates and hosts its appearances (pp.66–68). An ordinary mirror also fails to apprehend its own display; vimarśa is living self-presence (pp.69–70). These are separate operations, not a single generic warning about reflection. The source does not reduce every physical object to a private person's fantasy. Its concern is the appearing of the object and the one field through which inner and outer become intelligible.

At pp.71–72, experience leaves saṃskāras, recollection makes a past affection present as past, and imagination recomposes retained material into a new affection. Retention, recall and recomposition differ. A running action and a rapidly spoken sentence require a stable relation through changing phases: the last phase alone does not contain the act. Internal aham-vimarśa and outward object-directed reflection are two directions within awareness. Karma-vimarśa names outward unfolding; kriyā-vimarśa names unrestricted agency before a particular goal. A goal and its product cannot exhaust the power through which they become possible.

The four acts at p.74 are negating one's nature, identifying with another, holding the two together, and negating the merged determination. The first two cooperate in bheda, separation; the held relation gives bhedābheda; the return to undivided awareness gives abheda. Four acts and three levels must remain distinguishable. At p.75, vertical manifestation/withdrawal and horizontal approach/return in perception are two axes, not two names for the same sequence. Will, knowledge and action remain distinct powers. The transcript's p.80 experient/product argument gives the source relation its positive force: an experienced cessation still presupposes the experient for whom it occurs.

The complete core theorem and P2/P3 notebooks deepen articulation. Parā Vāk is the Word's undivided source; paśyantī is seeing/formative articulation, madhyamā inner distinction, vaikharī explicit expression. Speech is an event of formation before it becomes a visible string. I, you and it are relational offices: the addressed mountain can move from object to you to first-person articulation. The same person may occupy different offices across an exchange. Context holds the exchanges without being a fourth pronoun. Nāma includes feeling, perception, intention, contact and attention; a proper name can be empty of an exhaustive essence yet full of address, history and responsibility. The kaleidoscope's turn is non-arbitrary because the patterned material constrains the new arrangement. Centring discloses; decentring returns. The symbol opens by retaining its absent counterpart, and the pure notion uses a determinate thought to return through thought's limit.

The tattvic unit gives these operations their genealogy: Anuttara/Paramaśiva; Śiva–Śakti; the inverse universal orientations of Sadāśiva–Īśvara; Sadvidyā–Māyā; finite powers, Puruṣa, Prakṛti, inner instrument, faculties and elements; recognition. Universal “I am this” precedes the ego's appropriation of a particular role or possession. The five contractions are efficacy, knowledge, desire through incompletion, time, and ordering. The Sāṃkhya triad distinguishes ascertainment, appropriation and coordination; its passive-witness doctrine must retain its own historical attribution.

The gnomon is exact arithmetic: `36−25 = 6²−5² = (6−5)(6+5) = 11 = 5+1+5`. A new row and column of five meet at one corner. Taylor's offered reading places five pure and five contracted powers around Māyā above the twenty-fivefold apparatus, thereby exposing genesis and return rather than merely increasing an inventory. This does not require a historically invented claim that Abhinavagupta constructed the thirty-six by square arithmetic, nor a completed category-by-category six-by-six mapping.

## Whole image retained

The Māyā whole is the eye, productive veil, frame, horizon, dream, crystal, nested simulation, Other's reply, stained window, local reference, native eightfold and recognitive return. The veil makes a finite world possible; hybris is its **second** forgetting, the achieved frame claiming the horizon of reality. Dream establishes the possibility of objective experience without externally material dream objects. The dream's experienced urgency is real; it is not proof that the cosmos is a sleeper.

The later Neumann corrections preserve recognition before ego appropriation, and the entire eightfold mythic recapitulation.
