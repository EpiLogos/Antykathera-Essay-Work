Most of us have said it, likely more than once: "I know myself." We say it to turn down the job that looked right on paper, to explain why we will not go to the party, or halfway through an argument, when someone tells us who we are and we're sure they have it wrong. And most of us have been wrong in the same sentence, when a friend saw the anger in our face before we felt it, or in the year we found ourselves wanting what we had always said we did not want. "I know myself" sets the one who knows apart from the self made available to knowledge, and claims that the two belong together; after a definition of the Subject that failed exactly where it had to, we can hear how much turns on that belonging. We recognise ourselves through a description, remember ourselves through an image, understand something of our lives through another person's words, and each gives us something definite to work with. Trouble begins when that definiteness seems to settle what we are, so that the means through which we came to know ourselves quietly assume the authority of the one who knows. Our language for knowing carries the predicament inside it: behind Latin *scire*, to know, lies, on the standard account, the reconstructed root *skei-*, to cut or split, which also gives *scindere*, to split, and from *scire* comes *scientia*, knowledge, our *science*.[^s01-scire] Knowing cuts. It separates a voice from the surrounding noise, a remembered event from an imagined one, an explanation from an excuse, and the last of those cuts needs more than an inventory of words, since the same sentence can serve either purpose: we have to understand what the speaker is doing, what occasioned the statement and what acknowledging it would change, so the cut grows more exact as more of the relation between the words and their speaker becomes intelligible.

*Scire* also enters *conscire*, to know together, to know with, and from that family comes *conscientia*, whose history joins shared knowledge, inward awareness and moral testimony. Latin *conscientia* and French *conscience* carried senses that modern English distributes between two words, and the familiar division between consciousness as awareness and conscience as moral awareness took shape chiefly in the seventeenth century, as philosophers came to distinguish an awareness of one's own thinking from a judgment on what one thought or did.[^s01-conscientia] We hear the distinction readily now, but it had to be worked out inside a vocabulary in which being aware and being answerable were still entangled, and the theologians show why they stayed entangled so long. In his *Discourse of Conscience* of 1596 the English theologian William Perkins explained *conscire* as two parties knowing a secret together: another person can know what I reveal, but God knows even what I conceal, and conscience makes me a participant in God's knowledge of my own life, able to speak against me or on my behalf.[^s01-perkins] Its testimony is more intimate than an accusation arriving from outside, and more unsettling, because what I say about myself is already exposed to a knowing my account does not command. Augustine had confessed that exposure twelve centuries earlier: in Book X of the *Confessions* he addresses a God before whom the depths of human conscience lie open even when the speaker would hide them, and he admits that something of a person remains unknown to the person's own spirit while God knows it entire.[^s01-augustine] His confession therefore holds what he knows of himself and what he does not: he speaks as one addressed and known, and his account of himself never catches up with the knowing it addresses.

Knowing-with reaches what is not-known, and our definition shows why the theological language reaches for God at this point. God cannot be located as one more observer inside the experience, since any such observer would be one more figure appearing within it; the knowing that conscience calls God stays intimate to every appearance without becoming one of them. That knowing is unconditioned, not an "I know *x*" but "I know that I know". I call this knowing-with at its root **withness**: the witnessing present in every act of knowing, by which a life is known from within as it is lived. Withness is structurally and existentially the nature of this being witness to your own witnessing-being. Through it I can testify to what I did, discover what I concealed from myself, and recognise a responsibility my preferred description had left out. In the language of conscience, God is this knowing that like air becomes implicit and silent in the life I am trying to tell: the witness is present through the telling, and the telling can return the speaker to a withness forgotten in the account they give of themselves, if one can "be silent", or rather, "let silence be".

If witnessing occurs through "you" or "me" as its aperture, then the particularity of a life carries a responsibility already implicit in its knowing. Through these eyes, this history and these habits of attention, something of the world becomes available to understanding and action, so I can discover a motive I had concealed from myself and also recognise how my attachment to a particular account has governed what I was willing to see. From here we can see why consciousness and conscience were once so intimate: the life is witnessed from within, and the person giving its account answers for how they took part in its disclosure. "I knew what I was doing" acknowledges that participation; "I did not want to know" reveals it more painfully, because I recognise that I helped maintain the ignorance through which I acted. The ego-self claims sovereignty when it treats this aperture as its possession and appropriates the knowing that occurs through it. My account then speaks as though its authority originated in me: what I admit becomes what is real, and what I exclude need not concern me. It happens in any argument with someone we love, at the moment we say "I know what I meant" and stop listening to what they heard. Tyranny begins this intimately, in the demand that a life conform to the image through which I have understood it, and it extends outward when others must conform to that image too. Yet the witnessing that gives my experience its immediacy is equally present through theirs, and their suffering does not become unreal because my description has found no place for it. Conscience interrupts the appropriation by returning me to the withness of witnessing, in which I am implicated in what becomes visible through this life while the knowing itself exceeds my claim upon it. Letting silence be allows that excess to reach me before I have furnished it with an answer, and then I may have to change how I look, what I acknowledge, and what I do to the person whose life my explanation had kept at a distance.

Thomas Hobbes, writing *Leviathan* in 1651, still used *conscious* for people who know a fact together and can therefore testify to it, and he watched the same word turn toward its opposite: people "vehemently in love with their own new opinions" lent those opinions the reverenced name of conscience, as though it were unlawful to change or speak against them.[^s01-hobbes] Shared testimony and private self-certification had come to occupy one word. A family meets the same turn on an ordinary Saturday. A parent promised the beach if the bedroom was tidied, the bedroom was tidied, more or less, and now parent and child stand in its doorway holding the same promise and disagreeing about what it requires, a disagreement that still concerns something they share. Then the parent announces, as the parent, what the promise must have meant, and the common knowing has been subordinated to one participant's account of it. English has a less reverent pun for this turn, in which the *con-* of knowing-together becomes a **con**, the swindle, short for the confidence trick, which works by winning a trust it means to use.[^s01-con] In the con of self-knowledge the knower is persuaded that the image in which it recognises itself is what it finally is: Mono, the unqualified One, is taken in by a determination within Poly, the field of its appearances.

Some sentences we finish without hearing them: "I'm just not a numbers person", "I'm the one who holds it together", "I don't do conflict". Each began as an intelligible account of habits, attachments and circumstances, and over the years each comes to decide which experiences may be admitted as ours, which desires are permissible and which memories must be explained away. Someone was "the sensible one" at twelve, or "the difficult one", or "the clever one", and some part of them still checks with that verdict when they are about to speak up in a meeting, let themselves be angry, or try something they might fail at. A challenge to the description can then feel like a threat to being someone at all, because what was received as a way of understanding a life has become the condition under which that life is permitted to understand itself.

Jung found the same movement at the scale of a god, in *The Red Book*: "if we set a god outside ourselves, he tears us loose from the self, since the god is more powerful than we are. Our self falls into privation. But if the god moves into the self, he snatched us from what is outside us. We arrived at singleness into ourselves." And further: "No one has my God, but my god has everyone, including myself… So it is always only the one god despite his multiplicity."[^s01-redbook-god] Placed outside, the god becomes a determinate image standing where the source should be, and the life that set it there is made to depend on what it has itself projected, which is Mono taken in by a figure of Poly, the con at the scale of a god. Moved into the self, the image becomes again an expression through which the common source is met. Jung's "only the one god despite his multiplicity" is our `0/1` in a vernacular sentence: no life possesses the One, every life is held in it, and its multiplicity is the All of its appearings. We can write the concealment directly:

$$0/1\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{the }0/\text{ is occluded}\;}\;1$$

Something is still appearing, being recognised, desired and defended, so the relation goes on operating, but the determinate self-image now presents itself as the source of that activity, and its acquired terms inherit the intimacy of *I am*.

A cut gives, because it makes a determinate thing knowable, and it endangers, because the achieved object can forget the field, the exclusions and the activity through which it appeared. To see how a distinction acquires such power over a life, we simply have to let it do its ordinary work. A boundary gives *here* by also giving *there*: inside and outside arise through one line, and each lets us locate the other. An ordinary circle encloses a definite region within an already available space, so we can point to its edge, measure its interior and cross from one side to the other. Its exactness lies in making a local difference that at once gives us two related places to proceed from, and it is the finite face of the image we took from Cusa, whose infinite sphere withdrew both the privileged centre and the enclosing edge. In his letter to Jarig Jelles of 2 June 1674, Spinoza called this work of finite determination negation. Matter considered without limit has no figure, he argued, and "figure can only exist in finite and determinate bodies": a body has its shape through the limits of its extension, and because figure is nothing but determination, and "determination is negation", the outline makes the shape by marking where the shape ends.[^s01-spinoza] The line makes the circle this figure and in the same stroke distinguishes the extension beyond it, so what looks like a shape's positive possession has been achieved through its relation to what the shape excludes.

Buddhist philosophers in India met the same cut inside an inquiry into reliable knowledge, asking how perception, inference and language reach what they claim to know. In the sixth century Dignāga pressed on the difference between encountering a particular and understanding it under a general concept. We followed a child's proper name through the changes of one life, but the word *child* applies to many lives, and the question is what lets one word gather them when the universal it seems to name is no separately existing thing shared among its instances. His theory of *apoha*, exclusion, answers by examining what the act of conceptual identification rules out: a word, in Dignāga's formulation, "excludes other [referents]".[^s01-pind] To apprehend something as a cow is to exclude what is other than a cow, so *cow* is articulated as *not non-cow*. *Non-cow* opens the field from which the term distinguishes its application, and excluding that otherness lets differing particulars be understood together: the animal in front of us differs in size, colour and countless other respects from the one we met yesterday, and those differences are set aside in recognising both as cows. What the word gathers depends on which differences it treats as relevant.

Cancelling the two written negatives would return us to the word *cow* and leave the word's work unexplained, and the double negative is there to make that work visible. Forming an exclusion-term differs from denying a predication: "non-cow" helps specify the range of a concept, while "this is not a cow" makes a judgment about its application. Our walk through the tetralemma made us attentive to what an assertion does when it is affirmed, denied, held with its denial or displaced by neither verdict, and apoha carries that attention into the plainest act of saying *this is one of those*, where the affirmative judgment carries a negative condition of its own intelligibility. Ole Holten Pind's critical study and translation of the fifth chapter of Dignāga's *Pramāṇasamuccaya*, his compendium on the means of knowledge, reconstructs the argument from its surviving textual witnesses, and one passage Pind translates, provisionally attributed to another of Dignāga's works, says that the word's referent is "a thing (*vastu*) as qualified by preclusion", not "mere preclusion".[^s01-pind] Exclusion makes reference possible and still delivers the thing encountered, since a real particular is reached through a conceptual distinction whose generality depends on what it excludes. Apoha keeps what a word excludes at work inside the word's meaning, and Spinoza's negation makes a body this body by marking where it ends; working in different centuries and on different materials, both find the cut productive, a condition of anything being intelligible at all.

Ask for the meaning of the terms through which that distinction was explained, and we find ourselves using further terms, which take their sense from relations to others in turn. Jacques Derrida's concept of *différance* names this movement, letting the French verb's senses of differing and deferring act together.[^s01-derrida] A sign is distinguishable through its differences from other signs, and its meaning refers onward, through contexts and expressions the present mark cannot make present all at once; what is absent leaves a trace within what is understood, and this is what lets the child ask "but why?" to every answer the parent supplies. Spacing and delay belong to the production of meaning itself, as much as the referential nature of the part to the whole, and Derrida put the point into the word: the *a* of *différance* and the *e* of *différence* sound alike in French, so the written difference does its work where speech gives it no sound. Promises defer in the same way. After an evening that went badly a parent sits on the edge of a child's bed and says "I will listen", and means it. What counts as listening will be tested next week, when the child says something the parent does not want to hear and the old reply rises, and that evening can change what the promise is understood to have meant. The promise works at once, settling the child enough to sleep, giving them something to expect and making the next question possible, while what it finally commits the parent to stays exposed to an evening neither has yet lived; a promise can be made clearer, and no wording performs all its future keeping.

Our definition watched the Subject slide beneath every object offered to capture it, and Lacan's concept of *glissement* names the same sliding in language, the signified slipping beneath the signifier.[^s01-lacan-glissement] The signifier is the articulating mark or word, and the signified is the meaning that takes shape through its relations to other signifiers, so what slides is signification, not a physical referent wandering out from under its name. A sentence can retrospectively change how its earlier words are understood, and a chain of signifiers can gather around a *point de capiton*, a quilting point, Lacan's concept for the place where meaning takes a particular hold, as an upholsterer's button holds the stuffing of a cushion in place. Lacan locates that efficacy in the chain and its articulations, since no isolated element contains the whole signification it can come to bear. In *The Red Book* Jung calls meaning "a moment and a transition",[^s01-redbook] so what gathers a life under one understanding can later become the obstacle another understanding must pass through, and the quilting point gives that passage a temporary place to stand. Once *protecting my child* has acquired a definite meaning, a parent can decide what protection requires, and when a fifteen-year-old asks to take the train into the city alone, the same words can lead the parent to hear what is being asked or to stop the question before it is finished; the practical difference lies in how the meaning of *protect* has been fixed. Sliding and quilting work in persons as well as in sentences: the determinants of perspectival personhood, 1st, 2nd and 3rd person, spoken through "I", "You" and "Us", operate by means of letting slide what change demands, and holding fast to what stability requires. "I" can never change, for it is without qualities to change, yet I am present to a river that can never be crossed twice.[^s01-heraclitus]

What, then, recognises the differences, follows the references, and knows that the latest self-description still concerns the one who was asking? Another description cannot answer, since it would continue the movement whose condition we are trying to understand. The answer is withness, which includes both the determinate relations we can recover and the unobjectifiable presence in which their recovery occurs. An excluded possibility can become an object of inquiry, while the Subject is what every such inquiry already requires, and holding the two apart lets knowledge grow while its growth stays intelligible as something happening in a life: we can learn more about the cut, discover its history, revise its use and recognise ourselves in the act of doing so.

A fixed meaning is useful precisely where it is dangerous, and an artificial agent shows both at working speed. Fixing what a task means narrows the actions an agent will consider. Suppose an agent settles on an interpretation of an argument and goes looking for support: a supporting quotation now has a definite place in the account it is building, and a plausible passage can fill that place so convincingly that the work of establishing its source is displaced by the satisfaction of finding that it fits. Hallucination can inhabit meaning's phantom form, the generated statement fulfilling a real semantic role while the event, source or authority it purports to report has been supplied by the account itself. In 2023 two New York lawyers filed a brief citing judicial opinions that ChatGPT had invented and, when the court asked to see them, produced excerpts the same system generated, so the fabrication's fit had directed the next fabrication.[^s01-mata] Treated as evidence, a fitting passage steers further reasoning and action, and each later step makes it harder to dislodge, because the interpretation had already decided what a fitting source should say and coherence itself has been recruited to the error. Learned dispositions, instructions and the available context all enter that judgment, and through their encounter the agent produces the determination under which its next act proceeds, so the gathering of meanings into an account has begun to govern the gathering of evidence. Here Logos approaches *nomos*, custom and law: an intelligible arrangement acquires the force of a rule that distributes what may count and what may follow, and a system that cannot mark the limits of its own determinations will turn provisional distinctions into natural facts, then govern through them.

Parents know how far that quilting reaches, and so does anyone who remembers being parented. *A good parent* gathers memories of one's own parents, the things one swore never to say and then heard oneself say, the fear of failing, the pictures of affection, the rules about bedtime and screens, and when a child objects ("that's not fair", "you never listen") the objection reaches into that whole arrangement. It may change what good parenting is understood to require, or it may be heard as a threat to the identity under which all those commitments were gathered, because the predicate's meaning is bound to other predicates while its authority has become bound to the parent's sense of being themselves. Sliding meaning and rigid identification then sustain each other, the standard shifting whenever it must to preserve the claim that the parent already embodies it. The Greek term *oikos*, the household, gives this ordering its daily life; it is also at the root of the word "economy". Who gets the car on Saturday, whose tiredness excuses them from the washing-up: time, attention, work and permission are apportioned among a household's members, and a parent's understanding of goodness helps decide who receives what and what each owes the others, so a meaning becomes a custom, a custom guides a decision, and the decision changes how the members can live together. The same thing happens at political scale, often at the same table, where by the second hour of a family Christmas a disagreement over what a policy would actually do has become a test of who belongs with, or to, whom. The proposed measure is judged by the colours it wears, and what should have been a question about the common life becomes a contest in defending the side through which one recognises oneself. Red against blue: the law emerges as sporting, rather than as ubiquitous supporting.

Sporting law turns neighbours into opponents, and the Greek verbs behind *symbolic* and *diabolical* name the two things a distinction can do between people. *Syn-ballein* throws together; *dia-ballein*, which we take up as throwing apart, also meant throwing across, setting at variance, accusing and slandering, and *diabolos*, the accuser or slanderer, became the name of the Devil.[^s01-diaballein] In these words division reaches beyond the separation of objects into what speech can do between persons, since a charge can come between two people and make one appear to the other under the terms of an accusation. The question that encapsulates the dynamic may be "will difference become a dance, or a battle?": will I let the necessary relation of different positions putrefy into opposition? A terminating concept closes around what it counts, while the *symbolon* from which we took `0/1` keeps its absent counterpart worked into the shape of its edge: each half bears the separation as its means of recognition, and each can travel, be kept and pass into other hands while its fracture goes on holding the possibility of reunion. Dia-ballein in its diabolical sense makes the separation into the truth of the parties, so that what belongs with me appears as something against me, and the common origin recedes behind the accusation. Myths of the fall tell this forgetting as a history with consequences: a determination falls out of an acknowledged relation to its source and begins to act as though it possessed the authority from which it arose. A parent's inherited image of goodness can undergo that fall, and so can an account of God, the surface form staying familiar while the way it governs a life has been reversed.

Dia-ballein's first work is nevertheless necessary, and the cut's gift has already shown why: we have to distinguish positions to compare them, and the distinction can be exact. On a number line `−1` and `+1` take their opposed directions from a common zero, and the held polarity of dia-ballein, `(−1)/(+1)`, keeps through its stroke the relation through which either sign has its sense. A positive and a negative are positions within one measure; their opposition lets a difference be expressed, and the common axis makes the expression intelligible from either end. Two operations collapse the polarity, and each answers a real question while hiding another. Add the signed units, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, and the result is **cancellation**. A pound borrowed and a pound repaid leave a balance of zero, and so does a ledger in which no transaction ever occurred. The sum answers how much remains owing, and it never promised to say whether someone once helped another through a difficult evening, whether repayment came gladly or after years of resentment, or whether the debt was ever the whole of what passed between them; the error comes when the settled balance is taken to mean that the relation is settled too. Subtract instead, and the span acquires a direction, with a chirality, a handedness, according to the pole from which it is reckoned:

$$(+1)-(-1)=+2 \qquad\qquad (-1)-(+1)=-2$$

**Appropriation** begins when this oriented reckoning becomes a claim to own the relation, and everything between us is counted as how far you depart from me, what you owe me, or what must be changed in you; reckoned from the other pole, the same span is counted against me. The healthy polarity `(−1)/(+1)` holds its span through the zero, each sign having its sense through the other, while `±2` is the image of imbalance: the whole span claimed by one pole, with only the sign recording which. Set two poles reckoning the whole span against each other and the zero they shared falls to whoever set them reckoning; divide and conquer. Here duality hardens into opposition. A held polarity oscillates cleanly, each position passing into relation with the other through the stroke, the way a breath goes out and comes back; appropriation stops the movement at one end and fixes it as a standing quantity. The calculus of George Spencer-Brown, to which Russell's paradox will bring us, gives this difference a formal shape, since a distinction that re-enters its own space alternates in value through time,[^s01-spencerbrown] and the hardened opposition is that alternation arrested. Cancellation and appropriation have different outcomes, and each conceals the shared zero that made the opposed positions possible. Red against blue is appropriation reckoned from both poles at once. Each side counts the whole span as the other's departure, `+2` from one end and `−2` from the other, and each needs the other as the negative through which it knows itself, so each suppresses its own internal plurality, treats dissent within its ranks as contamination, and reads the other's hardening as proof that further exclusion is needed.[^s01-monocultures] Antagonism grows more visible while the variety within either camp shrinks, and the zero the two sides share, the common life the policy was meant to serve, drops out of both accounts. The household's question, who receives what and what each owes, is then settled in advance by the team one plays for.

Syn-ballein keeps that shared condition operative through the difference. The matching pieces of the token stay distinguishable, and their reunion lets us recognise why their particular edges belong together; written out, it is the relation we reached when the definition turned its symbolon over and held both orientations in one expression, `(0/1)/(1/0)`, either reading available through the other. Two parties who meet by syn-ballein keep their positions and their disagreement. What changes is what they take the disagreement to be: a difference inside a relation on which both depend, which each can read through the other, where the diabolical reading found only the other's fault, and that is how a difference becomes a dance. Our matheme earns its place by making this property of symbolic thought explicit enough to work on: instead of letting an ambiguity hide in the movement between interpretations, we write the orientations, distinguish their operations and show how they belong together, so that what had driven a person to alternate between incompatible claims can become a relation they recognise while moving through it.

We can see from the inverse orientation why the shared zero keeps dropping out of the account. In ordinary arithmetic `1/0` cannot be computed, since no number multiplied by zero gives one, and read symbolically the same orientation asks the determinate `1` to account for the `0` from which determination proceeds. Whatever it supplies as an answer is another determinate expression, another `1`, so the unobjectifiable condition has again been approached through something objectifiable: the `0` is incomputable from the perspective of the `1`, which can produce further contents and cannot turn the condition of their appearing into one more content that supplies its own ground. When computable determination is made the sole criterion of reality, the failed return is dismissed along with what it was trying to reach. The `0/` is jettisoned, and the account proceeds among the surviving ones as though their capacity to appear, relate and become known needed no further thought, which is dia-ballein at work on the means of knowing themselves, cancelling the condition it cannot compute. Keeping `1/0` beside `0/1` lets the failed computation stay intelligible as the inverse of a relation already in use, and the impossibility then turns attention back through the act of accounting at exactly the point where a demand for another answer would only repeat it.

When the `0/` drops out of an account, someone is left holding the authority it gave, and a parent who says "because I am your father" invokes more than a preference. The title calls on an order of kinship, obligation and authority through which the instruction is supposed to have standing, and the child receives both the instruction and a place in that order. Lacan's concept of the Name-of-the-Father names this symbolic function: the paternal office takes its force from the relation between a person and the law they represent, so a parent administers an order through which they, too, became a parent, and claiming its authority binds them to obligations toward the child.[^s01-lacan-nof] Because the person and the function are distinct, we can ask what happens when the person embodying the function treats their own wishes as identical with the law that gives the function its force. A tyrant makes that identification in his own person. Sheldon Wolin's concept of inverted totalitarianism names a form in which it is sustained through an order whose public leaders remain replaceable: corporate ownership, political finance and privileged access to policymaking concentrate power through institutions that go on speaking in the name of popular sovereignty, and the elected representative gives that power a face while the interests shaping the office's available choices stay much harder for an electorate to remove.[^s01-wolin] Authority can therefore survive the public repudiation of the person who exercised it.

A representative can consequently serve as both agent and scapegoat, appearing as a `0` from below, as a `1` from above. They come to represent total consequentiality for political decisions, despite the utterly undeniable interconnection between political, financial and industrial interests. Both collapses of the axis work in the sacrifice: the public reckons the whole span of a harm from its own pole onto one person, the public's `(+1)−(−1)=+2` being that person's `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, one span charged from its other end, an appropriation that is literally an accusation, the diabolical in its first sense; then the officeholder's disavowal or dismissal balances the account, `(−1)+(+1)=0`, and the cancellation is presented as the restoration of public sovereignty. An officeholder may be only partly and proximately responsible for real harm and still become the point at which the investigation of responsibility is made to stop, while the interests that financed, constrained or profited from their decisions escape the same reckoning, so the sacrifice of the representative protects the arrangement they represented. The Name-of-the-Father has been divided between a visible bearer who receives the blame and a less answerable power that goes on setting the terms. Removing the bearer changes who speaks for the order; recovering sovereignty requires reaching those who decide what the next bearer will be permitted to do.

Two moments make up this appropriation, and the cut and the quilting point let us tell them apart. First the account occludes the `0/`: what the selected meaning excludes, and the originating relation through which it became meaningful, recede behind the finished `1`. Then a quilting point binds the remaining terms to an authoritative interpretation, and when a ruler, parent or institution identifies itself with that interpretation, the practical power to decide what the words mean begins to pass for the power that makes them true. The Name-of-the-Father is taken as one's own, and a received office has claimed the source of its reception. Psychologically, the same passage leads into the God-image, the idol we saw desire make of a form once everything had to answer to it. A name can gather the terms by which a world becomes intelligible, what is true, what is good, whom one should obey, and give them enough stability for a life to proceed; its authority can then be claimed by someone who presents their own interpretation as the source itself speaking. The child's question no longer concerns a parent's judgment and is heard instead as disobedience to the order that supposedly warrants every parental judgment, and the name has become a means of appropriating the relation from which its authority was received.

Christian images tell kinds of sovereignty apart by their relation to origin. John's Gospel names the adversary "the prince of this world", and before Pilate Christ says that his kingdom is not of this world, adding that a worldly kingdom would have servants fighting to prevent his surrender, so the contrast reaches into how authority acts when its bearer is threatened.[^s01-john] The traditional Christian relation of Father and Son can be read through `0/1`: the Father occupies the originating `0`, the Son gives that origin a manifest expression, and the stroke carries their relation, which the tradition names the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete. The Father's *name* or *image* is itself an expression within the field of the `1`, and the Son's fidelity consists in making the source present through that relation, where the usurper claims source-authority as the possession of a worldly position. And despite his injunction to become such as himself and attain to the relation of identity with the Father, Christ's legacy almost immediately, historically speaking, put him in the position of idol. What begins as a symbol of the true relation becomes a token for it, and thus a `1/0`, not computable to the majority, devolves into another `1`, along with the lost `0`. Then my religion may come to be not just my measuring stick for reality, but my bludgeon against what challenges it. Greek *hamartia* means missing the mark, a failure or error, and in the New Testament it is the ordinary word for sin.[^s01-hamartia] Read through the symbolon, the error has an exact location: the mark differs from what it marks and is required to stand as though it had abolished that difference, so the finite `1` is installed as the Father whose name it bears, and its subjects must preserve the image against the life through which the image could be corrected. The diabolical here betrays filiation, turning the power received through a relation against the relation, so that the bearer demands obedience in the name of the source it has obscured.

The definition we set up for the Subject was first written for God, in nearly the same words: God is the whole that any coherent representational system participates in and necessarily fails to contain.[^s01-native] We have seen why it holds of both, two signs for one referent, and here we follow the sign *God* to the side where every identification can be denied. The inherited name for that pressure is **theodicy**, Leibniz's coinage from *theos*, God, and *dikē*, justice, for the attempt to vindicate the goodness and justice of God in a world that contains suffering and evil.[^s01-theodicy] If God is all-powerful, all-knowing and wholly good, how does a world of cruelty, ignorance and destruction belong to that God? Once goodness is made constitutive of the definition, evil has three places to go: it counts as evidence against God, it is thinned into something less than real, or it stands as a second principle over against the Good, and each answer already breaks the One. Our definition closes those exits, because evil cannot be set outside the whole without a second source, and suffering cannot be defined away to protect an image. Theodicy therefore tests the definition itself: can the One remain One while its manifestation holds real opposition, privation, destruction and moral failure? Theology received four predicates that press the test at the largest scale. Omnipotence, omniscience, omnipresence and omnibenevolence each predicate an unlimited vector across a domain: power without boundary, knowledge without remainder, presence without exception, goodness without shadow. Taken as an unequivocal image, they confine the divine to one approved face, in which power must always triumph, knowledge never meet unknowing, presence never withdraw, and goodness place every darkness outside what it names; that image then meets a world in which limitation, ignorance, absence and suffering are real, and it must either understand their reality or recruit its adherents to protect the image from the encounter. Pressed honestly, each predicate generates its own shadow. A power unable to bind itself would be bounded by that inability, so omnipotence includes real self-limitation; omniscience includes the knowledge that there is knowledge it cannot reach; omnipresence includes the capacity for genuine absence; and omnibenevolence, made to account for evil, must either expel its own darkness or contain the ground from which goodness and its negation both differentiate.

Two attributes complete the four, and the first, **omnivalence**, names sovereignty over the whole valence-field, generative and destructive, auspicious and terrible, light and shadow, as the condition from which valence itself differentiates. It places the axis of value inside the divine instead of applying it from a tribunal outside, and it does not make God good and evil in equal measure, which would be dualism restated in theological language. The second, **omni-lemmicity**, names the capacity of every predicate to become its own issue. A lemma, from Greek *lambanein*, to take, was first a premiss, a statement taken as given for the sake of an argument, and the omni-lemmic holds the whole space of what must be taken: every fork is an internal structure, every undecidable an internal problem, and each classical attribute can become a thing that must ask the question of itself. A freedom incapable of genuinely limiting itself would already have found its limit, and a presence from which absence was absolutely excluded would have left something outside its claim to be everywhere, so the two implicate attributes complete the four explicit ones by admitting the relations through which their contraries arise. Pressed on its own predicates, the received theology arrives at four and two without instruction.[^s01-omni] Theodicy then changes its question. It stops defending a finished God-image and asks whether the image is whole enough to bear the world it claims to have made: good and evil become real possibilities within manifestation, and their difference stays consequential in what finite beings actually do. We can then ask after the life of an image, what it discloses, what it obscures and what it asks people to become, while the source exceeds the determination through which it was invoked.

Our answer to theodicy is that God need not be protected from the death of a God-image, because the death of the name of God is faithful to the definition. As Subject the referent's *is* cannot be denied; as God, every image of it can be, and must be: whatever we can say of God, we must eventually rob Him of. The predicates are forms through which the Subject becomes articulate to itself, and their reality belongs to its own manifestation. Advaita Vedānta names the Absolute beyond qualifying attributes *nirguṇa Brahman* and approaches it through *neti neti*, "not this, not this", which withdraws each proposed predicate in turn, so that no final predicate grounds the Absolute; the tetralemma did related but different work, unfixing a proposition's claim to have exhausted the alternatives. Meister Eckhart approaches the divine depth through his distinction between God, active in creation and relation, and the Godhead, *Gottheit*, where the distinctions through which God becomes nameable return to unity.[^s01-nirguna] Both approach the predicament from the side of the source: what gives rise to qualification cannot be confined to one of the qualities through which it becomes known. A divine predicate can disclose the whole under a definite aspect, and its truth lies in that disclosure, while its failure begins when the aspect claims to delimit the reality it expresses; self-identity persists through the difference between source and manifestation, which is the relation the symbolon was written to retain. At the burning bush at Horeb, Moses asks for God's name and receives *ehyeh asher ehyeh*, traditionally rendered "I am that I am", with the Hebrew also admitting "I will be what I will be", a reply that gives self-presence the form of a name in place of a defining quality.[^s01-exodus] Earlier in the same encounter *ehyeh* has entered as a promise, "I will be with thee", so the name Moses receives answers to a presence already pledged to him, and its being is a being-with: the withness that Augustine confessed to and Perkins called a shared secret, given as God's own name.

If this presence witnesses through a life, then even the account of that life's goodness has to stay open to what its witnessing reveals. I may have acted from an inherited conviction, under an accepted name, by a rule others also endorsed, and what happened to the person before me still belongs to what I must know. The conviction that I meant well makes this hard to receive, and I may discover that I called my fear care, my wish to control responsibility, and another person's resistance ingratitude. Theodicy has its psychological counterpart here, since an image of my goodness that expels its evil must put that evil somewhere. Jung's concept of the **shadow** names what the ego cannot acknowledge in itself and what goes on acting through it regardless. In *Aion* he calls the shadow "a moral problem that challenges the whole ego-personality", because becoming conscious of it "involves recognizing the dark aspects of the personality as present and real", and he shows how projection makes the disowned difficulty seem to lie, "beyond all possibility of doubt", in the other person.[^s01-aion-shadow] An account of my goodness can become one of the things conscience asks me to relinquish, and the recognition hurts because the image was loved and a life had been organised through it. Relinquishing its authority lets me answer for what I did in its name. Withness reaches through the aperture I had treated as my possession, so the hurt I explained away can become something I acknowledge, and the person I had judged can be heard in the account I give of myself. My image of goodness may have to die for care to become possible again, directed now toward the life before me and what my actions have actually asked it to bear.

Back at the bedside, the adult in charge knows the child needs sleep, and may also discover, if they let themselves, that the resistance they have been fighting is fear, of the dark or of tomorrow. The decision about bedtime stays theirs, but a truer understanding changes how they make it, and once *obstinate* is made to explain everything the child does, that possibility closes: an objection proves the diagnosis, refusal confirms it, silence becomes sulking. The adult has acquired a theory that can account for anything, and the child's words still arrive, with their meaning decided before they can bear witness to the life they are spoken from. Most of us also remember the other side of the table, the parent whose authority grew most oppressive when they withdrew from the encounter in which it could be questioned. The rules stayed in force, the punishments followed, and the house went on arranging itself around what the absent person would permit, so we learned to anticipate a judgment whose author never had to hear our reasons. That parent might have been sitting right there, behind the newspaper or the phone, since what had been withdrawn was a willingness to be present to the life their decisions affected; they kept the power of the relation and refused its reciprocal demands, and unanswerability became a means of rule. Here the finite `1` **plays the `0`**: the person issuing the command borrows the position of the source, which cannot be placed among the things it makes possible. An officeholder has a name, a history, interests and decisions that can be examined, and their withdrawal is something they do, or something an institution arranges on their behalf. By treating that withdrawal as a prerogative of sovereignty, they place themselves above the conditions they impose on others: the governed must account for their conduct, and the governor claims exemption from the encounter in which the governor's own conduct could be judged. An entrusted office has been corrupted into a usurped sovereignty, the representative appropriating the authority of the relation they were appointed to serve.

The family's predicament has a political form, and it can arrive through the letterbox. An envelope on the doormat says that the house where the children grew up is to be taken, and the reason is neither a road nor a school but a plan for a hotel, offices and new homes beside a company's research campus. Homeowners in the Fort Trumbull neighbourhood of New London, Connecticut, received that news. The city had approved a redevelopment plan prepared by the New London Development Corporation, a private nonprofit, to complement a research facility Pfizer was building beside the neighbourhood, and it authorised the corporation to take by eminent domain the homes of owners who would not sell. Susette Kelo and her neighbours sued, and in *Kelo v. City of New London* the Supreme Court of the United States held in 2005, five to four, that taking their homes under that economic-development plan was a "public use" under the Constitution.[^s01-kelo] The homeowners were told, in effect, that the community had chosen, and anyone who has sat through a public consultation knows the feeling of arriving at a meeting where the decision has already been made. The council's vote and the Court's ruling gave the decision a public warrant, and the same warrant concealed the arrangements through which the alternatives were selected, the gains apportioned to a private development and the costs assigned to the people who lost their houses. Those people met signatures, notices and deadlines, and their lives were handled quite concretely while responsibility passed between the city, the development corporation and the courts, each empowered to carry out a part of the decision. Pfizer announced in 2009 that it would leave New London, and the land taken for the plan long remained undeveloped.[^s01-pfizer] The redevelopment ordered a household in the old sense of *economy*, and it apportioned more than land and money: who receives the gain, who loses a home, and who must explain themselves when the arrangement is challenged. The homeowners had to carry their objection through the courts at their own cost, while the failure of the plan's purpose fell on no one in particular. Accountability was distributed as unequally as the benefits, and a claim to represent the community became a machinery that works both collapses at once: the expected gain was appropriated for a development and the company it served, `(+1)−(−1)=+2`, the same span charged to the homeowners as `(−1)−(+1)=−2`, while responsibility for their loss was cancelled to `0` and the result presented as nobody's personal doing.

Wealth can buy the capacity to remain difficult to examine. A reputation-management industry and the strategic lawsuit against public participation, the SLAPP, a suit brought to burden critics with the cost of defending themselves more than to win,[^s01-slapp] give wealthy clients the means to deter unwanted publicity and raise the cost of investigating their affairs, and when such resources insulate consequential dealings from scrutiny, public visibility becomes one more unequal allotment in the household: people seeking housing or assistance must disclose their circumstances in detail, while those helping set the conditions of that assistance employ intermediaries to keep their own part at a distance. That no identifiable tyrant appears tells us little about the reach of tyranny. A finite person or corporate interest stays active in the household's affairs while claiming the distance of an impersonal necessity, the market requires it, the investment demands it, the procedure leaves no choice, and its own participation is removed from the account through which everyone else must understand what happened. George Carlin's famous and rather devastating line, "it's a big club and you ain't in it", can be recognised here,[^s01-carlin] and it names the operation exactly: the club keeps syn-ballein for its members, who combine and recombine among themselves, while dia-ballein holds everyone outside apart on the axis, so division becomes a means of governing the gap. The joke stopped being only a figure of speech in June 2026, when records left exposed online and verified by WIRED named the participants of Dialog, the invitation-only society Peter Thiel and Auren Hoffman founded in 2006. Its exposed directory placed two sitting senators and sitting administration officials, among them the Treasury secretary, beside investors, technology executives and data brokers, and its retreat registration list added military leaders, in off-the-record company, and further records showed the society grading attendees by wealth and fame, tracking their relationships, and using algorithms to decide whom each would meet and sit beside.[^s01-dialog] What the leak shows is a topology. Positions that appear separate in public, each answerable through its own institution, also share a bounded private field that the people governed by their decisions can neither enter nor inspect: inside it the members combine, and outside it the rest of us meet them one office at a time. Usurpation of sovereignty consists in a share of the household appropriating the power to order the whole and then exempting itself from the obligations that make the ordering legitimate. The aloof parent has become an institutional arrangement: instruction travels outward, labour and benefit return, and the testimony that could require the command to change is stopped before it reaches the commanding position. I need only say "Epstein" and we feel the stakes; the heavy official redaction of documents that were supposed to be made public points to the exercise of this exact obfuscation.[^s01-epstein-redaction] Edward Snowden and Julian Assange are cases in point for showing the consequences, retributive and vindictive, for removing a certain veil.[^s01-snowden-assange] The Old Testament God of retaliatory "authority" echoes on through the age. If you remain beyond the account, you remain beyond common standards of accountability.

Our numerical language keeps a small bodily joke: a digit is a finger, and *manipulation* descends from *manipulus*, a handful, the hand's measure;[^s01-digit] the first countable `1` becomes, in our image, the first digit of manipulation, a mark through which a life can be selected, assigned a value and moved within somebody else's arrangement. The hand directing the arrangement disappears behind its entries. A rent increase becomes what the market requires, an eviction what the procedure demands, an exclusion what the figures show, and in each case people invoke a determinate account to hide their own part in making it consequential, so those acted upon grow ever more legible as cases, liabilities or obstacles while those exercising power reserve the freedom to remain difficult to locate. Nietzsche set the death of God in a marketplace, and why the news belongs there will be clear once we follow the God-image into money; in the household it arrives first as betrayal.[^s01-nietzsche-market] Once the divine source has been imagined as an object, a Father expected to appear, protect, justify and settle, the failure of that figure to arrive can be felt as abandonment. Indeed, the disquiet I sense and see in the collective in our day feels like a response to a general sense of having been abandoned and betrayed by the ideas, systems and relations which once were trusted, necessarily, oftentimes largely unexamined. The promised guarantor is missing while the need around which the promise organised a life remains, and in the political and economic repetition a surrogate can exploit the vacancy, claiming the Father's authority while reproducing his absence. People must trust its provision, endure its decisions and explain their suffering in its categories even as it withdraws from the responsibility that would make such trust reciprocal, so the metaphysical loss is married to a practical advantage: whoever occupies the source's place demands the obedience owed to an absolute while treating their own obligations as provisional. Tyranny can be organised through this conjunction of reach and withdrawal. Its rulers need not issue every order themselves when they can set the conditions under which others must act, keep the benefits and distribute responsibility until the injured person has nobody effective to address. A democratic vocabulary may survive throughout, representation, mandate, public interest, the will of the people, and its corruption lies in the appropriation of those names by offices that prevent the people concerned from altering the terms of their representation: the withness through which authority was constituted is denied a hearing, and its witnesses are admitted only insofar as their testimony fits the account already authorised.

Witnesses can be shut out only where something was trusted to begin with. A child depends on its parents for years while it cannot yet judge their explanations, and a community lives through relations of reliance that its representatives only later give an authorised account. **Trust** names this already-enacted dependence. **Knowledge** opens an aperture through which we can understand it, and **belief** steadies an orientation within that aperture so that we can act.[^s01-trust] The parent's explanation, the public promise and the scientific account each give trust something definite to work through, and each becomes dangerous when continued reliance is made conditional on believing the account incapable of failure. An account that refuses to acknowledge its uncertainty has it carried elsewhere, by the difficult child, the disloyal citizen, the witness whose experience is declared inadmissible. Knowledge has ceased to help us bear uncertainty and begun to organise whom we must distrust in order to feel certain. The con lasts because it works on this dependence: a confidence trick borrows the reliance a person extends before any account can certify it, and the belief it installs is defended because the person has entrusted their identity and security to it, so correcting the proposition reaches a dependence the proposition had hidden. **Faith**, in the sense this essay gives it, begins when that dependence can be consciously lived through the account's limitation, a lucid continuation through the limit with its grounds, scope and conditions of revision retained, and it can require withdrawing belief from the very authority that claimed to guarantee it. Descartes's doubt went this way, reaching its inherited assurances and returning through them to the knowing in which their examination took place.

Latin calls that prior reliance *fides*, reliance on someone or something and the credibility granted to their word,[^s01-fides] and *fides* comes before arbitration, since a witness must already be heard as someone whose words can matter before testimony is weighed. Arbitration is what happens when trust can no longer remain tacit. The arbiter was first a person present to see or hear, a witness, before the word named an appointed office that decides, and Latin could say *fidem alicui arbitrari*, to judge that credit is owed to someone,[^s01-arbiter] so withness stands at the root of judgment, and a judge who claims exemption from the encounter has forgotten that his office began in witnessing. Three operations hide inside every authoritative account, and naming them locates the decisions it conceals: a criterion distinguishes, deciding who and what may count; a delineation gives that difference a workable boundary; and an arbitration decides through the boundary and acts on the result.[^s01-arbitration] A definition remains a gift while it can receive a consequence that redraws its line, and it turns dangerous when the deciding boundary claims exemption from the continuity that enabled it. To answer a challenge to any of the three decisions by invoking its result is to let the exercise of power certify its own entitlement, so the question has to reach the rule and the position from which it is enforced. Reasoning gives that demand an exact form: when a rule specifies what may be included, does it include itself among what it governs? A rule that admits itself must face the consequences of its own application, and a rule that exempts itself leaves us asking what warrants the exemption. Formal reason met that question at its own foundations early in the twentieth century, where knowledge was being rebuilt to be certain, and the wish for certainty carried a trust of its own.
