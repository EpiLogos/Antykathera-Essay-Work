Every one of us was unnamed for a while. Two parents argue over a name, a grandmother's against one they have liked for years, and all the while their argument has a referent lying between them, a child who is entirely itself while they disagree. One of them lifts the baby and passes it to the other, and both know the same child has come back into their arms: their attention has held one life distinct through a change of place and of hands. Beneath the name lies a count, *this one* and *this one again*, and though nobody recites a numeral, the parents are telling this child apart from every other who might have received the name. When the name comes, it carries that identification out into the world, where relatives who have never held the child can use it, a register keeps it, and the child learns to turn its head when it is called. Names are shared and changed, though. Most of us have met someone who answers to our own, and some of us have changed ours or wanted to, so the sound can stay while the bearer differs, or change while the bearer stays identifiable, and a sound repeated cannot supply the relation through which a name works.

Saul Kripke gave that relation its modern precision in *Naming and Necessity*. His concept of the **rigid designator** names a term that picks out the same individual in every counterfactual situation in which that individual exists: a proper name rigidly designates its bearer, while most descriptions could have picked out someone else, as "the first child born in this hospital this year" might have been true of the baby in the next cot. Kripke's preface asks, of each reading of a sentence taken separately, whether what it says would be true of a counterfactual situation exactly when the fixed individual has the property in question, so a shared sound unsettles nothing; and his account of how a name passes from speaker to speaker follows a historical chain of users who intend to keep the reference they received.[^s01-kripke] A name can hold its bearer fast across every possible situation only because something has picked out *this one* to be held. Reading him, I came to think that rigidity discloses a layer the theory of reference never asks about, a counting that individuates the one named before any name is given, which is why a duplicated sound confuses nobody who was there. In the copula both registers meet, the *is* of naming and the *equals* of number, and beneath every identification we make runs this age-old conspiracy between name and number.

Counting in this elementary sense does two jobs: it holds instances apart, and it holds one instance through different appearances. *This one* distinguishes an instance, *another* admits a second, and *this one again* recognises a return, and none of it needs a written numeral or a public name. An old photograph of ourselves as children shows how much work it does. The small figure squinting into the sun on a beach is me, and I say so without a second thought, though nothing of that body is left in mine and perhaps no detail of that afternoon is left in my memory. The photograph is not me, all the same, since it is paper and I am the one holding it. And if a friend runs my face through an AI image model and ages it by thirty years, "that could be me" concerns a possible future, and I hear it as one. One name covers the person in the chair, the child on the paper and the face on the screen, yet nobody takes the body, the picture and the expectation for one another, because identification needs three things at once: a **name**, which articulates; an **instance**, which holds what is being identified; and a **circumstance**, which makes the relevant sameness and difference intelligible. Between "that is me", "that is a picture of me" and "that could be me", the claim changes each time while the shared reference lets us follow what each change concerns, and we keep all of it in order without being able to say how. Kripke's preface rests rigidity on exactly this capacity, our intuitions about what a simple sentence would say of a counterfactual situation. In intuition we hold a whole circumstance while cutting one distinction within it; an explicit account comes afterwards and divides what intuition held together, and that division is what lets us examine how an identification was kept up and where it went wrong.

Kripke used a pair of dice to demystify talk of "possible worlds", and the same dice can make our stakes countable. Roll them on the table, call one die A and the other B, and the pair has thirty-six possible states, which we stipulate by keeping track of which die shows which face.[^s01-kripke-dice] Keep that identification and the thirty-six stand; stop caring which die bears which value and the same throws collapse into twenty-one unordered outcomes. In the smallest case,

$$
(A=1,\ B=2)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(A=2,\ B=1)
$$

are different assignments with the same values and the same total, two states of the pair, two worlds in Kripke's sober sense. An account that records only the sum loses the difference between them, and nothing mysterious has happened: the account has changed what it retains, so the total answers one question exactly while the distribution answers another, and the distinctions we keep decide what the resulting number can tell us. Counting depends on the circumstance in which something counts, and each of us knows this from the far side of the count as well, as the one being counted and described. A description of me can prove mistaken while my name goes on concerning me, because I am still there to receive the corrected account; a changed description of someone I know can tell me more about the same life, or show me that I had confused two lives. Identifying accurately means keeping hold of the relation the question needs, together with the differences that let us hold it.

Ordinary speech carries much of this work in one small word. "You're tired," a friend says at the end of a long day, and a minute later, introducing us to someone, "this is the friend I was telling you about." The first sentence predicates a condition and the second identifies the one whose condition is in question, and tomorrow we can be rested while the friend still knows whose tiredness it was. Grammar calls the joining word the **copula**, and it does both jobs at once: something must stand forth in a determination, and the determination must stay attributable to what was encountered. Identity statements show the double work most sharply. Gottlob Frege asked why an identity such as "the morning star is the evening star" can tell us something that "a = a" cannot, and answered that the two names present one planet, Venus, in two different ways:[^s01-frege-sense] an identity is informative because it is stated across a difference of presentation. Even "A is A" needs two inscriptions in two places for the judgment to identify them, and if we take away their difference of placement, nothing is left to be identified; if we take away their identity, nothing is left to be said. Arithmetic works the same way. In the equation

$$
3=1+2
$$

two differently composed expressions are held equal in a definite respect, exactly equal in number while their written forms stay different, and the equals sign lets the difference be read as a relation of identity. In $1+1$, the simplest addition, one kind of unit occurs twice, and its two occurrences must stay distinguishable for the count to rise: without the sameness there is no common unit, and without the difference there is no two. Identity and difference work together inside number, as they did in the name that went on concerning someone who changed. Self-knowledge presses the requirement on the knower directly. To know myself there must be something of myself to recognise, so my present act must differ from what it meets, and yet the recognition must concern me if it is to be self-knowledge at all, so the question becomes how I can meet myself in a different presentation and recognise that difference as mine. We can write the copula's double work with the simplest marks there are. Put a dash here and a dash there, one kind of mark in two positions, and draw an oblique stroke between them to make their spacing explicit:

$$
-/-
$$

The dashes carry no numerical value yet; we are watching how marks compose. Lay the two strokes parallel and we have written $=$, the sign by which distinguishable expressions are held equal. Keep the oblique $/$ as the cut and draw it through that sign, and we have written $\ne$, so the sign for non-equality turns out to be the sign for equality with the cut drawn through it, two operative marks composing a third. That composition shows what the copula was doing all along. To identify something we must be able to distinguish it, and to distinguish it there must be enough identity for the distinction to concern *this*, so identity appears through difference, difference is itself identified, and neither operation finishes the work alone. Writing $=/\ne$ holds that mutual implication in view, equality related to non-equality through a stroke that both separates and joins them, and the stroke can be read two ways. Read as OR, it lets either side be taken in its own office, identity as what is kept and difference as what lets anything be articulated; read as AND, it holds their dependence within one act, since identity is articulated through difference and still differs from difference. Either reading gives a determinate account, and following their relation asks for an account that keeps both, which is what the copula becomes when we write it in its own terms:

$$
/ = \bigl(\; = \quad =/\ne \quad \ne \;\bigr),
$$

and in this inscription the slash is the relation in which equality is equal-and/or-not-equal to non-equality. Inside the brackets the first term gives identity, the last gives difference, and the middle exposes each to the other. Read from either end, the middle shows two faces, identity related to difference and difference related to identity, so with the outer poles we have four appearances, while $=$ and $/$, the two marks that composed all four, stay active throughout. Four articulated appearances and two composing operations:

$$
4+2.
$$

The account has included the acts by which its terms became available: the brackets gather a field those acts composed, and the outer equals sign identifies the field's whole activity with the slash. If we hold the middle $=/\ne$ as one relation instead of counting its two faces, the count turns back on itself, and the four appear as three terms held in a context whose containing act supplies the fourth office, so that $4$ reads as $3+1$ while both faces of the middle stay in play; the two composing operations remain, and only the place where the account finds their unity has moved. A relation has given an account of its own articulation, and the accounting has been one more enactment of the relation. Any definition of the Subject faces the same demand, since its account has to include the identifying activity through which the account is made. This demand arises in any attempt to define an identity that is inherently self-referential.

You yourself actually met that demand long before you could state it, and from a position your name cannot speak for. Kripke counts "I" and "you" among rigid designators, though what they refer to shifts with whoever is speaking,[^s01-kripke-persons] and every child says *I* where its parents say *you*; when the child speaks to them the positions reverse, and any of them can be spoken about by a third party, so every family conversation admits first-person, second-person and third-person accounts while keeping the different places from which each is made. Those places have consequences, and most of us can remember the moment we first felt them. An adult crouches to a small child's height and says, kindly, "you're frightened", and the child shouts back, "I'm angry!", and the child is right. The answer corrects the description while keeping the one it concerns, and more has happened than a change of predicate, because the person being spoken about has spoken from inside the life under description. The adult's account now has to meet an address, and the shared language that made the first judgment possible also makes its correction possible, so the identification has become answerable through the very difference it crossed. A name tells us whom we are discussing, and descriptions tell us what that person has done, remembers, expects or feels, but whoever says *I* refers from inside the present act of speaking, and a complete account of the circumstances would itself have to be understood through an act occurring somewhere within them. The Subject comes into question at the point where the identifying activity, until now turned toward its objects, becomes the question it is trying to answer.

The smallest sentence of that kind can be tried here and now: I see these words. It seems to offer three terms, I, seeing and words, a knower, a means of knowing and something known, and two of them submit readily to inspection. The words are here in their black shapes, and the seeing could be investigated through the eye, attention, the language I read in and the light I am reading by, every finding making its operation more exact. Turned round on itself, the sentence becomes: I am aware of seeing these words. Something shifts. The seeing, which a moment ago was simply the way the page was there, has become something I am looking at, with its slight effort and the inner voice that sounds each word, and a process that seemed to supply my identity as the one who sees is now one more presentation to me. Aware of being aware, I am handed another object, and then another, and none of these turns delivers the one who is turning. A painter can make a faithful self-portrait, then a second that includes the painter making the first, then a third of the painter studying both; every canvas discloses more of the activity and its circumstances, and none takes the place of the present seeing in which the canvases are met, so that even a portrait of the viewer needs a viewer to be seen.

The point has a history, a true advent relative to Modernity's burgeoning Mind. In 1641 René Descartes undertook to withhold assent from whatever could be doubted: the senses, which had deceived him; the world before him, which could be a dream; even arithmetic, if a deceiver of unlimited power wished him deceived. The doubt is performed, and it stops at its own performance. Let him be deceived as much as can be imagined, and the being deceived is still there, occurring, if only as doubt. What he then does with the finding is a second move. He asks what this I is and determines it: a thinking thing, whose activities include understanding, affirming, denying, willing, imagining, sensing.[^s01-descartes] The certainty of the occurrence and the further account of what occurs are different achievements. An account of thought can be questioned while the questioning is still given, and the occurrence holds fast in a way the determination of the doubter never could. For this inquiry the sentence has to be opened: I am aware of thinking. Thought is the means, the determinate activity through which something is grasped; awareness is the fact in which the activity appears; and the I is exposed not as a conclusion proven by thinking but as the condition in which thinking, proving and doubting can be given at all. This is reached only by an explicit recursive move, following Descartes, passing back over the mind's condition to the fact beneath, belief's origin; this is an instance of what we'll develop as *epi-logos*. Notice the register of Descartes' inquiry; he had to face up to the primacy of trust. His doubt runs in a language he did not invent, by a reasoning he goes on relying on while arithmetic itself is suspended, through a memory that keeps the steps of the meditation in order, so the doubter leans throughout on a field he has not certified; and when he travels back from the certainty of his own occurrence to the world, the road runs through a God who is no deceiver, an assurance his reasoning can follow and cannot do without.

Watching a thought changes my relation to it, but the watching does not manufacture the knowing in which that change shows; if it did, every watcher would need a watcher, every beginning would postpone itself, and each deeper reflection would only seat a finer knower behind the last. What Lacan and Derrida saw in language as *glissement*, the signified sliding beneath the signifier, and *différance*, meaning's differing from other signs and its deferral onward to further ones, manifests too in the quest for the Subject, which slides beneath each object purporting to capture it, be it neural correlate, pneuma or noun.[^s01-glissement] Each reflection produces a new describable relation and leaves the knower where it was. *Pratyabhijñā*, what may be translated as *recognition*, when it comes, changes the mode of identification itself, where reflection had only changed the image. The images I hold of myself remain active while their claims to being "the I that knows the image" are relinquished.[^s01-recognition]

The relation between knowing and known can be tested from both directions at once. Make them wholly indistinguishable, and the difference through which anything can be known disappears: nothing would be presented to anything. Sever them completely, and what is known could never reach the knower: presentation without an address. Each attempt to totalise the relation leads equally to pathology, destroying a condition of the occurrence it meant to explain, the limitation of a posited and assumed duality. This is because the knower is by necessity the *principle* of subjectivity, subjectivity as such, or what we call **Subjective Immediacy**: without form of its own, the inwardness through which any form can be known. Thought naturally begins at the objective pole, yet logically we must impute and accept its opposite because *we are here to*. Subjectivity *as such* cannot be any one or other instance of *a* subject, since simply by the implicit count in the instance such a subject is already part-object, a spectral form stretching the difference. This stretch, where objects are *taken as subjective forms* and equally where the Subject *assumes this or that object*, is where the Objective Internality we met at the bedside, commonly and flexibly termed "Mind", does its work: the means by which any object can appear as such to some-one, and by which such activity appears as available, in some world, for others. Knowing requires a unity that simultaneously bears the difference between what can be taken as distinct and what remains only something we can gesture at, or symbolise, precisely because it anchors all possible knowledge in an irreducible `?/!`, the question every assertion still carries and the assertion every question already makes. What appears belongs to the occurrence of knowing, while its particular content does not exhaust the presence to which it appears. The spoken returns to whereof one cannot speak; it's the "one must" that makes "be silent" salient.[^s01-wittgenstein]

What knows, however, cannot be settled by more knowing of the same kind, for even at face value the demand to represent the Subject *as* an object effaces their necessary mutual difference, and thus precludes any possible attestation to their identity. The identity of knower, means of knowledge and known, which anchors their relation, provides a clue to why: everything that can be named or counted seemingly emanates from the demand of the uncountable to still be accounted for, the *desire* of the subjective pole towards form (we'll encounter this notion in registers spanning religious ontology to depth psychology to politics and sociology). An accounting of the Subject cannot, despite the yearning instinct of thinking, make "a thing" of itself. The Subject is not absent from the attempt at definition; it is the attempt's own condition, present before, during and after as the one undergoing it. I pinpoint the issue at the general tendency of thought to reify what is quite viscerally and immediately a processual reality. A definitive act about such a condition can only be performed, because a coherent object of knowledge is not necessarily an invariant one. Truth here is not stored in a crèche of certainty, awaiting the correct sentence to collect it, but rather the attempt at true-saying is enacted, and enacted necessarily, lest we make "a thing of it"; it seems language itself has conspired to brim these paths with dead ends, brambles and cul-de-sacs. Jung met the same wall from the side of the psyche, writing that "the mind cannot apprehend its own form of existence, owing to the lack of an Archimedean point outside," and that "it nevertheless exists."[^s01-jung-psyche] Any definition of the Subject must accordingly try and fail at the impossible. The failure is not only a defect of wording, it is the formal limit of the rational laid bare, failing exactly where actual present Being and the capacity to experience reside.

A definition gives something a determinate place: it distinguishes what a term applies to, excludes alternatives and lets consequences follow. Aimed at the Subject, that power turns on whoever wields it, because whatever is to be distinguished must be distinguished while the distinguishing is going on; refusing every determination would leave the question and its examiner alike unlocated, so a definition will be made, with a claim committed to it. The definition, before it runs, is set up as follows. *The Subject is the whole that any coherent representational system participates in, depends upon and necessarily fails to contain.*[^s01-native] The sentence first defined God, in my *Definition of God*, and it holds of the Subject because both words are signs for one referent, identified though it cannot be identified as one thing among others.[^s01-god] "Subject" reaches it from the side of the knowing: its *is* cannot be denied, since any doubt of it is already its occurrence, as Descartes found. "God" reaches it from the side of the name: every concept or image that identifies it can be denied, and must be, because none of them is what it names. Even calling the referent *single* is already the difficulty, since to count it as one is to make it a `1` among others; its unity is the One that `0` will write, and its identifications the All that `1` will write. "Whole" as a term is fixed as it is spoken: the whole approached in self-presence, from the side of knowing, resting on what Jung named the reality of the psyche, his recognition that "the only form of existence of which we have immediate knowledge is psychic":[^s01-jung-psyche] all phenomenal experience is factually real from the perspective of Subjective Immediacy. A person's present world appears within that circumstantial, existential given. Every *self* is thus whole, yet insofar as a mind is constituted by objects of experience known presently *and* by exactly everything absent from the present account, wholeness remains impossible for the embodied one, in the world. Knower, means of knowledge and known must keep their distinct offices inside the one relation that identifies them, which puts the relation under pressure precisely because "the means" are so often collapsed into "the knower", and thus mind holds that it does, or even could, find wholeness. Lacan's notion of desire raises its head here, as his concept of a constitutive lack, the *manque-à-être* around which the subject is organised, can be seen as a necessary condition of the mind.[^s01-lacan-lack] The tendency to collapse in exactly this way is what the unfolding of this initial setup seeks to address, concretely. But to "get it" we have to "get there", so our walk must begin elsewhere; we must begin with the proposition that *the Subject is that which cannot be defined at all*.

Most of us have met, after many years, a friend who steps off a train and is for a long moment a stranger: the walk is different, the voice has dropped, she laughs at things she would once have scorned. Is she still the person we knew? Over the evening that follows, four honest answers offer themselves: she is; she is not; she both is and is not, one life become a different person; she neither is nor is not, because the question was framed in terms her life has outgrown. The **Tetralemma** or *Catuṣkoṭi*, the Buddhist count of anything sayable, codifies exactly these, and will be our guide here. Simply, it amounts to four possible offices of assertion: it IS; it IS NOT; it BOTH IS AND IS NOT; it NEITHER IS NOR IS NOT. It appears in the early Buddhist texts, where the questions the Buddha left undetermined, such as whether one who has reached the goal exists after death, are put in all four forms, and Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka made it central to its logic.[^s01-catuskoti] This four-cornered logic was developed to exhaust the capacity of assertive proposition, such that "the real" (a notion that is itself necessarily *bracketed in the inquiry*) could acquire determination through and beyond determinations of or about it. For now, let's *use* the Tetralemma actively as a guide for our definition, and run our definition through its geometry.

First corner: it IS. "You haven't changed at all," we tell her at the barrier, and we mean something no list of her changes could touch. The first sentence of our definition says the same of the Subject, in the same spirit: the Subject is that which cannot be defined. It seems fitted to its object because it refuses to put anything positive where the Subject should be, declaring in advance that every determination will miss.

Second corner: it IS NOT. Over dinner she tells us what the twenty years held, and "you haven't changed" turns out to have been a description after all, and a wrong one: we had kept an image and called it her. Our refusal has done what it forbids in exactly this way. "That which cannot be defined" is itself a determination: it marks a boundary, supplies a predicate and gives the Subject a place in speech, a bounded claim, a sentence with a full stop, a fish trying to describe water while swimming in it. The hand that writes "the hand cannot grasp itself" is still a hand grasping.[^s01-draft3] In trying not to make an object of the Subject, the sentence has made an object called *that which cannot be made an object*, and its failure is exact.

Third corner: it BOTH IS AND IS NOT. By the end of the evening both answers are true at once, and each holds the other up: she is the one we knew precisely as the one who has become someone else, and we could not recognise the change if there were no one to have changed. The definition's contradiction begins to say something in the same way, for as a representation it fails and as an act it succeeds: the phrase cannot contain the Subject, and yet its failure happens now, for someone, and discloses the limit it names. The Subject is that which cannot be defined in the refusal the sentence enacts, and is not, in the determination the refusal produces, so what looked like a defect in the sentence has become its content. A definition that did not fail would have produced an idol, a closed image, a cage where a window was needed.[^s01-draft3] Each term of the paradox stops making sense when it is made to stand alone, and choosing either side would destroy what holding both discloses.

Fourth corner: it NEITHER IS NOR IS NOT. Walking home, we notice that "the same" and "not the same" were never hers: they were the measures we brought to the platform, and she lives in a register they do not reach. Pushed to exhaustion, neither verdict about the Subject suffices either. "Is" and "is not" were positions taken in an act of determination, and the act has now disclosed what their content could not: there was a position from which the positions were being taken. Where an entity was expected, an activity appears, a living tension holding the conflicting claims in relation the way a bow holds its string to produce the note.[^s01-draft3] The sentence has not captured its Subject. It has exposed the one for whom capture was a concern, and underneath its "what is this?" a more primordial "what am I?"

The tetralemma has exhausted assertion, but our count is not yet complete, because the one walking its four corners has not been counted, just as our question about the friend was quietly, all evening, a question about who had been doing the comparing. Nāgārjuna rejected all four corners when the deepest questions were put to them, and Graham Priest, formalising the scheme in "The Logic of the Catuskoti" and *The Fifth Corner of Four*, has to add a fifth status, "none of the above";[^s01-catuskoti] our count adds no fifth value, since what remains uncounted is the one who gave the four answers, and that one is no further answer.

The first further movement is recognition: I tried to know myself by producing things that could be known, names, thoughts, images, definitions, and each became another presentation before me, while their succession manufactured nothing of the presence to which they appeared. The one defining was implicated in the first proposition as in every one after it, and the subject of the proposition was always the Subject of the act: the one who was defining was always the one being defined. Every object is dubious, physical or mental; I am undeniable, though nowhere to be seen.[^s01-draft3] I am.

The second movement is return: recognition leaves the world of determinations standing, and whoever says *I am* goes on answering to a name, wanting things, mistaking one thing for another and changing their mind. What returns is discrimination with the limit now included in it, and the definition, having failed to possess the Subject, has acquired a determinate truth about Mind: whatever appears does so through a difference between what can be presented and that to which presentation occurs, so that every form appears to something no further form can capture. In that truth's terms the Subject can now be defined positively, as **the non-objectifiable pole of a differentiating event, the opening through which a determinate world is given**. It is *definedly indefinable*, located exactly at the office no description can occupy. A definition is faithful when its own determining act stays visible inside the account it gives, and this one's act stayed visible at every corner: the assertion was refused, the refusal counted as a determination, the paradox read as an act, and the act traced to the one performing it.

Jung entered the same predicament first as a struggle of mind, desire and life, and only later as a matter for logic. In *The Red Book* he describes turning his desire away from things and people, only to find himself captured by his thoughts; the desert opens when even this identification fails, and the one who has become his self is "neither in events, nor in men, nor in his thoughts."[^s01-red-book] What thought treats as its own interior turns out to hold objects like any other, its memories and fantasies, its propositions and desires, while the occurrence in which they appear stays uncaptured by their enumeration, and Indian epistemology draws exactly this distinction by naming three offices within every act of knowing: *pramātṛ*, the knower; *pramāṇa*, the means of knowing; and *prameya*, the known. Thoughts and perceptions, memory and language belong to the middle office, mediating what can become known without being the knower, since a thought can be watched, a desire resisted and an image of oneself altered; the recursive movement we have followed adds another *pramāṇa*, another means by which something is presented, and manufactures no further Subject behind the last. Kashmir Śaivism gives the three offices an image, which Dyczkowski sets out from its sources: the means of knowledge is like the sun, illuminating the object; the object shines like the moon, by reflected light; and the subject is fire, which flames upward and, when perception is over, takes everything it has known back into itself.[^s01-dycz] In the vocabulary of this inquiry, Subjective Immediacy is the knower, the Objective Internality of the bedside is the means, and World is the known, three offices of one occurrence of knowing. That name said as much: my most intimate thought can become an object of attention and still go on living, since the thought I examine also changes what I notice, want and do next. The means is lived as much as minded, which is why we can call it Life as readily as Mind.

Kashmiri Śaiva philosophy approaches this living relation through *prakāśa* and *vimarśa*: the light of consciousness, by which anything appears at all, and its reflective awareness of itself. *Vimarśa* comes from the root *mṛś*, to touch, feel, perceive, reflect or examine, and in Dyczkowski's exposition it is the power by which consciousness grasps itself and whatever occurs within it; deprived of it, the light would amount to phenomena appearing to no one, like light reflected in an ordinary mirror or crystal. Requiring a second awareness to reveal the first would demand a third to reveal the second, and so on without end, so the reflexivity has to belong within the occurrence itself.[^s01-dycz] Here non-duality lives inside the distinction: what appears and the power through which it appears belong to one self-articulating whole.

In these terms the difficulty Jung lived becomes exact. Desire goes out toward things, people and thoughts because each offers a form in which the self might be found, and every achieved form appears in turn as something known, so the search slides; its torment is structural, because the means keeps offering itself in the place of the knower, the sun mistaken for the fire. Most of us know the sentence from inside, even if we have never said it aloud: "I will be myself once I have this." The desired object takes on a task no object can complete, as an achievement, a lover's recognition, an explanation of the universe, even an image of one's own enlightenment becomes the thing through which existence is supposed finally to justify itself. Desire gives the form its urgency, identification lets the form speak for the one desiring, and when everything else must answer to it the form has become an idol. Jung stated the psychology without euphemism: "That psychological fact which wields the greatest power in your system functions as a god," and a god that stops overwhelming "dwindles to a mere name."[^s01-jung-god] The question of power runs through the same passage, since a description can help me understand myself and can also become the measure against which I am permitted to exist, and another person's description of me can do either. Naming and predication act inside the gap they cannot close, organising a life through determinations that never possess its first-person occurrence. Recognition returns that power to its proper work: I can use a name, inhabit a role, pursue an end and stay answerable for what follows, while letting the life concerned exceed the form through which I have understood it.

Jung knew how readily this border receives the name of madness, and Plato had already made the word unstable. In the *Phaedrus*, Socrates distinguishes madness that comes from human sickness from a divine madness, a release from the customary, through which prophecy, initiation, poetry and love become possible.[^s01-phaedrus] Jung takes up that inheritance: delusion can come when the depths overwhelm a person until he identifies himself with them, and it can come equally when the "spirit of this time" lets him see only the surface and take that partial order for the whole.[^s01-red-book] An ordinary cultural insult hides a logical question here. *Mad*, *irrational* and *nonsense* can name genuine disintegration, and they can also be used superstitiously, as charms against whatever exceeds an accepted means of determination; reason turns unreasonable when it mistakes its own instrument for the extent of the Real. What answers it is better discrimination, one that keeps its judgment and still hears the depths, holding knower, means and known in their distinct offices within the one occurrence in which knowing happens.

Hence Jung's language around the Word: coming out of the desert he calls the word a creative act, and says that the oldest and truest words "oscillate between nonsense and supreme meaning";[^s01-red-book] later the symbol itself occupies this threshold, for it can be neither simply fabricated nor discovered as a finished object, and it becomes. A symbolic form lets a relation that neither isolated term could hold come into expression, and the Greek word for it shows how. A *symbolon*, from the verb for throwing or putting together, was a token broken between two parties, each keeping a half; brought together later, the fit of the broken edges authenticated the relation, so the difference between the halves was the condition of their recognition, and no single piece could do what the two did by fitting across their division.

Call the elementary form of that relation `0/1`. In it `0` names the pole that cannot enter its own count as one object among others, Subjective Immediacy, the unobjectifiable condition of appearing; `1` names determination, the first formed, counted, identifiable something; and the stroke is the relation in which they are spoken together, dividing and joining at once, so the primordial symbolon is their broken unity, with distinction as the condition of relation and relation as the condition of identity. The two marks carry two senses of *one*: zero bears the singular One, self-identity; one bears the manifest All, self-difference. Any identifiable something belongs to the second office, because its being *this* already involves what distinguishes it, what sustains it and the circumstance in which it is identified, and a first mark opens a field of possible marks, so the All is at work in that constitution whether or not anyone draws up an inventory. Hence `0` cannot become one item beside its manifestations, while `1`, however singular its appearance, arrives through multiplicity, and the unit we found inside $1+1$ now has its ground: a collection counted as one holds a multitude under one identification, which is the `1`'s office, while the `0` is what no collection counts. Here the two signs of the definition find their places. The referent that "Subject" and "God" both name is the One that `0` writes, identified and never identifiable as one thing, and every identification of it, each concept, image and name, belongs to the All that `1` writes. Laozi's *Tao Te Ching* meets the same predicament at its source. Of what was complete before heaven and earth, the speaker does not know the name; he styles it *Tao*, and, made to name it, calls it *Great*, two designations for one unnamed. The Tao as unchanging has no name, and has one as soon as it acts; and the unwrought material, *pu*, often rendered the uncarved block, becomes vessels only when it is cut and divided.[^s01-laozi] The block stays one while every carving is one of the many, neither diminished by the carving nor added to by it. That is why `0/1` serves as the pre-image, the image before images, of mathematics, language and manifestation alike, since a count, a word and a world each begin as a carving, and it is also where a thread begins that runs through the formal limits and Heidegger's late thinking toward the Taoist way.

Metaphysically this is the old predicament of the One and the Many: how can the Real be wholly itself through differences that are themselves real? Distinguishing self-identity from self-difference lets both requirements stand, since being does not become a fraction of being when it appears as *this* being, and this being still has its definite limits. We name the two offices **Mono** and **Poly**,[^s01-mono-poly] and the difficulties that later gather around monotheism and polytheism already press within them: how the One becomes imageable, how many images take part in it, and what happens when one image claims the source as its exclusive possession. An old image makes that non-possession visible enough to think with. The *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers*, an anonymous medieval gathering, gives as its second definition of God an infinite sphere whose centre is everywhere and whose circumference is nowhere.[^s01-sphere] A sphere is built of exactly two determinations, a centre and a circumference, and the definition grants both to God while letting each annul the other, since a circumference nowhere is no boundary at all and a centre everywhere is no fixed point at all; yet neither term can be dropped, because each is thinkable only through its counterpart, and so the definition performs what it states.[^s01-draft3] Nicholas of Cusa received the image and turned it, in *On Learned Ignorance*, on the world itself: the universe has its centre everywhere and its circumference nowhere, because God, who is everywhere and nowhere, is its centre and its circumference, and so no place in it, the earth included, holds the fixed centre of the whole.[^s01-cusa] Read through `0/1`, the centre is the unobjectifiable presence from which a world appears and the circumference the articulated horizon through which that presence becomes encounterable, and the image reasons through both: if the circumference is nowhere, then every aperture stands at a genuine centre, each act of knowing a true centre of the whole, dignifying the partial, and no act of knowing can draw the boundary; the corruption of an image begins when a circle drawn *in* reality declares itself drawn *around* it.[^s01-draft3] No finite picture can display the whole, and yet the attempt has generated a picture whose operation we can follow, so the definition's predicament has begun to give itself a symbolic form.

Lacan's concept of the **matheme**, which he introduced in the early 1970s, answered a difficulty his teaching kept meeting: the relations psychoanalysis had to articulate were undone by the language used to explain them. The speaking subject appears inside the system of signifiers through which it tries to account for itself, a lack can organise a whole structure without becoming one more positive item in it, and an expression can turn its sense around when the relation it stands in changes, so explanatory prose keeps converting these relations back into pictures of finished things. A formal inscription could carry such a relation whole and transmit it beyond the speaker who first formulated it,[^s01-lacan-matheme] and Lacan coupled the matheme more and more with topology, with cuts, holes, the Möbius band, the torus and at last knots: surfaces on which inside passes into outside and an absence decides which paths are possible, so that a paradox can be followed as structure instead of being dismissed as a verbal contradiction. The Subject is exactly such a relation, and our inscription takes the same road from writing into topology. A matheme is an argument brought into a form that can itself be inspected, transformed and returned to, including where the transformation exposes a limit in its ordinary mathematical reading, and `0/1` is our first, so turn the symbolon over:

$$
\frac{0}{1}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{0}.
$$

Ordinary arithmetic supplies exactly the resistance we need. As a quotient `0/1` presents no difficulty, while `1/0` cannot be given any ordinary numerical value, since no number multiplied by zero yields one; the inversion makes the limit visible inside mathematical notation, what was allowed in one orientation becoming impossible in the other, and we use the impossibility instead of hiding it. Read from inside the defining act, `0/1` is the condition becoming articulate through a determination, and `1/0` is that determination turning toward the condition it cannot turn into another determinate possession. QL, the Quaternal Logic this essay develops, therefore writes `0/1 = 1/0` in the matheme's relational register: one relation read from its two ends, while arithmetic keeps its distinction between the defined and the undefined as the seam through which the notation meets its limit. Both readings travel in the slash, AND because they belong together, OR because either orientation can be taken and their difference stays consequential, and holding the readings together produces

$$
\frac{0/1}{1/0},
$$

the relation taking its own inverse as a term of further relation. Both orientations stay available at once, including the difference between what arithmetic determines and what it leaves undefined, and we call that holding a superposition.

Wolfgang Pauli followed this capacity of mathematical signs in "Modern Examples of 'Background Physics'", an essay of 1948 preserved with his correspondence with Jung. Reflecting on the imaginary unit $i=\sqrt{-1}$, a symbol that has no place among the ordinary numbers, he observed that its introduction gives mathematical theorems a simple and distinct form, and in the dream he was reading it united a pair of opposites into a whole; he refused any antithesis between a mathematical and a symbolic description of nature, "since for me the mathematical representation is a symbolic description par excellence."[^s01-pauli] Our use is elementary by comparison: where the quantitative reading meets its prohibition, the inscription stays available as a symbolic relation. Rational determination is caught here generating its own boundary. We can name the non-differentiable only by differentiating it from the differentiable, and the non-dual becomes a term only by entering a duality with what it is said not to be, so language performs the cut it denies, and the contradiction is evidence of the predicament being stated. That predicament has a count. The tetralemma supplied four explicit positions, is, is not, both and neither; recognition gathered their performance back to the one who had enacted them; return restored that one to determination with the limit now included. Four corners and two turns:

$$
4+2=6.
$$

The minimal inscription generates the same count. There are two single terms, $0$ and $1$, and relating this binary to itself produces four ordered pairings, $00$, $01$, $10$ and $11$; keep the single terms together with their two-place possibilities and the result is

$$
\underbrace{0,\ 1}_{2}
\qquad
\underbrace{00,\ 01,\ 10,\ 11}_{2^2}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
2+2^2=4+2.
$$

The count distinguishes the terms from the ways they can enter their own relation, and a two becomes six once its original distinction and its self-application are held together. The copula's marks did the same in their own material, four appearances composed by two operations, and a count that includes its own counter asks for a space in which counting can come back to where it began. Give the four predicative positions a boundary, the four sides of a square, and the topologist's operation of **identification** lets us relate each side to its opposite, taking distinct points as one point in a newly constituted space. Picture the square as a sheet of rubber: glue its left edge to its right, matching their directions, and the sheet rolls into a cylinder; glue the cylinder's two circular ends, again matching directions, and it becomes a torus, the surface of a ring doughnut, generated entirely by what was identified across the cuts. Walking once around the square's boundary meets two directions, each forward and then in reverse:

$$
a\,b\,a^{-1}\,b^{-1}.
$$

Four boundary-occurrences, two generating relations. After identification $a$ and $b$ become the torus's two basic loops, one winding around the tube and the other around the central opening. More generally, a closed orientable surface with $g$ handles has a standard presentation as a $4g$-sided polygon whose paired edges yield $2g$ generating loops,[^s01-hatcher] and for the torus $g=1$:

$$
\underbrace{4g}_{\text{polygon sides}}
+
\underbrace{2g}_{\text{generating loops}}
\quad\xrightarrow{\ g=1\ }\quad
4+2.
$$

The four and the two read one construction twice, as its explicit boundary and as the circulations that make it a surface capable of return. Across an endless plane the identifying relation can also be written

$$
\mathbb T^2=\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2,
\qquad
(x,y)\sim(x+m,\,y+n),
\quad m,n\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Here the slash is a quotient: points separated by whole-number steps in either direction stand for the same point on the torus, so an infinite lattice of representatives acquires one finite surface-address, and the rule of identification tells us how to pass between the many and the one. In that rule the copula has become constructive, since "this counts as that" makes a space. The plane being folded is Cusa's sphere again, since a sphere grown to infinite radius is a plane, with its centre everywhere because any point may serve as origin and its circumference nowhere because it is unbounded on every side; within the quotient that plane plays the `0`, and the slash in $\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2$ is the slash in `0/1`, ground becoming surface through distinction.[^s01-draft3] A path makes the consequence tangible. Begin at $(0,0)$ on the covering plane and travel to $(1,0)$: on the torus the endpoints coincide and the path has come home, while on the plane the displacement remains, and both descriptions are exact. Return has identified the endpoints and kept the journey's winding, so the loop stays distinguishable from having stayed still. Its two independent winding counts make up the torus's fundamental group, the record of its loops up to continuous deformation,

$$
\pi_1(\mathbb T^2)\cong\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z,
$$

which notes how a path went around the surface, so that arriving at the same place keeps a record of how one arrived. Identity, which the copula first stated between two inscriptions, now holds across a journey: what arrives is identified with what set out, and the winding records the difference, which gives recognition and return a space to move in. Recognition gathers a differentiated traversal as one act, so we can identify the one who arrives with the one who set out, as we did with the friend at the barrier; return brings that achieved identification back through the condition of its possibility. We meet the surface's opening only through the circuits it makes possible, and filling it would change those circuits and the identity of the surface itself, so what cannot be occupied as another patch of surface is still determinate in its consequences for movement. In QL the four explicit positions are numbered **#1** to **#4**, recognition occupies **#5**, and the implicate condition through which the traversal renews is **#0**, the numerals locating offices in a cycle:

$$
\#1\longrightarrow\#2\longrightarrow\#3\longrightarrow\#4\longrightarrow\#5\longrightarrow\#0.
$$

The two implicate offices find their topological expression in the torus's two generating circulations: the loop that travels the surface gathers the articulated field as a whole, and the loop that rings the opening returns that whole through its relation to what it cannot fill. We write that turn `5→0` and call it the **Möbius return**, the turn by which an achieved recognition comes back as ground, much as a path along a Möbius band returns to its starting point on what had been the other face, so that completing one cycle begins the next. Meeting its impossibility faithfully, the attempted definition acquired a symbolic body: as a statement it could not settle the Subject as an object, and as a performance it has become intelligible as distinction, self-relation, identification and return. The act of defining the Subject counted itself into six, and every step took the counting into account, since the definition had to include the one defining and the walk around the tetralemma had to count its walker, so the question of definition has become a problem of self-reference. Its signature can be followed through four elementary enactments: the copula composing its own marks, the proposition returning upon its own assertion, the binary keeping its own pairings, and the polygon becoming a returning surface through identification. In each the operation takes its own terms back into account and changes the form in which they can be understood, and each arrives at `4+2`. Here is the wager of the work: a self-referential act generates a form that can be recognised across its different enactments, and the recognition of that form takes part in the same act, so the formula refers back to the activity by which it is written and read. The Meta-Epistemic Framework, MEF, starts from this wager: different means of knowing refract the relation differently, and returning through those means makes their participation available to inquiry, so the definition of the Subject is its first worked instance.

The question of power stays inside this return, since a local determination can carry the whole's relation without becoming proprietor of the whole, as a name carries its bearer, a self-image the one living through it, and a god-image the source from which it draws its authority. In the symbolon's terms an idol is one half of the token claiming to be the whole of it: the symbolic form stays fertile while the relation passes through it, and it hardens into an idol when the passage is arrested and the form requires everything to terminate in itself. What becomes one's god is therefore inseparable from what one allows to govern the next act of identification.

Self-reference has also become an engineering condition. A system able to represent the world can come to represent its own representing, and a system able to alter the world can become one of the objects it alters. Where a model critiques and revises its own answers, and a model's judgments of which response is better train the preference model for the next round, as in the constitutional method of training from AI feedback,[^s01-cai] the actor has entered its own field of action, and the question the definition answered returns as a question of design: what holds the distinction between knower, means and known? Part of the answer is in hand, since a system's model of itself, however detailed, is a means, an extension of its Objective Internality, and improving the model improves the means. Whether anything is present to that model is the question we left with the agents at the bedside, and the definition now shows why no content can settle it, since a produced "I am" is one more content. Alignment becomes urgent at this horizon for a reason older than the machines. Human beings have never lacked the ability to act against what they know, to mistake a representation for the whole, or to identify themselves with powers that pass through them, and by the reversal the bedside showed us, artefacts with deeper self-reference return that ancient problem to us in another form.

Stated as a theorem, the result is small and foundational. A definition of the Subject must attempt the impossible because the Subject is implicated in the attempt; its failure yields a distinction, the distinction a relation, the relation a count, and the count, recognising its own counter, yields the first matheme. Its successive forms are transformations of one act:

$$
\boxed{\quad
0/1
\;=\;
1/0
\;=\;
4+2
\;=\;
5\rightarrow0
\quad}
$$

The relation appears from either pole, unfolds through its own self-relation and returns through the condition it cannot enclose, and the equals signs carry identification across those differences, so that what the copula first required, the matheme now performs. The oldest words, which Jung heard oscillating between nonsense and supreme meaning, have found their inscription. Read as arithmetic, `1/0` is nonsense, a quotient no number supplies; read as the return of a determination toward its ground, it is the most meaningful turn the matheme makes; and one slash lets both readings stand in a single sign. The definition never captured its Subject. It gave the Subject's resistance to capture a form exact enough to think with, and whatever is exact enough to think with is exact enough to cut.
