### Arabic reckoning and the Names — an account, an address and an undertaking

Arabic supplies several related offices, with their words and textual occasions distinguished. [Lane’s selected entries](../../sources/classical-philology/lane/lane-arabic-english-lexicon-online/SOURCE.md#passages) **historicise** *ḥisāb* as reckoning and the separate form-IV *aḥṣā* through enumeration, registration, memory and knowledge. The two are not one root. A shared English gloss for counting cannot decide what either does in a particular passage.

The [Qur’anic sequence, 17:12–15](../../sources/religion-theology/quran/quran-arberry-corpus-selected/SOURCE.md#quran-arberry-corpus-selected-p001) **sources** a movement from the signs by which years become reckonable to the book a person confronts at resurrection. The bearer must read; accountability returns to the life whose record is presented. The adjacent verse preserves individual responsibility. In the selected Arberry rendering, this is a divine judgment scene. E4 receives the record’s demand for answerability without recasting divine judgment as an institutional ledger whose criterion the judged person can revise. The account and the life are placed in relation; their relation is the scene’s work.

[Qur’an 7:180](../../sources/religion-theology/quran/quran-arberry-corpus-selected/SOURCE.md#quran-arberry-corpus-selected-p002) **sources** a different act: calling upon God through the Names. Its wording does not give their number. [Al-Bukhārī 7392](../../sources/religion-theology/al-bukhari/bukhari-sahih-sunnah-online/SOURCE.md#bukhari-sahih-sunnah-online-p001), in a ninth-century collection whose compiler died in 870 CE, **sources** the separate ninety-nine-Names report. The displayed Muhsin Khan translation interprets *aḥṣāhā* through memorisation. That interpretive choice is preserved beside the Arabic verb’s broader lexical range. Neither this report nor the Qur’anic verse supplies a complete list or a bead-recitation protocol. Their recovered practice is textual invocation and the undertaking associated with enumeration or retention, not an independently observed performance of a later devotional order.

The sequence returns to [the whole field](WHOLE-FIELD.md) at register 3. **Symbol answers to source:** a Name functions as address within a tradition of attribution, not as the interpreter’s possession of what is addressed. **Account does not replace source:** the count organises a finite undertaking while neither the numeral nor a translation exhausts its religious object. **Trust keeps the return route active:** the reported promise gives memorisation or enumeration an orientation toward fulfilment; E4’s further duty of answerability belongs to the essay and must not be silently attributed to the hadith. **Account re-enters source-field:** the retained wording can shape a subsequent act of invocation and a reader’s account of responsibility. This is the proposed operational consequence of using the text, not a reported psychological experiment or evidence that a promised afterlife outcome occurred.

This branch changes the comparison by refusing to let count stand for the whole event. It makes the object counted, the act performed, the attribution and the promised consequence separately available. Its Qur’anic, hadith and nineteenth-century lexicographic witnesses retain their own times and textual offices. No descent into the English words Symbol, Account or Trust, and no identity with the Egyptian name-preserving practice, follows.

## Exact WHOLE-FIELD return proposal

Insertion after the current “Branches kept in their own soils” return or another Laplace-selected register-3 return paragraph:

The [Arabic reckoning and Names branch](HISTORICAL-BRANCHES.md#arabic-reckoning-and-names) **qualifies** all four operations by distinguishing reckoning, address and an undertaken enumeration. A finite count can serve invocation without possessing what the Names address; a record can demand answerability without becoming the life recorded. The theological promise and the essay’s duty of revisability remain separate warrants.
