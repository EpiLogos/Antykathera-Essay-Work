### Egyptian rn — the name retained for a particular deceased person

The [TLA rn entry, lemma 94700](../../sources/classical-philology/tla/tla-hieroglyphic-hieratic-lexicon/SOURCE.md#tla-hieroglyphic-hieratic-lexicon-p001) **historicises** the hieroglyphic/hieratic noun for name. This lexical identity establishes the point of departure; the further claim about continued personhood needs a particular practice. The entry’s corpus-wide attestation span is not a date for every use, and its separate Demotic relations do not erase changes of language and period.

[Reri’s funerary papyrus, British Museum EA75044,4](../../sources/religion-theology/reri/reri-ptolemaic-funerary-papyrus/SOURCE.md#reri-ptolemaic-funerary-papyrus-p001) **sources** such a practice in Ptolemaic Thebes. The museum’s period range is 305–30 BCE; it is not an exact production year. In its hieratic Book of the Dead arrangement, spell 25 occupies the top of column 3 and concerns remembrance of the deceased’s name. Its neighbours restore speech and protect the heart. The name thus appears within a programme for the continued capacities of this deceased person, rather than as an isolated entry in a universal inventory of soul-parts.

The medium performs a prospective act. A surviving written arrangement supplies what the ritual anticipates the deceased needing beyond death. Its intended result is preservation of named continuity; the papyrus’s existence and the museum’s identification establish that this provision was made. They do not demonstrate the promised continuation itself. The consulted museum account labels spell 25’s purpose but does not furnish a complete hieratic transcription. Its illustrative translations for other spells come from a revised Faulkner rendering of Ani and are not silently reassigned to Reri. The present case therefore rests on a dated, identified funerary practice, not a manufactured quotation.

At register 3, **Symbol answers to source** through the name’s reference to this particular deceased person. **Account does not replace source** because the surviving object is evidence of a provision for Reri, not the whole person or an empirical proof of his continuing existence. **Trust keeps the return route active** in the essay’s comparison as an entrusted act of preservation whose intended recipient exceeds present verification. The Egyptian ritual conception and the native formal-limit argument remain distinct. **Account re-enters source-field** when the preserved name and arrangement condition later remembrance and interpretation. The material survival makes a later encounter possible; it does not prove that every later reader remembered Reri, or that the original rite produced its intended afterlife result.

This case gives the field an exact asymmetry. The historical person cannot be made to answer a modern curator as a living interlocutor. Answerability instead falls upon the present account: retain the object’s identity, period, attributed purpose and limits, and let a corrected reading change what is said of the person. The record can sustain an address without owning its bearer. Preserving the name is thereby a determinate practice with a source, not evidence that Egyptian rn and an Arabic divine Name name the same kind of being or derive from one word.

## Exact WHOLE-FIELD return proposal

The [Egyptian rn case](HISTORICAL-BRANCHES.md#egyptian-rn-reri) **qualifies** source-answerability where a named person survives for inquiry through a funerary record. Reri’s dated papyrus bears a provision for remembrance; the surviving provision neither exhausts its bearer nor verifies its hoped-for result. Trust’s register-3 comparison preserves that asymmetry. Account re-entry occurs in a subsequent encounter with the retained name, where present interpreters remain responsible for correcting their attribution rather than requiring the deceased to confirm it.
