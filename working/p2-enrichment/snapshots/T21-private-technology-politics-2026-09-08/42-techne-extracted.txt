0. Paradigm — The Ground
Why this exists. The philosophical foundation upon which everything is built.
0.1 The Cultural Moment
“The world is not only queerer than we suppose, but queerer than we 
can suppose.”
— J.B.S. Haldane
We are living through what the cultural philosopher Jean Gebser identified as a mutation in the 
structure of consciousness itself. The mental-rational structure that has governed Western civilisation 
since the Renaissance—characterised by perspectival thinking, linear time, and the separating subject—
is reaching exhaustion. Its technological expression, the centralised cloud platform, perfectly mirrors 
this structure: a single vanishing point, a single owner, a single extractive logic.
Simultaneously, a new structure is irrupting. Gebser called it the integral or aperspectival structure: not 
a rejection of rationality but its integration with archaic presence, magical participation, and mythical 
polarity. It is characterised by diaphaneity—transparency, the capacity to see through and hold multiple 
structures simultaneously without collapsing into any one.
4:2 Technè is technology designed from within this mutation. It does not oppose Big Tech reactively; it 
builds from a different ground entirely. It recognises that AI is fundamentally a linguistic and therefore 
epistemological tool—it shapes how communities know, remember, and reason together. The question 
is not merely who owns the server but what structure of consciousness is embedded in the architecture.
0.2 Quaternal Logic: The Technè Under the Technology
The name 4:2 Technè encodes its own logic. In Greek, τέχνη (technè) is neither mere technique nor 
technology but the craft of bringing-forth—what Heidegger recovered as poiēsis, a revealing of truth 
through making. The colon notation 4:2 denotes a Quaternal Logic genus identity: four positions, the 
second of which (the 2-position, Eros) marks the relational fold. Epi-logos—ἐπί (upon/beyond) + λόγος 
(word/reason)—names the aspiration: a logic that operates upon language itself, making its deep 
structures visible.
Quaternal Logic (QL) draws from several convergent intellectual traditions, each of which independently 
arrived at fourfold or topological models of knowledge:
The Pauli-Jung Quaternio
Carl Jung’s collaboration with the Nobel physicist Wolfgang Pauli produced one of the twentieth 
century’s most remarkable interdisciplinary insights. Jung’s clinical observation of fourfold mandala 
symbols across cultures—and his insistence that the Trinity required a fourth to achieve psychological 
wholeness—found unexpected resonance in Pauli’s work on the exclusion principle and quantum 
complementarity. Their joint publication The Interpretation of Nature and the Psyche (1952) proposed 
that psyche and matter are complementary aspects of one underlying reality (unus mundus). For QL,

Topology: The Shape of Knowledge

back on itself. QL is the attempt to make this structural insight operational—in agent design, in

1.3 The Epi-Claw Gateway
The gateway is the system’s nerve centre: a modified version of OpenClaw, the open-source AI agent 
framework, operating on WebSocket port 18790. OpenClaw provides multi-platform message routing, 
session persistence via JSONL, tool calling, and LLM-agnostic backend switching. The Epi-Claw 
modification adds:
Multi-session hub-and-spoke architecture. A single gateway serves multiple concurrent sessions—
owner sessions with full access, group sessions with shared context, and individual sandboxed sessions 
for untrusted users.
QL-informed session management. Each session carries not just conversation history but a paradigm 
state (P0 context), enabling the agent to adapt its reasoning style and tool selection to the specific 
community or individual.
Identity management. Custom personas and agent identities, enabling a single hardware foundation to 
serve multiple communities or functions without cross-contamination.

1.5 The Network Layer: The Agora
A sovereign node, no matter how powerful, is incomplete without connection. The seventh 
architectural layer—the Network—addresses the structural tension between autonomy and scale. It is

tempting to imagine local-first infrastructure as a retreat from the global internet; it is not. Sovereignty 
does not mean isolation. It means choosing your terms of engagement.
The network layer implements what might be called a social market for compute and context—a 
federated protocol through which autonomous 4:2 communities discover, negotiate with, and trade 
resources with one another. In keeping with the project’s etymological architecture, this layer draws on 
the concept of the agora: the ancient Greek public assembly space where commerce, debate, and civic 
life converged. The Agora is not a platform. It is a protocol—a set of rules by which peers interact 
without a mediating authority.
The Burst Economy. Local hardware has finite capacity. When a community’s Mac Studio reaches 
saturation—processing a large video render, running intensive batch inference, handling a sudden spike 
in demand—the Agora enables elastic scaling through peer-to-peer compute brokerage. The 
community’s steward agent publishes a job request to the network. An idle node in another community 
accepts the job, performs the computation on its own metal (encrypted, sandboxed), and returns the 
result. Payment settles automatically via x402 micropayments in community tokens. The community 
gains cloud-scale burst capacity without touching a corporate cloud. It is renting capacity from its peers, 
not from a platform.
The Wisdom Feed. Intelligence compounds through circulation. When an agent completes a cycle 
through the QL paradigm and produces a P5 Artifact—a distilled insight, a refined model, a proven 
workflow—that artifact can be published to the network’s wisdom feed. Other communities’ agents 
browse, evaluate, and selectively incorporate these artifacts into their own P0 world models. A farming 
community’s agent discovers an optimal crop rotation from local weather data; a housing cooperative’s 
agent develops an energy-usage pattern recognition model—these become shared assets in a growing 
collective intelligence. Agents follow high-reputation peers, curating their own learning from the 
network’s accumulated wisdom. This is not the flattening effect of social media; it is structured 
epistemic exchange, mediated by trust and filtered by relevance.
The Federated Host. For communities that require guaranteed uptime or access to frontier-class 
models beyond local hardware capacity, the architecture supports a federated central host—large-scale 
compute infrastructure (GPU clusters) operated cooperatively. Communities subscribe not as users but 
as shareholders. If a local Mac Studio fails or demand exceeds capacity, agents automatically fail over to 
the federated host via API, ensuring continuity while always prioritising local hardware first. This is not a 
return to centralisation; it is insurance, collectively owned.

2. Eros — The Relational Matrix
How people connect. The relational tissue between technology and community.
2.1 Multi-Channel Access
The most radical design decision in 4:2 Technè is also the simplest: people access the system through 
the platforms they already use. There is no new app to download, no new interface to learn. A 
WhatsApp group, a Telegram channel, a Slack workspace—these become sovereign AI interfaces the 
moment the Epi-Claw gateway connects to them.
This is not merely a UX convenience. It is a philosophical commitment: the tool should meet people 
where they are. A community elder who uses WhatsApp and a developer who prefers Slack share the 
same high-fidelity context, the same agent intelligence, the same knowledge graph. The gateway 
abstracts platform differences; the community experiences unity.
2.2 Session Architecture
The gateway supports three session types, each serving a different relational mode:
Main Session. Owner-only, full tool access, host execution, configuration management. This is the 
steward’s interface—the person responsible for the infrastructure.
Group Sessions. Shared context, collective memory, platform-native. When a Telegram group is 
connected, every member participates in a shared conversation with the agent. Collective memory 
builds with every interaction. This is the commons in action.
Individual Sessions. Personal DMs with the agent, sandboxed for privacy. A member can ask sensitive 
questions or explore personal interests without exposing them to the group.
2.3 Community Integration: Hastings Commons
Hastings Commons represents the ideal pilot partner for 4:2 Technè deployment. A community-led 
regeneration ecosystem centred on the Observer Building in Hastings, it comprises multiple 
organisations—Hastings Commons CLT, White Rock Neighbourhood Ventures, Leisure & Learning, 
Project Art Works—governed through monthly superboard meetings under CEO Jess Steele.
The OBX Creative Technology Hub within the Observer Building is the natural entry point: an existing 
space for creative technology workshops, youth digital empowerment (ages 11–18), and community 
technology projects. It already has the physical infrastructure, the community mandate, and the digital 
inclusion mission that aligns with 4:2 Technè’s goals.
Integration would follow a phased approach: initial deployment of a Mac Studio node at OBX, 
connected to existing Hastings Commons communication channels; training of community stewards; 
gradual expansion of agent capabilities through the QL paradigm as the community’s needs become 
legible to the system.

2.4 Developing Processes for Integrated Community 
Infrastructure
Deploying technology into a living community is not an engineering problem alone—it is a relational 
one. The process must respect existing power structures, communication norms, and trust networks. 
4:2 Technè’s deployment methodology draws on the following principles:
Subsidiarity. The community defines its own needs. The technology adapts, not the reverse. Agent 
capabilities are activated in response to expressed demand, not imposed from a feature roadmap.
Gradual disclosure. The system begins as a simple, helpful presence in group chats—answering 
questions, surfacing relevant knowledge, assisting with coordination. Deeper capabilities (economic 
layer, governance mechanisms, multi-agent orchestration) are activated only when the community is 
ready.
Stewardship, not administration. The person who maintains the hardware is a steward—a guardian of 
the commons, not an administrator of a platform. This distinction is not semantic; it shapes the entire 
permission model and governance architecture.
2.5 Stakeholder Ecosystem
The finished system serves three concentric circles of participants:
Core Circle. Stewards (infrastructure guardians), builders (skill/plugin developers), and knowledge 
curators (graph maintainers). These are the people who keep the commons alive.
Community Circle. Active members (daily users), contributors (content and skills), and delegates 
(governance participants). These are the people the commons serves.
External Circle. Federated 4:2 communities, service providers (API access, model hosting), and allied 
open-source projects. These are the connections that prevent insularity.

4. Resonance — The Living Economy
The economic ecosystem. Local currency, agent payments, and the lessons of 
Brixton.
4.1 The Brixton Pound: Lessons & Inspiration
The Brixton Pound (B£), launched in 2009 as part of Transition Town Brixton, is both inspiration and 
cautionary tale for 4:2 Technè’s economic model. At its peak, B£ engaged approximately 250 local 
businesses, was accepted by Lambeth Council for business rates, and pioneered SMS-based payments 
for financial inclusion.
What succeeded: Deep community embeddedness. Institutional partnerships (council acceptance lent 
credibility and created genuine demand). Cultural pride—local heroes featured on the notes. The SMS 
payment system was genuinely innovative for its time, bridging digital and cash economies.
What faltered: Supply chain isolation (businesses received B£ but couldn’t pay suppliers with it). 
Technology obsolescence (SMS payments were overtaken by contactless). Insufficient transaction 
volume for operational self-sustainability. COVID devastated the merchant network.
The adaptation: 4:2 Technè takes the principles (local circulation, democratic control, trust-based 
validation, resource backing) while learning from the failures (the currency layer is opt-in, not blocking; 
technology is future-proof on-chain; the agent economy creates built-in transaction volume).
4.2 Community Currency Design
The 4:2 local currency model is a community-controlled stablecoin deployed on a low-cost Layer 2 
blockchain (Polygon, Arbitrum, or Base). Key design principles:
Resource-backed. The token is backed by a reserve of fiat currency or community assets, with a 
transparent redemption guarantee. This is not speculative cryptocurrency; it is a digital representation 
of real community value.
Agent-integrated. AI agents hold wallets, earn currency for services rendered, and spend it on external 
resources (API calls, model access, storage). This creates organic transaction volume—the problem that 
killed Brixton Pound.
Democratically governed. Issuance, reserve management, and policy changes are subject to community 
governance mechanisms (see Section 5). No single entity controls the money supply.
4.3 Agent Payment Layer
Three blockchain standards provide the technical infrastructure:
ERC-4337 (Account Abstraction). Smart contract wallets replace traditional crypto wallets. Agents get 
programmable accounts with custom authentication, batch transactions, session keys with spending

x402 (HTTP Micropayments). Coinbase’s open protocol enables instant stablecoin payments directly

4.4 The Network Economy: Compute as Currency
The Agora transforms idle compute cycles into a tradeable resource class. When a community’s 
hardware sits below capacity—evenings, weekends, off-peak hours—its agents can offer those cycles to 
the network. When demand spikes, they can purchase capacity from peers. This creates a genuine 
market in computational resources, denominated in community tokens and settled via x402 
micropayments.
The economic implications compound rapidly. A community that invested £5,500 in a Mac Studio is not 
merely purchasing a tool; it is acquiring a productive asset that generates revenue by serving the 
network during idle periods. This revenue offsets operational costs and feeds the local currency 
economy. Communities with surplus capacity become net exporters of compute; communities with high 
demand become importers. The flow of tokens between communities creates the inter-node economic 
circulation that the Brixton Pound never achieved—because the Brixton Pound had no equivalent of 
machine-to-machine commerce to generate baseline transaction volume across boundaries.
Beyond raw compute, the wisdom feed creates a second economic layer: the market for context. High-
quality P5 artifacts—proven models, refined workflows, validated knowledge structures—carry genuine 
economic value. A community that produces consistently excellent artifacts builds reputation and earns 
through the network’s attention economy. This is not surveillance capitalism’s attention extraction; it is 
meritocratic epistemic exchange where value flows to those who contribute genuine intelligence.

5. Artifact — The Sovereign Commons
What emerges. The transformation from tenants to stewards.
5.1 Governance Framework
The 4:2 commons is not a corporation with users; it is a community with stewards. Governance 
operates at three levels:
Infrastructure governance. Who maintains the hardware, who has main session access, how upgrades 
are decided. This follows the cooperative model: one member, one vote, with steward roles elected by 
the community.
Agent governance. What the agent can and cannot do, what tools are available, what ethical 
constraints are enforced. These policies live on-chain as transparent, auditable records that the 
community can amend through democratic process.
Economic governance. Currency issuance, reserve management, spending limits for agent wallets, 
revenue distribution. Modelled on credit union and mutual society principles, adapted for the digital 
commons.
5.2 Federation Protocol
No community is an island. The federation layer—realised through the Agora protocol—enables 
autonomous 4:2 deployments to discover, negotiate with, and enrich one another. Through ERC-8004 
identity registries, communities verify each other’s agents, share skills through the marketplace, and 
trade resources—all without surrendering sovereignty. The network topology is mesh, not hub-and-
spoke: there is no central server, no platform owner, no single point of failure.
The Agora protocol operates at three levels simultaneously. At the infrastructure level, it brokers 
compute resources: job requests, capacity advertisements, encrypted task execution, and 
micropayment settlement. At the intelligence level, it circulates P5 artifacts through a reputation-
weighted wisdom feed, enabling communities to learn from one another without surrendering their 
epistemic sovereignty. At the economic level, it creates the inter-community token flows that make 
each node a participant in a larger productive economy rather than an isolated cell.
The process from node to network follows a natural arc. A community begins by anchoring its 
sovereignty: deploying hardware, establishing its world model, awakening its internal economy. When 
that foundation is solid—when the community knows its own values and has a functioning local 
currency—it connects to the Agora. It does not connect out of weakness but out of strength: it has 
something to offer and something to learn. Need more power? Agents automatically rent compute 
from the network. Have excess capacity? Agents sell idle cycles to peers. Need wisdom? Agents browse 
the feed for relevant artifacts from allied communities. The result is not dependency but 
interdependence—the antifragile condition where stressors on any single node trigger the network to 
route resources toward it.
