## Source scholarship

This is the hinge work in which Baudrillard breaks with Marxism and assembles the theoretical apparatus the later works deploy. Its core moves:

- **The four values.** Use value, exchange value, sign value (value within a system of objects — prestige, distinction, differential position), and symbolic value (Maussian gift exchange: the irreversible, the counter-exchange that cannot be settled or squared). The first three are the economic and semiotic registers; the fourth is the symbolic order that the code cannot absorb.
- **The code.** The system of sign-value becomes the universal equivalent: every object and relation is convertible through its sign-position, exactly as money makes goods commensurable through price. The code is the political economy of the sign taken to its completion.
- **The orders of simulacra.** The book contains the genealogy of simulacra later refined in *Simulacra and Simulation*: the first order (counterfeit — the unique object standing for the natural, the premodern), the second order (production — mass reproducibility, the industrial), the third order (simulation — the code and the model generating the real, the contemporary). The "death" of the title is the death of the referent: the signifier's bond to a real that can no longer anchor it.
- **The end of the signifier / the masses.** The book develops the implosion of the social and the masses as the absorbing black hole of meaning — the material later carried by *In the Shadow of the Silent Majorities* (1978).

Scholarly status: Baudrillard here rejects Marxism from within (critique of the political economy of the sign), which positions the book as a rupture document in the post-'68 field. Mark Poster's editor's critique — that Baudrillard "fails to define key terms, such as the code; his writing style is hyperbolic and declarative" — applies with particular force to this text and is recorded as a gate for any use.

## Argument relations (candidate — for the harmonisation pass to weigh)

- **The code ↔ the occupied slash / counterfeit Mono.** The code as universal equivalent of sign-value is the *nomisma* measure taken to completion: the nomos/numerus/nomisma/oikonomia braid of the copula thread, with the measure itself occupying the ground it was meant to mediate. Candidate reading: the code is the slash privatised absolutely — the gauge occupied at every scale at once, which is the endpoint of the technical addendum's gauge tower (softmax gauge at token scale; Bradley–Terry at preference scale; institutional occupation at governance scale). Baudrillard's "regime of total equivalency" is what the gauge tower looks like when every gauge is occupied and nobody remembers one was there. *Relation proposed for weighing, not decided.*
- **Sign-value ↔ currency whose backing is deleted.** The trust thread's "every 'is' is a credit event": predication extends credit against a ground that cannot be exhibited. Sign-value is the credit event denied while drawn upon — the ledger posted as its own collateral. The *credere / recordari* etymology of the copula thread meets the code at the point where circulation continues after the ground's deletion.
- **The four values ↔ the four falls of 0/1 (candidate mapping).** Use value and exchange value operate at the determinate level of the mark; sign value operates at the differential level of the sign-system; symbolic value names what the code cannot convert — the irreversible, the counter-exchange, the remainder. Candidate reading to be tested at harmonisation: the symbolic is the slashed relation (0/1) that the code's accounting (the fallen (−1)+(+1)=0) cannot process; the code's refusal of the symbolic is the refusal of return. *Candidate only.*
- **The orders of simulacra (genealogy).** The counterfeit → production → simulation sequence is the historical itinerary of the sign's severance from its ground — a candidate inversion of the zero's history in the essay: instead of zero returning to ontology, the sign loses its referent. The genealogy originates here and is refined in *Simulacra and Simulation* (1981); cross-referencing between the two houses will be needed at harmonisation.
