## Reckoning, report and a split debt record

**Registers 1 and 2, separately.** English *account* is traced through Middle English and Anglo-French forms associated with counting. Its attested senses include transaction records, explanations and narrative reports. This supports the relation between reckoning and telling without making every act of narration financial. The entry does not warrant promoting the protected HISTORY's “cut together” gloss to the public etymology of *account*. [Merriam-Webster, “account,” Word History and senses 1–3](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/account).

**Material historical witness.** The Bank of England Museum's wooden tally **A013/1** records one of the Bank's first loans to the Government in **1694**. The notched hazelwood was split lengthwise, leaving each party a record of the debt. Its material division and shared reckoning supply an independently documented historical comparison with the Greek token. No borrowing route between the two institutions has been established here. [Bank of England Museum, “Payments through time,” Wooden tally stick](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/museum/whats-on/2019/325-years-exhibition/payments-through-time).

The comparison returns to [Account's source relation](WHOLE-FIELD.md#account-does-not-replace-source) at register 3. A record can preserve the amount and identify an obligation while depending on persons, institutions and resources for its discharge. Matching records support the account's integrity; payment and the conditions of repayment are further events. The particular tally's museum record is not evidence for a universal history of money, a complete account of Exchequer procedure or the moral legitimacy of a debt.

The protected branches on *computare*, *reckon*, *rule*, *right*, *credit*, *record*, *coin*, *claim*, *fiat*, *value*, *score* and *digit* retain their individual source tasks. ## Reliance and entrusted office

**Register 1 — bounded lexical claim.** Merriam-Webster gives Middle English *trust* a **probably Scandinavian** origin, relating it to Old Norse *traust* and Old English *trēowe*. The uncertainty belongs to that transmission claim and remains explicit. **Register 2:** the entry distinguishes reliance, contingent hope, custody or responsible office, and credit for future payment. Those senses locate several historical-language offices without identifying them all with the essay's formal-limit argument. [Merriam-Webster, “trust,” senses 1, 4–5 and Word History](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/trust).

The [Trust operation](WHOLE-FIELD.md#trust-keeps-the-return-route-active) draws a further **register-3** distinction: someone can know the stated obligation and still have to commit to a relation in which its fulfilment remains consequential. The public dictionary does not derive knowledge's dependence on trust, prove a theological doctrine of faith or prescribe the essay's covenant architecture. Those operations have their own authorial and argument carriers. Fides/credere, covenant traditions and comparative institutional histories remain in their existing homes.
