## #1 — Meaningful coincidence makes the concepts of cause and time difficult

In letter **35P**, Zollikon-Zürich, **7 November 1948**, printed pp. 34–35, Pauli thanks Jung for the previous day's discussion and asks whether a dream and event separated by two or three months are still called synchronous. The question is already more exact than a general fascination with coincidence. It asks which temporal relation the word denotes. His accompanying rotation image employs connected sheets and a central passage to describe a proposed ordering relation. The dream's diagram is a symbolic construction within the correspondence; it does not document a new physical topology of the psyche.

Jung subsequently sends his synchronicity manuscript for criticism. Pauli's **37P**, **28 June 1949**, printed pp. 36–42, distinguishes Rhine's experiments from the other material Jung presents because he cannot see the same archetypal basis in them. This is Pauli's assessment of a manuscript, not this dossier's independent assessment of Rhine's evidence. He also asks for more precision in “acausal”: absence of a causal chain through space and time is different from absence of an ordering relation. The term's meaning cannot simply be carried unchanged between the two practices.

Time produces the harder problem. Pauli objects that a formulation beginning with simultaneity then includes prediction, where the events do not occur together. He considers an ordering by meaning that would contain ordinary time as a special case, while marking his formulations as provisional. His subsequent radioactive-stranger thought experiment explicitly uses a parable. The comparison differentiates the physical decay clock from symbolic “radioactivity” in dream language, and culminates in a demand for a neutral language that does not yet exist. A scientific term appearing in the dream therefore begins translation work rather than ending it.

The complete exchange gives synchronicity a positive research office: investigate the proposed ordering of meaningful events while preserving what “meaning,” “event,” “cause” and “time” denote in each account. Its recorded conjectures and reports are historical evidence that these questions were pursued. They do not constitute an independently controlled demonstration of the proposed connection. [Homologia / Analogia](../etymologies/homology-and-analogy/WHOLE-FIELD.md) **qualifies** the crossing at this point: specify the operation and preserved relation before claiming common cause or formal identity. Its [historical branches](../etymologies/homology-and-analogy/HISTORICAL-BRANCHES.md) **historicise** comparison's distinct lexical and disciplinary criteria.

## #2 — Neutrality is negotiated rather than presumed

The 1953 exchange supplies a sustained disagreement over the scope of the psychic. In **60P**, Zürich, **31 March 1953**, printed pp. 102–110, Pauli accepts the distinction between what is ascertainable here and now and what is not ascertainable in itself. He nevertheless argues that ordering concepts must exceed immediate ascertainability if different observers are to discuss the same object. At pp. 105–106 he resists calling that entire ordering field psychic. A concept meant to mediate psyche and physis should not acquire one pole's name in advance.

His *homo-usia* proposal remains expressly a working hypothesis. Mathematical ideas seem to him a candidate language because they are applicable in physics without making the object of mathematics identical to the mathematician's psychic experience. At p. 107 he combines this difference with an archetypal reading of number's symbolic significance. The source thus preserves both a strong intuition of relatedness and the distinction needed to formulate it. “Mathematical” does not become a certificate that a proposed psychological relation has already been formalised.

Pauli then compares three languages: physical dream symbolism, theological-metaphysical description and analytical psychology. His example is the Sommerfeld dream, followed by the quaternity dream now developed in the [Pauli egg whole](../../mytheme/worlds/analytical-psychology/pauli-egg-dream/WHOLE.md), which **figures** the passage from spontaneous division through conscious participation to a circle. **Printed p. 107 and footnote 9 identify the first dream as occurring in 1948, the essay “Modern Examples of Background Physics” as June 1948, and the following dream as a path to quaternary wholeness.** Appendix 3, §4, pp. 192–196 gives the seven-picture telling and the four-day mid-March interval. Fierz's Appendix 2 description of an undated surviving typescript remains an archival description; it does not cancel Pauli's retrospective dating witness. No exact day or location for the egg scene is supplied.

At p. 110 the problem moves into relationship. Pauli adapts the three-rings story and locates the sought unity in human relation rather than in a winning conceptual language. His postscript makes the relation among rings and among people the genuine ring around an empty centre. This is Pauli's own symbolic interpretation, with its personal stakes. It gives the dossier an outgoing demand: the proposed language must change how its speakers relate, including how Pauli relates to Jung's psychology and to Jung as a person.

Jung's **61J**, Zürich, **4 May 1953**, pp. 111–117, clarifies his use of psychoid as a speculative approach to neutral language suggesting a nonpsychic essence. He accepts the need for what is not presently ascertainable while preserving the psyche as the medium through which anything becomes known. At pp. 112–113 he distinguishes knowing an object psychically from absorbing the object's existence into psychic substance. He also disputes Pauli's inference from the physical language of his dreams: source-language and interpretation cannot be made identical by profession alone. Their partial agreement retains this disagreement about how a dream is to be read.

## #3 — Number's two offices meet the cost of disciplinary adoption

Pauli's **62P**, Zürich, **27 May 1953**, begins by acknowledging what Jung's clarification has settled. At pp. 118–119 he nonetheless insists that the physical-mathematical terms initially struck him as strange rather than as the natural language of a physicist. Reductive explanations failed to satisfy his experience. His proposed future adoption of psychology by an enlarged physics remains a hypothesis, accompanied by a demanding analogy: the adopting discipline must undergo the more radical change because it is the one being extended. He cites physics's transformation before its conceptual incorporation of chemistry. This is a position in the exchange, not the dossier's claim that psychology has been incorporated into physics.

Jung's **64J**, Küsnacht-Zürich, **24 October 1953**, pp. 125–129, takes up the proposal after a delay he attributes partly to its difficulty. At p. 127 he describes numbers as inwardly available forms and outward quantities, and asks whether they are invented or discovered. He seeks an inventory of their mathematical properties and symbolic amplification. At p. 128 he explicitly rejects classifying psychology inside a general physics on the chemistry model, citing psychic measurement and epistemological difficulties. The p. 127 formulation consequently cannot be cited as a shared declaration that the disciplines have already fused. Number and synchronicity remain two proposed points of contact.

This exchange differentiates at least three offices of number. A mathematical identity has stipulated objects and an exact relation. A quantity occurs in a described or measured physical situation. A number in a dream can carry particular figures, affect and symbolic associations. Pauli states the latter distinction at p. 109; Jung's p. 116 account gives number substance, individual form, meaning and relations to other archetypes. Those psychological assertions retain their speakers and context. Repeating a numeral across the three offices does not establish which relation connects them.

The later **74P**, Zürich, **June 1957**, pp. 158–159, makes this discipline especially concrete. Asked to assess W. M. van Dusen's *Mind in Hyperspace*, Pauli distinguishes metric structure from topology and criticises a use of dimension number without adequate mathematical grounding. His tentative psyche/physis mirror-image proposal occurs inside that criticism. He then offers Jung psychological conjectures about a healing path projected into hyperspaces. The dossier reports those as Pauli's conjectures about the manuscript; it adopts no diagnosis of its author. The passage's consequence is exact: a compelling image of wholeness cannot supply missing mathematics, and a psychological interpretation cannot be made objective cosmology by numbering its stages.

[Arche-Topos](../arguments/A16-Arche-Topos-as-Differential-Field.md) **grounds** the native return with its phase, cover, winding, atlas and attractor operations distinguished. The letters' comparison receives those distinctions without becoming their derivation. Jung's isolated use of an unknown `X` in the correspondence likewise supplies no origin for Taylor's `X/x` relation.
