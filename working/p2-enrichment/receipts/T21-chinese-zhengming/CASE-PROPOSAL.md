## Proposed E4 companion insertion

Proposed explicit anchor: `chinese-rectification-of-names`. Insert within the comparative branch, preserving all other language tasks and the three-term/four-operation field.

*Zhèngmíng* supplies a political case in which a name must sustain the action it authorises. The Ministry of Education dictionary records the correction of names toward correspondence with actuality and cites Analects 13.3. Its separate sense concerning substantive office is a further lexical use, not the hidden meaning of that passage. This is an independently witnessed Chinese semantic field; it contributes no ancestry for English Symbol, Account or Trust.

In the received exchange, Zilu asks what should come first if the Master governs Wei. Rectification initially appears to him remote from practical affairs. The answer makes that apparent remoteness consequential: names condition speech, speech the conduct of affairs, affairs the flourishing of rites and music, and these the fitting administration of punishment. The final exposure falls upon people who can no longer orient their actions. The opening restraint concerning what one does not know and the closing demand that speech be practicable belong to the same argument. A name has not answered merely because an authority has pronounced it. This is the received text's normative political sequence; its representation of government does not establish that the sequence was implemented or empirically verified.

E4's authorial comparison follows the office of the account through this sequence. **Symbol answers to source:** the designation must retain a relation to the conduct or role it presents. **Account does not replace source:** an orderly description of office cannot alone perform its obligations, and the consequences of its administration remain part of what the description must answer for. These operations preserve the political specificity of the source. They do not turn 名 into a word for every token, model or ledger, nor turn the passage into a QL derivation.

**Trust keeps the return route active** adds E4's own requirement: people who must act under a designation need more than a legible command. The relation between declared office, practicable speech and its consequences must remain answerable. Analects 13.15 offers a particular comparison at this point. The pleasure of never being contradicted becomes dangerous where the ruler's words are bad; the passage distinguishes that case from unopposed good counsel and resists the claim that a single sentence mechanically makes or ruins a state. It supplies neither a modern right of dissent nor proof that every stable standard is protected. The comparison locates a failure precisely where an office's speech can no longer meet the correction its consequences require.

**Account re-enters source-field:** in E4, the return must be able to reach the authority and criterion sustaining the name, rather than blaming every failure upon those named. This final operation is Taylor's Argued development. The received political text makes the cost of naming concrete and supplies a warning about unopposed bad counsel; the requirement for an effective route of revision keeps its own authorial standing. Trust here names E4's enacted relation, not a Chinese lexical translation supplied by 13.3.
