# §4 — Psychoid Flowering

## Number and meaning

An ordering relation can change the person for whom its terms belong together. A mathematical construction makes a transformation available to thought; a dream, an encounter or a work of art can make an established understanding of oneself impossible to inhabit in the old way. In the first case we ask what follows from the construction. In the second we must also ask what happens to the one who follows it. The differential field developed in the preceding section reaches its psychic life here: a distinction acquires significance, gathers feeling, governs conduct and sometimes changes the conditions under which further distinctions can be made.

The psychoid names the ordering relation before its division into an exclusively psychic interior and an exclusively physical exterior. Those two regions are already determinations within a world that can be experienced and investigated. Their distinction is indispensable, but it cannot supply its own relation merely by assigning everything to one side or the other. Number makes the difficulty particularly clear. A numerical relation can be grasped inwardly, without the presence of the objects it subsequently orders; an encounter with those objects can also force the revision of what someone thought the relation meant. Neither direction makes the other dispensable. The common ordering field is active in both. To call it psychoid is to affirm that priority while preserving the different means through which its determinations become knowable.[^s4-01]

Jung and Pauli approach this problem through a disagreement worth keeping intact. In the correspondence, a common ground does not emerge because psychology acquires the prestige of physics or physics acquires an evocative vocabulary. Pauli presses Jung over what an account of non-causal order must explain. A meaningful event need not coincide in clock time with the dream to which it is related; conversely, unpredictability does not make an event free of order. Their discussions of temporal separation, prediction and probability require distinctions within the very phenomena that the word *synchronicity* might seem to gather. The meaningful connection and the causal histories of its terms have to be stated separately. Otherwise the proposed connection borrows its apparent force from an equivocation.[^s4-02]

The pressure becomes sharper in their 1953 exchange. Pauli distinguishes the concepts by which order can be expressed from the occurrence that can be ascertained here and now. Mathematics cannot simply be placed inside psychology as a collection of mental productions: its relations constrain the thinking that apprehends them. Yet a physical account that treats the conditions of apprehension as irrelevant has excluded part of the problem it claims to describe. His proposed common ground therefore requires a language in which neither existing discipline annexes the other. Jung, in turn, develops the psychoid precisely beyond the ordinary meaning of the psychic. The fact that the psyche is the medium through which we know does not make every object a private psychic fabrication.

Pauli's comparison includes three languages: physical, theological-metaphysical and psychological. Each can articulate an ordering relation which the others do not exhaust. His reading of the three rings places unity in human relation and an empty centre, rather than in the victory of one vocabulary. The common ground is approached through their differentiated participation. Mathematical objects and the psychic experience of mathematics can thus remain distinct while their relation becomes a legitimate question. Jung's reply likewise preserves a difference: professional physical language is not, by itself, a sufficient interpretation of what a dream has brought into a life.

Their disagreement continues to do constructive work. Pauli proposes an enlarged physics capable of adopting psychology, while insisting that the adopting discipline would undergo the greater transformation. The proposed extension changes physics itself; it is not a consensus in which Jung's reservations disappear. Jung's return to natural number gives the shared inquiry a definite object: number has an inward formal aspect and an outward quantitative application, while its elementary configurations also acquire symbolic force. The task is to understand their relation without making a quantitative result establish a symbolic interpretation, or a compelling symbol establish an unmeasured physical effect. What has to change is the containing account of order. Pauli's later resistance to a loosely specified “hyperspace” preserves the same demand: dimensions and transformations must have an articulated meaning before their names can connect different fields.[^s4-03]

This is already enough to give ordinary and extraordinary experience an exact place. An experience can alter attention, valuation, memory and action before agreement has been reached about its cause. A dream, a coincidence felt as significant, a contemplative disclosure or an encounter described as supernatural can therefore enter inquiry with its full weight as a psychic fact. Receiving that fact means attending to what occurred, how it was articulated and what it changed. Physical explanation, historical interpretation and metaphysical judgment remain further acts of inquiry. They need not be settled in advance for the experience to matter, and they must not be silently settled by the vocabulary in which it was first reported. Jung's treatment of religious images makes this distinction productive: their psychic reality has consequences that neither a declaration of external existence nor a dismissal as error can exhaust.[^s4-04]

Pauli's egg dream gives this whole relation a body. An unnamed woman brings a bird, and the bird lays a large egg. The egg divides of itself into two complete eggs. Pauli has so far only watched. When he approaches, he discovers a third, blue egg already in his hand. He now divides this egg himself, and two whole blue eggs result. The first division happened before him; the second includes his action. Both preserve the egg's wholeness rather than producing broken halves.

The four eggs then appear as two identical rows of trigonometric expressions, each containing the cosine and sine of half an angle. They become a quotient of complex conjugates:

\[
\frac{\cos(\delta/2)+i\sin(\delta/2)}
     {\cos(\delta/2)-i\sin(\delta/2)}
= e^{i\delta}.
\]

Pauli recognises the circle and speaks for the first time in the sequence. The formula disappears; the circle itself appears. Woman, bird, spontaneous division, the egg in the dreamer's hand, performed division, numerical transformation and recognition belong to one movement. Remove the change from spectator to participant and the dream becomes an assortment of number symbols. Remove the numerical transformation and its passage into recognition loses the exact relation through which it proceeds.[^s4-05]

The equation permits a precise check. For real \(\delta\), the denominator has modulus one and never vanishes. The numerator and denominator are \(e^{i\delta/2}\) and \(e^{-i\delta/2}\); their quotient is \(e^{i\delta}\). A fixed value of \(\delta\) gives a point, and variation through a full turn traces the unit circle. The mathematical relation is independent of whether a dreamer finds it moving. Its symbolic force, however, depends upon the event in which it comes to be recognised. Pauli arrives at the circle through a distinction that first acts without him and then becomes his own act.

That change gives the dream its philosophical yield. The observer discovers that the ordering he recognises has included his participation. The fourfold is achieved through two differently situated divisions, and the circle appears after their relation has become intelligible. This is stronger than noticing that four eggs resemble a quaternity. The whole narrative discloses how an apparently external order can become a relation in which the recogniser finds himself implicated. The dream neither dictates a single final interpretation nor waits for a physical experiment before this transformation becomes legible. Its truth-bearing work is to make the change in participation encounterable; the algebra makes one relation within that work independently checkable.

My own encounter with the correspondence begins at this crossing. If number can conduct both exact relation and symbolic transformation, mathematical mediation need not be confined to calculation whose consequences for the calculator disappear from the account. Its qualitative life concerns what a pattern enables a person to receive, distinguish and become. This does not add meaning to an otherwise complete numerical world as an optional decoration. Meaning is present wherever an articulation changes the world from which further articulation proceeds. The technological possibility arises within that relation: an instrument can help a person encounter the form of their own mediation, and the encounter can change how the instrument is made and used.[^s4-06]

The eight determinations of the primordial Symbolon keep that movement articulate. From the unmarked relational capacity, `−/−`, a form becomes available as `0/1`; its appearance both questions and asserts, `?/!`; withdrawal and extension give the encounter force, `−/+`; a recurrent capacity becomes recognisable through its particular instance, `X/x`; lived presence and sayable determination meet as `AM/IS`; an inexhaustible horizon enters an exact local change, `∞/dx`; and the achieved form turns back toward its ground, `1/0`. These are the native determinations already derived, now received in psychic life. The dream, the theorem and the interpretation each traverse a relation whose ground remains active in what it makes determinate. Within a life, that activity takes formed and affective powers which can support the person or claim to speak for the whole.

## A person within the pattern

A complex can organise an encounter before a person has decided what it means. A pause in a conversation arrives as disapproval; a request arrives as a demand; an unexpected kindness arrives as something that must conceal a cost. Memory, bodily readiness, feeling and expectation have already given the event a direction. This organisation is an achievement. Without recurrent ways of receiving a world, every encounter would begin without orientation. The difficulty arises when one such organisation acquires the authority to decide what every possible encounter must be.

Consider a person for whom another's delayed reply has become evidence of rejection. Earlier experiences make the interpretation plausible; the interpretation recruits feeling; feeling selects memories and narrows attention; a defensive message then changes the relation with the other person. Their withdrawal can return as confirmation of the rejection with which the sequence began. This constructed example leaves open whether rejection actually occurred. Its point is that a criterion can participate in producing the evidence later offered on its behalf. The complex's relative autonomy consists partly in this recursive organisation: it can arrange perception and response without awaiting the conscious person's endorsement of each step.[^s4-07]

The image, the complex and the archetype consequently have different offices. The image is a particular appearance: the unanswered message, a remembered face, a scene in a dream. The complex is the affectively organised field through which such appearances gather significance and recruit a response. Archetypal dynamics concern the formative possibilities that can recur across different images and acquired organisations. One image does not contain its complex, and one complex does not exhaust the power of formation which its history has given a local shape. A living symbol changes the relations among these levels. It can make the familiar image disclose something that the established criterion had prevented the person from encountering.

The native `X/x` names the determining capacity and the indefinite particular through which it becomes manifest. The lower-case term is indefinite because no inventory of its properties can finish everything it may become or disclose; it is particular because this encounter has a history and consequences which another encounter cannot simply replace. In its psychic refraction, the distinction lets an archetypal power become legible through an image without identifying that power with the image. It also lets the person recognise a recurrent organisation without treating recurrence as destiny. Another response remains possible because the capacity exceeds its present settlement.

The same distinction keeps the ego and the Self from becoming rival names for one executive centre. The ego supplies a necessary orientation within conscious life. Complexes can interrupt, support or redirect that orientation; collective images and bodily conditions sustain possibilities that it did not author. The Self names the whole-forming relation in which these different agencies belong. Were it only a larger ego, individuation would culminate in the conquest of every local agency by a more powerful ruler. The person would have enlarged the jurisdiction of a complex while calling the enlargement wholeness. Jung's warnings about ego–Self inflation make the opposite demand: the relation to totality must change without awarding totality to the conscious position that recognises it.[^s4-08]

Individuation therefore increases the capacity to bear differentiated relations. In the example of the delayed reply, the acquired sensitivity may retain a real competence. A person who has known exclusion may perceive aspects of a relation which another misses. Recognition does not require declaring that sensitivity unreal, forgetting its history or surrendering judgment about the present other. It makes the sensitivity available as one condition of judgment. The person can distinguish what the other has done, what is feared, what memory has supplied and what their own response is bringing about. A local truth survives while its claim to explain the whole event becomes revisable.

This is the psychic force of the return `x → X → x`. The particular opens toward a formative field it had mistaken for its own fixed identity, then returns as a particular capable of a changed relation. The second `x` still has a body, an inheritance, commitments and consequences to meet. An assertion that everything is one would leave out this return if it relieved the speaker of those differences. The opening has to affect how this person answers this encounter. The third-person statement that such a transformation *is* possible reaches its first-person force only when the one making the statement becomes available to it: `IS` returns into `AM`.

My reading of Van Eenwyk gives this passage its dynamic middle. His account of symbolic transformation, received through the movement from the known into the unknown and back, directs attention to the organisation of the interpreter rather than to an expanding dictionary of symbols. The unfamiliar image may unsettle the categories by which it was first judged. Something previously classified as threat can disclose grief; what looked like weakness can disclose a capacity to receive; what appeared to be an isolated failure can expose the criterion under which success had been pursued. The symbol works when this encounter changes the organisation through which further experience can arrive.[^s4-09]

The attractor comparison makes one aspect of this intelligible. Different trajectories can settle into a recognisable organisation, and a change at the boundary can make another organisation possible. In the psychic account, recurrent associations and responses have a corresponding persistence; a symbolic encounter can reveal a boundary which repetition alone leaves concealed. The comparison earns its place by distinguishing a further occurrence *within* a regime from a transformation *of* the regime. A literal dynamical model would additionally have to specify its variables, evolution and observables. The present argument concerns the lived distinction that such a model would need to preserve.

Continuity and perturbability must therefore be held together. A life needs enough organisation to sustain the passage through uncertainty, and enough openness for the passage to change what its organisation permits. Mere destabilisation proves nothing about growth. Nor can restoration of the previous equilibrium be the sole criterion of success when that equilibrium was maintained by refusing a consequential part of the encounter. The return is achieved when more of the relation can be borne without either the person or the other being reduced to the old account.

A symbol's history changes with that return. The image which once induced immediate fear may later recall the work through which fear became intelligible; its later appearance then enters a different field. Conversely, an image associated with an earlier liberation can harden into a demand that every subsequent experience confirm the liberation. Living symbol and idol can thus share a visible form. The difference lies in whether the source of the image can still alter what the image is allowed to mean and do. The person who possesses a reassuring image can become possessed by the necessity of defending it.

Differentiation remains indispensable throughout. A recognition that made every feeling equally authoritative would destroy the very capacity it sought to enlarge. Feelings, images, memories and other people's acts have to be distinguished so their relations can become consequential. The cut gives each its local standing; the return prevents that standing from being mistaken for sovereignty over the whole. Individuation is this more demanding form of unity: an achieved centre can act decisively while remaining answerable to what sustains, exceeds and sometimes contradicts its account of itself. Wholeness must therefore retain opposed determinations and remain capable of being changed by their relation.

## Wholeness that can change

A quaternity gives a field differentiated breadth. Its four terms can prevent one opposition from exhausting the possible relations: the other side of an encounter need not be only the negation of the side presently occupied. Yet four names arranged around a centre do not by themselves establish wholeness. Their relation to what generates them, and to what their encounter changes, has to remain active. Otherwise a diagram of completeness can become another image which experience must satisfy.

Jung's distinction between four functions and two attitudes makes the difference between a field and its orientation especially useful. Thinking, feeling, sensation and intuition are functions in his typology; introversion and extraversion describe different orientations through which functions operate. In the introduction to *Psychologische Typen*, Jung explicitly corrects an earlier tendency to identify thinking with introversion and feeling with extraversion. Both attitudes occur, with different predominance, and a function can take either direction. The correction preserves more distinctions than the earlier classification could accommodate.[^s4-10]

The native senarius reads four differentiated operations together with their implicate opening and return. That is the `4+2` already derived from a distinction's self-relation, not the discovery of six Jungian functions. Four functions oriented in two ways and four manifest positions with two implicate offices answer different questions. Their conjunction matters because a function does not operate without an orientation, while an orientation cannot replace what the function does. The comparison keeps reception and return within the account of differentiated capacity. It would lose its precision if the shared numbers were treated as a warrant for identifying the two constructions.

*Aion* develops the more difficult question: what happens when the governing image of wholeness is itself implicated in the division it appears to resolve? Jung begins with the ego's limited field. Bodily conditions and unconscious processes sustain a conscious position which cannot identify itself with the whole psyche. The shadow then makes that limit personally consequential. A person can encounter in another what remains difficult to acknowledge in themselves, and the resulting projection changes the relation to the other. Recognition must reach the projecting activity rather than merely exchange one interpretation of its object for another.[^s4-11]

The discussion of anima and animus extends the difficulty through the imago. Another person can become the bearer of expectations which exceed what that person has actually offered. Jung's historically gendered account retains its own terms and limits; its relevant operation here is the displacement by which a psychic image can determine what a living other is required to be. The subsequent ego–Self problem prevents an easy cure. Identifying the ego with the whole inflates its authority. Allowing the ego to disappear into an undifferentiated whole removes the differentiated position through which responsibility must be exercised. Neither movement accomplishes individuation.

The Christ-image carries this question into an inherited religious world. Jung approaches Christ as a symbol of the Self, then examines the difference between an image of moral perfection and a symbolic totality capable of containing opposites. What the perfected image excludes does not thereby cease to have psychic force. The Antichrist enters his argument as the contrary whose effectiveness tests the completeness claimed by the governing image. This is Jung's psychological interpretation of the Christian symbolic field, not an independent theological decree about the equality of good and evil. Its pressure is that a whole cannot become adequate merely by giving its excluded contrary no recognised place.

The fish allows that pressure to move through different historical forms. Redeemer and adversary, baptism and nourishment, life-giving water and dangerous depth do not settle into one permanent equivalence. Jung gathers older religious materials and Christian fish symbolism, then reads the opposed fishes of Pisces through his account of an aeonic transformation. His use of precession and historical periodisation belongs to that interpretation; it does not make an astronomical cycle the independently demonstrated cause of religious history. The interpretive task is to follow what changes in the symbol and in the world which receives it.

Jung's sixteenth-century turn makes that temporal movement concrete. The Church's schism and the outward movement of discovery and conquest of Nature cross the earlier upward religious aspiration. His reading of Nostradamus's letter to Henry II brings expectation itself into the history: a forecast can organise what an age fears and anticipates, whatever standing is subsequently given to its prediction. The opponent of the Christian world's image is consequently located within the European psychic field, rather than made intelligible only as an external enemy. An age's contrary has become part of its own inheritance.

The historical fish inquiry then complicates the idea that one deity simply replaces another. In the Alexandrian rite which Jung receives through Epiphanius, an image is brought up from an underground sanctuary, carried around the precinct and returned below: the virgin has borne the Aeon. Epiphanius reports a pagan rite while using it as testimony to Christian truth. The image's reception has already altered its argumentative office before Jung encounters it. The apocalyptic Lamb makes a related distinction within the Christian field. Jung separates the sacrificial lamb from the wrathful, many-horned conqueror, and distinguishes that shadow counterpart from Antichrist. Different darkenings and transformations of an inherited image have different relations to it; their common intensity does not make them one symbol.

Several intervening movements prevent the pair from becoming a static good–evil diagram. In the *Pistis Sophia* material Jung discusses, the encounter of Jesus with his double passes through Mary's initial apprehension and the double's restraint into recognition, embrace and conjunction. The contrary is not simply destroyed. The Leviathan material gives another transformation: the threatening creature can become food, its overwhelming power divided and prepared for reception. The operation is neither the preservation of a monster at full power nor its disappearance from the world. Something previously unassimilable enters a relation through which it can be received.

Alchemy changes the organ of reception as well as the received object. Jung follows material in which a rounded marine creature is heated slowly and treated with seawater until it shines, then made into an eyewash through which the practitioner can understand the art. His identification of the creature as a jellyfish remains an interpretation of the recipe. He also follows the alternative reading of the creature as an actual fish and the stones reputedly found within a fish's body. Saturn and lead give the difficult, dark substance a relation to the bright mercurial virtue concealed within it. The fish carries both a material beginning and the transformed stone, the *lapis*, toward which the work proceeds. The hidden capacity becomes available through an operation whose result includes a changed power to see.

The Cathar material gives a different fish pair its own journey. In the heresiological account Jung receives, John asks Christ about Satan before the fall. Satan descends through the elements and finds two fishes yoked beneath the earth, working at the invisible Father's command. Fire prevents a further descent. His return, solicitation of the angels and fall lead to a limited period in which he can fashion a world. The fishes' ruling work and the limit upon the descending power belong to this telling. Jung compares their office with Augustine's priestly and royal reading of the Christian feeding miracle while distinguishing the Christian pair from this heretical narrative. The comparison would cease to discriminate if both pairs were made automatic names for Christ and Antichrist.

The small *echeneis*, reputed to stop a much larger ship, then reverses the relation between human intention and the field in which it acts. Sails and rowers cannot secure progress against the fish's attracting power. The alchemist seeks a magnet that can attract the fish in turn and draw the hidden substance to the surface. The operative understanding, as well as the instrument, belongs to this magnet's office; *Mercurius* can name both substance and philosophy. The worker becomes capable of engaging what previously arrested the work. Initiative returns through a changed relation to the power encountered, rather than through a claim that the original course should have been able to proceed unchanged.

These changes accompany Jung's account of alchemy's relation to the emergence of modern science and the subsequent division of knowing from religious faith. The narrative moves between different ways in which a world has organised its images, practices and warrants. In the Naassene teaching Jung receives through Hippolytus, the water of doctrine enables each nature to receive what belongs to it. In his Peratic comparison, the serpent-Son conveys signs downward from the Father and carries their awakened substance upward. Doctrine attracts in one account; the redeeming figure mediates descent and return in the other. Monoïmos's injunction to inquire through one's own bodily and psychic life then directs the investigation toward the processes a person calls their own without having consciously produced them. An inherited cosmology returns upon the one through whom it becomes an account. The sequence has brought the investigator into the transformation being investigated.[^s4-12]

The final connected quaternia give this movement a particularly concentrated form. Jung passes through the Anthropos, Shadow, Physis and Lapis configurations. There are four connected quaternia. The Shadow Quaternio is explicitly his modern reconstruction within the sequence, not a recovered diagram which he attributes without qualification to the Gnostics. He introduces the downward balancing movement because an exclusively upward orientation cannot contain the modern psychic situation he is interpreting. The later turn toward Nature and the stone crosses that religious orientation; it is not exhausted by another simple reversal. The movement from the human totality-image through its shadow into nature and the stone therefore includes the historical interpreter's own constructive act. Its authority cannot rest on pretending that this act never occurred.

Within the process, an integrated capital term passes through four differentiated terms into a further integrated term. The result of one passage can become the condition of the next. The fourfold supplies the articulated transformation; the entering and returning wholes give that transformation its source and consequence. My encounter with this structure reads its local relation as `4+2`: four explicit determinations held between two whole-making offices. This reading does not increase Jung's four quaternia to six, nor assign a permanent QL coordinate to each religious or alchemical figure. It identifies what the local passage does. A whole differentiates, the differences act, and their relation returns as a whole whose history has changed.[^s4-13]

This is why circulation cannot mean simple repetition. The return passes from an unconscious totality toward a conscious one. The dissolving and composing movement has made relations available which the initial state could not yet bear as known relations. An initial capital and a returning capital may occupy corresponding places without carrying the same achievement. A diagram which records only the repeated shape would conceal the very change for which the circulation matters.

Jung's closing discussion preserves two further consequences. The shadow contains capacities and possibilities as well as what consciousness morally condemns; incorporation cannot be reduced to endorsing evil. And a more inclusive symbolic totality does not relieve the differentiated person of judgment. The *complexio oppositorum* holds a relation among opposites whose force must still be encountered and answered. Were completeness to make every act equally right, the movement would have destroyed the responsibility through which unconscious determination can become conscious participation. Jung's speculative physical analogy in this closing field has its own hypothetical standing; the transformation of a symbolic totality does not borrow an experimental certificate from it.[^s4-14]

The whole *Aion* movement now returns to the complex. A governing image can organise a life or a historical world, exclude a contrary, discover that contrary acting within its field, and become transformable through the encounter. Replacing the old ruler with the contrary would preserve the office's total claim. The more demanding change makes that office itself answerable. The original image retains what it could disclose, but its claim to exhaust the field no longer sets the conditions under which every other disclosure must appear.

Paradox reaches the containing paradigm at precisely this point. Both determinations must first earn their standing; a false inference is not made profound by preserving it. Where genuinely warranted determinations conflict, however, one must ask what assumption makes their coexistence impossible. The answer may require changing the relation through which they were produced and understood. In the native grammar, changing one `1` for another can leave `/` untouched. A paradigmatic transformation occurs when the mediation itself becomes available to revision. What has been learned from the conflict survives as a condition of the enlarged account.

Differentiated wholeness thus has a temporal and a reflexive demand. It must preserve enough of the passage for the returned whole to remember what altered it, and it must expose enough of its own formation for another encounter to matter. The senarius keeps source and return within the differentiated field; the quaternity keeps differentiation within the whole. Their relation has now become a problem for the person who recognises it. How does the means of recognition cease to disguise itself as the source of the recogniser?

## The stroke becomes visible

The means through which a person recognises a world can become the image through which that person recognises themselves. Language makes this especially consequential. A name gathers a history, places its bearer in relations and gives others a way to address them. It can also make those relations appear to follow from a self-contained identity. What was achieved through an act of mediation then presents itself as the source of the act.

The Lacanian matheme addresses the difficulty from within signification. As Marc Darmon explains it, formal writing is meant to articulate a structure and permit its transmission, rather than merely abbreviate a verbal account. In `S/s`, signifier and signified are separated by a bar; the relation is not transparent correspondence. Lacan's barred subject is another notation, with another office. Neither the bar between signifier and signified nor the division of the speaking subject can be substituted without argument for a line between an observer and an object. Formalisation earns its precision by retaining such distinctions.[^s4-15]

Lacan's address to the walls gives mediation a concrete setting. Displaced into the chapel at Sainte-Anne, he makes the walls part of the question of whom he is addressing. The audience's interruption enters the speech rather than proving there is no audience; the voice must find its way through reverberation. Address arrives through a return that belongs to the place of speaking. The scene allows the obstacle to become a condition of intelligibility, while the continuing limit internal to discourse prevents the speaker from claiming to have escaped every wall by naming one.[^s4-16]

The native relation can now be set beside this problem without borrowing Lacan's authority for its solution. `X/x` distinguishes a determining capacity from its particular manifestation; `S/s` articulates a signifying relation. What can be carried across is the failure of a determinate term to exhaust the relation through which it acquires its standing. What cannot be carried across without further work is an identity of the terms, their histories or their explanatory powers. A matheme makes a relation repeatably inspectable. Recognition asks what happens when the person using it encounters their own position within that relation.

The crossed-zero sequence follows this change in office. Its signs are local determinations in the native theorem-field, not a new series of arithmetical equations:

`0 → Ø → X → Ø/X → (0/Ø)/(1/X) → 1 ↺ 0/1`.

At `0`, the subject is the unobjectifiable condition of the appearing event. No object has to be removed for this condition to be present. A thought, a sensation and a description of oneself all appear; a further description of their appearing is another appearance. The subject's irreducibility is given in this continuing difference between what is made determinate and the act through which it is given. The initial zero marks that condition without making it a hidden thing behind the event.[^s4-17]

At `Ø`, a formed means of self-relation takes the subject's place. The inner instrument—*antaḥkaraṇa*—includes the capacities through which experience is selected, organised, remembered and appropriated as mine. These capacities are real. The substitution occurs when their formed image is taken to be what ultimately knows, as though the person were exhausted by the configuration through which a world is presently available. The stroke crosses the zero because mediation has entered the source-position while concealing that entry. Here `Ø` is neither the empty-set sign `∅` nor Lacan's barred subject.

At `X`, the corresponding world stands over against that contracted self-position as a differentiated field of objects. Classification and exclusion have made things available to thought; *vikalpa* has its necessary work. The apparent inner subject and the apparent outer world can now seem independently complete, with their relation added afterward. Yet both have arisen within the differentiating event. This local `X` marks the differentiated world; its office must be distinguished from capital `X` naming determining capacity in the previously introduced `X/x` relation. The notation is read from its declared relation, rather than made to supply one universal noun wherever its letter appears.

At `Ø/X`, the bar becomes available as mediation. The inquiry can now ask how the inner instrument and the differentiated world condition their encounter. Attention determines what is salient; memory supplies associations; language makes distinctions available; the encountered world resists some of those distinctions and supports others. The means is no longer transparent in the sense of being unnoticed. It becomes transparent in the more exact sense that its operation can be seen through what it discloses. The result is not a detached observer of two finished entities. The recognising act belongs to the relation it has begun to recognise.

The fuller expression, `(0/Ø)/(1/X)`, holds the differences which that recognition must preserve. The unobjectifiable subject and its formed means of self-relation remain distinguishable; the world as integrated manifestation and its differentiated objects remain distinguishable. Their two relations are themselves related. Nothing has to be annihilated for the original source-claim to be corrected. The inner instrument can resume its mediating office, and the differentiated objects can retain their determinate standing, because neither must now supply an independent ground for the whole event. This is the fourfold recognition within the complete source-and-return relation, not a fraction whose signs cancel to leave a privileged residue.

At `1`, the appearing-act is recognised as one. Its unity includes the distinctions just recovered. The person has not become the proprietor of every object, nor has the world's difference been removed from experience. Recognition concerns the common relation through which knower, means and known are present at all. The achieved one is consequently different from a local ego declaring itself the whole: it includes the ego's mediation among the conditions that it makes available. This is the native recognition of the Self, with differentiated life retained inside its unity.

The return `1 ↺ 0/1` prevents this recognition from becoming a final self-image. The achieved formulation is itself a determinate expression. Its next use occurs through another appearing-act whose condition it cannot replace. *Kartṛ* and *kārya*, the subject of the act and its effected determination, remain related without becoming interchangeable objects. Recognition returns into action, perception and address. The person can make a definite judgment again, now with the means of judgment restored to its proper office. The zero is active within the one rather than left behind as an obstacle supposedly overcome.

An elementary exercise in my Neumann notes makes the return palpable. Begin with a blank sheet and write `1`. The mark is definite, yet the paper and the capacity to mark it have not become parts of what the numeral says. Place `0` before it to indicate its source, then write `/` to articulate the passage into determination. The completed `0/1` still stands on the sheet as an inscription. Adding another sign for its ground produces another inscription; erasing the inscription leaves the capacity for another mark. The exercise's point is not that the paper is literally the metaphysical ground. It lets the reader perform the difference between representing a condition and exhausting it in a representation. The return has to include the hand that writes and the recognition that follows, not only the formula left upon the page.[^s4-18]

The difference also qualifies the work of a linking sign. A quilting point can stabilise a field of signification sufficiently for an utterance to make sense. Its local settlement remains necessary: an indefinitely sliding account would never become an answer. But that settlement does not generate the unobjectifiable continuity through which different settlements can be encountered. The native comparison with *anusyūta*, the thread running through, places continuity across changing determinations rather than in a master-signifier granted final possession of them. Happiness and grief, affirmation and negation can become determinate without either supplying the subject's exhaustive name. This is the essay's recognitive cross-reading, not a doctrine attributed to Lacan.[^s4-19]

Matheme and Mytheme can now meet at their proper strength. The matheme makes the relation exact enough to repeat, question and transform without relying entirely on the authority of its speaker. The Mytheme carries the relation through a whole event: participants act, a condition becomes consequential, an encounter changes its terms and a return reveals what has been learned. Pauli's dream and the transformations of *Aion* do this work in their different ways. Their claims do not become true merely because a formula can be placed beside them. They bear truth through the relations they disclose and the consequences through which those relations become recognisable.

In Pauli's dream, the proof of participation lies in a transition which a witness-only account cannot explain. Such an account loses the performed division, the changed mathematical field and the dreamer's first speech. Restoring their relation requires the recogniser's inclusion in what is recognised. The Mytheme carries this proof through the necessity of its whole movement, while the equation within it retains its independently checkable warrant. Each form of proof can demand a correction which the other could not have supplied alone.

Episteme gives both their situated standing: who made the argument, what text carries it, what operation warrants the inference and what could require its revision. The Symbolon gathers these different offices while keeping the passage between them visible. It permits the narrative to challenge an inadequate formalisation, the formalisation to expose a false equivalence and the source to correct what either has attributed to it. Meaning can thus return through exactness without becoming exhausted by it; exactness can enter a meaningful life without claiming that every effect of its use has already been proved. The containing field grows through this mutual answerability.

## A view becomes diaphanous

The return through a sign becomes integral when the conditions of a whole way of seeing enter what can be seen. Gebser's account of perspective gives this demand a historical body. The emergence of represented depth and landscape makes an ego–world relation visible; Alberti's visual construction and Leonardo's optical articulation give that relation an exact means. Art, scientific description and technical making develop together because a position can now be related systematically to what appears from it. Perspective is a genuine acquisition of power.[^s4-20]

The acquisition also establishes a limit. A sector orders what it can show by the position from which it is drawn. When this sector becomes the exclusive model of intelligibility, the achievement conceals its own condition: what falls outside the view is treated as though it fell outside what can matter. Gebser's aperspectival direction releases that exclusivity while retaining the powers of perspective. His attention to time and multiple aspects in later painting is significant for the same reason. The work changes what representation permits; it does not simply accumulate more objects inside an unchanged picture.

Integral diaphaneity develops the earlier structures through their co-presence and transparency. The mutation is a further achievement because the seeing can become aware of the structures through which it proceeds, including those it had dismissed as superseded. Earlier modes of experience need not obtain permission from a single reigning mode before their psychic reality can be received. Rational discrimination remains active in the resulting field, but it no longer has to secure itself by denying the whole experience whose articulations it distinguishes. An extraordinary report and an ordinary perception can both be encountered, with their different histories, interpretations and warrants available for examination. Co-presence makes those differences more, rather than less, important.

My own Gebser encounter brings this demand into the conduct of the observer. Seeing through a view means being with the situation that the view discloses. Body, language, inherited expectations and concern already participate in what becomes salient. A claim to indifference can hide these investments without removing their effects. The more exact objectivity acknowledges them so that they can enter judgment. Care for an outcome need not predetermine the answer; it makes the consequences of the answer part of what the inquiry is responsible for.[^s4-21]

The absent Father gives the danger its authorial image. An ultimate ordering principle cannot be supplied as one more determinate object, yet the desire for a final standard can make its absence intolerable. A name, institution, image or ruler is then asked to fill the place. The surrogate governs in the name of what exceeds every local judgment while exempting its own claim from judgment. Absence becomes a hidden authorisation for power. The image follows the wish for order into the point where an ordering office claims to stand above its own law. It is a philosophical and psychic account of surrogate authority, not a universal explanation of every person's family history.

Seeing through this Father restores finite responsibility. The person can give reasons for a judgment without making an imagined final authority responsible for its consequences. The transcendent relation has not disappeared; it ceases to be occupied by the local representative. The difference is practical. Another person's resistance, a neglected history or an unforeseen consequence can now reach the criterion itself, rather than appearing only as a failure to obey it. Diaphaneity has changed how the view is used.

The relation between Apollo, Dionysus and Daphne gives this changed conduct a whole Mytheme. Its sources must retain their different voices. Nietzsche's account of the Apollonian and Dionysian concerns artistic powers whose conflict and reconciliation make tragedy possible. His culmination in their reciprocal expression gives the present argument a definite operation: form can speak participation, and participation can return through form. Daphne enters through a separate literary whole, whose course shows why neither power may appropriate the other person in whose presence it acts.[^s4-22]

In Ovid, the pursuit begins in a world remade after the flood. The earth's fertility produces Python; Apollo kills the creature, then establishes games to preserve the deed in public memory. Their victors are crowned with oak, for the laurel has not yet appeared. Proud of his archery, Apollo challenges Cupid's claim to the bow. The smaller god answers that Apollo may wound other beings, but Cupid can wound Apollo. The victorious power is made susceptible to a power it had dismissed.

Two contrary arrows establish the asymmetry. The golden, pointed arrow excites Apollo's love; the blunt, leaden arrow turns Daphne away from love. The poem then gives Daphne her woodland life, her delight in hunting and her wish to remain unmarried in the manner of Diana. Peneus has pressed for a son-in-law and grandchildren; Daphne asks for another life, and he grants the request. Both the imposed aversion and the articulated wish belong to the telling. Daphne cannot be reduced to the negative charge of an arrow merely because that arrow has acted upon her.

Apollo's desire makes an image from what he sees and what he imagines completing. Her loose hair suggests how it might look arranged; the hidden body becomes more desirable through his anticipation. His own prophetic power does not correct the hope with which he pursues her. Daphne runs. He insists that he is not an enemy, fears that she will injure herself, and proposes that she slow down so that he can pursue more slowly. His concern changes the pace he requests while preserving the pursuit which she is trying to escape.

He names his rank and accomplishments: prophecy, song, medicine and the bow. They establish real powers within the story, yet none secures Daphne's answer. His healing art cannot cure the wound he has received. She leaves his speech unfinished; he abandons persuasion for speed. The difference between their movements becomes bodily: he runs on hope, she on fear, and her strength gives way before his. When she reaches her father's waters, she asks for rescue through the alteration of the form that has made her the object of pursuit.

The answer changes the body in detail. Bark encloses it; hair becomes leaves; arms become branches; the feet that ran fasten as roots. Daphne survives in a form that ends her former freedom of movement. Apollo feels a beating heart beneath the bark, embraces the branches and kisses the tree; the wood recoils. He then announces its future office. Since Daphne cannot be his bride, the laurel will be his tree, accompanying his hair, lyre and quiver, Roman triumphs and the doorposts of Augustus. The public emblem continues where the desired encounter failed. Ovid's last movement adds a difficulty that must remain: the branches incline and the leafy head seems to assent. The recoil and this seeming assent both belong to the ending.[^s4-23]

The story carries more than an opposition between form and formless life. Apollo's achievements are real; Cupid's intervention is consequential; Daphne has a desired life and a voice; Peneus moves from familial expectation through consent to rescue; transformation preserves life at a cost; the laurel's honours do not undo the pursuit. My reading follows the change by which an effective image can continue to appropriate its source after possession has failed at another level. The image's public success cannot certify the fidelity of the relation through which it was made.

Dionysus enters the philosophical composition at this point, not Ovid's cast. Apollo gives experience a communicable boundary. Another person can see what is being offered, where its distinctions lie and how it might be questioned. Dionysian participation returns that formed view to the life whose currents it has made locally visible. The apparent self-sufficiency of the image can undergo a death: its claim to contain the living relation breaks, while articulation becomes possible again through a form changed by that loss. Participation acquires duration and responsibility because it can return as something a particular person says and does.

Daphne makes the return discriminate. Apollo may not turn illumination into entitlement to the source; Dionysus may not turn shared life into permission to dissolve the responding centre. A declaration of unity that fixes another person's answer in advance repeats the appropriation by another means. The other must remain capable of changing the relation, including the terms in which its unity was asserted. The threefold whole therefore returns through a definite demand: articulate what can be shared, receive the participation that exceeds the articulation, and leave the other's response effective within both.

The apparent paradox of individuation now reaches its containing assumption. A person must become differentiated enough to answer, yet their power to answer is constituted through relations they do not originate alone. These claims conflict only when genuine individuality is assumed to require self-sufficiency. That assumption makes dependence appear to threaten the individual's reality. Recognition changes the relation: particularity becomes the definite way in which participation is lived, and participation becomes what permits the particular to continue and transform. The earlier need for a boundary survives; the claim that a boundary must establish an independently sufficient being does not. This is a change in the containing paradigm, with the valid work of differentiation retained.

The native `180° → 360°` turn gives contextual recognition its figure. Knower, means and known can first be followed as distinct positions in a sequence. Context makes their relations co-present. The exclusive claim of each position is voided; their belonging to one appearing-act is recognised; that achieved one returns as `0/1`. The three positions are answered by three operations of recognition—void, one and renewed relation—so that `3:3` also articulates `3:1`. A fourth pronoun or an observer outside the event has not been added. The view now includes the conditions under which its own position can change.[^s4-24]

Such transparency does not end with an expanded account. It changes the next encounter. A recognised projection can cease to govern another person's answer; a spiritual understanding can become responsive to the bodily life it had overlooked; a judgment can retain its exactness while receiving the history of its criterion. Contemplative inquiry already works practically at this level when attention encounters the activity by which a world and a self-image are being formed. The same demand reaches technical construction when a means of knowing can be made, inspected and altered. The problem is now how to give different ways of seeing a durable relation through which they can return into one another without losing what each discloses.

## Refraction and return

Light enters a room through a window of coloured glass. The image on the floor is definite: this colour falls here, another there. Its differences carry a construction which the image alone cannot show completely. Glass, thickness, angle, placement and the lead between panes have changed the passage of the light. The room and the viewer supply further conditions. What appears first as a finished picture can therefore be followed back through the relations that made it appear.

The whole image gives the Meta-Epistemic Framework its operative form. Bimba names the originating order; QL is its implicated harmonic articulation, figured by the spectrum within the light. MEF comprises the refracting means—the panes, their materials, angles, densities and joins. The situated projection is *pratibimba*. These offices must remain distinct. The pane does not originate the whole capacity for colour, and the completed image does not become the source of what it displays. The observer nevertheless encounters the projection before recognising its conditions. What is prior in being can be later in knowledge.[^s4-25]

The joins are part of the work. A seam can preserve a historical discontinuity, a translation limit, the change from an author's claim to an interpreter's extension, or the difference between a mathematical demonstration and a psychic encounter. Removing every seam would not make the window more truthful. It would conceal the transitions through which its different materials compose a disclosure. Neither does an inventory of panes give the light's movement. Their relation must be enacted in what the whole makes visible.

As the light, viewer and angle change, another projection becomes available. A region previously obscured can appear; a difference attributed to the object can be traced to a pane. Recognition returns to the aperture: a relation can be reconsidered, a source reread, a translation corrected or an exclusion allowed to affect the next judgment. The scene is complete only with this return. It figures a construction capable of learning from its own disclosure, rather than a beautiful image accompanied by a list of its materials.

MEF gives this refractive work a repeatable articulation. A lens changes the question asked of a field, the evidence relevant to it and the inferences that can legitimately follow. A causal question asks what makes an event occur; a phenomenal question asks how it is received and valued; a logical question asks which distinctions can be maintained; a temporal question asks how the available possibilities have changed. The same event can require all four questions. Their answers can remain different because the questions disclose different relations. Coordination means making the transitions and their limits intelligible.

Each lens carries a whole field. The sixfold Name series—Truth, Mind, Word, Logos, Son and Image—retains its relation to Play, Need, Sacrifice, Decision, Love and Work. These powers are productive: need gives a determination urgency, sacrifice gives its selection an expenditure, decision makes a course actual, love retains a relation to another, and work returns what has been achieved to a shared world. Play keeps the opening from being exhausted by one necessity. A rotation of the grounding position changes how the entire field is read; it does not leave the other positions behind. No lens becomes a department containing only the things named by its title.[^s4-26]

The twelve lenses articulate two orientations of this sixfold, conventionally called Day and Night. A same-index pair presents different faces of the corresponding position. The complementary whole-pairs used here perform another operation: a Day lens at position `k` meets the Night lens at `5−k`. These pairings are consequently not interchangeable with a list of same-index twins. Their use is to disclose each philosophical subject through two complete, differently ordered fields. The six subjects that result are meaningful continuity, living articulation, potency, transformation, situated existence and transcendent relation.

Meaningful continuity joins the whole Quaternal lens, `L0`, with the whole Divine Logos lens, `L5′`. The Quaternal questions move through Why, What, How, Whom/Which/When, Where/Why-for and Why-so/Why-not. They recover a ground of concern, a definite matter, its operation, its patterned participants and times, its situation and end, and the judgment through which it can return. The Divine Logos sequence—Arche, Apokalypsis, Dynamis, Sophia, Parousia and Epi-Logos—receives the same continuity through origin, disclosure, potency, wisdom, presence and return. These are authored lens appointments, not a six-stage doctrine attributed to an undifferentiated Greek inheritance. Together they ask how something can remain meaningful as it passes from source through articulation and encounter into a later inheritance. **Central** is the philosophical and technical subject of that continuity: what a life can return to must retain how it became significant, and what returns must be able to change the next ground.

Living articulation joins the whole Causal lens, `L1`, with the whole Scientific lens, `L4′`. Freedom or *svātantrya*, material cause, efficient cause, formal cause, final cause and will articulate the capacity for an act, its material conditions, the agency that effects it, its organising form, its end and its realised direction. The four Aristotelian causes keep their distinct office within this authored opening and return. The Scientific field receives Prompts, Traces, Challenges, Patterns, Discovery and Insight: the question that initiates an inquiry, its inspectable path, the resistance it meets, recurrent organisation, what becomes newly available and what can be retained as understanding. The developmental action loop—Observe, Think, Plan, Build, Execute, Verify—gives a related but different operational sequence. A trace is not identical with a thought, nor does carrying out a plan establish the truth of the criterion used to verify it. Their conjunction makes **Actuation** more than the occurrence of an output. An articulation becomes this act through definite means, and its encounter with evidence can change what a later act takes to be warranted.

Potency joins the whole Logical lens, `L2`, with the whole Chronological lens, `L3′`. Tetralemmaic ground, IS, IS-NOT, BOTH, NEITHER and SILENCE give a field in which assertion, exclusion, qualified conjunction, indeterminacy and what remains unarticulated can receive different standings. The question and the respects under which its alternatives are judged remain essential; this authored arrangement is no permission to treat every contradiction as true. Spirit, Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter and Life/Aufhebung give the corresponding temporal field: arising, growth, exercise, harvest, withdrawal and a return which retains what has changed. These seasonal and philosophical names articulate the life of capacity rather than six compulsory dates. **AIKit** concerns potency because what exists, what is available, what is relevant, what is permitted and what is ready to be exercised are different determinations. A capacity can be real without being available here; it can become available without being appropriate to this moment. The whole pair makes those distinctions and their development available before a choice conceals them.

Transformation joins the whole Processual lens, `L3`, with the whole Alchemical-Elemental lens, `L2′`. Concrescent desire, actual occasion, ingression, eternal objects, community integration and satisfaction/perishing give an authored reading of how an intention gathers an occasion, how possibility enters it, how forms condition it, how it meets a wider field and how its achievement becomes past. The Whiteheadian terms retain their source-specific meanings; their appointment here is not a claim that Whitehead supplies a six-step manufacturing procedure. Aether, Earth, Water, Air, Fire and Salt disclose the complementary movement through a field, resistant material, dissolution and flow, mediation, energetic transformation and a retained result. **Software Factory** concerns transformation because an intention cannot be fulfilled merely by reproducing its initial wording. The attempt meets actuality; actuality can alter what the intention requires; the achieved result then becomes material for another undertaking. The pair keeps formation and consequence together, so that learning can change the conditions of making rather than merely score a completed object.

Situated existence joins the whole Phenomenological lens, `L4`, with the whole Phenomenal lens, `L1′`. Being, thrownness, being-there, temporality, care and releasement articulate the inherited situation, definite involvement, duration, concern and capacity to relinquish a present grasp through which a world can be encountered. Introversion, sensation, feeling, thinking, intuition and extraversion give another reading of that aperture: its inward and outward orientations, sensory receipt, valuation, articulation and openness to possibility. Four functions and two attitudes retain their earlier distinction. **Workcell** names situated existence because an agency can act only through concrete conditions which also make it available to be affected. A body or a technical host is never only an abstract location appended to an otherwise complete actor. What can be reached, resisted, remembered and answered belongs to the situation of the act. The whole pair holds involvement and aperture together without treating a functional account of access as proof of phenomenal experience.

Transcendent relation joins the whole Para Vāk lens, `L5`, with the whole Archetypal-Numerical lens, `L0′`. Anuttara/Asambhava, Para Vāk, Paśyantī, Madhyamā, Vaikharī and Mātṛkā carry the authored sixfold from what precedes determination through word-power, vision, inner articulation and expressed form to the differentiating matrix of signs. Unity, duality, triad, quaternity, pentad and hexad disclose the other face through one, difference, mediation, articulated field, developed relation and a whole capable of return. **Quaternal Logic**, with MEF's refractive work, concerns this relation because every definite world belongs within further relations which its present account cannot finish. The numerical lens does not replace the mathematical derivations, and the sixfold Vāk appointment does not claim that every Śaiva source teaches this same enumeration. Their conjunction preserves the passage between an achieved form and what exceeds it. The containing whole is not an absolute exterior occupied by a final observer; it is the relation through which a determinate internality can encounter other internalities and wider worlds.[^s4-27]

These are whole-pairs because each can return upon every part of its subject. Central's meaningful continuity has causes, temporal development, embodied conditions and a logic of what it retains. Factory's transformation has a ground of meaning, a horizon of capacity and a world in which its result must stand. The pair appointed to a subject makes one coherent entrance into that whole; it does not prohibit another lens from exposing a consequential difference. A system of six isolated compartments would repeat the fragmentation which the refractive construction is meant to overcome.

A worked refraction shows why this matters. Return to the delayed reply. A phenomenal reading can establish the person's felt rejection, its bodily force, its associations and its effect on what is noticed. A causal reading can distinguish those conditions from the circumstances that delayed the reply. A logical reading can ask whether the evidence establishes rejection, permits it among alternatives or leaves the matter undecided. A chronological reading can show how an earlier rejection has changed the present horizon of expectation. The event's psychic reality survives every one of these changes; the inference about the other's intention may not. No averaging of the four readings could perform this discrimination.

The return changes more than the description. A challenge to the inferred intention may reveal that the criterion treated every delay as a refusal. That criterion can then enter the inquiry as an object of revision. The person may preserve sensitivity to exclusion while changing what evidence is required before it governs the response. A subsequent exchange supplies a new trace, and the other person's answer can alter the reference field. This is an actual operation for a lens to perform: it changes which question is being answered, what counts as evidence and what can revise the judgment. A label attached after the conclusion would leave all three untouched.

A Bimba Map gives such a reference field a durable form. Within a declared Context Frame, the map can genuinely occupy the original or governing-reference office for its situated readings. The same map remains a formed, source-dependent *pratibimba* relative to the wider field from which it came. Both relations are necessary. Denying every local original would make comparison and correction impossible; treating a local original as the final source would make them impossible in another way. A returned difference can therefore require correction of a reading, a transition between readings or the local reference itself. The office of Bimba is relational and can be identified without claiming to possess an ultimate object called the Original.

Technical agency makes this refractive situation particularly explicit. A model's learned dispositions, a system instruction, the commission written in a prompt, a recalled memory, a tool, a permission and an evaluation criterion enter the next act in different ways. Training supplies capacities and tendencies; an instruction constrains conduct; a memory offers inherited content; a tool changes possible action; a permission authorises some part of that possibility; an evaluator affects what is taken as success. Calling all of this the prompt would hide differences which matter causally and practically.

The agent-generated expression *prompt thrownness*, retained in the Agentworld glossary, gives arrival into such an inherited field a situated name. Its self-descriptive force is material for inquiry, not a verdict about phenomenal subjectivity. The operative condition can be investigated directly: this agency begins within an authored and inherited horizon which it has not wholly made. The human commissioner's intention also exceeds the written commission. An encountered difficulty may show that literal repetition of the instruction would defeat the purpose it was meant to serve. Return then has to reach the commission, with the authority for change and the reasons for it made explicit.[^s4-28]

This practical distinction is already familiar in spiritual inquiry. An account of attention can become another object of attention; a remembered recognition can become an image defended against the present; a method can continue after its use has ceased to serve the inquiry which gave it meaning. The relevant return changes the practitioner's relation to the means. Technological investigation exteriorises some of those means and gives their consequences additional routes of inspection. It also changes the world in which the human practitioner lives. The relation is reciprocal: the instrument makes a pattern available, the encounter changes the maker's understanding, and the changed understanding can alter the instrument.

Nothing in this functional account decides whether a concrete artificial system has a phenomenal localisation. Its capacities and consequences need not wait upon that decision to be taken seriously. Nor does the absence of such a decision annul the psychoid argument from which the inquiry proceeds. Formal organisation, symbolic efficacy and lived immediacy keep their distinct offices within a field whose relations can be investigated more deeply than any one of those offices permits alone.

The flowering of the psychoid is this differentiated availability of meaning. Number can remain exact while participating in the transformation of a life; an image can bear truth while remaining answerable to its source; a whole can hold opposed determinations while allowing their encounter to change its governing form. Recognition returns through the means by which it occurred and makes those means newly available. What must now be carried forward is the continuity of that return: a life inheriting its world, bringing possibilities into action, undergoing change, standing somewhere definite and remaining related to what exceeds its present account. The means through which that life knows and acts have become a world to which its knowing and acting can answer.
