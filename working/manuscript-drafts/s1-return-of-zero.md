# §1 — The Return of Zero

## A sign migrates between worlds

The means through which a world becomes intelligible can be so familiar that we see straight through them. A numeral offers a small, exact instance. In the inscription 205, the middle character contributes no tens, yet removing it changes two hundred and five into twenty-five. The absence has a place, and the place affects the values on either side. Someone who understands the notation does not have to stop at the zero and interpret its emptiness. They read the number. The sign has succeeded by making its work almost disappear.

Three things cooperate here: a mark, a positional rule, and a reader who can use the rule. The mark alone supplies none of the other two. A circle drawn elsewhere may indicate a boundary, an opening, or the letter O. In this numeral it holds the tens position empty while preserving the relation of hundreds to units. Its meaning belongs to an operation. Once that operation becomes repeatable, an absence can travel on a page without having to be reconstructed afresh from the circumstances of the original reckoning.

Zero's history follows several such changes of permission. A Babylonian place-holder could distinguish an empty internal position within a sexagesimal inscription without possessing all the functions of a free-standing number. A sign for an absent measure in an astronomical table had another bounded task. Indian arithmetic gave zero explicit relations to positive and negative quantities; Arabic mathematical writing and its later European reception carried the numeral and procedures into further practices. These developments are connected, but they are not one invention repeated wherever a similar shape appears. Kaplan's history is most useful when its journey is read through the different work the sign becomes able to do.[^1]

A place-holder answers where an absence belongs. An arithmetic operand answers what happens when an operation meets it. The difference is consequential. One could reserve a blank column without knowing what to do when its value is multiplied, subtracted, or made a divisor. Conversely, a rule can concern a zero quantity even when a different inscription is used to express it. The history has to keep the numeral, the number, and the procedures distinct long enough for their cooperation to become visible.

What changes when a sign acquires an operation is therefore more than the vocabulary of a system. A new permission changes the possible passage from one inscription to another. It becomes possible to calculate through a condition which previously had to be handled outside the written calculation, or by an exceptional instruction. The empty position becomes something a procedure can encounter. Once its treatment is stable enough to teach, the procedure can be entrusted to another reader. A change in notation has become a change in the practical world of thought.

The mathematical world inherited by European modernity was already intercultural. Its capacity to abstract from particular situations depended upon actual languages, manuscripts, teaching, and exchanges through which those abstractions became available. The universality of a valid operation is compatible with the particularity of its arrival. Forgetting the arrival does not make the operation more universal; it makes its acquired powers appear to have no conditions.

The Sanskrit *śūnya*, empty, enters this history with a breadth that a single English noun cannot settle. Something is empty of something, within a situation in which its emptiness matters. Mathematical use gives that word a determinate task. Philosophical uses can ask after the status of the emptiness, the field in which it appears, or the one to whom it appears. Their relation must be sought in what they actually do. Merely repeating “nothing” across disciplines would conceal the very differentiation this history discloses.

The sign's first advent into arithmetic will concern that differentiation: how an empty quantity can have exact effects. Its later return will ask what the effects disclose about the reckoning itself. Between those moments, zero has to acquire a life of its own within mathematics. Otherwise the philosophical return would bring back only what philosophy had placed there in advance.

## Śūnya becomes operational

Adding nothing to seven leaves seven. Taking no groups of seven yields no quantity. The same zero therefore participates in two different operations. In addition, it preserves the other term; in multiplication, it absorbs the difference between terms. Written with a letter standing for any quantity in the familiar number systems, the rules become

\[
a+0=a,\qquad a\cdot0=0.
\]

The first operation lets a quantity remain itself through the encounter. The second sends every quantity to the same result. Six and seventeen remain different numbers even though multiplying either by zero yields zero. Their distinction has ceased to be recoverable from that product. The word *zero* names the common participant; the operation determines what its participation means.

Brahmagupta's rules already give these differences an explicit arithmetic body. In Colebrooke's translation, the relevant verses specify the effects of adding zero to positive and negative quantities and of multiplying them by zero. A reader can follow these instructions with particular quantities and check the resulting sums and products. The empty quantity has acquired an operative place within the calculation. Its relation to the other quantities is specified by the procedure.[^2]

Division makes a further demand. To divide twelve by three is to seek the quantity which, multiplied by three, gives twelve. Four answers because three times four is twelve. The quotient is thus governed by a return test: multiplication must recover what was divided. When zero occupies the divisor's place, the question becomes what quantity, multiplied by zero, could recover the original quantity. The rule of multiplication has already made the difficulty visible. Before one names the answer, one has to determine whether an answer of the required kind can exist.

Bhāskara's treatment retains the exceptional expression. Colebrooke translates the rule: “a quantity, divided by cipher, becomes a fraction the denominator of which is cipher.” Two verses later, the text compares the stated invariance of such a quantity under insertion or extraction with the “infinite and immutable God,” unchanged through the creation and destruction of worlds.[^3] The theological image is present beside the mathematical rule in the primary translated text. It has not been supplied by a modern reader looking for a picturesque correspondence.

The comparison has a definite operation. Finite additions and removals are set against something whose identity is said to remain unchanged through them. The world can be put forth or absorbed without the divine source becoming a larger or smaller quantity. The analogy asks the reader to distinguish the many that change from the unity which their changes do not measure. That is already more precise than a general association between India, infinity, and mystical emptiness.

Its arithmetic nevertheless has to answer an arithmetic question. Under which laws is the zero-denominator expression being retained? Dutta's modern reconstruction presses exactly this point: expressions can remain available for further treatment under restrictions on cancellation and cross-multiplication, rather than receiving every permission of ordinary fractions. This reading is a reconstructive defence of a mathematical practice, not a critical settlement of every Sanskrit witness. Its value here is to require the laws to be stated. Retaining the expression and granting zero an unrestricted multiplicative inverse are different acts.[^4]

The distinction also sharpens the philosophical encounter with Kashmir. The conventional chronology used here places Abhinavagupta around 975–1025; the *Bījagaṇita* is dated to 1150 in the Sanskrit Library's edition metadata. The upper end of that conventional chronology precedes the mathematical text by roughly a century and a quarter. These are not contemporaneous twelfth-century authors, and 1150 is not a date for the first invention of zero. The interval locates a relation between source-distinct developments; it does not establish a line of transmission from Abhinavagupta to Bhāskara.[^5]

Within the Śaiva account, *prakāśa* names the luminosity through which something appears at all. *Vimarśa* names awareness's reflexive power: its appearing is available to itself rather than remaining an inert illumination. Their inseparability permits manifestation to be understood as consciousness's own differentiation. Knower, means of knowing, and known can then be articulated as distinct offices of one act, without the knowing being placed outside the act as an additional object. The finite knower's powers are real powers of determination, although no determination exhausts the awareness through which it is present.[^6]

This gives the mathematical–metaphysical braid its substance. The Śaiva inquiry concerns how differentiation can be internal to an undivided reality. Bhāskara's explicit analogy concerns an invariance through the emission and withdrawal of worlds. The arithmetic asks which transformations a retained expression can undergo while keeping its assigned identity. Their objects and warrants differ; their meeting concerns the relation between an unchanged condition and its changing articulations. Mathematics gives the question a discipline of operations. Metaphysics asks what it means for the condition to be present through those operations without becoming one more quantity among their results.

The braid therefore carries both strands. To make the theological image validate the quotient would remove mathematics' right to test it. To make an arithmetic objection erase the philosophical problem would remove thought's right to ask what that objection has disclosed. In the first case a relation becomes an endorsement; in the second a local failure is made to pronounce on an entire register. The more exact return lets the failed or restricted operation change our understanding of the relation while preserving the different work through which each strand became intelligible.

This precision is itself inherited. A reader begins by relying on signs, a translation, and a practice of checking. Investigation can examine each of these dependencies and discover a mistake in any of them. Yet the examination proceeds through further acts of reading, remembering, and comparing. Trust has already entered the work before the work can give an account of its trustworthiness. The arithmetic does not depend upon the translator's personal authority once its steps have been reconstructed; the historical attribution still depends upon the transmitted text. One result can therefore require two different kinds of answerability.

## The empty set generates one

A different construction begins with a collection which has no members. Set theory writes this empty set as \(\varnothing\). The sign denotes a perfectly definite mathematical object: a set, with no elements belonging to it. Now form a new set whose only member is that empty set. The new collection has one member, because the empty set is something that can itself be collected. The contents of its member remain empty; the containing set does not.

The braces make the change visible:

\[
\varnothing\qquad\longrightarrow\qquad\{\varnothing\}.
\]

On the left there are no members. On the right there is one member, namely the set on the left. Counting the members of the outer set does not require its member to contain anything. Emptiness has been gathered into a new, countable relation.

In the von Neumann construction of the finite ordinals, these two sets serve as zero and one. Two is then the set containing both zero and one; three contains zero, one, and two. Each number collects all its predecessors:

\[
0=\varnothing,\qquad
1=\{0\},\qquad
2=\{0,1\},\qquad
3=\{0,1,2\}.
\]

The rule can now be compressed. Take the set already constructed, keep all its members, and adjoin that set itself as one further member. The union sign, \(\cup\), means gathering the members of the two indicated sets. Accordingly,

\[
n+1=n\cup\{n\}.
\]

Applied to zero, the rule gathers no previous members and then adjoins zero itself: the result is \(\{0\}\), or one. Applied to one, it keeps zero and adjoins one: the result is \(\{0,1\}\), or two. Repeating the operation constructs every finite stage. The prior number remains within what succeeds it, while the successor is genuinely different from it.[^7]

The construction depends upon zero and one being different. It also depends upon a framework in which sets can be formed and the successor operation repeated. Mathematical emptiness is already determinate; the act of gathering is already specified. Their cooperation produces the sequence. Its philosophical force begins with this exact production: a zero becomes the content of a one through an operation whose conditions remain indispensable to the result.

The first one has a remarkable structure. Its entire content is the zero it holds. It is a one by virtue of an inclusion, and the included zero has not had to become a positive heap of members in order to be included. What changes is the level of relation. There is an empty set, and there is the singleton whose member it is. Confusing those levels would make the gain unintelligible. Preserving them allows the gain to be exact.

The same distinction limits the philosophical appropriation of the example. The empty set is an object of thought; the subject to whom the construction appears is not another empty set. Giving the subject the latter's symbol would not establish that they possess the same mathematical properties. What the construction makes available is a disciplined question about the relation between a determination and what it gathers. A mark can make its condition locally articulate without having produced or exhausted every condition of that articulation.

The distinction is already active in reading the formula. We can represent the braces, their rule, the sequence, and our own acts of following it. Those representations enlarge what is available for examination. Each enlargement is also an act through which something becomes available. An account of the act and the occurrence of the act have different offices, even when the account is accurate. The unobjectifiable pole is not an omitted set waiting to be entered into the braces. It is present in the fact that the braces, the omission, and the further account are known at all.

One thus becomes the first symbolic linker: the achieved mark through which a field can be held together and continued. Its authority lies in the relation it performs. The danger will be to treat the mark as though it had supplied the ground of that relation from itself.

## The crossed zero

The native image for that danger is a zero crossed by a stroke: **Ø**. It is distinct from the set-theoretic \(\varnothing\). Here the glyph is asked to show what happens when a medium of determination disappears into the apparent identity of the one who determines. The stroke and the zero arrive as a single character; the reader can cease to see the operation of crossing and see only a finished self.[^8]

Consider the ordinary statement, “I see this.” A body, a language, a history of attention, and the discriminations by which *this* becomes identifiable participate in what is seen. The statement also gathers an I-position: someone sees, from here, in this way. These are positive achievements of the mediating field. They allow an encounter to become a world with recognisable objects and a situated participant.

The occlusion occurs when that participant's representation of itself takes the office of the subject without remainder. “I am the one who sees” becomes “this description contains what the I is.” The learned distinction, remembered identity, social name, or adopted criterion appears to author the very capacity through which it is known. The inner instrument has identified itself as subject. Its operation on the world and its operation on the self become two finished products facing one another, while the activity through which both became determinate withdraws from view.

The crossed zero condenses this withdrawal. The stroke does not fill the zero with the substance of a subject. It covers the distinction between the subject-pole and the instrument's image of that pole. A self-description can then be defended as though a challenge to its terms threatened the fact of being at all. In the same movement, an object can be treated as wholly external to the criterion through which it was disclosed. Both certainties depend upon the mediation which their apparent self-sufficiency conceals.

For example, take a judgment organised by the phrase “a successful person.” The phrase permits distinctions: achievements can be recognised, compared, remembered, and pursued. If its criterion becomes identical with the judging self's worth, a contrary encounter can reach the person only as success or failure under that criterion. Someone's refusal of the measure appears as another failure to satisfy it. What has disappeared is the possibility of returning to the measure itself. The operation continues to discriminate effectively while the terms of discrimination become protected from what they meet.

Making the stroke visible changes the available question. One can ask who taught the criterion, what it selects, what it leaves outside, and which encounter could alter it. The person retains memory, judgment, and the capacity to act. These powers become available as powers rather than as the whole identity of the subject. The return does not require the destruction of the instrument. It requires that the instrument cease to conceal its office.

That is why uncrossing the zero cannot simply mean erasing the stroke. Erasure would remove the determinations through which recognition has become possible. The change lies in the stroke's relation to what it crosses: its operation is recognised as mediation, and the represented self becomes answerable to an experience which it can describe without possessing. The first mark has begun to function as a linker rather than a substitute for its source.

The criterion and its products can now be distinguished without losing what the criterion made visible. A successful representation has become available together with something of the relation through which it succeeds. In arithmetic, an exceptional expression forces a related disclosure: the demand for a result returns attention to the law under which a result can be obtained.

## The frame disclosed at zero

Return to the proposed quotient of one by zero. We are working first in an ordinary field: a number system with addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division by nonzero elements, in which zero and one are distinct. A quotient of one by zero would have to be an element \(q\) satisfying

\[
0\cdot q=1.
\]

The obstruction is not that nobody has yet found a sufficiently unusual \(q\). The distributive law and subtraction already rule it out. Since \(0=0+0\), multiplication gives

\[
0q=(0+0)q=0q+0q.
\]

Subtract \(0q\) from both sides. The result is \(0q=0\). This holds for every element of the field. Requiring the same product to equal one therefore makes zero equal one, contrary to the system's defining distinction. Adding a new element cannot repair the problem while retaining those laws. The proof will apply to the new element as well.

Zero divided by zero fails differently. Every \(q\) satisfies \(0q=0\), so the inverse question provides no unique quotient. For a nonzero numerator there is no solution; for a zero numerator the multiplication equation fails to select one. These are different failures. Calling the first infinity and the second all numbers would not, by itself, supply either expression with a lawful arithmetic operation.

The cancellation error is equally exact. From

\[
6\cdot0=17\cdot0
\]

one cannot cancel zero and infer that six equals seventeen. Multiplication by zero has sent distinct inputs to the same output; the output cannot identify which input occurred. Cancellation would pretend to recover the distinction after the operation had discarded it. Kaplan uses this elementary difficulty in his discussion of Bhāskara. The argument succeeds because the rule is specific, irrespective of any broader interpretation placed upon Indian thought.[^9]

There are productive ways to continue. We could define a new reciprocal function which agrees with the ordinary reciprocal everywhere except at zero, and assign it the value zero there. Call it \(R\):

\[
R(x)=1/x\quad\text{for }x\ne0,\qquad R(0)=0.
\]

The function is now defined at every real number. But the identity \(xR(x)=1\) still holds only for nonzero \(x\); at zero its left side equals zero. We have filled a function's missing value by changing what the function promises. We have not solved the former inverse equation. The distinction between those achievements is the account the continuation owes.

A different continuation enlarges the space. The extended complex plane adjoins a point at infinity and lets reciprocal inversion exchange zero and infinity. Away from those two points the familiar map still sends \(z\) to \(1/z\). The extension makes inversion available across the whole enlarged space, but it does not turn that space into a field in which zero has an ordinary multiplicative inverse. The product \(0\cdot\infty\) cannot be restored to the field calculation above without meeting its contradiction. NIST's account explicitly distinguishes its extended-plane conventions from the restrictions on products involving zero.[^10]

These continuations answer different questions. One assigns an exceptional value to a function; the other changes the space and the interpretation of its exceptional points. The original field calculation remains correct in each case. What changes is the scope and kind of the new operation. Writing the same fraction-shaped expression across the change does not make the change disappear.

Here the immutable gap has its mathematical form. A determinate expression has returned to the conditions of its own determination, and those conditions cannot be supplied as just one more value of the unchanged operation. The demand for an answer has to become an account of what will count as an answer. That account can preserve the original law and leave the quotient undefined, or state a new framework and accept its consequences. It cannot preserve every old permission, introduce the contradictory new one, and call the result a completion.

The gap is immutable in this sense: the difference between a result and the conditions which determine its validity cannot be eliminated by increasing the inventory of results. Its particular mathematical expression can change; the work of declaring and using a frame remains. A new calculus makes more possible because it changes a condition under which possibility was previously determined. Its achievement depends upon keeping that change legible.

The conditions themselves can be written down. Mathematics can investigate its own rules, encode proofs, compare structures, and give rigorous accounts of the transformations between them. None of this is excluded by the argument. What remains distinct is a rule as an object of an account and that rule's operative use in giving the account. Self-description makes the activity available for further examination while remaining an activity through which something is understood. Its success preserves both offices.

This is the same burden that the subjective inquiry has carried from the beginning, now performed through a mathematical obstruction. The finite determination is real; its limit is equally real; the return can transform the containing account while preserving the work already achieved. The field proof establishes its own conclusion. The philosophical argument recognises in that conclusion the distinction between determining conditions and determinate possession. Neither needs the other to counterfeit its warrant.

Zero therefore keeps one foot outside the completed account precisely by being fully active within it. The exception is where the account's own commitments become available for examination. What lay silently in the ordinary operation now has to be said, chosen, or changed. Reason encounters its condition through the exactness of its reasoning.

## The loan returns

A last construction lets the same two written forms, \(0/1\) and \(1/0\), acquire a new operation in plain sight. Place them at the boundaries of an arrangement and form a new pair by adding their upper entries and adding their lower entries. The result is \(1/1\). Repeating the procedure between \(0/1\) and \(1/1\) gives \(1/2\); between \(1/1\) and \(1/0\), it gives \(2/1\).

This operation is called taking a mediant:

\[
\frac{a}{b}\ ,\ \frac{c}{d}
\quad\longmapsto\quad
\frac{a+c}{b+d}.
\]

It differs from adding fractions. The mediant of \(1/3\) and \(1/2\) is \(2/5\), whereas their sum is \(5/6\). The rule also depends upon the written pairs: replacing \(1/2\) by \(2/4\) leaves its numerical value unchanged but changes the mediant with \(1/3\) to \(3/7\). Retaining or specifying the representatives is part of specifying the operation. With positive denominators, the mediant lies between two distinct fractions. Suppose \(a/b<c/d\), so that \(bc-ad>0\). Subtracting the lower fraction from the mediant, and the mediant from the higher fraction, gives

\[
\frac{a+c}{b+d}-\frac ab=\frac{bc-ad}{b(b+d)}>0,
\qquad
\frac cd-\frac{a+c}{b+d}=\frac{bc-ad}{d(b+d)}>0.
\]

Both differences are positive because their numerators and denominators are positive. The intermediate position follows from the operation. The boundary expression \(1/0\) has a different office: it is a formal endpoint, not an ordinary quotient which has suddenly become valid. Its entries participate in the insertion rule without first being evaluated as field division.[^11]

The endpoints can remain in place while further fractions arise between them. Absence and an exceptional boundary have become conditions of a generative arrangement. Their usefulness depends upon a declared operation, exactly as the earlier place-holder depended upon position and the ordinal successor upon gathering. Each construction keeps the term's work bound to a different operation. What recurs is the need to retain the relation through which a mark becomes productive.

The philosophical loan can now return with something it did not possess when it was first taken. Zero has become an exact arithmetic participant; an empty set can be gathered into one; a zero denominator can expose a system's laws; and a retained boundary form can acquire a different, productive use. The mathematical achievements survive the return. Their precision allows the sign to carry a philosophical relation without the relation having to masquerade as an arithmetic result.

In the native notation, \(0\) names the unobjectifiable condition, and \(1\) the definite appearance. The stroke holds their relation. Ground becomes encounterable through a mark; the mark is present through a ground it does not enclose. The notation is

\[
0/1.
\]

The slash now has a declared relational office. It holds the two together while keeping them distinguishable: *and*, because both belong to the one appearing-act; *or*, because their offices can be differentiated and the act can be approached from either side. This AND/OR is not an additional formula in elementary Boolean logic. It names the refusal to obtain unity by erasing difference, or difference by expelling its condition. The mathematical signs have entered an authored operation whose meaning must be followed rather than computed as a fraction.

Reversing the orientation writes

\[
1/0.
\]

The achieved determination turns toward what lets it appear. It can make its conditions increasingly explicit, but it cannot make the ground into a final object which abolishes the act of knowing it. The forward and return expressions are two orientations of one relation. Their arithmetic asymmetry remains intact: \(0/1\) evaluates to zero in an ordinary field, while \(1/0\) has no field value. The native relation retains that asymmetry rather than using symbolic unity to erase it.[^12]

This is the first adequate approach to the Two Ones. One names the unity through which there is a field at all; the other names the whole field as articulated through difference. The first is marked by zero because it cannot take its place as another counted item alongside its manifestations. The second is marked by one because every manifestation arrives as a determination, something distinguishable here. Read through that first distinction, one also opens onto the All: the full field of determinate appearing, expressed in each particular without being reduced to their sum.

The One is present through the All, and the All has its coherence through the One. These are different relations of dependence. Manifestation makes unity encounterable; unity keeps manifestation from becoming a collection of absolutely unrelated substances. Nothing requires an invisible object to stand behind the visible ones or an aggregate to become a subject by growing large enough. A particular life or assertion belongs wholly to the field while remaining a particular which no other can simply occupy.

The determinate one is relational from the beginning. As the singleton held zero without making zero equal to one, a determinate form can bear its source relation without replacing its source. The set construction supplies an exact distinction of levels; the native argument gives that distinction an ontological office. Zero as One and One as All can be affirmed together because their difference has been retained, not because their symbols have been declared interchangeable.

Reckoning now has to reckon with that relation. Latin *ratio* carries counting and calculation into an account which can be rendered, alongside its uses for reasoning and judgment. The lexical range does not prove an ontology. It preserves a task which the operations themselves have made visible: a result is intelligible through what was reckoned and through the terms under which the reckoning was made.[^13]

Ten is ten; whether ten is enough depends upon a unit, a need, and a situation. A count of those present may be accurate while leaving out those affected. Revising the criterion preserves the arithmetic and changes the question it answers. Similarly, two people can bear the same name without being the same person, and one person can carry several names without becoming several people. Naming presupposes a distinction whose bearer has to remain identifiable through the name's use. Counting and naming become answerable when the encounter can reach back into their terms.

This return gives the person counted a place from which to answer. The account can disclose something true and still meet a reply which changes what should have been asked. Increasing the amount of information under the old criterion may then be less adequate than changing the criterion. An account approaches its source more faithfully by allowing such a change than by declaring the source exhausted by what has already been said of it.

Trust carries that examined account into life. The Latin field helps distinguish two moments: *fides*, reliance and credibility already active in receiving another's word; *credere*, entrusting, lending, and believing. Their before-and-after relation here is the essay's construction: reliance makes inquiry possible; inquiry can make renewed entrustment more answerable. It is not a claim that the words emerged in that chronological sequence.[^14]

A returned account can identify who speaks, what was tested, what remains uncertain, and who will answer for a consequence. These are real gains. They leave a commitment to be made. No accumulation of entries can perform another person's act of trusting, just as confidence in an account cannot guarantee the future it concerns. Refusal and withdrawal remain possible where the encountered relation gives reason for them. Trust becomes conscious of its limits without having to turn the limits into an excuse for either credulity or paralysis.

The loan thus returns as a changed capacity to determine. Mathematics has not abandoned zero to a vaguer language; philosophy receives a sign whose acquired operations make its own claims more exact. The first advent gives absence an operative place. An integral return will have to carry that achievement into the conduct of thought, so that a determination's source can remain consequential after the determination has become useful, authoritative, or familiar.

The Greek *symbolon* gives this return a material form. Among its attested senses is a token whose corresponding pieces make later recognition possible.[^15] A present piece bears the shape of a separation. When its counterpart is encountered, the fit can disclose that these two belonged together. A generic resemblance would establish less than the answering fracture; fusion would remove the difference through which the fit is tested. Each piece is definite, yet neither can perform the recognition alone.

Zero and one can now be held as a root *symbolon*: a form which carries a relation while preserving the difference through which it is recognised. The token illustrates that operation; the native argument has earned it through the relation between condition and determination. The next question is what thought does with the difference. It can preserve the common relation while distinguishing its terms, or let the terms' achieved independence conceal their provenance. The difference cannot be escaped. The way it is held can change.
