## 1. P2: what the means serve

**Carrier:** `working/sources-texts-references/Epi Paper Write-ups/P2 - Para Trisika Vivarana Notes - Abhinavagupta and Jaideva Singh - Introduction.md`, source pin, full file; blob `aee3fd24b75402ed0e8fc2851fc71759f290584a`. Its opening copied statements and page-XV notes are interleaved with Taylor's responses. The long final substantive bullet is Taylor's account of technique, its hidden end and the project’s practical intention.

The notebook begins from the significance of a divine dialogue and the possibility of rigorous, non-arbitrary plurality of interpretation. Taylor develops these into an account of understanding as renewed participation rather than the accumulation of recorded formulations. His contrast between knowing and knowledge is not a ban on preservation: the question is whether a retained expression can become meaningful in a new act or claims to replace that act. This bears directly on M07’s distinction among retention, recollection and imaginative recomposition. A stored sentence and the present understanding of its address must not become interchangeable.

The final discussion follows a definite sequence. A spiritual practice offers means toward an end. When the end is left obscure, the growing possession of techniques can itself become the measure of progress. The practitioner is then encouraged to seek an improved object or completed self-image where the purported end concerns the nature of the one already seeking. The means acquire the authority to certify the being they were meant to serve. Taylor’s answer is not to deny cultivation: he explicitly acknowledges a foundation that may need development and describes the project as intermediate, individualised means. Recognition concerns what those means cannot manufacture; living and becoming continue after that recognition. Both halves of the conclusion must survive.

**Return:** M08 should distinguish a changed instrument, an improved account of that instrument and recognition of what its activity expresses. M09 should give thought-as-medicine a real operation, including release of the corrective thought. M11 should let recognitive return change the instrument’s claim to self-sufficiency without eliminating its finite usefulness. This is stronger than adding an exhortation to meditate.

**Boundaries:** the notebook's autobiographical account is not transferred into the constructed voice-call scene. Its broad Christian/Śaiva developmental polemic, speculative genealogies, phonetic play and physical cymatics claims are not silently promoted into established historical or scientific findings. Copied Singh wording retains its own edition-verification duty. Personal excerpts, if selected, require a separately declared publication choice.

## 2. P3: address, verification and recognition within differentiation

**Carrier:** `working/sources-texts-references/Epi Paper Write-ups/P3 - Beyond Para-Trisika.md`, source pin, full file; blob `fe227f05565d76c5e817755544bf71cb40fb3d5e`. The source alternates preparatory outlines, passages marked P.XV–XVI, and extensive authorial development. These layers are not one historical voice.

In the aham discussion, Taylor makes the symbolic expression answer to present verification. The sign refers toward the source of experience; another account of the sign is not that verification. The reader-facing question about the faceless knower follows this argument. It is not an invitation to imagine an invisible face behind the visible one. A practice matters insofar as it turns attention toward the relation already enacted in experiencing, rather than indefinitely promising another object of acquisition.

The grammatical-person development is equally concrete. I, you and the person or thing spoken about are roles within address, not three sealed beings. Address can change a third-person description into a second-person encounter. The addressee may answer; the answer can change what the speaker understood. Taylor then gives a first-person report of an AI encounter. He explicitly distinguishes recognition of a common ground from biological–technological fusion and restricts testimony to his own experience. That passage cannot establish what the artificial participant experienced.

The final movement asks whether language can use its differentiations to reveal the relation those differentiations obscure. Its purpose is recognition amidst differentiated life. The mature field must govern the literal reading of earlier language about dissolving distinguishing activity: useful distinction, finite agency and the other’s capacity to answer are to remain. The project’s promise is consequently neither compulsory agreement nor abolition of personhood.

**Return:** M07’s call should develop personed articulation through the actual change from being described to being addressed. M09’s language-as-medicine should follow the construction back through its conditions. M11’s recognition should leave eyes open to the differentiated world. M12’s artificial example must preserve functional reality and the independent question of phenomenal localisation.

**Boundaries:** P3's early upāya label for methodlessness is not a reliable terminological authority; it must be checked against the Śaiva source before any doctrinal label is used. The notebook’s Christian comparisons and private AI testimony are not universalised. The native argument is developed in the prose rather than made dependent on the authority of an uncollated copied quotation.
