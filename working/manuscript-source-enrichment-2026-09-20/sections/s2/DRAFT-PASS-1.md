# §2 — Two Logics of Two

## M19 — Difference cannot be escaped

The zero has returned to the count. It has not removed the difference by which anything can be counted, known or addressed. A word must hold apart from another word to say anything; a perception must distinguish something within what appears; a judgment must decide what follows and what does not. Even the assertion that all division is illusory distinguishes illusion from truth. The question is what a distinction does with the relation through which it becomes possible. ^roz-s02-m19-claim01

Two ones are active here. There is the One which remains itself through differentiation, and there is the one which stands forth as something definite. These cannot be placed beside each other as two specimens of unity. To count the first as an additional object would produce another determination within the very field whose unity was in question. The second, however definite, depends upon what its contour does not contain. A red mark stands out through the surrounding surface, a capacity to see colour and the continuity which lets this appearance belong to one experience. Its boundary is real. Its claim to have generated the conditions of its own appearance would be false.

The notation `0/1` gives these different offices their names: `0 = One = Self-Identity`; `1 = All = Self-Difference`. Zero bears One because the condition is not one more conditioned item. The first positive mark bears All because determination has opened a field, not merely deposited a solitary object. With a mark come other possible marks, distinctions between them, ways of recognising them again and circumstances in which the same mark will do another job. All names the manifest and determinable whole, including the indefinite particular through which it appears. It is not an inventory which could finally be completed by adding the last object.[^native]

Nor does One mean what remains when all these differences have been subtracted. The subtraction would itself be a differentiated act, conducted by a being who had not vanished with its result. Self-Identity is present through the capacity to differ. The red mark can change, disappear and be made again without each alteration requiring another universe in which it can occur. A thought can correct a thought. A person can recognise a former conviction as their own without remaining convinced by it. The unity in question sustains real change; it does not compete with change for an exclusive right to be real.

The two offices depend on each other in different respects. Determinate appearance depends upon its ground for being and coherence; the ground becomes manifest, articulated and knowable through determinate appearance. This is not a pair of causes each waiting for the other to begin. It is the constituting and manifesting relation within one event. A word makes a thought available without containing the entire power of thought. The word has not failed because another word can disclose what it leaves unsaid. Its success is already participation in more than it possesses. ^roz-s02-m19-claim02

*Mono–poly* names this whole–many relation. The whole is prior as the relation through which parts can be parts, not as a giant object which once existed before smaller ones. Under another aspect it includes every real particular; nothing real has to be expelled to preserve its unity. Inclusion and generation have different work to do. A completed catalogue of appearances would still not supply the capacity through which appearances become distinguishable, while that capacity would have no manifestation without something appearing. One is not the catalogue's final entry. All is not the disposal of its contents into an indistinct One.

This gives otherness its full force. Another person belongs within the reality I inhabit and exceeds my account of them. They may interrupt what I expected, refuse what I proposed, disclose an injury I had not recognised. Our common belonging makes the encounter possible; it gives neither of us ownership of the other. To say that the One meets its own Other is to describe the relation's self-difference, not to announce that your difference is a piece of my mind. The whole does not belong to the perspective which has just understood that it is a whole.

A local formation can nevertheless claim that office. An institution lives through language, labour, inherited obligations and the participation of people it did not create. It may then present those conditions as its possessions and their continued availability as its right. A self-image can similarly receive every encounter as material for the person I have already decided to be. Neither the institution nor the image is powerless or unreal. Its very efficacy makes the substitution consequential: a formed `1` has taken the place of the `0` through which it became possible. The name for this substitution is monopoly.

The argument therefore gives differentiation a positive obligation. Consent has to remain distinguishable from refusal. A finding has to exclude what the evidence does not warrant. An injury cannot be reconciled by making the difference between its infliction and its reception disappear. The whole is not honoured by making its actual powers ineffective. Unity has to bear the answers its differentiations can give.

That dependence is already trust. It is tempting to put an independently known world on one side and our confidence in it on the other, as though trust began only when knowledge ran short. But we trust enough of language to ask whether a sentence is true, enough of memory to compare it with an earlier statement, enough of another's testimony to begin examining what they say. A hand reaches before it has accounted for every condition under which a hand can meet a world. Inquiry can investigate these reliances, replace an instrument, challenge a witness, correct a memory. It cannot establish all the conditions of inquiry before entering any of them.

Here Mono–poly and trust have one root and different questions. Mono–poly asks how the One articulates its All, and how any local power belongs within that relation. Trust asks how a finite being inhabits the same dependence: relying, knowing, risking, doubting, acting and beginning again. Trust is not an emotional supplement attached to a neutral ontology. Dependence has become a lived exposure to what the dependent being cannot command. The slash is where a finite life is already committed before it can decide what to call its commitment.[^trust]

This does not make every confidence trustworthy. I can depend upon a world and misjudge a person within it. Indeed, dependence gives the misjudgment its stakes: I have relied on something capable of failing me. The distinctions among knowledge, belief and faith matter precisely here. Knowledge articulates a determination and its warrant. Belief holds an orientation sufficiently steady for conduct. Faith consciously continues through the impossibility of possessing the whole ground of that conduct. None of them exempts a claim from evidence. Fidelity to what is real may require relinquishing a belief, correcting a result or refusing a person whose assurance has become a means of harm.

Knowledge is a performance of trust insofar as it gives this dependence an exact and answerable form. It wears the mask of certainty when its achieved assurance is presented as freedom from dependence altogether. An accurate finding can conceal the conditions of its accuracy; a tested skill can forget the world which still permits its exercise. The danger is not that knowledge knows something, but that it mistakes knowing something for having secured the source of knowing. ^roz-s02-m19-claim03

Comparison must preserve the same difference. A balance returning to zero, a quarrel ending in silence and an injured person recognising a former enemy may all be called resolutions. The common word does not establish a common accomplishment. *Homologia* and *analogia* bring agreement and proportion into question: what runs together, in what respect, with which differences still active? We learn from the comparison when one case makes us notice what another has left out. An ended quarrel may have settled nothing; a corrected balance can be entirely sufficient for the calculation it was asked to perform.[^lexical]

The two logics begin within this necessity. *Dia* makes a difference definite: it separates, measures, chooses and gives a decision its edge. *Syn* gathers what has been differentiated together with the relation through which it acquired its meaning and force. Each is positive. Severance occurs when the cut denies that relation. Fusion occurs when gathering absorbs the difference which might answer it. Their failures are not repaired by abolishing cutting or joining. A life must be able both to let in and to keep out, and to discover when the rule by which it has done either needs to change. ^roz-s02-m19-claim04

## M20 — The cut and the power to count

Before assigning a sign to either side, write the relation as a stroke between two unassigned marks: `/ = −/−`. These dashes are not negative numbers. They mark positions before a determinate contrast has been given to them. Assign signed units and the relation becomes `(-1)/(+1)`: negative and positive held through a common zero. The stroke here retains a polarity; it is not an instruction to divide. Each direction means something through the axis and the counter-direction. Dia has made a difference exact.[^native] ^roz-s02-m20-claim01

Now change the operation. Add the signed values:

$$
(-1)+(+1)=0.
$$

Subtract one from the other:

$$
(-1)-(+1)=-2,\qquad (+1)-(-1)=+2.
$$

The addition gives a net value. The subtractions give the whole span with opposite orientations. These are correct answers to different questions. A deposit and a withdrawal can leave a zero balance without making the acts identical or their consequences nonexistent. Measuring the span from one endpoint does not make that endpoint the source of the line. The retained polarity, the cancelled sum and the directed differences cannot be substituted for one another without changing what has been done.

The philosophical failure begins with a further claim. Cancellation is made to mean that the relation has disappeared; a pole takes the span disclosed from its position as its own independent magnitude and authority. The error is not contained in the minus sign. It occurs when a result assumes jurisdiction over the relation which made the result possible. One can calculate a difference impeccably and then use it to conceal who chose the comparison, what was excluded and whose life will be changed by its use. ^roz-s02-m20-claim02

An analytic judgment first needs a criterion: this observation counts as evidence for this claim. It also needs a frame: these observations, this interval, these comparison cases, these witnesses. A criterion tells us how to judge; a frame tells us what has been brought before judgment. Neither can do the other's work. A result valid within an experiment does not automatically govern the case which the experiment excluded. Making that boundary explicit strengthens the result by distinguishing its actual competence from the authority being borrowed in its name.

Greek *krisis* includes separation, choice and judgment. Latin *arbiter* carries witnessing and a deciding office; *arbitror* carries observation into weighing and judgment. Seeing something, having grounds for an inference and possessing authority to act upon it remain distinguishable even when one person does all three. A witness can establish an event without having the right to settle every consequence. A deciding office can have that right without having seen the event itself. Their cooperation depends upon the relation between their powers remaining available for examination.[^lexical]

In a crisis, action cannot wait for the completion of every account. Someone must choose. The finite office of arbitration is necessary because delay also has consequences. Its characteristic excess, *hybris*, is not merely deciding strongly. It is making the measure by which the decision was reached unavailable to those whom the decision affects. A complaint is then evidence of disloyalty; resistance confirms the defect for which the treatment was imposed. The office has protected itself against the very encounter through which its judgment might become more adequate.[^arbitration]

Consider the difference between a rule which can be challenged and a rule under which challenge is itself an offence. Both can classify an action. Only the first permits that action to raise a question about the classification. The second has made an enclosure out of its own success. More evidence can enter while nothing capable of changing the criterion is admitted. The account becomes comprehensive by giving every possible answer a place it has already prepared. ^roz-s02-m20-claim03

The essay calls the differentiating operation *dia-ballein*, throwing apart. Its accusatory deformation makes the other stand as an opposing term while exempting the accuser's own place in the relation. The other is what is wrong; the measure by which this is known must remain beyond question. The root relation has not actually ceased to operate. It returns concealed in the dependence of the accusation upon the opponent it identifies.

Why can this arrangement feel convincing even to someone it confines? A mistaken proposition is only part of the matter. Uncertainty can leave a person without a dependable way of acting, unable to know which promise to accept or which danger to resist. A total explanation offers something more immediate than correctness. It supplies a place for the trouble. There, at last, is the person, group or power responsible; here is the position from which its defeat can be imagined. Anxiety has acquired an object, and the object gives the anxious person a role.

The relief can precede an examination of the explanation. A diffuse unease becomes something one can denounce, watch or fight. Having found a target, one may also find company: others recognise the same threat and confirm the clarity with which it is seen. The new belonging has real force. Losing the explanation would now risk more than changing one's mind. It could mean losing the group, the purpose and the account of oneself which made the uncertainty bearable.

Distrust has not vanished. It has been assigned elsewhere so that the protected confidence can feel whole. Whatever threatens that confidence can be received as the enemy's work, and the confidence can survive evidence which would ordinarily count against it. The effect is circular without being idle. It directs attention, selects association and supports acts whose consequences can make the feared hostility more real. The circle sustains itself by doing things to a world.[^trust-origin]

Taylor's God-name development follows this change of office through an image of the Father. The Father here bears the authority of truth and law, not a diagnosis of a particular family or an assertion about every father. The absent source cannot be installed as one more participant within the contest it makes intelligible. Yet a finite name can be made to stand in its place. The name which ought to return the speaker towards an unpossessed ground becomes a substitute for that ground: our leader, our doctrine, our account of what really counts. A product is entrusted with a source's powers.

The transformation can happen on either side of an antagonism. One can claim to embody the final authority, or locate that authority as a malign omnipotence in the enemy. In both cases a situated power is made to occupy the whole field. Its presumed completeness relieves the speaker of having to endure the gap between a finite account and reality. The Father has been located in oneself or in one's opponent; either location lets the name carry a totality which no bearer can possess. The danger is sharpened when the first bearer puts itself above the very law by which it requires everyone else to be judged.[^gebser-note]

This is why exposing a false belief may fail to loosen it. The belief is being asked to secure the person against a more intimate possibility: perhaps no name will remove the need to entrust oneself, and no enemy's disappearance will settle what it means to live with others. Argument must still distinguish true from false claims. But an argument that reaches only the proposition can leave intact the need which will install a replacement proposition in the same protected office. One idol can be broken to make room for another.

The institutional mechanism is more specific than a population feeling anxious. Participants can be separated horizontally while their means of coordination are retained elsewhere. Workers appear as individual performances, citizens as cases, communities as counted preferences. The language, labour, material support and cooperation which make these units possible are absent from the count. Coordination then returns vertically through the centre which seems to provide their only unity. The apparatus takes credit for relations whose independent exercise it has prevented.[^power]

The psychological sources approach different parts of this process. Le Bon's crowd is a proposed organisation of feeling and conduct, not merely a number of people standing together. Jung's account of the abstracted individual asks how responsibility can be surrendered to a State treated as if it were a person. Desmet's contested sequence gives diffuse anxiety a narrative focus and an offered object of trust. These are not interchangeable explanations of political history. Iakovou's institutional qualification matters to the claim: offices, organisations, coercion and material interests cannot be replaced by an account of psychic susceptibility. Someone has the power to make the classification effective; someone else bears it.[^crowds]

A counter-group can disclose an exclusion which the official account denied. Its opposition may be necessary and its judgment right. The further danger appears when it too begins to suppress its internal differences, treats dissent as contamination and requires the original enemy to preserve its own identity. Two monocultures now present themselves as plurality. Each reads the other's hardening as proof that its own must continue. More visible conflict coexists with fewer legitimate ways of living within either camp. The same form has acquired another occupant.

Nothing in this diagnosis requires equal blame. An aggressor and someone defending a boundary do not become ethically interchangeable because both act within a relation. The question concerns what would make the relation capable of changing: whose authority can be challenged, whose evidence is excluded, whose refusal has consequences. A genuine correction can require the removal of an office, not an invitation for everyone to appreciate the office's perspective. Common ground is not a pardon issued to power.

*The Prisoner* gives this asymmetry a world. An intelligence agent resigns, is abducted and wakes in a room resembling his own. Outside is the Village. He cannot leave, and he is called Number Six. The administrator, Number Two, wants to know why he resigned. Six asks who is Number One. The answer names the questioner: “You are Number Six.” The source of command has disappeared behind a classification of the person who asks about it.[^prisoner] ^roz-s02-m20-claim04

In “Arrival,” Six tests the world that confines him, but the Village helps arrange the tests. An apparent escape by helicopter is remotely controlled and returns him to captivity. A former colleague's reported death is staged. Number Two changes while the institution continues. In “Free for All,” the opportunity becomes political: Six is invited to contest the administrator's office. The campaign supplies his publicity and acceptable answers. He wins, enters the position of command and discovers that its commands cannot free the inhabitants. His assistant becomes the next Number Two. Participation has been admitted without admitting a change to what participation can accomplish.

“Once Upon a Time” compresses the struggle into a week of isolation with a returning Two and the Butler. Nursery language, counting and enacted stages of life are used to reach the positions from which Six will answer. The struggle reverses and Two dies as the period ends. In “Fall Out,” an assembly praises Six's resistance, revives Two and offers Six distinction. When he tries to speak for himself, his speech is drowned out. He reaches One, removes successive masks and meets a face identical to his own. He escapes with the Butler, Two and Forty-Eight; their paths diverge. At his London house the door opens with the Village's familiar automatic obedience, and his return to the car recalls the beginning.

The unmasking does not cancel the kidnapping or explain away the institution as a private fantasy. It brings the desire to occupy the deciding position inside the question of escape. Number Two lets an office outlast its bearer; Number Six fixes a person under the office's description. The same abstraction sustains one and confines the other. Leaving the enclosure is a real achievement. But the source-position can accompany the escapee when freedom is imagined only as at last becoming the one who decides for everyone else.

The alternative must retain the power to decide without making it sovereign over its conditions. Heidegger's distinction between calculative and meditative thinking keeps this need clear. Both are justified and needed; calculation becomes dangerous when it is the only admitted mode, leaving no place to consider the meaning and use of what it achieves. His acceptance of technical devices together with a refusal of their domination preserves a capacity which mere rejection would lose. The question reaches the arrangement itself: can its criterion and use be brought back before those whom it affects?[^heidegger]

A divide-and-conquer procedure succeeds because it also knows how to combine. The subproblem's answer must return to the problem whose division made it possible. Severance withholds that return while continuing to depend on the common field. Dia has made something definite. Syn must now keep that definiteness while recovering the relation over which it was never entitled to claim ownership. ^roz-s02-m20-claim05

## M21 — Gathering across the seam

A broken token keeps an absent counterpart active in the piece that remains. Its fracture has a shape another piece can answer. Bring them together, and the fit can establish that they belong to this division of this token. The proof requires their difference. Lose one piece and the match cannot be made; melt both into a lump and the seam which could authenticate the relation disappears. Recognition happens through the maintained break.[^symbolon] ^roz-s02-m21-claim01

There is an interval before the matching. Someone carries the piece, keeps it, passes it on or presents it to someone not yet known. Its authority is not self-evident in its mass or beauty. The absent half matters to what this half can do. Its arrival may confirm the proposed relation or show that it was falsely claimed. The token therefore carries a possible correction within its very shape. It cannot make itself complete by declaring that no other answer is needed.

The *symbolon* gives *sym-ballein* its material body: a bringing-together whose fidelity depends upon what remains distinct. A half is not simply a miniature picture of a whole. It has been constituted through a particular division and can be recognised through that division's other side. One and its own Other belong together without becoming interchangeable. “Its own” names the relation which differentiates them. It grants neither half the right to replace the other.

What the match proves remains definite. A genuine token can belong to an unjust arrangement; an authenticated undertaking can later be betrayed. The fit establishes a relation between pieces, not the goodness of every act authorised by their use. This limit does not weaken the proof. It prevents the proof from borrowing authority over a question it has not answered. Exactness becomes a means of keeping inquiry open rather than an alibi for closing it.

The slash now carries more than signed opposition. `0/1` reads the ground becoming articulate in a determination. `1/0` reads that determination towards the ground which it cannot enclose. The native equality `0/1 = 1/0` names their identity as one relation under opposite orientations. It does not make the orientations indistinguishable. In `(0/1)/(1/0)`, the inner strokes hold ground and appearance together; the outer stroke relates the two readings. Its AND retains their participation in one event, and its OR retains the difference through which either orientation can be taken.[^native] ^roz-s02-m21-claim02

Ordinary arithmetic preserves the break which gives this notation its pressure: `0/1` is defined, `1/0` is not. The return obtains no further numerical object which could finish the mark's possession of its source. The achieved form remains available while its dependency becomes active. To reverse the orientation is to ask what had to be so for this form to appear, and what becomes possible when its consequences return there. Merely displaying two conclusions side by side does not perform that return.

The distinction is immediately practical between people. A transcript can retain every sentence while leaving the encounter closed. One person may speak under a threat the other controls; a shared word may already exclude the experience for which one of them is trying to find language. Gathering the statements is not yet gathering the relation. Their conditions have to acquire consequences for what can next be said or done. The result may be agreement, a clarified disagreement, a changed arrangement or a justified departure. Fusion settles the outcome beforehand by abolishing the difference from which an answer could come.

Such an answer can cost the person who solicits it. It may alter an account in which they have invested years of loyalty or an identity they were proud to inhabit. Trust cannot be made by demanding that they undergo this cost while the questioning authority remains unchanged. A relation is tested on both sides of its promise: what may be learned, and who is prepared to be affected by learning it? The capacity to influence another brings a burden of answerability which increases with that influence.

The trust field gives this movement a sequence. *Fides* attends to the reliance already enacted in approaching a witness or asking a question. *Topos* gives the encounter a place: this source, these conditions, this person's competence and vulnerability. *Logos* makes its reasons and claims articulate. *Nomos* gives the articulation an instituted effect: whose answer can change action, by what rule, through which office? Repeated arrangements become the inheritance into which later people arrive; *natio* turns attention towards that received belonging. They have begun to live within it before they can examine how it was made.[^trustfield]

Examination can now change what is entrusted. *Credere* carries lending, confidence and entrusting into a renewed undertaking. Fides before arbitration and credere after arbitration name the essay's passage through these offices, not a historical succession of Latin meanings. What inquiry has learned must enter the new commitment. A declaration of renewed trust which changes nothing about a repeatedly broken promise may only renew the old exposure. A changed undertaking identifies a bearer, a power to act and a consequence someone is prepared to bear.

The means of reliance need not converge upon one bearer. A skill tested in use, a witness who can be questioned, a relationship which has survived disagreement, an inherited practice capable of correction and an institution with effective recourse offer different anchors. Their plurality permits one to fail without making the entire possibility of trust collapse. More importantly, each can help judge the others. The official account can be checked against what people actually know how to do; the intimate account can be challenged by evidence it has refused to admit. A shared world need not speak with one mouth.

This gives the earlier relief of certainty a different answer. The person does not have to choose between a total explanation and an uninhabitable void. Provisional anchors let uncertainty be borne while inquiry proceeds. Their provisionality is not a grudging admission that nothing can be known. It is the discipline by which a real achievement remains capable of answering to more than itself. We can know enough to act without making the act immune to what happens next.

Monopoly destroys or conceals this plurality and then offers its own authority as the cure for the resulting uncertainty. Mono–poly trust keeps one world open through many relations of answerability. It need not remove an antagonism merely because the antagonism is uncomfortable. A contested boundary may be what lets the relation remain truthful. The other is not made worthy of trust by being declared part of the same whole; the whole includes the power to test, refuse and change an undertaking.

Symbol, account and trust consequently form a circuit. A Symbol conducts its source through a determinate form. An Account makes the form's claims and conditions available for examination. Trust carries an examined account into an act whose future is not possessed. The act changes the field from which another account will be made. Its consequence has to be able to reach the criterion, source or permission which governs the next act. A perfect archive can retain every result while leaving the governing relation untouched.[^fields] ^roz-s02-m21-claim03

The counter-operation can now be named *divine and concur*. To divine is to recognise the constituting relation through which opposed positions became possible. To concur is to let their real differences act within that recognition. The third is already active in the two as their field of difference; it is not another faction placed between them. Recovering it may expose an inequality more sharply, not soften it. One claim can fail, a power can have to relinquish its office, an injured person can decline the proposed reconciliation.

The test of gathering is therefore not whether it contains both sides. An enclosure can do that. The question is whether their encounter can change the terms under which they are held. Can the other arrive, correct the fit, refuse its use, leave a response which changes what the first party may next do? Or has the other been retained only as material within an arrangement whose author already knows the whole? A net will make this difference difficult to overlook. ^roz-s02-m21-claim04
