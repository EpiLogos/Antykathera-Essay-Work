## A person within the pattern

A person waiting for a reply can inhabit a world already organised by its absence. The delay seems to disclose an intention: I am being ignored. Attention begins to search the preceding exchange for the moment at which affection cooled. An ambiguous sentence acquires a hard edge. Memories of other silences become available, while occasions on which a delay meant nothing are harder to recall. Before any answer arrives, the person has begun to answer the silence.

This is the ordinary situation considered earlier, now followed into the activity that gives the interpretation its force. A defensive message may provoke the withdrawal it anticipated. Refusing to send anything may become another way of testing the other: surely someone who cared would know to ask. Each response can return material that the original interpretation knows how to receive. The account becomes more coherent as the encounter becomes less open. The other may, of course, actually be withdrawing. What matters is whether that possibility remains one question in the relationship or has become the condition under which every answer must be understood.

An image of rejection, the complex that organises its reception, and the archetypal capacity manifested through such relationships have different scopes. The image is this appearance: a silent screen, a remembered face, a sentence heard in a particular tone. The complex gathers the image into an affective organisation of memory, expectation, bodily response and action. The archetypal question concerns the formative recurrence through which such an organisation can take shape across different images and lives. Confusing these scopes makes the visible image seem to contain the whole power through which it acts. Distinguishing them makes it possible to ask how a particular image might change the organisation that receives it.[m32-1]

Jung's individuation concerns this differentiated person. The ego gives experience a centre from which commitments can be made and responsibility undertaken. A person who could never sustain a position could scarcely return to examine one. Yet the conscious centre does not comprise the whole psyche, and a complex does not acquire whole-person authority merely because its feeling is compelling. Nor is the Self a larger executive that wins the contest among these agencies. Its whole-forming office concerns the relation within which a local centre can become more specifically itself without claiming to originate everything that constitutes it.[m32-2]

There is an energetic reason why recognising an error may fail to loosen it. The rejection story is painful, but it also does something for the person who holds it. It ends the exposure of waiting. It supplies a cause, identifies an adversary and prepares an action. It can preserve a familiar identity: the one who always sees disappointment coming, the one who will not be made a fool of again. Abandoning the story can therefore feel less like correcting a proposition than surrendering a protection. An interpretation may hurt and still be difficult to relinquish because the hurt is organised, whereas what comes after it is not yet known.

Libido names the psychic investment through which this difference matters. It concerns the disposition of attention, desire, fear and value: what draws a person toward something, what makes them recoil, what they will expend themselves defending. A description of the pattern can tell us how one thought recruits another. An energetic account asks why these thoughts have acquired the power to hold a life. The first can map a circuit while leaving unexplained the commitment that keeps the person returning to it. Frank's original account of Symbolon Dynamics places precisely this charge between a formal pattern and its lived efficacy.[m32-1]

A favourable replacement story may leave that charge untouched. Perhaps the other is simply busy. Perhaps they are composing a considerate reply. Either is possible, but adopting the more comforting hypothesis can preserve the same demand: I must know in advance that I am safe. The person remains occupied with obtaining a guarantee from an image. What has changed is the image's sign, not the dependence upon it. The next delay can reactivate the same jurisdiction.

A more consequential change begins when the protective function itself becomes perceptible. The person notices not only the thought of rejection but the relief supplied by having settled what the silence means. This places something formerly concealed within reach of attention. The story is no longer simply the world announcing itself; it is also an act through which the person has tried to make the world bearable. Its history may deserve considerable care. The sensitivity to withdrawal may have been learned where attention to small signs was useful. Recognising that history does not require the sensitivity to disappear. It changes the claim that every present relation must be governed by the conditions under which that sensitivity first became necessary.

The uncertainty now has to be lived. There is no completed interpretation to stand in for the other's answer. The person can ask a question whose answer is not secretly prescribed, wait without making the waiting a punishment, or acknowledge a wish without turning it into an accusation. These possibilities have a cost: they expose a desire which a defensive certainty had protected. They also make another kind of knowledge possible. An answer may disappoint, reassure, complicate or refuse. The relation can become clearer because something other than the demand for confirmation has been allowed to determine what happens next.

The change is therefore not measured by whether the answer is pleasant. A refusal received as a refusal may be a more truthful encounter than an affectionate answer compulsively interrogated until it sounds like rejection. Care turns toward the reality of the relationship, including the other person's freedom within it. The original desire has not been extinguished. It has become capable of sharing the field with a desire whose course it does not control. The power previously spent on maintaining certainty can enter the more exact work of responding.

This is the passage Van Eenwyk helps to distinguish from the restoration of a familiar equilibrium. A recurring organisation can hold many different experiences within its range. A symbolic encounter can also expose the boundary of that organisation and make another way of receiving experience possible. The second movement changes the interpreter, not just the interpretation. Its unfamiliarity is part of its difficulty: the knowledge that will become possible cannot all be possessed before the passage is made. Enough continuity must remain for the person to bear the change. The point is not to glorify destabilisation but to distinguish renewed capacity from the reinstatement of a closure that the encounter has already shown to be inadequate.[m32-3]

The native relation `x → X → x` makes the return precise. A particular formation becomes transparent to the determining capacity it had expressed without containing. That recognition returns into another particular formation: this person, with this history, asking this question differently. The return does not replace a personal name with a universal one. It changes the relation in which the person bears their name. Likewise, `IS → AM` returns an account to the identifying activity of the one who gives it. “This is a pattern of rejection” becomes consequential when the speaker can recognise their own participation in its repetition. The description then reaches the act by which description is being made.

The same movement explains why an account of recognition cannot be wholly impersonal. A theory can be correctly repeated by someone whose criterion remains untouched. Frank's earlier encounter with *Prometheus and Atlas* locates the required change in an existential resolution: knowledge must be able to alter the knower's dependence upon the forms through which they know. The possibility of such a change gives inquiry its motive. What has become settled can reopen, and what is reopened can acquire a more adequate form. Permanent tension is no more the goal than premature agreement. A tension has done its work when a real resolution becomes possible without sacrificing the differences that made it necessary.[m32-4]

The energetic account extends through the body. In the vocabulary of prāṇa, life is articulated as circulation: inhaling and exhaling, drawing in and giving out, currents differentiated within a living activity. A frightened interpretation may be accompanied by a narrowed breath, tightened attention and readiness to act. The words, the feeling and the bodily preparation belong to the same lived situation without becoming identical descriptions of it. To attend to the rhythm is to approach the situation through another of its actual conditions. A pause in the rush toward an answer can become a place in which the demand behind the answer is felt.

The Śāktic account asks a further question: what is the activity by which a world becomes manifest in these determinate forms? Śakti names manifestation-power, including its capacities to disclose, sustain, conceal and recognise. In the contracted situation, an interpretation is illuminated so completely that the act of illumination disappears from view. A world of signs seems simply given. Recognition turns toward the making-visible itself. The field disclosed through fear is recognised as a field disclosed through fear; this reveals both the fear's reality and its limitation. The power of appearance is still active, but the appearance no longer stands alone as its own source.

Spanda gives withdrawal and extension their place within this activity. A manifestation comes forth; its formed limit does not exhaust the capacity from which it comes. Withdrawal retains a reserve from which another determination can arise. The native `−/+` holds these directions together. If every withdrawal is interpreted as extinction, a formed position must cling to itself at any cost. If withdrawal can preserve the capacity to appear otherwise, relinquishment is no longer identical with annihilation. The possibility of another act depends on precisely what the achieved act could not use up.

Libido, prāṇa, Śakti and Spanda consequently do different work. Psychic investment tells us what matters and where a person's energies are bound. Vital circulation tells us how life is rhythmically articulated. Manifestation-power concerns the appearing and concealing through which a determinate world is possible. Pulsation concerns the inseparability of extension from its reserve. Their coordination follows an activity across distinct modes of disclosure. It would lose its content if one vocabulary were merely substituted for the others. In mantra, for example, a formed sound can organise breath, gather attention and become the occasion for recognising its own appearing. The same practice has several operations; none is explained by naming all of them at once.[m32-5]

A material counterpart makes the relation more exact. In a collisionless plasma model, the distribution of charged particles contributes charge and current to the field equations. The fields, in turn, condition particle motion. A self-consistent solution must satisfy both directions together. The collective field is not simply imposed upon passive constituents, nor can the motion of the constituents be specified while disregarding the field they help produce. Cordonnier and his collaborators make this dependence explicit by calculating source terms from the distribution and solving the resulting coupled equilibrium equations.[m32-6]

Two elementary plasma processes distinguish the operations within this relation. Around a small perturbing charge, a near-equilibrium plasma redistributes so that a cloud of opposite charge screens its potential over a characteristic distance. The response is distributed; the local charge has not been abolished. In an elementary plasma oscillation, a small displacement of a charged population produces a restoring electric field. Inertia carries the return beyond equilibrium and an oscillation follows under the model's assumptions. Separation supplies the condition of a return whose motion retains the difference of the charges. Screening and oscillation are different physical results, not two names for an undifferentiated wholeness.[m32-7]

What crosses the registers is an organising relation: a local determination contributes to a field which conditions what that determination can subsequently do. The psychological inquiry asks how a local image reorganises attention and is then received through the organisation it has helped sustain. The physical inquiry specifies distributions, fields, forces and boundary conditions. The relation can have formal reality in both without electrical charge becoming psychic investment. Their differences allow the comparison to teach us something. A resemblance between two circular pictures would teach considerably less.

The psyche also has to assimilate what it receives. In his reading of Neumann, Frank follows this requirement through the body's most elementary traffic: hunger, intake, digestion and issue. What enters can become a new capacity of the creature that takes it in. Receiving information is an earlier moment than becoming able to live from it. The difference is visible in the reply example. A person may collect increasingly subtle descriptions of their difficulty and still receive the next silence as before. An assimilated encounter changes the conditions of the next reception. Knowledge has become nourishment when it can sustain a different act.[m32-8]

The devouring counterpart makes the limit equally clear. A system of interpretation can consume everything without allowing anything to change it. Each new voice becomes material for its existing certainty. Its hunger is maintained by the success with which it converts difference into the same. Assimilation, by contrast, permits what has been received to alter the receiver's capacities. It can culminate in enough: enough understanding to act, enough satisfaction for the present task to end, enough confidence to leave another person unpossessed. A living return includes release.

This is also why the ethical encounter cannot be described as a greater force overcoming a lesser one on a fixed scale. Watson's engagement with Levinas, and Frank's subsequent energetic question, concern a change in the organisation of value itself. The other may interrupt what counted as satisfaction. In the present example, obtaining reassurance gives way to learning how this relation can actually be lived. The person's wish remains, but it no longer dictates the measure of everything that appears. A change of measure can release capacities which no amount of confirmation under the old measure could provide.[m32-9]

Individuation names this becoming more distinctly oneself through a wider relation. The person retains a history, a body, a capacity for judgment and the consequences of their acts. Recognition resituates what these have achieved. It gives a local organisation its proper place within a life whose possibilities it does not exhaust. The whole is active here as the capacity to bear and transform differentiated relations. That capacity has a more difficult test than the reconciliation of two favourable interpretations: it must also bear an opposition whose contrary cannot be removed without falsifying the whole.
