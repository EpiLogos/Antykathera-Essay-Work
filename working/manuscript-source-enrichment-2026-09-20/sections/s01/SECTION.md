# §0/1 — The Integral Threshold

<!-- movement:M01 -->

What is it that knows? The question turns towards the one asking it. We can investigate a brain, describe a person, reconstruct a history of learning, or trace the operations by which an answer was produced. Each inquiry can discover something that matters. Yet as its account becomes more complete, the account is being understood. The activity through which something becomes known has entered the inquiry again. We encounter it in the question and in our recognition of an answer.

What I know of myself can be mistaken. A remembered event may have happened differently; another person may recognise a habit I have never noticed; a motive can become apparent only through what I do. Discovery changes the person who undertakes it. But the knowing in which a mistake becomes apparent is not another forgotten event waiting to be retrieved. The recollection has a content which can be corrected; recognising the correction is an occurrence of knowing. Increasing the accuracy of the first does not dispense with the second. Our question concerns their relation.

The distinction matters wherever an account of intelligence begins to govern what intelligence is allowed to be. An instrument evaluates an answer under a criterion. We can ask whether the answer satisfies that criterion, how the instrument reached its judgment, and whether the criterion answers the question that mattered. These are different investigations. A more reliable score does not, through its increased reliability, settle the authority of what it measures. Its success may make that authority more consequential. The stakes become personal when the account is an account of us: a category can decide which parts of a life are heard and which are treated as irrelevant.

Benjamin Bratton's *Agentworld* brief places the diffusion of artificial agency within this larger perplexity. The philosophical, psychological and symbolic question of living among intelligences exceeds the engineering of task completion.[^s01-1] Reserving for humanity whichever measurable capacity a machine has not yet acquired would make the meaning of human experience depend on a moving technical frontier. Conversely, a machine's acquisition of a capacity does not by itself establish that it experiences its activity. Effective agency, an account of its formation and the presence of experience have different evidential requirements. The first two can be investigated while the third remains open.

Before deciding what another intelligence is, there is work to do in the present act of deciding. We already know through forms: words, images, classifications, remembered situations, instruments. What happens when one of these forms is asked to contain the one who knows through it?

A definition gives something a determinate place. It distinguishes what a term applies to, excludes alternatives and allows consequences to follow. Its boundary is useful: without it, we could not tell whether we had answered a question or changed the subject. To define the Subject, however, is to turn this activity upon the one undertaking it. The attempt has to preserve both the power to distinguish and the presence which distinguishes. Refusing to define anything would leave the problem untouched.

Self-knowledge supplies the first requirement. For something to know itself, there must be a difference between its knowing and the presentation through which it becomes known. Otherwise there is no disclosure to undertake. Yet an identity must also be retained: without it, the supposed self-knowledge would concern something else. Difference makes the encounter possible; identity makes it an encounter with oneself. Neither can do the work alone.[^s01-native]

The word “is” already carries this crossing. In “A is A,” the second occurrence differs from the first as an inscription, while the judgment identifies what they stand for. The equation “3 = 1 + 2” likewise holds two different expressions equal in a definite respect. Identification does not require their inscriptions to become indistinguishable. It requires a relation through which their difference can be read as belonging to the same. This relating work is the copula's positive office. It keeps together what the statement must distinguish in order to say anything.

Names make the requirement concrete. Two people can bear the same name; one person can change theirs. Repeating the sound cannot by itself decide which bearer is meant, and changing the sound does not by itself produce a different person. In his encounter with Kripke's *Naming and Necessity*, Taylor follows this difficulty beneath the name to the discrimination of an instance: this one, another one, the same one again. Counting here is the elementary holding-apart of instances, before a numeral is recited. Naming gives that discrimination an expression which can be repeated and shared.[^s01-kripke]

The repetition still needs a circumstance. The person here, the person remembered, and a fictional figure with the same name do not become interchangeable because a word can designate them all. An identification depends on which one is at issue and what would count as identifying that one again. Name, instance and circumstance therefore have separate work. The name articulates; the instance is what is identified; the circumstance makes the relevant sameness and difference intelligible. The question of identity cannot be settled by inspecting the name alone.

Predication says something of the one identified. A person was frightened, became angry, learned a skill, changed a conviction. Identification lets these changes belong to someone; the changes give identification more to accomplish than the repetition of a fixed portrait. The person can answer to an earlier name while disputing the description attached to it. Keeping that difference alive is part of knowing them.

Now the one who identifies becomes the question. “I see these words” appears to offer three terms ready for inspection: I, seeing, words. The words can be inspected; the seeing can be investigated through the eye, attention, language and the circumstances of reading. Yet “I am aware of seeing these words” makes a change within the inquiry. Seeing is now explicitly something known. A process which seemed to supply the knower's identity has become a presentation to the knower. An account of that process is another presentation. Its increased detail does not make the difference disappear.

The movement can be followed without distrusting the account. A self-portrait may be faithful. A second portrait can include the artist making the first; a third can include the artist studying both. The additions disclose more of the activity and its circumstances. None takes the place of the present seeing in which the portraits are encountered. Even a portrait of the viewer is a portrait *for* a viewer. The limit appears through representation's success, not only through something it has failed to depict.

Descartes arrives at this pressure through doubt. In the second *Meditation*, the supposition that he is deceived cannot remove the occurrence of being deceived. Doubting the apparent world has left the doubting present. He then asks what this I is and determines it as a thinking thing, whose activities include understanding, affirming, denying, willing, imagining and sensing.[^s01-descartes] The distinction our inquiry needs lies between these moves. The certainty of the occurrence and the further account of what occurs are not the same achievement. An account of thought can be questioned while the questioning is still given. Taylor's return to the cogito therefore opens the sentence: I am aware of thinking. Thinking has become distinguishable as a means without making awareness another item found beside it.

Let a definition now take the risk of saying what this establishes: **the Subject is the self-identical presence to which a world, and every attempt to define that presence, is given.** The definition identifies a relation we have just undertaken. It distinguishes the present knowing from the portraits, processes and predicates through which that knowing can be represented. It also commits itself to a test: the definition must apply to the act in which it is understood. It cannot reserve an exemption for its own use.[^s01-native]

Apply it. The sentence has made the Subject a subject of predication. Its words can be inspected, agreed with, rejected or remembered. They have become something known. If the sentence claimed to contain the presence it defines, it would have to contain its own present apprehension as that apprehension, rather than offer another description of it. Add the description and there is more to understand. The added content does not perform the understanding on the reader's behalf. The attempt at enclosure renews the relation it was supposed to close.

This is the necessary failure. It concerns the demand that the account take over the office of its own knowing. It does not make the account false, nor make the reader unknowable in every respect. Body, biography, attention and the history of these words remain available to investigation. Their discovery cannot turn the knowing which receives them into one more discovery of the same kind. “Indefinable” has acquired an exact sense: the presence can be identified, while the demand to exhaust it as a bounded object defeats itself in its execution.

The failure changes what the definition can do. At first, its referent seemed to be missing because no final portrait had been found. Following the failure shows where to look again: the presence sought was already active in looking. The sentence can lead its reader back through what it says to the knowing in which it is said. Its finite form has become a means of recognition. A frame which was being used as a cage can be used as a window; the change preserves the frame while relinquishing its claim to enclose everything it discloses.

This is success through failure in two different respects. Enclosure fails; disclosure succeeds because that failure reveals the reader's actual relation to the words. Nothing contradictory has been proved about an object. The same finite act has discovered that it was asking its product to do something only its use could accomplish. The definition becomes faithful when it lets this discovery alter how it is used. Its content remains exact enough to be disputed. What it relinquishes is the demand that a true description replace the act in which its truth becomes intelligible.

The three positions—assertion, failed enclosure and successful disclosure—can now become available together. Their circumstance enters the inquiry. There is someone reading, in a place, with an inherited language and a concern which gives the question force. Success and failure were being judged from within that concern. The positions no longer have to be occupied one at a time as though each were a complete verdict. They belong to one activity whose changes of position can be understood. Context is this availability of the relations, including the standpoint from which their adequacy is being judged.

Even such an account can leave its reader untouched. I may agree that the inquiry is situated, explain the whole sequence accurately, and still imagine its Subject as the final topic over there. I have described participation without changing the position from which I describe it. A diagram of my involvement remains a diagram. The question which the diagram cannot undertake for me is whether I recognise the presence it has been trying to place.

I have tried to know myself by producing something I can know. I have found words, images, acts and explanations. Each is present to me. Describing that presence again gives me another description to understand; it does not create the knowing on which understanding the first already depended. The one sought through the definition was already the one undertaking it. **I am.** The sentence has returned in the first person. What had been treated as the subject of a proposition is recognised as the Subject of the present act. This is more than the judgment that I belong within a described situation. The knowing which was sought as an object is occurring as my knowing of the situation, including these words.

The recognition does not attach another quality to a self-image. “I am the one who has achieved recognition” would be another description to examine. Nor does the failure of an image require abolishing images. The difference which made self-knowledge possible remains: I can appear to myself in a thought without being exhausted by that appearance. The identity which made it self-knowledge remains too: the difference between my earlier description and my present recognition is a difference I can recognise as mine. The account has returned to the one whose act gave it its question.

The theological formulation develops the same return with an explicit claim about the whole: **“God is the whole that any coherent representational system participates in and necessarily fails to contain.”**[^s01-god] Here “whole” cannot mean a final object assembled from all the other objects. A representation of everything, its production and its apprehension would still occur within what is. Universality of reference does not provide an exterior to reality. The attempted definition belongs to its referent, including the activity by which the referent becomes known.

The God/Subject identification makes a further, affirmative move. The whole is present to itself in this knowing, and its self-presence is what the performance recognises as Subject. The identification concerns this source of appearing, rather than an equality between my finite description and everything real. The source which a description could not place before the knower is recognised in the knower's immediacy. The God-worded performance therefore ends by speaking from the first-person presence which the definition had tried to describe. Its recognition is “I Am,” not the acquisition of a picture of a higher being. This is the affirmative metaphysical claim of the performance. It leaves the finite person, with their limited knowledge and particular history, distinct from an imagined proprietor of the whole.

An older theological image makes this non-possession encounterable. The anonymous *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers* asks what God is and offers, in its second definition, an infinite sphere whose centre is everywhere and circumference nowhere.[^s01-sphere] An ordinary sphere assigns its centre a unique position within a boundary. The image withdraws both restrictions: access to the centre cannot be monopolised at one place, and the circumference cannot be stationed beyond the whole. Applied here, its force is that presence is intimate without being private property. My immediacy supplies no perimeter within which another person's life must be contained. The image's theological work and the preceding argument have distinct forms of warrant; its work would be lost by drawing a very large finite ball.

The Śaiva distinction between knower, means of knowing and known gives this relation a further articulation. Its account of awareness as luminous concerns the manifestness of an experience, including sound and thought, rather than physical brightness. Its reflexive power concerns awareness's apprehension of what appears. Dyczkowski approaches the relation through the ordinary experience of seeing something.[^s01-2] Seeing, attending and remembering can themselves appear within this awareness. A remembered word may direct attention; a shift of attention may disclose a mistake. The means make real differences to what becomes known while remaining distinguishable from the one to whom they are known.

Recognition returns that investigation to its work. A person still has a name, a history, a body and obligations; another person can answer in ways the first person's account did not anticipate. The source has not become the possession of the recogniser. What has changed is the account's office. It can distinguish truly and be changed through another encounter, because its distinction no longer has to defend the impossible claim that it originated the presence in which truth matters.

We need the distinction's power. Without a difference between this and that, a name cannot reach its bearer, a question cannot distinguish its possible answers, and a mistake cannot be corrected. Definition gives thought somewhere to act. The preceding failure was the failure of a particular demand upon definition; it leaves the achievement of determinate sense intact.

Spinoza approaches that achievement through figure. In his letter to Jarig Jelles, “Determination is negation” concerns finite, determinate bodies: a figure has this shape because its extension is bounded in this way.[^s01-3] The outline makes a portion of extension apprehensible as a figure. Its limit is constitutive of what can be measured.

Dignāga's account of *apoha* supplies a different operation. A word excludes other referents in making its reference determinate. What does not count under a term participates in what does. Pind's translation preserves an important positive distinction: the referent is a thing qualified by preclusion, rather than mere preclusion. The fragment carrying that formulation has a provisional textual attribution, but the difference between the qualified thing and bare exclusion remains intelligible.[^s01-4] Exclusion can make a referent available without replacing it with a collection of absences.

The geometric and semantic operations meet at a limited, productive relation. A boundary does positive work through what it leaves out. Keeping the excluded relation available changes how the achieved form is used: a word can refer, a figure can be measured, and a distinction can guide action without claiming to have absorbed the field of its application. The argument requires both the exactness of the cut and an account of what that exactness permits.

One material sense of the Greek *symbolon* gives this dependence a body: corresponding pieces held for later recognition.[^s01-5] One piece is brought to meet its counterpart. The edge is particular; a vaguely similar piece will not necessarily fit. The surviving form carries an absent relation into an encounter which can test it. Possessing the piece gives neither possession of the other party nor the power to decree the fit. What is absent can answer the form that is present.

A definition has a comparable obligation. Consider a count of those present at a meeting. It can be impeccably accurate and still fail to count everyone affected by the decision. The defect arises when attendance is made to stand for affectedness without an argument for the substitution. Refining the attendance total cannot repair that change of criterion. The excluded people bear upon what the count was entitled to mean.

We can correct a count under a given criterion, and we can reconsider whether that criterion answers the question. A sound distinction may survive both examinations. But suppose attendance becomes the sole test of standing: those outside the meeting are denied the right to question its criterion because they were not present. The account now protects the condition under dispute. A response which could expose the criterion's limit is translated into a failure to satisfy it. The instrument which made a relation legible prevents that relation from reaching the judgment.

The danger does not lie in having made a finite account. It lies in allowing that account to adjudicate every challenge to the basis of its own authority. The lost counterpart becomes consequential precisely because the counting continues to work. The same question can be put to a language or a system of proof: what happens when its rules are asked to determine the conditions under which those rules themselves count?

Russell met this question within the attempt to secure mathematical reasoning. Suppose every definable condition determines a set. Collect all sets which are not members of themselves. Is this collection a member of itself? If it is, it satisfies the condition for exclusion; if it is not, it satisfies the condition for inclusion. The contradiction follows from the unrestricted collecting permission. No overlooked member will repair it.

Russell's type-theoretic response changes which expressions may legitimately be formed. His vicious-circle principle states: “No totality can contain members defined in terms of itself.”[^s01-6] Distinguishing logical levels blocks the offending construction. A prohibition here has positive mathematical work: it makes a disciplined domain of reasoning possible. What has been repaired is the permission to form and substitute expressions, not every possible question a mathematical theory can raise about itself.

Gödel found a different difficulty in sufficiently strong, effectively axiomatised arithmetic. Expressions and finite deductions can be encoded by numbers, allowing the theory to represent a relation between a statement and a proposed proof. A sentence can then be constructed which says, arithmetically, that no number codes a proof of that very sentence. If the theory is consistent, it cannot prove this sentence: a proof would supply the numerical witness whose existence the sentence denies. Gödel's original stronger condition of *ω-consistency* also excludes a proof of its negation.[^s01-7]

This sentence is well formed. Its difficulty concerns derivability within the specified theory, where Russell's example exposed a defective formation permission. An extension can prove something its predecessor could not. Under the relevant hypotheses, a fresh sentence raises the limit for the extension. Growth in what can be proved is real; it does not remove the distinction between a truth of arithmetic and a proof available within a particular theory. The theorem's force depends on effective axiomatization, arithmetical strength and the stated consistency conditions. Without them, the claim that every system is incomplete would be a different claim with its argument missing.

The early Wittgenstein asks what lets a proposition represent at all. In the *Tractatus*, a proposition can picture a state of affairs because it shares logical form with what it depicts. That form shows itself in the representing; it is not a further depicted fact alongside the others. “What can be shown cannot be said.” The closing ladder brings the book's own propositions within this difficulty. Understanding has to pass through their elucidatory use, rather than preserve them as a final enclosure of what they make visible.[^s01-8] A representation's ability to disclose something and its ability to state the condition of that disclosure have come apart.

The later *Philosophical Investigations* follows a rule into its application. If every application needs a further interpretation, another formulation of the rule supplies no point at which interpretation begins to act. The signpost is followed within an established practice. Obeying and violating a rule are distinguishable there; merely thinking oneself obedient does not establish obedience.[^s01-9] The inquiry can examine how a practice is learned, maintained and corrected. Its life is not contained in an isolated expression waiting to determine every possible use from outside the occasions of using it.

These difficulties also give positive construction a direction. In Whitehead's process philosophy, determinate unity is itself an accomplishment. An actual occasion receives a diversity of data and acquires its character through the way it gathers them. *Concrescence* names the formation of this novel togetherness; *satisfaction* its completion as an achieved unity available to subsequent becoming.[^s01-10] What is immediate in forming has a different standing when another occasion inherits it as a fact. The achieved form can do further work precisely because its becoming has acquired a definite outcome.

Whitehead thus changes the question from how completed things can be connected to how their definite togetherness comes about. His process philosophy has its own metaphysical commitments; it is not an inference from an incompleteness theorem. Its contribution here is the distinction between an act's self-formation and its inheritance by another act. A description of the completed occasion belongs to the latter relation. The description can be true without supplying the forming which its truth concerns.

Formal construction can also change what a system admits. Spencer-Brown's calculus of distinction makes re-entry—the return of a distinction into its own space—a specific problem. In a two-state reading, crossing changes either state into the other. A value required to equal its own crossing has no stable solution among those two states; repeated crossing alternates them. Varela's extension introduces an autonomous third state with rules governing its reduction alongside the others.[^s01-11] The new possibility depends on those rules. It is a formal construction which can be examined, not an assurance that invoking self-reference has solved every problem of closure.

Formal repair enlarges what a system can do. The next inquiry asks which restriction changed, which operation became possible, and what remains outside the result's warrant. Russell's blocked expression and Gödel's unprovable sentence can both instruct that inquiry because their difficulties are distinct. A shared concern with self-reference does not make them one theorem. The Subject argument likewise has to retain its own reasoning through the relation between the account and its present apprehension.

Nicholas of Cusa approaches the finite intellect's relation to truth through a mathematical image. Increase the sides of a regular polygon inscribed in a circle. The fit improves, while each finite polygon still has straight edges. The improvement is genuine; the particular approximation has not become identical with its measure. Cusa uses the image within his theology of learned ignorance.[^s01-12] Modern mathematics can define a convergent sequence and its circular limit exactly. His philosophical pressure concerns the finite knower's possession of infinite truth, not a prohibition on mathematical convergence. The polygon's approach and the earlier infinite-sphere image perform different operations: approximation within a figure, and the withdrawal of an exclusive centre and enclosing edge.

Qualified predication makes another affirmative distinction available. Flasch's reading of Eckhart holds positive and negative theology within the same philosophical labour: a true determination need not possess the divine exhaustively.[^s01-13] “Insofar as” gives this difference an ordinary expression. To consider a person insofar as they act justly is to attend to a real determination without making justice an inventory of that person. Insofar as the act is just, justice is realised here. The qualification restricts the respect of the claim; it need not weaken what is realised under that respect. A predicate can be wholly true in the respect it names without becoming the whole truth of its subject. Relinquishing finality differs from retracting the truth which has been articulated.

The issue becomes consequential for science when its abstractions are treated as supplying their own conditions of evidence. Adam Frank, Marcelo Gleiser and Evan Thompson's *The Blind Spot* diagnoses the substitution by which abstractions are taken as more fundamental than the experience through which observation, measurement and scientific justification become possible.[^s01-14] Reading an instrument, recognising a discrepancy and judging that a repetition bears on a result belong to inquiry's activity. A record can make those acts available for examination; understanding and using the record are further acts.

The immutable gap is this non-coincidence renewed through determination. Its immutability does not mean that our information cannot improve. We may learn which memory was false, which rule produced an error, which source was excluded or which bodily condition altered perception. Such discoveries can transform what we know and how we know it. The transformed inquiry still has to be undertaken. An account of that undertaking adds determinate knowledge without substituting for the knowing in which the addition is received.

Taylor condenses the double movement in an early aphorism: “The Mind of Man is just a series of failed attempts to deal with the Unknown, in the process of which, however, we deal with a lot of unknowns.”[^s01-unknown] Its second clause is indispensable. The failure of final capture leaves a world of effective investigations, each capable of giving something real. A God lodged in a currently unexplained event would retreat as the event was explained. The source named in the defining performance has another relation to discovery: the explanation and its apprehension belong within what is. Its presence does not depend on keeping a particular fact unknown.

A finite inquirer nevertheless has to proceed. Memory, language, instruments and other people's testimony are relied upon before any result can certify them all. Inquiry can test and strengthen those dependencies; the testing continues through some actual means. Trust is present in that continuation. It becomes responsible when its particular dependencies can be questioned, corrected or relinquished. The limit therefore returns us to the making and use of forms: how can what they disclose include a working relation to their own conditions?

Jean Gebser gives the question a cultural and historical body. He reads changes in consciousness through the means by which a world is rendered. His account of Renaissance perspective follows the articulation of depth around a positioned viewer; Alberti's visual pyramid belongs to this achievement. The scene becomes proportioned through a determinate relation between eye and world. Perspective makes something newly explicit while giving its disclosure a sector and a position.[^s01-15]

That power remains indispensable. Gebser's *aperspectivity* concerns the sector's claim to exclusive validity. His archaic, magical, mythical and mental structures are not merely discarded stages. Their possibilities remain co-present, capable of shaping a present life. Integral consciousness can recognise their activity and relations while retaining the differentiation achieved by the mental-rational structure. A person may participate in an image and discern how that participation organises perception. The power of the image remains alive in a changed relation to it.

Heidegger's account of the world-picture approaches the other face of this placement. The modern world-picture concerns the world grasped as something represented before a subject, with the representer becoming its normative centre. The world so presented and the standpoint which judges its presentation belong to one event.[^s01-heidegger] Gebser's perspectival history and Heidegger's diagnosis give our inquiry different operations to join. A view discloses from somewhere; its produced picture can become the standard by which further disclosure is judged. The judging position may then disappear behind the apparent self-sufficiency of the picture.

Context requires more than adding perspectives. Accumulating every available viewpoint still leaves the situation from which they are collected and judged. Averaging them would erase the differences which make their meeting illuminating. A myth, a perceptual report, a logical inference and a physical measurement accomplish different things. Their conjunction needs the terms of their meeting to be visible, including where a translation fails or a contradiction remains. *Diaphaneity* names this readability of conditions through what appears.

Light enters a room through stained glass. Each pane admits and colours it differently; the lead holds the pieces together and marks their limits. An image falls inside, where a viewer encounters its shape and colour. Looking only at the image conceals the aperture which made it possible. Looking only at the glass misses the event of light passing through it. Source, pane, seam, room and viewer participate in the appearance without becoming interchangeable parts of one picture.

As the light changes, colour crosses a surface which was previously dark. As the viewer moves, the image acquires another relation to the room. A condition of the earlier appearance becomes legible through the difference. A pane can be recognised as a source of colouring, a seam as an obstruction; the new view may change how the window is approached or made. Nothing requires pretending the glass has vanished. Mediation becomes intelligible through what it lets through, and its disclosure can change the next act of mediation.

An account can acquire this transparency. Its sources, selected contrasts, language and purpose become readable in what it discloses. The reader can follow a claim together with the passage through which it arrived. A mathematical proof retains its inferential obligations; a historical attribution, its textual warrant; an image, the action through which a relation becomes encounterable. Their differences give the joined work its articulacy. Erasing the seams would erase the means of discerning what each passage established.

In Taylor's P5 development, seeing through a situation also requires being with it: attending to its text and subtext and caring what becomes of it.[^s01-p5] Someone who retains the power to decide remains involved through the decision and its consequences. Detachment cannot make that involvement cease. Impartiality has to be worked for within the relation, through attention to what can challenge the judgment and to whose experience its categories may have excluded.

The temptation becomes acute when an ultimate standard is invoked but cannot itself be brought into the room. A local image can fill its place: the last word, the final measure, the account to which all responses must conform. Its authority is exercised here while its source is placed beyond answer. The defining performance has already exposed the substitution. An account of the whole belongs within the whole; it cannot use the whole's transcendence of its terms to exempt those terms from examination. Fidelity to the source requires the finite account to answer for what it does.

The same issue becomes operational in a constructed technical example. An assistant is asked to explain why a request went unanswered. Its accessible archive contains a complaint that no answer arrived, but permissions exclude the folder containing the original delivery failure. It reports that the recipient declined to respond. Adding a note that the answer was produced “in context” changes nothing about this result. The relevant evidence must have an authorised route into the inquiry, its bearing on the inference must be recognised, and the conclusion must become revisable. The context was already doing work before anyone described it: access rules and retained evidence helped determine what could count as an answer.

Transparency has a practical test here. Displaying the exclusion may help a person discover the problem. Correcting the relevant permission or inference changes what the next inquiry can establish. Description and intervention have distinct offices, and both may be necessary. The world-picture problem has entered the constitution of an answering system: its frame helps produce the picture, while the picture can conceal the frame's effects. Making the operative conditions answerable is consequently more than an improvement in the presentation of results. The later technical inquiry inherits this specific demand, without requiring the opening to decide whether the assistant experiences its activity.

Co-presence also gives experience a positive standing which no explanatory picture can grant retrospectively. A dream can be real as an experience without its figures having stood beside the bed. The terror has been lived; the figure's identity and the cause of its appearance remain questions. A numinous encounter, a sensed presence, a vision or an ordinary perception can likewise be received before one register has decided everything that may be said of it. Occurrence, narration, symbolic articulation and causal interpretation have different evidential tasks.

Receiving the occurrence does not authenticate every account of it. A report is examined as a report; a causal interpretation must meet the evidence relevant to a cause. These distinctions allow the phenomenon to remain available for inquiry. Treating “psychic” as a synonym for unreal would remove it before inquiry began. A person changed by a dream need not deny the change because the dream's image was not an external object. That change itself becomes something to understand.

The contextual turn therefore has two directions. The world becomes articulate to someone; what becomes articulate also discloses the person's situation. Body, language, history, other people and material means enter what can appear and what can be done. Their reciprocity retains an asymmetry: my account of another person cannot supply their reply. Addressed, they can change the terms under which I approached them. The uncounted person can return as a speaker whose answer bears upon the construction of the question. The account becomes more adequate when that change can reach its judgment, rather than being added as another item under a judgment held fixed.

The limit has brought the activity of knowing into view. Distinctions still hold; words can refer; arguments can compel; an encounter can correct an error. What has changed is the standing of the achieved form. Its success includes a relation to the conditions through which it succeeds, and those conditions remain able to bear upon its next use.

For the condition which cannot be placed among the account's objects, let zero enter as a promissory sign. The written zero is itself determinate. It marks the difference between acknowledging a place in an account and occupying that place with another representation. The present knowing has not become absent because no final portrait can take its place. What the mark leaves open is already active in the act which reads it. This use marks a limit of representation; it has not yet supplied zero with its mathematical operations.

There is a positive activity to investigate in every determinate experience: how something is distinguished, held together, remembered and made significant. A learned word, a revised distinction or another person's answer can change what next becomes possible to think. To inquire into Mind is to enter this formative activity without mistaking its instrument for its Subject.

Bohm describes determinate things through movements of enfolding and unfolding. His vortex is a relatively stable form of moving water: a recognisable shape persists through the activity sustaining it. In his proposed implicate order, relatively discrete things emerge within a more primary movement, their relations belonging to their formation.[^s01-16] This gives achieved form a sustaining movement without erasing its determinacy. The physical pattern does not supply its own apprehension; it brings the question of formative activity into focus.

The inquiry returns to “I see this” with a different attention. Knower, seeing and seen are distinguishable within one occurrence. Something appears, can be attended to, enters memory, acquires a name. The means are real enough to investigate and to change. How does awareness differentiate itself into the means by which it knows, and the world it knows through them?
