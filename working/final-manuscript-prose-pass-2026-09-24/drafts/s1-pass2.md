## §1 — The Return of Zero

<a id="M13"></a>
<!-- movement:M13 -->

### A sign migrates between worlds

In 205, the middle character contributes no tens. Remove it, and two hundred and five becomes twenty-five. The absent quantity is doing work: its place keeps the hundreds from becoming tens. A reader who knows the notation sees the number without reconstructing that relation. Zero has become so effective that its intervention is almost invisible.

That ordinary act of reading has a history in which the familiar means were still being acquired. Once a sign's value depends upon where it stands, an empty place can alter everything around it. A scribe must distinguish a gap which belongs to the number from a gap which merely separates inscriptions. The reader must know which is which. A mark for the gap lets that distinction survive the departure of the person who made it. Robert Kaplan's history follows these changing offices one after another—not as a parade of inventions, but as a sequence of permissions that alter what can next be asked.[^s1-1]

Take a modern transcription of a sexagesimal number, with places worth successive powers of sixty. The entries $(1,0,1)$, at a fixed scale, give $1\times60^2+0\times60+1=3601$; $(1,1)$ gives sixty-one. This is an illustration of the positional problem, not a transcription of a particular tablet. The surrounding units remain the same while the missing middle place changes their relation. Babylonian scribal practice could make such an internal absence visible with a separator. Yet the separator did not thereby become a freely usable terminal digit, and the scale of the number could still depend on context. One difficulty had been solved without every later power of zero having arrived.

A different need meets the circular mark in Hellenistic astronomy. A measure can have no whole degrees while retaining minutes, or have degrees and seconds with no intervening minutes. An empty entry in a table must remain legible as an absent measure of the specified kind. The divisions of the measure give the sign its office. The circle is useful because a reader can distinguish no minutes from a missing or displaced entry. It does not have to be a number capable of undergoing every arithmetical operation to perform that task. Greek mathematics and astronomical notation therefore need to be read through their different practices, rather than arranged around a single verdict that the Greeks either possessed or lacked zero.[^s1-1]

The movement from an empty place to an arithmetic participant passes through another rich vocabulary. Indian uses of *kha*—a word for space or an opening—enter positional reckoning; Varāhamihira gathers *kha*, *ambara*, *ākāśa* and *śūnya*, words whose wider lives concern space, sky and emptiness. A term can name the absence of a quantity because that absence is intelligible within an ordered expanse. The mathematical use makes the absence exact. The word-world in which it becomes sayable is already larger than the calculation.[^s1-2]

That breadth matters to zero's metaphysical provenance. Emptiness need not mean the annihilation of everything. An opening can be clear of a particular content and still be the condition under which a content appears. Space does not become a larger space each time another object enters it. The relation between what is absent, what can appear and what remains through its appearance gives thought a problem as well as a vocabulary. In the Indian mathematical–metaphysical encounter, that problem enters the working of the sign. It becomes explicit when Bhāskara compares a zero-divided quantity with divine immutability through the creation and destruction of worlds. The comparison is written into his algebra.

Several achievements remain distinct even when they share a mark. A word for vacancy, a written dot or hollow circle, a positional procedure and a rule for multiplication are different things to acquire. The Gwalior inscription gives the history a visible hollow mark; boards, dots and spaces ask how an absence could be given a durable written form. The surviving mark can show a use. It cannot, by itself, reveal every earlier use or establish the single event in which a whole concept was invented. Following the operations lets the historical question grow more precise: what could this inscription's users now do, and what further difficulty did that leave?[^s1-2]

Brahmagupta makes the further difficulty unavoidable by stating what happens when positive quantities, negative quantities and zero meet. Zero is no longer merely somewhere a quantity has not been entered. It becomes something that addition, subtraction and multiplication must encounter. Once these rules can be expressed and taught, someone else can calculate with them without sharing the original occasion of discovery. A local act of reckoning becomes a procedure with a life beyond its first use.

That life travels through Arabic mathematical writing and Latin reception. Al-Khwārizmī belongs to the history of making Indian calculation teachable in another language; *ṣifr* belongs to the sign's changing vocabulary. The journey includes manuscripts, astronomical tables, teaching and commercial calculation. Each receiver has to acquire more than recognisable figures. A counting board already gives position a material support. A written algorithm must preserve positions as figures are entered, carried, copied and checked. Learning the new notation means learning how to trust the next written step.[^s1-3]

Fibonacci's *Liber Abaci* makes that undertaking visible at its entrance. The nine Indian figures are set before the reader together with the sign zero; the book then demonstrates how numbers can be written and calculated with them. In the nineteenth-century printing reproduced by the Mathematical Association of America, the explanation proceeds to Roman-numeral comparisons and to arithmetic tables. Familiar quantities are made writable in another system, and the new system is put to work. An encounter with another mathematical practice becomes something a further reader can learn. Fibonacci receives and teaches; he is not the European origin of the sign.[^s1-4]

Two people can disagree about the divine, the nature of space or the meaning of emptiness and still obtain the same result from a specified calculation. They can identify an error by reconstructing its steps. Mathematical reason need not settle every question about reality before a valid operation can pass between people. That portability is an achievement.

Something is also relinquished—not spirituality as a vague atmosphere, but a whole world of meanings that no longer has to accompany every zero in a ledger. The theological question beside Bhāskara's rule can drop away while the written sign continues its journey. The sign becomes exact in part by allowing these meanings to remain outside its immediate use. Mathematical universality is purchased without transporting the whole symbolic world in which the sign was meaningful. The loss is consequential precisely because the gain is real. Later return becomes possible only because the sign has acquired operations, prohibitions and history of its own.[^s1-5]

Putting the old meanings back and discarding the calculation would undo what the sign has learned to do. Those operations expose their own conditions; the metaphysical question reappears with an acquired precision. To receive what comes back, we first have to follow zero into the arithmetic which gave it this independent life.

### Śūnya becomes operational

Adding nothing to seven leaves seven. Taking no groups of seven yields no quantity. The same zero enters two operations with different consequences:

$$
a+0=a,\qquad a\cdot0=0.
$$

In addition, the other quantity survives unchanged. In multiplication, different quantities are sent to the same result. Six and seventeen remain different numbers, although six times zero and seventeen times zero are both zero. Their difference cannot be recovered from the product. The operation has absorbed a distinction which the numbers themselves still possess.

Brahmagupta's rules give this contrast an explicit arithmetic form. In the verses preserved in Colebrooke's translation, adding zero to a positive or negative quantity preserves it, while multiplication by zero yields zero. The rules can be followed with particular numbers. The reader encounters an empty quantity which has definite effects, rather than an absence which calculation must simply leave behind.[^s1-6]

This entry into arithmetic does not give every operation the same relation to zero. Division asks for a return. Twelve divided by three is four because three times four recovers twelve. The quotient is answerable to the multiplication which would undo the division. Replacing three with zero changes that question: what could be multiplied by zero to recover twelve? Multiplication has already erased the difference between possible multipliers. The difficulty is present in the rule before a proposed answer is named.

Bhāskara retains the written expression. In the *Bījagaṇita*, a quantity divided by zero becomes a fraction whose denominator is zero; *khahara* names this zero-divided form. Colebrooke's translation then describes its invariance under the insertion or extraction of quantities through an analogy with the “infinite and immutable God.” As worlds are created or destroyed and beings enter or leave the divine, the divine is neither enlarged nor diminished.[^s1-7]

The analogy asks for an act of thought. Ordinarily, adding a quantity makes the receiving quantity greater, and removing it makes that quantity smaller. Here finite increase and decrease are set against an identity which they do not measure. Creation cannot be treated as adding pieces to God; destruction cannot be treated as taking pieces away. What appears and disappears is real as manifestation, but its appearing does not make the source a quantity among the quantities it sustains. The comparison holds the changing many and an unchanged unity together without counting that unity as their largest member.

This is a metaphysical source within the mathematical history itself. The algebraic expression and the divine image meet at the question of what can remain invariant through transformations. Neither is incidental to the other in the passage. The zero denominator makes an exceptional mathematical claim; the divine comparison teaches the reader how that claimed invariance is to be conceived. The text places the mind between finite operations and a condition which those operations cannot augment or exhaust.

Its mathematical proposal still needs its own laws. Amartya Kumar Dutta's reconstruction asks us to attend to the retained expressions and their permissible treatment rather than read each as a failed modern fraction. In his account, cancellation and cross-multiplication cannot simply be carried unchanged into the zero-divided regime. Retaining an expression for further operations is different from granting zero an ordinary multiplicative inverse. This is a modern reconstruction of the historical practice, not a critical settlement of every Sanskrit witness. Its useful demand is exact: judge an operation under the laws it claims, and state where those laws differ from the ones being used to judge it.[^s1-8]

Arithmetic determines which transformations a retained expression can sustain. Metaphysics asks how a source remains itself through its manifestations. Read together, the inquiries make the relation between invariance and change answerable in two ways. The finite operation can expose a mistaken inference; the divine image can expose the assumption that the source must change as one quantity changes when another is added to it.

The Kashmiri account developed through Abhinavagupta gives the second question a philosophical body we can now recognise—as a separately developed anatomy, not as a transmitted proof of the mathematical rule. Prakāśa and vimarśa have already shown how a colour and its contrast can be manifest and apprehended within one awareness. The changing content does not have to become a second substance outside that awareness in order to be different.[^s1-9] Here this anatomy meets the mathematical text's question of invariance: the source can remain itself through a real differentiation whose particular forms do change.

Śakti gives this difference its positive force. The power to appear, discriminate and recognise belongs to the unity being considered. Manifestation gives that power a differentiated actuality. A finite presentation is therefore neither the whole of the source nor a mere exterior accident. It is a particular articulation of the source's power. The philosophical difficulty is to retain both facts: this appearance has its own determinate character, and its character does not sever it from the reality through which it appears.

Bhāskara's image and the Śaiva argument meet on a specific question. How can a changing articulation belong wholly to a source whose identity is not enlarged or reduced by the articulation? In the algebraic passage, insertion and extraction make that question visible through a claimed invariant. In the Śaiva account, manifestation and reflexive apprehension make it visible through an internally differentiated unity. A finite operation and a metaphysics of manifestation address different objects, but they address the source–manifestation relation with a shared seriousness. The relation of apparent absence to inexhaustible presence has become thinkable through these different operations.

Chronology is part of the discipline of that meeting. The conventional dates used here place Abhinavagupta around 975–1025 and the *Bījagaṇita* in 1150: approximately a century and a quarter separates the conventional terminal date from the mathematical work. Placing two developed inquiries in relation does not invent descent between them. Neither date marks the invention of zero. Nor do these dates establish that Bhāskara received his rule from Abhinavagupta. The primary theological comparison supplies the mathematical text's own metaphysical witness; the Kashmiri philosophy supplies a separately developed account of the relation that concerns us.[^s1-10]

A history which retained only the numeral would lose the question of manifestation beside its arithmetic. A philosophy which retained only the divine image would lose the resistance of the calculation. Zero brings the two into an encounter where a mark's capacity to operate and a source's capacity to appear can illuminate each other without becoming interchangeable. The sign's subsequent independence carries this encounter forward even where its earlier metaphysical language is no longer used.

There are already two ways of answering for what has been received. To establish the historical attribution, we need the transmitted text and a responsible translation. To establish an arithmetic step, we reconstruct the operation. The translator's standing does not make a multiplication correct; a correct multiplication does not prove that the translator has rendered a verse faithfully. Learning to distinguish these dependencies is part of the gain. It permits a received authority to be tested without pretending that the inquiry began with no inheritance at all.

### The empty set generates one

Near the end of this history, zero and one enter a different set of constructions: what can be generated from an apparently meagre beginning. One of the smallest is the empty product. If multiplying a list of factors gives a value, appending no further factors should leave that value unchanged. The value assigned to the product of no factors is therefore one, the multiplicative identity. This is not multiplication by zero. Entering a zero factor would make the product zero; entering no factor leaves it as it was.[^s1-11]

The distinction matters because ordinary language makes both situations sound like multiplying “nothing.” Mathematics asks what was actually done. The sum of no terms is zero because zero preserves addition. The product of no factors is one because one preserves multiplication. The empty case receives its value from the operation which must remain coherent through it. Absence has no single mathematical effect independently of its place in a rule.

Set theory supplies another beginning. The empty set, written $\varnothing$, is a set with no members. Now form a new set whose only member is that empty set:

$$
\varnothing\quad\longrightarrow\quad\{\varnothing\}.
$$

The first set has no members. The second has one. Its member remains empty, but the set which contains that member is not empty. Nothing has been put inside the original set; a new relation has been formed around it. The braces let us see exactly where the one appears.

In von Neumann's representation of the finite ordinals, these sets serve as zero and one. Two contains zero and one; three contains zero, one and two:

$$
0=\varnothing,\qquad
1=\{0\},\qquad
2=\{0,1\},\qquad
3=\{0,1,2\}.
$$

Each new number retains its predecessors. To obtain the successor of an already constructed number, keep that number's members and adjoin the number itself as one further member. Written compactly,

$$
S(n)=n\cup\{n\}.
$$

For zero, there are no previous members to keep, so adjoining zero gives $\{0\}$. For one, the retained member is zero, and the newly adjoined member is one: $\{0,1\}$. The previous number has become a member of what succeeds it. It remains distinct from the successor which contains it.[^s1-12]

We can now distinguish three relations which a loose use of “contains” would conceal. Zero is a member of one; every member of zero is also a member of one, since zero has none; zero and one are nevertheless unequal. Membership, inclusion and identity do different work. The construction continues because those differences are preserved. Finite iteration reaches each finite ordinal; it does not, just by displaying a few stages, supply the further set-theoretic warrant for a set containing all of them.

The first one is particularly instructive. Its entire content is the zero it holds, yet holding zero does not turn it into zero. What produces a countable one is the new level of relation. The empty object becomes something which can be gathered without ceasing to be empty. This is an acquired mathematical articulation of how a beginning can remain within what develops from it. There is no need to cancel the earlier term for the later one to be real.

The philosophical question must retain that achieved distinction. The empty set is already an object of thought, constituted under a definite mathematical framework. The Subject to whom the construction appears is not another empty object. We can represent the set, the braces, the rule and the act of following it; doing so makes more of the operation available for examination. Each such examination is also a knowing. Adding a representation of that knowing does not turn the occurrence of knowing into a member of the original set. A represented condition and the condition in its operative presence have different offices.

Keeping the mathematical levels distinct makes the symbolic use precise. One contains zero under the stated representation. It neither creates an unobjectifiable Subject nor encloses one. What the construction gives symbolic thought is a positive discipline: an achieved form can retain a relation to its beginning without becoming identical with it. The more precisely we can state what the form contains, the more precisely we can distinguish that content from the activity through which it becomes available.

Boolean logic changes the operation again. Let $p$ and $q$ stand for truth values. NOR, written $p\downarrow q$, is true just when both inputs are false:

$$
p\downarrow q=\neg(p\lor q).
$$

Apply it to the same input twice. “Neither $p$ nor $p$” is true exactly when $p$ is false, so $p\downarrow p$ gives negation. Apply NOR to its own result twice and the double denial yields disjunction. Apply it to the separately negated inputs and it yields conjunction:

$$
\neg p=p\downarrow p,\qquad
p\lor q=(p\downarrow q)\downarrow(p\downarrow q),\qquad
p\land q=(p\downarrow p)\downarrow(q\downarrow q).
$$

These operations suffice to build a truth function from its truth table. For each input pattern on which the answer should be true, conjoin the inputs or their negations so that the conjunction selects just that pattern. Then join the selected patterns with OR. The always-false result is also available as $p\downarrow(p\downarrow p)$, and its negation is always true. Thus repeated NOR can express every Boolean truth function of a positive finite number of inputs. Arrangement preserves differentiated answers even though the same connective is used throughout.

Functional completeness concerns this defined field of truth values and their combinations. The QL slash must also articulate the relation of a determination to its unobjectifiable condition, which is not another Boolean input.[^s1-13]

The empty product, the ordinal successor and the logical generator each make a spare beginning productive by a stated operation. Their kinship becomes useful only after we can distinguish them. The product keeps multiplication coherent; the successor retains an earlier set in a new member relation; NOR generates truth functions through repeated denial. Symbolic thought receives three distinct ways to articulate a generative relation.

One can now serve as a linker: a determinate presentation through which a relation is held and continued. Its efficacy depends on what it relates. The next difficulty occurs when the presentation takes itself to have supplied that efficacy from itself, and its source relation disappears behind its success.

### The crossed zero

A zero crossed by a stroke, **Ø**, gives that disappearance a visible form. This is the native crossed zero, distinct from the set-theoretic sign $\varnothing$. The crossing makes a finished character out of a relation whose operation can then cease to be seen. A representation of the self appears to contain the whole fact of being a subject.[^s1-14]

“I see this” already gathers several achievements. A body is situated; attention selects; language and memory make something recognisable as *this*. The statement also gathers an I-position from which the seeing is reported. These powers make a world intelligible. Occlusion begins when their representation of the participant claims the subject's office without remainder: this description is no longer something I can recognise about myself; it has become what I must be.

Consider the phrase “a successful person.” Its criterion can help someone recognise an achievement, compare possibilities and pursue a purpose. When that criterion becomes identical with the person's worth, however, an encounter which questions it can arrive only as another success or failure under its terms. Refusing the measure looks like failing the measure. The criterion still discriminates effectively, but its effectiveness protects it from being examined.

The crossed zero marks this identification. The stroke does not produce the subject whose identity it purports to secure. It covers the distinction between the subject and an instrument's image of the subject. A challenge to a description can then be suffered as a threat to existence itself. The mark which enabled recognition has become a substitute for the source of recognition.

The gap disclosed here also gives the notion of God a place. An account of determinate things encounters the source it cannot enter as another determination; the notion arises at that gap and fills its conceptual place. What the notion does there matters. It can direct thought towards the unobjectifiable condition, or be mistaken for a final item which completes the inventory. The name occupies a place in the account; the reality to which it refers is not thereby contained in that place. Particular unanswered questions may receive answers while this source-relation remains.[^s1-15]

Making the stroke visible returns an instrument to use. Someone can ask where the success criterion came from, what it selects and which experience could change it. Judgment, memory and aspiration survive; their authority becomes answerable to the life they organise. Uncrossing preserves what the stroke made possible while recovering its relation. A description can become more accurate as it relinquishes the demand to be the source of the one described. The linker works by remaining available to that recovery. In arithmetic, zero presents an especially exact version of the difficulty: a requested result can force the operation to disclose the law under which any result could be obtained.

### The frame disclosed at zero

Return to the quotient of one by zero. Begin in an ordinary field, where addition, subtraction, multiplication and division by nonzero elements obey their familiar laws, and zero differs from one. An inverse for zero would have to be an element $q$ satisfying

$$
0\cdot q=1.
$$

The difficulty is not that a sufficiently unusual candidate has yet to be found. Distributivity already gives

$$
0q=(0+0)q=0q+0q.
$$

Subtract $0q$ from both sides. Then $0q=0$, for every element of the field. Requiring the same product to equal one makes zero equal one, contrary to the defining distinction. Adjoining another element while retaining those laws cannot help: the proof applies to the new element too.

Zero divided by zero fails differently. Every $q$ satisfies $0q=0$, so the return test cannot identify a unique quotient. One divided by zero has no solution of the required kind; zero divided by zero has no uniquely determined solution. A common description of both as undefined must not obscure the different reasons.

The equality $6\cdot0=17\cdot0$ is true. Cancelling the common zero would identify six with seventeen. What failed was the assumption that multiplication by zero can be undone as multiplication by a nonzero number can. The operation's loss of distinction cannot be reversed by simply deleting its sign.[^s1-16]

A different mathematical setting must say what it changes. A total reciprocal function may be assigned a value at zero, but the assignment does not make that value a multiplicative inverse there. An added point at infinity can complete a space for specified purposes without turning the space into a nontrivial field with an inverse for zero. The exceptional expression becomes workable only through a declared change of operation, domain or laws. The mark's appearance on the page supplies none of those changes by itself.

Abraham Robinson's enlargement of the number system allows a nonzero infinitesimal increment: something smaller in magnitude than every positive real number, yet not zero. For a real $x$ and such an increment $\varepsilon$, the elementary calculation

$$
\frac{(x+\varepsilon)^2-x^2}{\varepsilon}=2x+\varepsilon
$$

is legitimate because the denominator is nonzero. Taking the standard part of the result gives $2x$, the derivative of $x^2$. Substituting zero for the increment before division would instead leave $0/0$. The extension and the standard-part operation explain why these are different procedures. They do not rescue ordinary division by zero.[^s1-17]

Here the immutable gap has its mathematical form. A law-governed operation cannot furnish, under those same laws, the result whose possibility they exclude. The obstruction is internal and demonstrable. It turns attention from the missing value to the containing field—and to the one who works inside that field. Mathematics can respond creatively because it can make its conditions explicit and construct a different setting; its power grows through knowing what the first setting cannot do.

An account can render an ever greater part of its conditions explicit. What it adds remains available through the act of knowing, rather than converting that act's unobjectifiable pole into a final object which abolishes the relation. A richer account is a real achievement; its richness does not change the office of an account into that of its ground. The arithmetic proof establishes its local impossibility, while the Subject inquiry establishes this constitutive difference. Their encounter lets the formal limit become a precise expression of the gap, without making the proof of a field law stand in for the whole inquiry.

Zero is fully mathematical, and its exceptional use obliges that world to become explicit as a world with laws. What changes is no longer a request for an unexplained extra number. It is a changed capacity to understand what a number, a quotient and a rule are doing together—and who is held by that understanding.

### The loan returns

The construction places $0/1$ and $1/0$ at its ends. After the failed quotient, the second expression looks provocative. Its new office is precise: retain its two entries as a formal boundary pair, rather than evaluate it as division in a field. Between two neighbouring pairs, add the numerators and add the denominators. From the two boundaries the first insertion is

$$
\frac{0+1}{1+0}=\frac11.
$$

Inserting again between the left boundary and this result gives $1/2$; inserting between the result and the right boundary gives $2/1$. The endpoints remain while new terms become available between them. No inverse of zero has been used.[^s1-18]

The operation is the mediant. For fractions represented by the chosen pairs $(a,b)$ and $(c,d)$, it forms

$$
\frac{a+c}{b+d}.
$$

Its difference from ordinary addition is easy to test. The mediant of $1/3$ and $1/2$ is $2/5$; their sum is $5/6$. Their written representatives matter too. Replacing $1/2$ with $2/4$ preserves the fraction's value but changes this mediant with $1/3$ to $3/7$. The rule operates on specified pairs. Knowing a pair's numerical value does not make every alternative representation interchangeable within the construction.

For positive denominators, the mediant lies between two distinct ordered fractions. If $a/b<c/d$, then $bc-ad>0$, and

$$
\frac{a+c}{b+d}-\frac ab=\frac{bc-ad}{b(b+d)}>0,
\qquad
\frac cd-\frac{a+c}{b+d}=\frac{bc-ad}{d(b+d)}>0.
$$

Both differences are positive, establishing the between-relation. The endpoint with denominator zero has its separate formal office; it is not an instance of this positive-denominator proof.

What could not be an ordinary quotient becomes an effective boundary. Its entries participate in generating intermediate terms while the boundary remains. The earlier prohibition has not been defeated; a new relation has made the retained form productive. This difference between obtaining a value and retaining a boundary is one of the things zero brings back from its mathematical journey.

Other acquisitions travel with it. The placeholder preserves an absent position. Arithmetic distinguishes preservation by addition from absorption by multiplication. The empty product preserves an operation through the absence of factors. The successor gathers a beginning into a new level of membership. NOR generates differentiated truth functions through reiterated denial. A zero denominator discloses laws; a mediant gives a boundary pair another exact use. Each gain was made through a particular operation. Their differences give what comes back its substance.

The metaphysical question can now be written with these acquired distinctions in view. Let $0$ name the unobjectifiable condition, and $1$ the determinate appearing field. The first office cannot be counted as one further member of the second. Yet a determination is present through its condition, and the condition becomes articulate through determination. Their relation is written

$$
0/1.
$$

The slash keeps the terms together and distinguishable. It allows *and*: both belong to the one appearing-act. It allows *or*: either office can be considered without being confused with the other. This AND/OR names a relational operation, not another elementary Boolean connective. The two terms do not first become self-sufficient objects and then acquire a link. Their distinction becomes available through the relation itself.[^s1-19]

Reversing the orientation gives

$$
1/0.
$$

The achieved determination turns towards its condition. It can make its source relation more articulate without making the source into a further possession. The arithmetic asymmetry remains: $0/1$ has the field value zero, whereas $1/0$ has no field value. Symbolic use does not repair that quotient. It retains the difference which made the turn necessary and gives it an explicit office within thought.

These are the Two Ones. Zero names **One as Self-Identity**, the uncounted unity present through determination. One names **All as Self-Difference**, the whole manifest and determinable field and each particular through which that field appears. All is not merely a heap of isolated things: their relations, possibilities and differences already enter what makes any one of them this one. Nor is the uncounted One a hidden item which the heap would finally include if it became large enough.

Their dependence runs in different respects. Manifestation depends on unity for its being and coherence; unity depends on manifestation for its articulation and knowability. These are not two causes making each other at different moments. The source becomes encounterable through what depends on it. A particular form belongs wholly to the field while remaining particular, and that belonging does not authorise it to occupy the field's source-position. The first one's exact set-theoretic relation to zero helped us distinguish a containing form from what it contains. Here the further distinction concerns manifestation itself: a form can bear its source relation without enclosing the source as another form.

The earlier Indian encounter returns in a changed language. Bhāskara's creation and destruction brought finite change before an invariance it could not measure. The Śaiva account placed real powers of differentiation within an undivided reality. Mathematics has since given the question further means: it can distinguish the operation from its result, the member from its containing set, the exceptional value from the boundary, the presentation from the rule which makes it productive. These means allow the symbolic relation to become more articulate without requiring its source to be another finite object. Discrimination has entered the contemplation of unity with a history of its own.

That history also changes the reckoner's position. A count depends on a unit and on the circumstances under which something is counted as one. The dependence remains when the count is correct. Ten is ten; whether ten is enough depends upon a need, a purpose and the people concerned.

Suppose an overseer finishes a tally of those present for a harvest share and finds the arithmetic sound. A woman who worked the field but was not named on the list answers that the reckoning, correct as a total of names entered, still fails her. She does not dispute that ten were written. She changes what the count was for. Counting those present can leave out those affected. A reply from someone omitted need not dispute the arithmetic. It can change what the reckoning ought to include.

The reckoner also inherits the naming problem met at the threshold. An inscription remains useful only while it lets the bearer be recognised in the relevant circumstances. When someone omitted by a count answers, that recognition can expose the inadequacy of the unit or criterion without disputing the total calculated under it. The count can be right and the account it was used to settle still need correction. Naming, counting and answering meet in the use made of the result—and in the person who must live with that use.

The Latin *ratio* carries reckoning into an account rendered, and into reasoning and judgment. Its lexical range does not prove the relation; it preserves a vocabulary for the task. An account can state what was counted and the terms under which the count was made. Once those terms are available, they too can receive an answer. Increased information under an unchanged criterion is not always the needed gain. The decisive gain may be a changed criterion which lets something previously excluded matter.[^s1-20]

Trust already participates in that passage. We receive a language, a report or a procedure before we can examine all that supports it. Examination can then alter what we are prepared to entrust. *Fides* gives a vocabulary of reliance and credibility; *credere* of believing and entrusting. Their before-and-after relation here is an argued movement of inquiry, not a claim about the chronological birth of the words. A received account can become more answerable through checking while its use remains a commitment made in life.[^s1-21]

That commitment can include refusal. An accurate account of a proposal does not oblige the person it concerns to accept it. The source-relation remains alive when the other's reply can change what follows, including whether the undertaking proceeds at all. Exactness survives; the claim that exactness alone supplies its own purpose does not. What changes is the authority a determination may claim over the life to which it returns—and over the questioner who must answer for it.

This is the direction of an integral recovery. In the historical braid developed through Gebser, the achievements of mental-rational differentiation are retained while their constituting relation becomes available to consciousness. Adding another perspective would only provide another view. Recognising the dependence of viewing changes how any view can be held. The precise object, the finite knower and the powers of investigation remain; their apparent separation no longer has to conceal the activity through which they belong together.[^s1-22]

The history of zero has prepared that change without completing the later psychic and practical work it requires. The sign became transmissible by allowing much of its numinous life to fall outside each calculation. It comes back because calculation has made the relation of its terms to their conditions increasingly exact. What was once carried through the image of a source unchanged by worlds can now also be approached through operations which preserve difference, reveal an obstruction and change the containing field. The mathematical sign becomes capable of symbolic life again without ceasing to be a mathematical sign.

The corresponding token met at the threshold carries this relation in its edge: the present piece remains answerable to the counterpart from which it was divided.[^s1-23] Zero and one can be held in that way. A determination is real, exact and consequential; its source remains consequential within its use. What matters now is how the powers of differentiation hold that relation when they distinguish, decide and gather.
