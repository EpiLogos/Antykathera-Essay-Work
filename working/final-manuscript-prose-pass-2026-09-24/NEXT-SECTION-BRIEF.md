**The slash double-read (L55) — the load-bearing paragraph:**
> "The collapse is resisted in the notation by the slash, which works double: read as arithmetic it is the fraction-stroke, the most domesticated sign in arithmetic's stock, and as the sign for division; read as a logical operator it is AND/OR, a stroke that declines to decide between conjunction and disjunction and holds both. We are adding a structured irrationality to what in standard arithmetic is the defining form of a rational number. Under that stroke, 0/1 reads: the ground as expressed through the distinction. The same relation reversed reads 1/0: the distinction as held within and by the ground. Neither is a second relation. Each is the one relation looked through from its other end, so the two readings are the same AND different — the notation generates its own counter-reading in the act of being written, 0/1 and 1/0 in co-oscillation, distinct yet interdependent, a _non-dual-two_ — and since both carry the same 0, the two ones are one: one mark, doubly oriented, joined through the ground each contains, a distinction that does not acquiesce to separation."

> "The matheme's full form for the pair is **(0/1)/(1/0)** — a superposition of superpositions, the stroke now standing between the two orientations of itself — and the full form locates the irrationality exactly. … (0/1)/(1/0) yokes the defined and the undefined across a single stroke: four digits and three strokes holding the sayable to its own limit. The quality of the ones is determined not by a valence (+ or −) but by a relational orientation (0/ or /0)."

- **NEITHER** (L454) — what the crossing adds: "At BOTH, AND and OR can still be held as a paradoxical computation. - L456: "the Catuṣkoṭi crossing is the lived pressure of that acknowledgment."
- **SILENCE** (L458): "not a fifth position placed beside the four. It is what it is like to have passed through all four and found them belonging to one ground" — located at #5, "the and/or as lived."
- L460: "We return to the opening situation with logic included and surpassed."
- Effect on the determinations (L464): "We began from encounter, submitted that encounter to the logic of IS and IS-NOT, allowed AND/OR to carry us through BOTH and NEITHER, and returned through silence to encounter again."
