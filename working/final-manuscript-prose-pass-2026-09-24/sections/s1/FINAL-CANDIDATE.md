## §1 — The Return of Zero

<a id="M13"></a>

### A sign migrates between worlds

In 205, the middle character contributes no tens. Remove it, and two hundred and five becomes twenty-five. The absent quantity is doing work: its place keeps the hundreds from becoming tens.

Do the sum once by hand. The 2 counts two hundreds because it stands two places above the units, and because the places between it and the 5 are accounted for. Leave the tens place empty and the account still holds — zero tens, five units. Strike the empty place and the 2 falls into the tens position, dragging the whole number down with it. A positional script makes every mark's value depend on where it stands, and that dependence turns an unentered place into a danger. The blank could be part of the number; it could be the gap between two numbers; the paper cannot say. The person who wrote it knew which. Once that person has gone, the difference has to travel in the writing itself.

Scribes in the ancient Near East were the first to meet that danger under conditions that made it consequential. They calculated in base sixty, so a number's places counted successive powers of sixty, and a power that happened to contribute nothing was still a place. Take a modern transcription, with places worth successive powers of sixty: the entries $(1,0,1)$ give $1\times60^2+0\times60+1=3601$, while $(1,1)$ gives sixty-one. This is an illustration of the positional problem, not a transcription of a particular tablet. Early practice could leave the empty middle place to the reader's judgement of scale; a skilled hand supplied what the clay omitted. Later practice inserted a separator, and the separator did one thing: it kept an empty place legible through copying, so that the absence survived as part of the number rather than as wear or accident. The mark was internal. It never stood at the end of a number, and the overall scale could still ride on context. One permission had been granted. Arithmetic was still to come.

Hellenistic astronomy met a different need and answered it with a different mark. In a table an entry can have degrees and seconds and no minutes in between, and an empty cell in such a table must remain legible as an absent measure of a specified kind. The sexagesimal divisions of the measure gave the sign — a small circle — its office: it said *no minutes here*, inside a table whose columns were checked by other calculators and copied for centuries. The circle was useful precisely because a reader could tell a genuine zero-minute from a lost or displaced entry. It held absence inside measurement, and it could do that work without ever entering calculation as a quantity — which, for a long time, it did not do. Greek mathematics accomplished its immense constructions without a positional zero, while Greek astronomy used a sign that behaved like punctuation inside a measure.

The mark then travels east, and its history becomes a history of words before it becomes a history of rules. The route followed here — separator, circle, word, rule, reception — is the one Robert Kaplan's natural history of zero lays out. His question is how a number got made out of nothing. His manner is to follow the mark through the hands that used it, and to let the oddest episodes of that traffic carry the largest questions. The Indian scenes are where his book is richest, because there the vocabulary grew long before the arithmetic did. Sanskrit astronomers reckoning positionally needed names for the unfilled place, and they took them from a word-world already fluent in openness: *kha*, space or an opening; *ambara*, sky; *ākāśa*, the boundless; *śūnya*, empty. Varāhamihira's list gathers several of these as names for one vacancy in a row of figures. A term could name the absence of a quantity because that absence was intelligible within an ordered expanse — an opening is clear of something, and remains the space in which something appears. The reckoner who wrote *kha* for the empty place was drawing on that breadth. Emptiness, in this vocabulary, need not mean the annihilation of everything; it can mean a place held open.

Material marks followed the words, and the record keeps only parts of that story. A ninth-century inscription at Gwalior preserves a hollow mark standing in a number where nothing was entered — the visible ancestor of the printed 0. The calculating boards of the dust tradition suggest another route: a dot on a surface normally erased between calculations, an absence written where writing was customarily brushed away. These are hypotheses about origins, and the manuscripts argue about their own dates. The safe claim is smaller and firmer: a word for vacancy, a written dot or hollow circle, a positional procedure and a rule for multiplication are different things to acquire even when they share a mark, and the surviving objects show uses without settling a single moment of invention.

What the mark still could not do was say what happens when it meets another quantity. An empty place is legible; it has no stated effects. The rules that gave it effects were written down in the seventh century, and they are the next movement's subject. The sign, though, did not stay home with its rules: it crossed half the world, and the crossing is part of what it became. That crossing is the rest of this movement.

Calculation travelled from India into the Arabic-speaking world through teaching and translation. Al-Khwārizmī's book on reckoning with the Indian figures made a procedure teachable in another language, and the sign's names travelled with it: Arabic *ṣifr*, the empty, moved into Latin in forms that would eventually settle into *cifra* and *zero*. A notation is transmitted through manuscripts, astronomical tables, commercial arithmetic and the instruction of apprentices, and each receiver had to acquire more than recognisable shapes. A counting board already gave position a material support; a written algorithm had to preserve positions while figures were entered, carried, copied and checked. Learning the new notation meant learning to reconstruct the next written step from the last one — a discipline of the hand as much as of the eye.

At the start of the thirteenth century Leonardo of Pisa, known as Fibonacci, opened his *Liber Abaci* with that discipline. The nine Indian figures are set before the reader together with the sign for nothing; the book then shows how numbers are written and worked in the new system, compares it with Roman numerals, and proceeds to tables for merchants. In the book's opening pages the pedagogy stands visible at the entrance: familiar quantities are made writable in another system, and the system is immediately put to use. Fibonacci receives and teaches. His is a scene of reception, where the labour of acquiring a notation becomes visible; he stands nowhere near the origin of the sign.

A procedure that carries its correctness inside itself can pass between people who share no cosmology. Two people can disagree about the divine, the nature of space or the meaning of emptiness and still obtain the same result from a specified calculation; each can find the other's error by reconstructing its steps. That is the migration's gain. The price is exact. The sign became portable by letting its wider meanings fall outside each act of calculation: the reckoner who writes *kha* need not carry the word's whole sky into the sum. A world of meanings was held off so that a specified procedure could remain reconstructible, and what survived the holding-off was the mark itself, now carrying operations, prohibitions and a history of its own.

Had the sign remained a sacred emblem of vacancy, it could be contemplated; because it became a marked place with stated effects, it can be reasoned with — and its reasoning can eventually be turned back on the question of vacancy itself. That turning begins where the mark first earned effects.

### Śūnya becomes operational

Adding nothing to seven leaves seven. Taking no groups of seven yields no quantity. The same sign enters two operations with different consequences:

$$
a+0=a,\qquad a\cdot0=0.
$$

In addition, the other quantity survives unchanged. In multiplication, different quantities are sent to the same result. Six and seventeen remain different numbers, although six times zero and seventeen times zero are both zero; their difference cannot be recovered from the product. The operation has absorbed a distinction the numbers themselves still possess — and this absorption, innocuous here, is the exact mechanism that will later make division by zero impossible.

In a seventh-century treatise, Brahmagupta set zero among the affirmative and negative quantities and stated its effects in verses a reckoner could apply. In Colebrooke's 1817 translation — itself a historical layer, nineteenth-century English standing between the reader and the Sanskrit — the rules read: "The sum of cipher and negative is negative; of affirmative and nought is positive; of two ciphers is cipher. [...] The product of cipher and negative, or of cipher and affirmative, is nought; of two ciphers, is cipher." Cipher has here joined the company it acts on. A sign that once held a place now participates in a calculation: it can be added, multiplied, carried. Zero acquires arithmetic citizenship through rules, one rule at a time.

Division asks a third question, and it is of another kind. Division is recovery through multiplication: twelve divided by three is four because three times four recovers twelve. The quotient is answerable to the multiplication that would undo the division. Replace three with zero and the question turns on itself: what could be multiplied by zero to recover twelve? Absorption has already answered — nothing can, because every candidate multiplies to the same zero. The difficulty sits in the rule before any answer is proposed.

Bhāskara, writing the *Bījagaṇita* more than five centuries after Brahmagupta's rules, faced that difficulty and wrote through it. His rule is the hinge of this movement. In Colebrooke's translation: "a quantity, divided by cipher, becomes a fraction the denominator of which is cipher." The form has a name, *khahara* — a name for exactly this form, the quantity the verse calls divided by cipher — and the decisive word is *becomes*. The expression is retained as a written object with a stated character, like a fraction with any other denominator, rather than being evaluated on the spot. A rule that could not finish has produced a form that can be carried.

What can be done with the carried form is the next verse, and Bhāskara reached for a comparison that still asks to be read. In Colebrooke's rendering: "In this quantity consisting of that which has cipher for its divisor, there is no alteration, though many be inserted or extracted; as no change takes place in the infinite and immutable God, at the period of the destruction or creation of worlds, though numerous orders of beings are absorbed or put forth."

Ordinarily, inserting a quantity makes the receiving quantity greater, and extracting one makes it smaller; increase and decrease are the grammar of finite quantity. The zero-divided form breaks that grammar: add to it or subtract from it, and it remains what it was. Finite operations pass over it without altering it. Bhāskara then names the analogue he has in view, and the analogue is precise: a source whose worlds are created and destroyed without the source being enlarged or diminished. Creation, in this image, is the putting forth of beings, and destruction their absorption; neither adds pieces to God nor takes pieces away, because the source is a different kind of thing from the quantities it sustains. The image holds the changing many and an unchanged unity together without counting that unity as their largest member. The algebraic form and the theological image meet at a single question — what remains invariant under finite transformation — and the meeting stands in the primary text itself, not in a later need for an ally. Neither side certifies the other: the analogy does not prove the arithmetic, and the arithmetic does not establish the doctrine.

A modern historian of Indian mathematics, Amartya Kumar Dutta, has reconstructed what such retained forms could and could not do. His reconstruction is a proposal about regime: within it, cancellation and cross-multiplication — moves that are safe for ordinary fractions — are restricted for zero-divided expressions, and evaluation may be postponed. The reconstruction changes what the historical algebra can be claimed to contain. It leaves the expressions other than ordinary field quotients, and it licenses no cancelling of a common zero anywhere; it shows that a form may be retained under laws of its own, and that those laws must travel with the form when anyone reasons with it. The demand is exact: judge an operation under the laws it claims, and state where those laws differ from the ones being used to judge it.

Beside the mathematical verse stands a philosophical answer to the question it raises, developed in the same civilisational world by another practice. Bhāskara's image says that a source is neither enlarged nor diminished by its manifestations; it leaves open how a changing articulation can belong, wholly and really, to such a source. Kashmiri Śaivism answered precisely that — the tradition Abhinavagupta brought to its classical form, whose root problem is recognition, *pratyabhijñā*: the coming to know of what was never absent. Consciousness, in this anatomy, is self-luminous and self-differentiating: its light is capable of distinction, and the power by which forms appear, are discriminated and are recognised — Śakti — belongs to the source itself. A finite presentation is therefore neither a piece cut from the whole nor an accident appended to it; it is a real articulation of the source's own power, and the light that manifests it is the same light that knows it. Manifestation is the source's own act, with nothing withheld, without interval between the appearing world and the awareness in which it appears.

Abhinavagupta's conventional dates are about 975 to 1025; the *Bījagaṇita* is dated 1150. Roughly a century and a quarter separates the tradition's classical terminus from the algebra. Neither date is the invention of zero — the positional mark and its first rules predate both — and no line of influence runs from the Kashmiri philosopher to the mathematician; their disciplines, texts and purposes are distinct. What the interval gives, and what this essay claims from it, is co-presence with depth: within one Indian intellectual world, two developed inquiries had made the relation between finite determination and an exceeding condition thinkable in exact but non-identical ways — one through an algebra that retains an invariant form, one through an awareness whose manifestations neither exhaust nor deplete it. That co-presence is the braid, and the braid is the claim. The dates themselves exclude the ordering in which metaphysics waits for mathematics to discover a formal limit — the metaphysics is centuries earlier — and they leave both inquiries inside one world rather than dividing a contemplative East from a calculating West. The arithmetic, once earned, becomes this section's instrument.

### The empty set generates one

Multiplying no factors sounds like a joke until an operation forces the question. Take a running product — multiply a list of factors, one after another — and ask what value the list has before any factor is entered. If the empty start were zero, every product would be zero, since zero absorbs whatever multiplies it. The value that lets composition survive the absence of factors is one: $1\cdot a=a$. The product of no factors is therefore one, and the sum of no terms is zero, by the same reasoning in the other operation — zero preserves addition. In each case the empty case takes its value from the operation it must leave coherent. Entering a zero factor does something different again: it makes the product zero. "Nothing entered" and "zero entered" are distinguished by what the operation needs, and mathematics keeps the distinction because losing it would lose the calculation.

Set theory makes a starker beginning exact. The empty set, written $\varnothing$, is the set with no members. Now form a new set whose only member is that empty set:

$$
\varnothing\quad\longrightarrow\quad\{\varnothing\}.
$$

The first set has no members; the second has one. Nothing has been put inside the original set — a new relation has been formed around it. The braces let the eye see exactly where the one appears: in the gathering, not in the gathered.

In von Neumann's representation of the finite ordinals, these sets become zero and one, and a successor operation repeats the move:

$$
0=\varnothing,\qquad
1=\{0\},\qquad
2=\{0,1\},\qquad
3=\{0,1,2\},
\qquad S(n)=n\cup\{n\}.
$$

Each number is the set of its predecessors. To construct the successor of a number already built, keep its members and adjoin the number itself as one further member. Zero has no members to keep, so adjoining it yields $\{0\}$. For one, the retained member is zero and the newly adjoined member is one: $\{0,1\}$. The previous number has become a member of what succeeds it, and it remains distinct from the successor that contains it.

The relations here are three, and the construction depends on keeping them separate. Zero is a member of one. Every member of zero is also a member of one, since zero has none — so zero is a subset of one, and indeed of every set. Membership does not follow inclusion: within this construction, zero is a member of each later ordinal, whose successor installs its predecessor as a member. And zero and one are nevertheless unequal. Membership, inclusion and identity do different work; collapsing them produces pseudo-results. The first one is the instructive case: its entire content is the zero it holds, yet holding zero does not turn it into zero. What produces a countable one is the new level of relation — the braces, the gathering — and that is the construction's whole teaching. A form can retain its beginning without becoming identical with it.

This floor is exact, and its boundary must be drawn at the same time. The empty set is an object constituted within a definite framework; it can be represented, pointed at, manipulated. The Subject to whom the construction appears is another kind of thing: representing that representation is a further act of knowing, and adding acts to the account never turns the occurrence of knowing into a member of the set on the page. One contains zero under the stated representation. The construction neither creates an unobjectifiable subject nor encloses one — and that refusal is its precision, since a floor that claimed metaphysical reach would collapse the very difference it exists to show.

Logic offers a third generativity, and it is the strangest. Let $p$ and $q$ stand for truth values, and define NOR, "neither p nor q":

$$
p\downarrow q=\neg(p\lor q).
$$

The connective is true just when both inputs are false — denial holding two things at once. Apply it to the same input twice and negation appears: $p\downarrow p$ is true exactly when $p$ is false. Apply it to its own doubled result and the double denial yields disjunction. Apply it to the separately negated inputs and conjunction follows:

$$
\neg p=p\downarrow p,\qquad
p\lor q=(p\downarrow q)\downarrow(p\downarrow q),\qquad
p\land q=(p\downarrow p)\downarrow(q\downarrow q).
$$

From here completeness can be shown rather than asserted. Take any truth function of finitely many inputs. For each input pattern on which the function should be true, conjoin the inputs or their negations so that the conjunction selects exactly that pattern; the always-false function is available directly from the same denial. Join the selected patterns with the disjunction just built. Every Boolean truth function of a positive finite number of inputs is thereby expressed with one connective, reiterated. Arrangement preserves differentiated answers even though the same denial is used throughout. NAND, "not both," is a different connective with a parallel completeness of its own; the two are not interchangeable, and the question of who first exhibited which generator belongs to a separate scholarly debt this section does not settle.

The product keeps composition coherent through absence; the successor retains its predecessor in a new member relation; NOR generates the whole Boolean field from reiterated denial. In none of them does anything come from nothing. In each, a spare beginning is productive through a stated operation — the operation's law, and never the pathos of emptiness, is what does the work. And what the constructions presuppose, everywhere, is the relation between the operation and the terms it gathers: a relation they use, and leave unmarked.

### The crossed zero

Draw a zero and cross it with a stroke: **Ø**. This is the essay's own sign, distinct from the set-theoretic $\varnothing$ of the last movement, and it records an occlusion rather than an absence. The stroke is present in the figure — determination has occurred, a world has been marked out — but it is fused into the zero it crosses. The act of determining has vanished into its success. In the native matheme this is the first movement of recognition: $0 \longrightarrow Ø$, the indicated condition passing into a figure whose mediating stroke no longer displays itself as mediation.

What the fusion produces is a specific misreading. "I see this" gathers several achievements into one utterance: a body situated somewhere, attention selecting, language and memory making something recognisable as *this*, and an I-position from which the seeing is reported. These powers make a world intelligible, and the crossed zero can be read as if the I they served were one more item in the world — a determinate someone who owns or produces the scene. The stroke that mediates the appearing has collapsed into the apparent self, and the self appears as a thing among things, only larger.

A working example shows the collapse as more than a picture. Consider "a successful person." The criterion can help someone recognise an achievement, compare possibilities, pursue a purpose. Let the criterion become identical with the person's worth, and every encounter that questions it can arrive only as another success or failure under its own terms: refusing the measure looks like failing the measure. The criterion still discriminates; its discrimination is what protects it from examination. The stroke covers the distinction between the person and the measure's image of the person, and covers it efficiently.

The gap this example discloses is also where the notion of God has its work. An account of determinate things runs, eventually, into what it cannot enter as another determination: the condition under which its determinations appear. The notion of God arises at that gap and fills its conceptual place — and what the notion does there decides whether it illuminates or obstructs. It can direct thought toward the unobjectifiable condition, or it can be mistaken for a final item that completes the inventory. The name occupies a place in the account; the reality to which it refers is contained by that place no more than the sky is contained by the word *kha*. So empirical gaps may close, one after another, without anything threatening this pole: each answered question removes a sheltering mystery, and the gap where the notion arose remains what it always was — the condition of there being questions and answers at all. A capacity migrating from human performance into a machine has the same structure: it changes the objective account of mediation, and it leaves the subject unshrunk, because the subject was never the list of functions being migrated.

To uncross the zero is to let the stroke become legible as mediation. The criterion can then be asked where it came from, what it selects, and what experience could change it; judgment, memory and aspiration survive the asking, with their authority answerable to the life they organise. A measure describes the person it measures more accurately once it gives up the demand to be that person's source. The stroke has now become a problem in its own right — and arithmetic poses the problem again, in its own medium, the moment a determinate quantity turns back toward zero.

### Zero keeps one foot outside mathematics

Return to the quotient of one by zero, and stay inside an ordinary field — a system where addition, subtraction, multiplication and division by nonzero elements obey their familiar laws, and zero differs from one. An inverse for zero would be an element $q$ satisfying

$$
0\cdot q=1.
$$

Distributivity alone decides the question. Since $0=0+0$,

$$
0q=(0+0)q=0q+0q,
$$

and subtracting $0q$ from both sides gives $0q=0$ for every element of the field. Requiring the same product to equal one makes zero equal one, against the field's defining distinction. Nor does it help to move to a larger system while keeping these laws: the proof applies to any new element too. If some $z$ were granted the inverse outright, the collapse could be run on every number at once — $a=a\cdot1=a(0z)=(a0)z=0z=1$ — and the whole field would contract into a single value. Multiplication's absorption, the first rule this section taught, is the exact mechanism here.

Zero divided by zero fails differently, and the difference matters. Every $q$ satisfies $0q=0$, so the return test cannot identify a quotient: one divided by zero has no candidate of the required kind, while zero divided by zero has too many to be unique. A common word like "undefined" covers both cases and conceals both reasons.

Cancellation fails at the same place, and the failure is the oldest demonstration. The equality $6\cdot0=17\cdot0$ is true. Cancel the common zero and six equals seventeen. Kaplan, whose cancellation lesson this is, shows by it what multiplication by zero has done: it has made the equality of products compatible with every inequality of factors. His book then offers a cultural explanation of why Indian mathematicians supposedly stopped short of the modern move, and the explanation can be declined without losing the algebra: the prohibition follows from the laws as stated, in any country, and needs no theory of a national mind.

When a mathematical setting does give the exceptional expression a workable life, it says what it changed. A total reciprocal function may assign a value at zero — an assignment, and no inverse, since no multiplication recovers one. The projective line adjoins a point at infinity and gives directions a common destination; the Riemann sphere folds the extended plane onto a sphere where complex infinity has a place; wheels, meadows and transreal arithmetics preserve different operations by accepting different laws. Each construction buys its totality with named laws, and each remains answerable to them. Writing $1/0$ on the page performs none of these changes by itself.

Nonstandard analysis draws the boundary once more, from the other side. Abraham Robinson's enlargement of the numbers admits infinitesimals: magnitudes smaller than every positive real and still not zero. For a real $x$ and a nonzero infinitesimal $\varepsilon$,

$$
\frac{(x+\varepsilon)^2-x^2}{\varepsilon}=2x+\varepsilon,
$$

a legitimate division because the denominator is nonzero. Taking the standard part of the result gives $2x$, the derivative of $x^2$. Substitute zero for the increment before dividing and the expression collapses to $0/0$. This division is legitimate for one reason only: the increment is nonzero. The enlargement is a changed numerical setting with a declared continuation — and no redemption of ordinary division.

Division by zero is the point at which a formal system must disclose itself: the operation cannot proceed, the failure is internal and demonstrable, and the failure turns attention from a missing value to the containing field — its laws, their interdependence, and the one working inside them. Mathematics responds creatively, constructing settings in which the blocked expression lives under new laws; its power grows precisely by knowing what the first setting could not do. Zero is therefore fully mathematical and, at this edge, frame-disclosing: "outside mathematics" names the disclosure of the frame, never the supernatural. Here, at this register, the essay's immutable gap takes mathematical form — a determination's return toward its condition cannot be completed inside the determination's own laws. The formal result warrants the operation; the identification of that operation with the gap between account and condition is the essay's own argued claim, carried by the mathematics and distinct from it.

The opening of this essay borrowed zero as a sign for the subject that cannot be counted as one more object. The loan has been earning interest ever since: the sign gained a place, rules, a retained form, generative constructions, a disclosed frame. It can now be repaid — with the exact history of what it learned.

### The loan returns

Repay it in the coin the sign actually earned. Take the construction Kaplan gives at his book's close, which places the pair where this section has been heading. Set $0/1$ and $1/0$ at the two ends of a row and treat them as formal boundary markers — entries of the construction, evaluations of neither. Between any two neighbouring pairs, add the numerators and add the denominators. The first insertion gives

$$
\frac{0+1}{1+0}=\frac11.
$$

Insert again between the left boundary and this result: $1/2$. Between the result and the right boundary: $2/1$. Each round of insertion crowds new fractions between old neighbours, and the positive rationals ramify between retained endpoints. No inverse of zero has been used at any step. The operation is the mediant: for pairs $(a,b)$ and $(c,d)$ it forms

$$
\frac{a+c}{b+d}.
$$

Two cautions keep the construction honest. It is addition's cousin, and addition it is not: the mediant of $1/3$ and $1/2$ is $2/5$, while their sum is $5/6$. And it operates on written representatives, not on values in the abstract: replace $1/2$ by $2/4$ — the same number — and the mediant with $1/3$ changes from $2/5$ to $3/7$. For positive denominators the mediant provably lies between its parents: if $a/b<c/d$, then $bc-ad>0$, and

$$
\frac{a+c}{b+d}-\frac ab=\frac{bc-ad}{b(b+d)}>0,
\qquad
\frac cd-\frac{a+c}{b+d}=\frac{bc-ad}{d(b+d)}>0.
$$

The endpoint with denominator zero keeps its separate office throughout; the proof never touches it.

What could not be an ordinary quotient has become an effective boundary: the prohibition above stands untouched, and a different relation has made the retained form productive. Writing $1/0$ did not repair the field quotient; a construction gave the form work it can lawfully do.

Now take the sign back, with everything it has learned. The formal-limit opening of this essay used zero as a sign for what cannot be counted as one more object, and every movement since has enlarged what the sign can answer for: a place that holds positions through absence; rules that preserve by addition and absorb by multiplication; a retained zero-divided form that discloses the laws around it; constructions in which nothing, exactly, generates one; a crossed figure naming the fusion of mediation with its product; an operation whose failure makes a frame visible.

Let $0$ name the unobjectifiable condition — the One, self-identity present through determination without entering its inventory as an item — and let $1$ name the determinate appearing field — the All, self-difference, the whole manifest and determinable world together with each indefinite particular in which that world appears. Their relation is written

$$
0/1.
$$

The slash holds the two offices together and keeps them distinct: it permits *and* — both belong to one appearing-act — and it permits *or* — either office can be considered without being confused with the other. The two terms did not first become self-sufficient objects and then acquire a link; their distinction becomes available through the relation itself, which is why the slash is an act of differentiation and a separator on the page at once. This AND/OR is a relational operation: it relates a determination to the condition under which it appears, and no truth-functional input is being processed — it stands beside the Boolean connectives above with its own office, a boundary the last movement's constructions help to keep exact.

Reverse the orientation:

$$
1/0.
$$

The achieved determination turns toward its condition. It can make its source-relation more articulate — this whole section is such an articulation — while the condition remains what it was, unconvertible into one further possession. The arithmetic asymmetry survives inside the notation, and the notation is stronger for it: $0/1$ computes to zero, $1/0$ has no field value, and the native reading records that asymmetry instead of dissolving it. What the symbolic return changed is the sign's capacity to mean this; what stayed fixed is the mathematics that makes the meaning exact.

These are the Two Ones, and their dependence runs in different respects. The manifest field depends on the uncounted condition for its being and coherence; the condition becomes manifest, articulate and knowable through the field. There were never two causes making each other at different moments, and there was never a time when zero existed alone. A particular form belongs wholly to the field while remaining particular, and that belonging gives it no title to the field's source-position; a form can bear its source-relation without enclosing the source as one more form. The history of this section returns inside the relation, each acquisition now load-bearing: the difference between operation and result, between member and set, between boundary and value, between the presentation and the rule that makes it productive — these are the distinctions that keep the Two Ones from melting into one another. Bhāskara's invariant returns here at its earned strength: finite change held before an identity it cannot measure. So does the Kashmiri articulation: real differentiation within an undivided reality, manifestation as the source's own act. What the braid gave as historical co-presence, the notation now carries as structure.

An exact sign, however, does not only describe. It counts — and counting has a criterion, and criteria have consequences for people. A count depends on a unit and on the circumstances under which something is counted as one, and the dependence survives the count's correctness. Suppose an overseer finishes a harvest tally — those present, those owed a share — and finds the arithmetic sound. A woman who worked the field but was not named on the list answers that the reckoning, correct as a total of names entered, still fails her. She does not dispute that ten were written. She changes what the count was for. A reply from someone omitted need not dispute the arithmetic; it can change what the reckoning ought to include.

Reckoning becomes account rendered, and account becomes reasoning and judgment: the Latin *ratio* keeps the vocabulary for it. The word's range does not prove the relation; it keeps the task in view. An account can state what was counted and under what terms the count was made — and once those terms are stated, they too can receive an answer. More information under an unchanged criterion is not always the needed gain. The decisive gain may be a changed criterion, one that lets something previously excluded matter. And this answerability runs on trust before it runs on proof: every count, report and language is received before it can be examined, and examination — the discipline this section has practised on zero's rules, constructions and laws — can then alter what we are prepared to entrust. An account that has been examined and still holds earns renewed entrustment, an entrustment that now knows which laws it rests on.

What holds at the scale of a tally holds at the scale of a consciousness. The achievements of differentiating reason remain — the exact object, the finite knower, the powers of investigation — while their constituting relation becomes available to view, so that recognising the dependence of any viewing changes how a view can be held. This is the co-presence Gebser named: perspective aware of the relation that makes perspective possible, for which merely adding one more perspective would leave the dependence hidden.

Name the notation the root *symbolon*: a mark whose terms remain mathematical signs, logical offices, phenomenological poles and directions of return — simultaneously, and lawfully, because each reading is distinguished and held to its own account. Its overdetermination is not a confusion to be resolved but a generativity to be governed: the two orientations can be inverted, related, and made to take their own relation as a further term, which is the work of the movements to come. What was once carried by the image of a source unchanged by the worlds it absorbs and puts forth can now also be approached through operations that preserve difference, expose an obstruction, and change the containing field when they must. The mathematical sign has become capable of symbolic life again — without ceasing to be a mathematical sign. The token fractured at the threshold of this essay carries the same relation in miniature: a present piece answerable to the counterpart it was divided from.

Zero's return has left differentiation in possession of everything it can do. It has also exposed an old ambiguity at the word *binary*: a two can be the cut that cancels its poles into zero, or the relation that keeps two held across their difference — and the sign just earned the means to tell these apart. Which two, which cut, and what each does with the relation that makes it.
