## 3. Specific failed rhythms and moves to avoid

1. Movement-opening surname procession: "Bertrand Russell met this question…", "Kurt Gödel turned the inquiry towards…", "Ludwig Wittgenstein, in his early work, asks…", "Jean Gebser gives the question a cultural and historical body." (Source-as-agent template; each limit must happen before its name enters, or the name enters as the event's own.)
2. Balanced contribution/limit couplet for every thinker (uniform cadence that flattens real differences of encounter).
3. Announced need as opener: "We need the distinction's power." (M03, assembly.)
4. Announcement verbs replacing occurrence: "makes their relation available as context"; "The fourth turn makes…" — show the turn landing.
5. The M01 five-block pulse (question / inventory / stakes / venue / prospectus), each ~85 words, each completing itself.
6. Retrospective hinge openers ("The limit has brought…", "Distinctions still hold; words can refer…") at M06 — the return should arrive through the earned object, not a summary of gains.
7. Slogan-shaped closers on a timer; a portable maxim ending every paragraph.
8. Stray undeveloped theological aside ("A God lodged in a currently unexplained event would retreat as the event was explained") — a god-of-the-gaps negation introduced only to be dismissed; either earn the boundary or cut it.
9. Whole-paragraph restatement of the previous paragraph's point (the assembly's M02 "fourth/fifth" and M05 transparency passages repeated their own glosses).
10. Mechanically merged long paragraphs (the failed candidate compressed M02 paragraphs 9–10 and 11–12 into single blocks that now do three jobs each without internal articulation) — merges must follow the thought's joints, not a word-count target.
