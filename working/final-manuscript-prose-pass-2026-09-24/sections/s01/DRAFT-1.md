# DRAFT 1 — §0/1 The Integral Threshold

## §0/1 — The Integral Threshold

What knows?

Not a finished description. Every description falls short insofar as it “knows”. We can study the brain, recount a life, reconstruct how an answer was arrived at, or follow the steps by which an instrument reached its judgment, and what we know grows. Follow any thread far enough and you'll have to reach to the moon and back: as the account grows fuller, the demand to account for the world returns to the one who knows it. Completeness cannot sidestep the region in which the account settles; it depends on it more exactly. Our questions return with their answers; what's found doesn't settle the questioner.
<!-- movement:M01 (continuation) -->

I may misremember. I fix a meeting on Thursday and hold to Thursday, and a reply comes back quoting my own message with Wednesday in it. The quotation determines the day more exactly than my remembering did; it does not perform the correction. I have to take it as bearing on what I was sure of, and that taking is not another item the message can carry. Left unread, it leaves the meeting on Thursday and the memory exactly as it stood. Each further document — a calendar, a second reply, the restaurant's record — narrows the day and leaves the taking still mine. I proceed by relying on what no determination of the day has yet determined: that I will recognise the correction when it arrives, know it for a correction of mine, and let it count against me.

The reliance is structural, and it reaches further than memory. Memory corrects memory only through a present act of comparing; language examines language only through the language doing the examining; a criterion is applied, and then questioned, by the one applying it. At every scale the account settles somewhere, and its settling is not one of its findings. This is why the growing account of anything — a brain, a life, an instrument's path to its judgment — never settles the one for whom the account settles. The dependence is not an item the account overlooked; it is the condition of the account's counting as an account.

When the determination is an instrument's, the same structure returns with harder edges. I can check that a reading satisfies the test the instrument was set to run; I can reconstruct the steps by which it reached its judgment. Both checks make the reading more exact, and neither says what the test was for: who asked, what hung on the answer, which alternatives were admitted in advance. That I have to judge — and a more reliable figure makes the judging heavier, since a precise number is harder to set aside than a vague impression. Where the subject of the reading is a life, the category in use can decide which parts of the life are heard and which are treated as irrelevant, and the panel does not display the horizon of its own category. I rely on that horizon to know what the reading is for.

Wherever an account of intelligence begins to govern what intelligence is allowed to be, this private structural fact becomes a public one. An evaluator scores an answer; the score is used; the use trains the next answer. Asking whether the score was computed correctly differs from asking what the score measured and who answers for the asking. Benjamin Bratton's *Agentworld* brief gathers the present diffusion of artificial agency into exactly this perplexity: living among intelligences exceeds the engineering of task completion.[^s01-bratton] Its scenarios are offered as scenarios — a constructed parasociety whose appearances are in many respects fictionalised — and its sharpest observation cuts performed subjectivity apart from functional agency: that a system performs a subject settles nothing about whether one is present, any more than a failure of performance settles absence.[^s01-bratton2] Reserving for humanity whichever measurable capacity no machine has yet acquired would stake the meaning of human experience on a moving technical frontier. The brief's own charge to philosophy — to invent the conceptual systems the present demands, rather than project old axioms onto new circumstances — is received here as an obligation the essay will have to meet in its own person, not as a vocabulary to borrow. Before deciding what another intelligence is, there is the present act of deciding to account for.

We already know through forms — words, images, classifications, remembered situations, instruments. An instrument panel is the honest emblem of such knowing: every reading on it depends upon an observer, a situation and a horizon that the panel does not display. The one thing the panel cannot do is display its own depending on us. So the question has to be taken up where it stands, in the act that would define what knows.

A definition gives something a determinate place. It distinguishes what a term applies to, excludes alternatives, lets consequences follow; without that boundary we could not tell whether we had answered a question or changed the subject. Aimed at the Subject, this power turns on the one wielding it: whatever is to be distinguished must be distinguished while the distinguishing is going on. Refusing every determination would leave both the question and its examiner unlocated. So a definition will be made — made seriously, with a claim committed to it — and what it does will be watched.

Self-knowledge sets the first test. "A is A" makes a minimal journey: the second inscription is not the first, yet the judgment identifies them. The equation 3 = 1 + 2 holds two different expressions equal in a definite respect. Identification requires a difference to traverse and a relation able to traverse it; the copula's office is to hold together what a statement must distinguish in order to say anything at all. Difference makes the encounter possible; identity makes it an encounter with oneself.

Names give the requirement a body. Two people can bear the same name; one person can change theirs. Repeating the sound cannot by itself decide which bearer is meant, and changing the sound does not by itself make a different person. The puzzle has an exact modern form, pressed by Saul Kripke in *Naming and Necessity*: how does a name keep concerning one bearer while every description attached to that bearer changes, overlaps, or fails? Kripke's answer gives the name a rigidity — it reaches the same individual in every situation in which that individual exists, while most descriptions would pick out different individuals across situations — and gives its reach a social shape: the name arrives by a chain of transmission, each user taking it from earlier users of the same bearer, rather than from a private inventory of marks.[^s01-kripke] The inquiry here carries the difficulty further, and the further carrying is ours: beneath the name, a counting. This one; another; this one again. Counting in this sense is the elementary holding-apart of instances before any numeral is recited, and the instance is held relative to a circumstance — the person here, the person remembered, the fictional figure with the same name do not become interchangeable because one string can designate them all. Name, instance and circumstance divide the work: the name articulates; the instance is what is identified; the circumstance makes the relevant sameness and difference intelligible. Predication then has someone to attach to. Frightened, then angry; unskilled, then skilled; certain, then corrected — identification lets the changes belong to someone, which is why a person can answer to an earlier name while disputing the description that came with it.

Now the one who identifies becomes the question. "I see these words" seems to offer three terms for inspection: I, seeing, words. The words can be inspected. The seeing can be investigated — eye, attention, language, the circumstances of reading — and every finding is exact. "I am aware of seeing these words" changes the inquiry rather than extending it: the seeing has become something known, a process that seemed to supply the knower's identity now a presentation to the knower. An account of that process is another presentation. A self-portrait can be faithful; a second can include the artist making the first; a third, the artist studying both. The additions disclose more of the activity and its circumstances, and none takes the place of the present seeing in which the portraits are encountered. Even a portrait of the viewer is a portrait *for* a viewer.

A specific historical effort brings the point to a sharpness worth returning to. In 1641 René Descartes undertook to withhold assent from whatever could be doubted: the senses, which had deceived him; the world before him, which could be a dream; even arithmetic, if a deceiver of unlimited power wished him deceived. The doubt is performed, and it stops at its own performance. Let him be deceived as much as can be imagined — the being deceived is still there, occurring, if only as doubt. What he then does with the finding is a second move. He asks what this I is and determines it: a thinking thing, whose activities include understanding, affirming, denying, willing, imagining, sensing.[^s01-descartes] The certainty of the occurrence and the further account of what occurs are different achievements. An account of thought can be questioned while the questioning is still given; the occurrence held fast in a way the determination of the doubter never could. For this inquiry the sentence has to be opened: I am aware of thinking. Thought is the means — the determinate activity through which something is grasped; awareness is the fact in which the activity appears; and the I is exposed not as a conclusion proven by thinking but as the condition in which thinking, proving and doubting can be given at all.

That presence was there before the reflection began. Watching a thought changes my relation to the thought; it does not manufacture the knowing in which the change is apparent — otherwise the watcher would need a watcher before anything could be known, and every beginning would postpone itself again. The correction matters because a natural misreading lies that way: that each deepening reflection produces a new, finer knower behind the last. What each reflection produces is a new describable relation. Recognition, when it comes, is a change of identification — the image of myself remaining active while its claim to be the source of experience is relinquished.[^s01-recognition]

The relation between knowing and known can be tested from both directions at once. Make them wholly indistinguishable, and the difference through which anything can be known disappears: nothing would be presented to anything. Sever them completely, and what is known could never reach the knower: presentation without an address. Each attempt destroys a condition of the occurrence it meant to explain. Knowing therefore requires a unity that bears difference — what appears belongs to the occurrence of knowing, while its particular content does not exhaust the presence to which it appears.

The defining proposition takes its stand there: **the Subject is the whole that any coherent representational system participates in and necessarily fails to contain.**[^s01-native] The sentence's exact recovered wording names God; it is reappointed here at the Subject pole by explicit authorial instruction — one defining movement, read from either end of the relation it performs — and no separately worded Subject sentence exists to substitute for it.[^s01-god] "Whole" is fixed at once: the whole as approached in self-presence, from the side of the knowing; a person's present inventory of knowledge is something that appears within it, and the inventory's growth is the opening question again. Knower, means and known keep their distinct offices inside the one relation.

A positive determination has been made, and it commits its maker. This sentence and its present apprehension belong inside the relation it describes; the sentence cannot exempt its own use from the test it gives every other representation. Apply the test. The words have made the Subject a subject of predication — inspectable, disputable, translatable. To contain what they define, they would have to contain their own present apprehension *as that apprehension*, not as a further report. Add "and someone is understanding this sentence": something has been added to understand. Describe that understanding: more of the same. The additions can be exact and cumulative, and none of them undertakes the understanding on the reader's behalf. The attempt to close the account renews the relation it was meant to close.

This is the definition's necessary failure, and its object is precise: the demand for final enclosure. Body, biography, perception and language remain investigable, and every genuine discovery changes what can truly be said of them; what fails is only the further demand that the description take over the office of its own knowing. Indefinability has acquired a determinate meaning — the Subject is identified through the relation that makes exhaustive objectification fail. Then the failure changes the use. What had seemed a missing final portrait turns out to have its substitute already at work: the presence doing the looking. The sentence returns its reader through what it says to the knowing in which it is said. The frame survives; what was being used as a cage has become a window, an opening onto what it cannot hold captive. Enclosure fails; disclosure succeeds, because the failed demand makes participation legible — different respects of one performance. A vague profession that everything exceeds words would have established neither the relation nor the place where its own words changed office.

Assertion, failed enclosure and successful disclosure belong to one inquiry whose terms have changed through use. And the inquiry has a body the first formulation never mentioned: a person reading, an inherited language, a concern that gives the question its force, a standard under which success was sought, a standpoint from which failure became visible. These were active before they were named; naming them makes the definition's circumstances part of what an adequate account must answer for. Source, circumstance, perspective, other persons, use — the fourth turn gathers them, and the gathering is a real gain. It remains an account. I can explain, accurately, that the reader participates, place my own image inside the explanation, and call the result a complete description of involvement; the accuracy leaves the first-person turn still to be made. A diagram of my participation cannot recognise on my behalf the presence through which I see the diagram.

So the turn is made where it can be — from within. I have tried to know myself by producing something I could know: words, images, acts, explanations. Each is present to me. The knowing sought through them was already here in the seeking. **I am.** Nothing new has arrived — no watcher behind the watchers — and nothing of the describable person has been erased. I can know a thought as mine without requiring the thought to be everything I am. What began as "the Subject is" has returned as "I am": the same relation, spoken this time from inside it.

The same performance runs in the other direction, and the two directions meet. Begin with the whole and call it God; begin with the intimacy of knowing and call it Subject: the defining movement is one, which is why the reappointment above is a reading of the same sentence and not a second theorem. A representation of everything, together with its production and its apprehension, still occurs within what is; universality of reference supplies no exterior to reality. The whole is present to itself here, in the knowing the definition could indicate and could not replace. Nothing follows about a finite personality's authority over any other life: my present self-description belongs among the determinations whose claim to own their source has just failed.

An old image makes that non-possession visible enough to think with. The *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers*, an anonymous medieval gathering, answers its second question — what God is — with an infinite sphere whose centre is everywhere and whose circumference is nowhere.[^s01-sphere] An ordinary sphere gives its centre a unique position and stations a boundary beyond every interior. The image withdraws both at once: access to the centre cannot be monopolised at one place, and the enclosing edge cannot be stationed past the whole. Presence is intimate without becoming private property. My immediacy supplies no perimeter within which another life must be contained — and inflating the image into a very large finite ball would lose its whole operation, since a large ball still has a centre one could stand at and a skin one could stand within. Nicholas of Cusa received and transformed this tradition four centuries of transmission later; it was no coinage of his, and its force here does not depend on who first wrote it down.

The definition's return can be said with a further precision it has walked into. Śaiva epistemology distinguishes, within one occurrence, the knower, the means of knowing and the known — pramātṛ, pramāṇa, prameya — and in Mark Dyczkowski's exposition awareness is luminous because an experience is manifest, colour or sound or thought, and reflexive because that manifesting is already known.[^s01-dycz] The theorem has led back to this knowing without replacing it, and the distinction it teaches is the one the whole performance needs: producing a further reflection would only add an instrument, while recognition is the means returning to their work. So the return completes itself in ordinary discrimination. A person still has a name, a history, a body, obligations; another person can answer in ways no account anticipated. "I am the one who has achieved recognition" is itself another description to examine. Definition has kept its power and given up the demand to possess the source of that power — which is what a faithful definition is for.

Nothing above has weakened the cut. Without a difference between this and that, a name cannot reach its bearer, a question cannot distinguish its answers, a mistake cannot be corrected. The theorem failed one demand on definition — final enclosure — and left the achievement of determinate sense standing. What the failure actually raises is a question about the cut itself: what does a boundary do with what it excludes?

Two witnesses, working independently, describe that operation from different sides. In a letter of 1674 to Jarig Jelles, Spinoza gives the geometric face: determination is negation, and his example is figure — a body has this shape because its extension is bounded this way, the outline making a portion of extension measurable as this figure.[^s01-spinoza] Dignāga gives the semantic face: a word makes its reference determinate by excluding other referents; what does not count under the term participates in what does. The Buddhist analysis in the form Pind's translation preserves refuses the cheap version of its own doctrine: the referent is a thing qualified by preclusion, not the preclusion itself — exclusion does its work without replacing the thing with a bundle of absences.[^s01-pind] The two operations are not identical, and need not be. Figure and word agree in this: the boundary does positive work through what it leaves out, and the achieved form stays answerable to the field it was cut from.

One Greek practice gives the dependence a body. A *symbolon* could be a token broken between parties: each keeps a piece, and the pieces mean nothing singly — a vaguely similar piece will not fit. The edge is particular; the meeting is the test; the surviving half carries an absent relation into an encounter that can fail. Possessing the piece confers neither possession of the other party nor the power to decree the fit. What is absent can still answer the form that is present.

Now put a number where the token was. A meeting counts its attendees; the count can be impeccable and still count nothing about who is affected by the decision. The defect is not in the arithmetic but in the substitution — attendance standing in for affectedness without an argument — and no refinement of the tally repairs a change of criterion. The excluded bear on what the count was entitled to mean. The danger shows its teeth when attendance becomes the sole test of standing: those outside are denied standing to question the criterion, because they were not present. The account now protects the condition under dispute; the response that could have exposed the limit arrives translated into a failure to satisfy it. The instrument that made a relation legible has been turned against the relation reaching the judgment. A finite account was never the danger; the danger is an account adjudicating every challenge to the basis of its own authority. The same question can now be put to a system of proof: what happens when its rules are asked to determine the conditions under which the rules themselves count?

<!-- movement:M04 — the formal-limit genealogy -->

Mathematics met that question at the turn of the twentieth century, and met it as a construction. Bertrand Russell, sorting which collections logic may admit, considered the collection of all collections that are not members of themselves. Is it a member of itself? If it is, it qualifies for exclusion; if it is not, it qualifies for inclusion. The contradiction follows from the collecting permission itself — no overlooked member repairs it. Russell's repair, the theory of types, changes which expressions may be formed: a totality may not contain members defined in terms of that very totality, and the levels this introduces block the offending construction.[^s01-russell] The prohibition does positive mathematical work — a disciplined domain of reasoning becomes possible that was not possible before. What has been repaired is the permission to form and substitute expressions; the repair settles nothing about every question a theory can raise on itself.

Two decades later Kurt Gödel reached a limit of a different make. He arithmetised: in a formal theory strong enough for arithmetic, statements and finite proofs can be encoded as numbers, so the theory can represent its own proof-relation. A sentence can then be built that says, arithmetically, that no number codes a proof of this very sentence. If the theory is consistent, no such proof exists — a proof would supply the witness whose non-existence the sentence asserts — and Gödel's original stronger hypothesis, ω-consistency, excludes a proof of the negation as well.[^s01-godel] The sentence is well formed; its difficulty is derivability within a theory whose formation Russell's example had already disciplined. The theorem's force is bound to its hypotheses — effective axiomatisation, arithmetical strength, the stated consistency conditions — and without them the slogan that every system is incomplete is a different claim with its argument missing. What the theorem gives, under its conditions, is exact: growth in provability is real, an extension can prove what its predecessor could not, and the distinction between arithmetical truth and provability in a given theory does not close.

Ludwig Wittgenstein's *Tractatus* carries the same pressure into language. A proposition pictures a state of affairs by sharing logical form with it; that form shows itself in the saying and is not one more said thing among the others — the limit of what can be said is no item inside it. The book's own propositions obey its finding: the reader climbs the ladder and throws it away, having used it to see.[^s01-wittgenstein] What a representation can disclose and what it can state about its disclosing have come apart. The later *Philosophical Investigations* moves the pressure into practice: follow a rule, and if every application needs a further interpretation, no formulation of the rule supplies the point where interpretation begins to act. The signpost is followed within a practice that learns, maintains and corrects it; obeying and disobeying are distinguished by what is done, not by a feeling of compliance. A rule's life is not contained in the expression as meaning in a box.

Formal construction can also change what a calculus admits. G. Spencer-Brown begins with the act of drawing a distinction — a marked state, an unmarked state. Let the distinction be re-entered: let a state's value be the operation of crossing applied to it. In the two-state reading nothing stable answers — crossing turns either state into the other, and the required self-equality alternates without end. Francisco Varela's response is a construction change rather than a commentary: admit an autonomous value, with rules for its reduction alongside the others, and the re-entering form has somewhere to be.[^s01-varela] No consciousness follows from the third state; what follows is the exact point this section needs — a self-referential operation can demand a reorganisation of the space that was to contain it.

Nicholas of Cusa had put the finite mind's case with a diagram centuries earlier. Inscribe a regular polygon in a circle and increase its sides: the fit improves without limit, yet every finite polygon has straight edges and the circle has none. The improvement is genuine and never completes as possession. Cusa works the image inside his theology of learned ignorance — the knowledge, precisely had, that we do not know even as we come to know more truly.[^s01-cusa] Modern mathematics can define the convergent sequence and its circular limit exactly; Cusa's pressure survives the exactness intact, because it concerns the finite knower's relation to what it measures. And the polygon differs from the sphere that opened this inquiry: one image approximates within a figure, the other withdraws the exclusive centre and the enclosing edge. Two operations, each doing work the other cannot.

Contemporary philosophy of science names the stakes in its own register. *The Blind Spot* — Adam Frank, Marcelo Gleiser and Evan Thompson — diagnoses the surreptitious substitution by which scientific abstractions are taken as more fundamental than the experience through which observation, measurement and justification become possible.[^s01-blindspot] Reading an instrument, noticing a discrepancy, judging that a repetition bears on a result: these belong to inquiry's life. A record can make them available for examination; understanding and using the record are further acts of the same life. The authors' diagnosis stays within their programme, and the essay's stronger claim — that the omission is structural rather than incidental — is argued on grounds of its own.

Gathered, the genealogy teaches by non-identity. A formation permission, a derivability bound, the divide between saying and showing, a rule's life in practice, a calculus's admitted states, an approximation that never possesses, an abstraction that quietly omits the experiencer who warrants it — each is a differently made limit, and the common proposition is argued across their differences: a sufficiently articulate system cannot convert every condition of its articulation into ordinary internal terms without remainder. The remainder is no wall at the edge of things; it recurs through every enlargement, which is why improvement never exhausts it. And before any result is arbitrated, the investigation has already been undertaken in reliance — on language, memory, instruments, the testimony of others; the arbitrating arrives into a relation it did not create. An old aphorism holds both sides together: "The Mind of Man is just a series of failed attempts to deal with the Unknown, in the process of which, however, we deal with a lot of unknowns."[^s01-unknown] Its second clause is indispensable: the failure of final capture leaves a world of effective investigations, each able to give something real. The reliance is a relation to be made responsible — questioned, corrected, relinquished where consequence requires. How can what forms disclose include a working relation to their own conditions? The genealogy's constructive half — repairs that changed what a system could admit — points the way: the response is perspective becoming transparent to what makes it.

<!-- movement:M05 — Gebserian diaphaneity -->

Depth had to be discovered once. Jean Gebser reads the change in the means by which a world was rendered: Giotto coaxing weight and space out of flat gold ground; Petrarch stopping on a mountain and finding a landscape worth the look; Alberti fixing the painter's craft in the geometry of the visual pyramid; Leonardo carrying perspective into optics and machine.[^s01-gebser] A world became proportioned through a positioned eye. That is Gebser's claim exactly: people had always seen, but seeing acquired a structure — the scene rendered from a sector, the viewer placed outside what is viewed, the world newly available because positioned. The achievement is permanent and partial at once. It makes explicit, and it fixes.

His name for what the fixing conceals is the sector's claim to exclusive validity, and his account of consciousness runs through structures — archaic, magical, mythical, mental — that do not expire when the next arrives. They remain co-present: capacities, and distortions, in any present life. Integral consciousness, in his vocabulary, is the mutation in which the co-presence becomes transparent: the mental-rational achievement retained while its sector-claim is released, the earlier structures available without being either revived or dismissed. A person can participate in an image and discern, at once, how that participation organises perception. Nothing is lost but the pretence that the sector is the world.

Martin Heidegger, working from inside the same metaphysical tradition, names the placement from its other face. The modern age, he argues, is defined by the world becoming picture — the world conceived and grasped as picture — and by man's becoming *subiectum*, the relational centre in which beings as a whole must stand.[^s01-heidegger] What Gebser traces through art history, Heidegger exposes as authority: the representing subject becomes the standard against which disclosure is judged, and the judging position disappears behind the picture's apparent self-sufficiency. The two diagnoses arose independently; no documented exchange connects them; the twinning is this essay's own, argued because each supplies what the other withholds — Gebser the historical body, Heidegger the metaphysical mechanism and its technological consequence.

Neither diagnosis alone says what to want instead. Adding perspectives changes nothing essential: the collection still has a collector, and the collection's frame still decides what counts as a perspective. Averaging them would be worse — it erases exactly the differences that make their meeting informative. A myth, a perceptual report, a logical inference and a physical measurement accomplish different things, and their conjunction is legible only where the terms of meeting stay visible, including where a translation fails. *Diaphaneity* names this: a perspective becoming transparent to the context that makes its seeing possible, without ceasing to see.

Light through stained glass carries the whole relation. White light arrives with a capacity for colour that no window gave it; each pane admits and selects; the lead seams hold difference in place; a coloured image falls into the room where someone stands. Look only at the image and the aperture disappears; look only at the glass and the event of lighting goes missing. Move the sun or the viewer and the floor-pattern changes, and the change is information: this pane colours, that seam obstructs, the room's angle matters. Finishing the picture on the floor never produced the light that made it visible, and the source is disclosed through the ordered variations, never assembled by averaging the colours. Nothing requires pretending the glass has vanished. Mediation becomes intelligible through what it lets through, and what it lets through can change the next act of mediation.

Transparency of this kind is the opposite of detachment. It asks being-with: attending to a situation's text and subtext, caring what becomes of it.[^s01-p5] The one who retains the power to decide is involved through the decision; impartiality has to be won inside the relation, by attention to what can challenge the judgment and to whose experience its categories exclude. The failure mode has an old name in this essay's working: the absent Father — the ultimate standard invoked but never brought into the room, a vacancy that a surrogate is always ready to occupy. The final measure, the ruler, the account to which every reply must conform: each borrows a transcendence it did not earn. The defining performance has already exposed the substitution — an account of the whole belongs within the whole — and the practical consequence is a specific fidelity: the finite account answers for what it does.

A constructed case shows the same structure inside an answering system. An assistant is asked why a request went unanswered. Its archive holds a complaint that no answer arrived; its permissions exclude the folder with the original delivery failure; it reports that the recipient declined to respond. A note that the answer was produced "in context" changes nothing. The context was already working: access rules and retained evidence had helped determine what could count as an answer before anyone described them. Displaying the exclusion may let a person discover the problem; correcting the permission or the inference changes what the next inquiry can establish. Description and intervention are different offices, and both matter, because the world-picture problem has entered the machinery of answering: the frame helps produce the picture, and the picture conceals the frame's effects.

Co-presence also settles how this essay will receive what it cannot yet explain. A dream is real as an experience without its figures having stood beside the bed: the terror was lived; who the figure was and what caused it remain open. A numinous encounter, a sensed presence, a vision, an ordinary perception — each can be received as something that occurred, to someone, before one register decides what it ultimately was. Occurrence, report, symbolic articulation and causal interpretation keep different evidential tasks, and keeping them distinct is what preserves the phenomenon for inquiry. Treating "psychic" as a synonym for unreal removes the datum before inquiry begins; the person changed by the dream need not deny the change because the image was not an external object.

The contextual turn runs both ways and keeps its asymmetry. The world becomes articulate to someone, and what becomes articulate discloses the sitter's situation — body, language, history, other people, material means all enter what can appear and what can be done. And my account of another person cannot supply their reply. Addressed, they can change the terms I approached them in, and the account becomes adequate only when that change can reach its judgment — not when it is filed as another item under a judgment held fixed. The section can now ask its final question with everything earned behind it. A condition operative everywhere — in the failed enclosure, the unowned cut, the bounded proof, the fixed sector — has been named in images but given no sign. What mark could position the condition that positions everything else?

<!-- movement:M06 — the return to zero -->

What the essay proposes, for trial, is zero. Let it enter as a promissory sign and no more. The written zero is perfectly determinate — a glyph, a digit, a mark with a history this section has not yet given it. What is proposed here is its office: `0` marks the condition under which counting occurs, the place no counted item occupies, since every counted item takes the position of one. The present knowing did not become absent when no final portrait could take its place; what the mark leaves open is already active in the act that reads it. History, arithmetic and formal derivation are withheld: the section has earned the position, and the position is all it claims.[^s01-zero]

For form held within movement, one image before the hand-off. David Bohm liked to point at the vortex a river makes around a stone: a shape you can name, standing in the water, sustained only by the water's moving — divert the flow and the vortex is gone without a trace of remains.[^s01-bohm] In his proposed implicate order, relatively discrete things stand to a primary movement the way the vortex stands to the river: their relations belong to their formation. The image focuses the question the section ends with. The stable form is not its own sustaining, and the physical pattern does not supply its own apprehension.

The inquiry returns to where it started, with different attention. "I see this": knower, seeing and seen distinguishable within one occurrence; something appears, can be attended to, enters memory, takes a name. What knows has turned out to be inseparable from what it knows through — and the means are real enough to investigate and to change. How does awareness differentiate itself into the means by which it knows, and the world it knows through them?
