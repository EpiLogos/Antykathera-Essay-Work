# §0/1 — The Integral Threshold

<!-- movement:M01 -->

What knows?

Not a finished description. Every description falls short insofar as it “knows”. We can study the brain, recount a life, reconstruct how an answer was arrived at, or follow the steps by which an instrument reached its judgment, and what we know grows. Follow any thread far enough and you'll have to reach to the moon and back: as the account grows fuller, the demand to account for the world returns to the one who knows it. Completeness cannot sidestep the region in which the account settles; it depends on it more exactly. Our questions return with their answers; what's found doesn't settle the questioner.

I may misremember. A letter can put the day, the words, or the person right where my recollection had them wrong, and still the letter does not perform the finding that it bears on what I had been sure of. That finding is mine to make; leave the letter unread as a correction and both letter and memory stay where they were. A witness, a date, a second letter can go on determining the event more exactly, and each addition still leaves the correction to be received. I am relying on a knowing that the documents cannot turn into one more document among themselves. What I know of myself can be mistaken in content—habit another person sees before I do, motive that becomes apparent only through what I do—while the occurrence in which a mistake becomes apparent is not another forgotten item waiting on the shelf. Discovery changes the person who undertakes it; increasing the accuracy of the recollection does not dispense with the knowing in which accuracy and error are recognised. When the determination is an instrument’s reading, I can check whether the answer satisfies the test the instrument was set to run, and I can reconstruct the steps by which it reached its judgment. Those checks make the reading more exact. They do not decide whether the test answered the question that mattered: who asked, what hung on the answer, which alternatives were admitted. I have to judge that, and a more reliable figure makes the judgment heavier, because the figure is harder to set aside. When the subject of the reading is a life, the category in use can decide which parts of that life are heard and which are treated as irrelevant. The panel does not display the horizon of the category. I rely on that horizon in order to know what the reading is for. Effective agency, an account of its formation, and the presence of experience have different evidential requirements; the first two can be investigated while the third remains open. Benjamin Bratton’s *Agentworld* brief places the diffusion of artificial agency inside this larger perplexity: living among intelligences exceeds the engineering of task completion.[^s01-1] Reserving for humanity whichever measurable capacity a machine has not yet acquired would make the meaning of human experience depend on a moving technical frontier; a machine’s acquisition of a capacity does not by itself establish that it experiences its activity. Before deciding what another intelligence is, there is work in the present act of deciding. We already know through forms—words, images, classifications, remembered situations, instruments. What happens when one of these forms is asked to contain the one who knows through it?

A definition gives something a determinate place. It distinguishes what a term applies to, excludes alternatives, and allows consequences to follow. Without that boundary we could not tell whether we had answered a question or changed the subject. Defining the Subject turns this activity upon the one undertaking it. We must distinguish what is being sought while preserving the activity through which the distinction becomes intelligible. Refusing every determination would leave both tasks undone. Self-knowledge already requires both difference and identity. Something must differ from the presentation through which it becomes known—otherwise there is no disclosure to undertake—yet identity must also be retained, or the supposed self-knowledge would concern something else. Difference makes the encounter possible; identity makes it an encounter with oneself.[^s01-native] The word “is” carries this crossing. In “A is A,” the second occurrence differs from the first as an inscription, while the judgment identifies what they stand for; “3 = 1 + 2” holds two different expressions equal in a definite respect. Identification requires a difference to traverse and a relation through which it can be traversed. This is the copula’s positive office: it keeps together what the statement must distinguish in order to say anything. Two people can bear the same name; one person can change theirs. Repeating the sound cannot by itself decide which bearer is meant, and changing the sound does not by itself produce a different person. In *Naming and Necessity* Saul Kripke presses how a name can continue to concern one bearer when the descriptions presently associated with that bearer change, overlap, or fail—how designation can lean on a historical chain of transmission rather than on a private inventory of identifying marks.[^s01-kripke] Once the names are fixed, necessary identity concerns that same bearer across counterfactual circumstances; the descriptions I presently attach need not travel with the name in order for the name to keep its hold. The inquiry pursued here carries that difficulty further into the discrimination of an instance: this one, another one, the same one again. Counting here is the elementary holding-apart of instances before a numeral is recited; naming gives that discrimination an expression which can be repeated and shared. The person here, the person remembered, and a fictional figure with the same name do not become interchangeable because a word can designate them all. Name, instance and circumstance therefore have separate work: the name articulates; the instance is what is identified; the circumstance makes the relevant sameness and difference intelligible. Predication then says something of the one identified—frightened, angry, skilled, changed—so that identification lets these changes belong to someone, and the person can answer to an earlier name while disputing the description attached to it. Keeping that difference alive is part of knowing them. “I see these words” appears to offer three terms ready for inspection: I, seeing, words. The words can be inspected; the seeing can be investigated through the eye, attention, language and the circumstances of reading. “I am aware of seeing these words” changes the inquiry: seeing is now explicitly something known, a process which seemed to supply the knower’s identity become a presentation to the knower. An account of that process is another presentation; its increased detail does not make the difference disappear. A self-portrait may be faithful; a second can include the artist making the first; a third the artist studying both. The additions disclose more of the activity and its circumstances. None takes the place of the present seeing in which the portraits are encountered. Even a portrait of the viewer is a portrait *for* a viewer. Representation can succeed at every stage while the demand that it replace its own apprehension remains unfulfilled.

René Descartes reaches the question by trying to withhold assent from everything he can doubt. In the second *Meditation*, the supposition that he is deceived cannot remove the occurrence of being deceived; doubting the apparent world has left the doubting present. He then asks what this I is and determines it as a thinking thing, whose activities include understanding, affirming, denying, willing, imagining and sensing.[^s01-descartes] The certainty of the occurrence and the further account of what occurs are different achievements. An account of thought can be questioned while the questioning is still given. The cogito opens, for our inquiry, into: I am aware of thinking. Thought becomes distinguishable as a means of knowing within the presence which its occurrence already involves. That presence was there before the reflection began. Watching a thought produces a changed relation to the thought; it does not manufacture the knowing in which the change is apparent. Otherwise the watcher would require another watcher before anything could be known, and every supposed beginning would postpone beginning again. An image of myself can remain active while its claim to be the source of all experience is relinquished. Recognition concerns this change of identification, not the production of a more rarefied image behind the first.[^s01-recognition] Make knowing and known wholly indistinguishable, and the difference through which something is known disappears. Sever them completely, and what is known could never reach the knower. Each attempt destroys a condition of the occurrence it is meant to explain. Knowing requires a unity which bears difference: what appears belongs to the occurrence of knowing, while its particular content does not exhaust the presence to which it appears. The distinction is real as a difference of office; the unity is real as the occurrence in which the offices meet. Yet a sentence stating their conjunction can itself be known, and its correctness would not make it the one who knows it. Neither an isolated term nor a completed description of the relation supplies the presence through which the terms and their description have become available. The account has reached its own participation in what it accounts for.

The defining proposition takes that participation as its subject: **the Subject is the whole that any coherent representational system participates in and necessarily fails to contain.**[^s01-native] Here the whole is approached in its self-presence. It is not the inventory of what a particular person presently knows. Words, objects, memories, means of knowing and the inquiry into their relation appear within it; the knowing presence cannot be added to their inventory as the last item that completes it. The definition speaks from the Subject pole of this whole relation, preserving the distinction between the knower, the means and the known. A positive determination has been made. It identifies what the term is to reach and distinguishes that referent from a further object. It also commits the definition to its own claim: this sentence and its present apprehension belong within the relation it describes. The sentence cannot exempt its own use from the test it gives every other representation. Apply the test. The words have made the Subject a subject of predication. They can be inspected, disputed, remembered or translated. To contain the presence they define, they would have to contain their present apprehension *as that apprehension*, rather than offer another account of it. Adding “and someone is understanding this sentence” adds something to understand. Describing that understanding adds more. The additions can be exact, but no addition undertakes understanding on the reader’s behalf. The attempt to close the account renews the relation it was supposed to close. This is the definition’s necessary failure. Its object is the demand for final enclosure. Body, biography, perception and language remain investigable, and every genuine discovery changes what can be said about them. What fails is the further demand that the resulting description take over the office of its own knowing. Indefinability has acquired a determinate meaning: the Subject can be identified through the relation which makes exhaustive objectification fail. At first a final portrait appeared to be missing; following the attempt shows that the presence sought was already active in looking. The sentence returns its reader through what it says to the knowing in which it is said. The frame survives, but what was being used as a cage has become a window: an opening onto what it cannot hold captive. Enclosure fails; disclosure succeeds because the failed demand makes participation legible—different respects of one performance. A vague profession that everything exceeds words would have established less: it would neither have determined the relation nor shown where its own words changed office. The faithful definition remains definite enough to be challenged and becomes capable of receiving what its first demand had excluded. Assertion, failed enclosure and successful disclosure belong together once the inquiry’s terms have changed through being used. There is a person reading, with a body, an inherited language and a concern which gives the question force—conditions active before they were named. Source, circumstance, perspective, other persons and use enter what an adequate account must answer for: the standard under which success was sought and the standpoint from which failure became recognisable. Another speaker’s answer, an inherited naming chain, the instrument I am reading, and the circumstance of application alter what an adequate account must answer for; they do not arrive as an inventory after the fact, but as the change in what the definition is now answerable to. Even this fuller account can leave me describing a Subject over there. I may accurately explain that the reader participates, place my own image within the explanation, and call the result complete involvement; its accuracy would still leave the first-person turn to be made. A diagram of my participation cannot recognise on my behalf the presence through which I see the diagram.

I have tried to know myself by producing something I can know. I have found words, images, acts and explanations. Each is present to me. The knowing sought through them was already here in the seeking. **I am.** The sentence has returned in the first person: what had been treated as the subject of a proposition is recognised as the Subject of the present act. The recognition does not depend on finding one more thing to say about myself. It concerns the presence without which none of those things could have been sought, found or disputed. Context makes my involvement describable; recognition identifies the one undertaking the description. No new knower has arrived, and the describable person has not disappeared. I can know a thought as mine without requiring that thought to be everything I am. What began as “the Subject is” has returned as “I am.” The theological direction begins with the whole and calls it God; the subjective direction begins with the intimacy of knowing. Their defining movement is one. A representation of everything, its production and its apprehension still occurs within what is. Universality of reference supplies no exterior to reality. The God/Subject identification follows the performance back into this participation: the whole is present to itself here, in the knowing that the definition could indicate but could not replace.[^s01-god] The exact recovered sentence names God—“God is the whole that any coherent representational system participates in and necessarily fails to contain”—and Subject enters here by authorial reappointment of that same defining movement, not by an invented verbatim Subject sentence. A finite personality has acquired no authority to speak for every other life. Its present self-description belongs among the very determinations whose claim to own their source has failed. An older theological image makes that non-possession encounterable. The anonymous *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers* asks what God is and offers, in its second definition, an infinite sphere whose centre is everywhere and circumference nowhere—a formula later received and transformed in the Cusan tradition, not coined there as a private invention.[^s01-sphere] An ordinary sphere assigns its centre a unique position within a boundary. The image withdraws both restrictions: access to the centre cannot be monopolised at one place, and the circumference cannot be stationed beyond the whole. Presence is intimate without becoming private property. My immediacy supplies no perimeter within which another person’s life must be contained. Drawing a very large finite ball would lose the image’s whole operation. Śaiva epistemology names knower, means of knowing and known as distinguishable within one occurrence. In Mark Dyczkowski’s exposition, awareness is luminous because an experience is manifest—whether a colour, a sound or a thought—and reflexive because that appearing is already known.[^s01-2] A common mirror can display a reflection without knowing it; another reflection in that mirror would add an image rather than supply the missing knowing. Conscious reflexivity belongs to the occurrence itself. Seeing, attending and remembering can each become known within this awareness, while their differences remain consequential for what is disclosed. Recognition returns those means to their work. A person still has a name, a history, a body and obligations; another person can answer in ways the first person’s account did not anticipate. The statement “I am the one who has achieved recognition” is itself another self-description to examine. The return is complete when the gain can enter ordinary discrimination: a name can identify, a judgment can be corrected, an image can disclose, and the person addressed remains able to answer. Definition has retained its power while relinquishing the demand to possess the source of that power.

Without a difference between this and that, a name cannot reach its bearer, a question cannot distinguish its possible answers, and a mistake cannot be corrected. Definition still gives thought somewhere to act. The preceding failure was the failure of a particular demand upon definition; it leaves the achievement of determinate sense intact. Benedict de Spinoza approaches the power of a limit through figure. In his letter to Jarig Jelles, “Determination is negation” concerns finite, determinate bodies: a figure has this shape because its extension is bounded in this way.[^s01-3] The outline makes a portion of extension apprehensible as a figure; its limit is constitutive of what can be measured. Dignāga’s account of *apoha* supplies a different operation. A word excludes other referents in making its reference determinate; what does not count under a term participates in what does. Pind’s translation preserves an important positive distinction: the referent is a thing qualified by preclusion, rather than mere preclusion.[^s01-4] Exclusion can make a referent available without replacing it with a collection of absences. A boundary does positive work through what it leaves out. Keeping the excluded relation available changes how the achieved form is used: a word can refer, a figure can be measured, and a distinction can guide action without claiming to have absorbed the field of its application. One material sense of the Greek *symbolon* gives this dependence a body: corresponding pieces held for later recognition.[^s01-5] One piece is brought to meet its counterpart. The edge is particular; a vaguely similar piece will not necessarily fit. The surviving form carries an absent relation into an encounter which can test it. Possessing the piece gives neither possession of the other party nor the power to decree the fit. What is absent can answer the form that is present. Consider a count of those present at a meeting. It can be impeccably accurate and still fail to count everyone affected by the decision. The defect arises when attendance is made to stand for affectedness without an argument for the substitution; refining the attendance total cannot repair that change of what the count was entitled to mean. The excluded people bear upon the meaning of the tally. We can correct a count under a given test, and we can reconsider whether that test answers the question. A sound distinction may survive both examinations. But suppose attendance becomes the sole test of standing: those outside the meeting are denied the right to question its basis because they were not present. The account now protects the condition under dispute. A response which could expose the limit is translated into a failure to satisfy it. The instrument which made a relation legible prevents that relation from reaching the judgment. The danger does not lie in having made a finite account. It lies in allowing that account to adjudicate every challenge to the basis of its own standing. The lost counterpart becomes consequential precisely because the counting continues to work. The same question can be put to a language or a system of proof: what happens when its rules are asked to determine the conditions under which those rules themselves count?

The same pressure reaches formal articulation. Bertrand Russell met it while trying to determine which constructions mathematical reasoning could safely admit. Suppose every definable condition determines a set. Collect all sets which are not members of themselves. Is this collection a member of itself? If it is, it satisfies the condition for exclusion; if it is not, it satisfies the condition for inclusion. The contradiction follows from the unrestricted collecting permission. No overlooked member will repair it. Russell’s type-theoretic response changes which expressions may legitimately be formed. His vicious-circle principle states: “No totality can contain members defined in terms of itself.”[^s01-6] Distinguishing logical levels blocks the offending construction. A prohibition here has positive mathematical work: it makes a disciplined domain of reasoning possible. What has been repaired is the permission to form and substitute expressions, not every possible question a mathematical theory can raise about itself. Kurt Gödel turned toward what sufficiently strong, effectively axiomatised arithmetic could prove about its own proofs. Expressions and finite deductions can be encoded by numbers, allowing the theory to represent a relation between a statement and a proposed proof. A sentence can then be constructed which says, arithmetically, that no number codes a proof of that very sentence. If the theory is consistent, it cannot prove this sentence: a proof would supply the numerical witness whose existence the sentence denies. Gödel’s original stronger condition of *ω-consistency* also excludes a proof of its negation.[^s01-7] This sentence is well formed. Its difficulty concerns derivability within the specified theory, where Russell’s example exposed a defective formation permission. An extension can prove something its predecessor could not. Under the relevant hypotheses, a fresh sentence raises the limit for the extension. Growth in what can be proved is real; it does not remove the distinction between a truth of arithmetic and a proof available within a particular theory. The theorem’s force depends on effective axiomatization, arithmetical strength and the stated consistency conditions. Without them, the claim that every system is incomplete would be a different claim with its argument missing.

Ludwig Wittgenstein, in the *Tractatus*, asks what lets a proposition represent at all. A proposition can picture a state of affairs because it shares logical form with what it depicts; that form shows itself in the representing—it is not a further depicted fact alongside the others. “What can be shown cannot be said.” The closing ladder brings the book’s own propositions within this difficulty: understanding has to pass through their elucidatory use rather than preserve them as a final enclosure of what they make visible.[^s01-8] The later *Philosophical Investigations* follows a rule into its application. If every application needs a further interpretation, another formulation of the rule supplies no point at which interpretation begins to act. The signpost is followed within an established practice. Obeying and violating a rule are distinguishable there; merely thinking oneself obedient does not establish obedience.[^s01-9] The inquiry can examine how a practice is learned, maintained and corrected. Its life is not contained in an isolated expression waiting to determine every possible use from outside the occasions of using it. Alfred North Whitehead approaches the formation of a definite thing as an accomplishment in its own right. An actual occasion receives a diversity of data and acquires its character through the way it gathers them; *concrescence* names the formation of this novel togetherness, *satisfaction* its completion as an achieved unity available to subsequent becoming.[^s01-10] What is immediate in forming has a different standing when another occasion inherits it as a fact. A description of the completed occasion belongs to that latter relation and can be true without supplying the forming which its truth concerns. G. Spencer-Brown’s calculus begins with the act of making a distinction. When that distinction re-enters the space it distinguishes, a two-state reading encounters a difficulty: crossing changes either state into the other, so a value required to equal its own crossing has no stable solution among those states—repeated crossing alternates them. Francisco Varela takes up this self-reference problem by introducing an autonomous third state and rules governing its reduction alongside the others.[^s01-11] What becomes possible depends on that changed construction; the return into the marked space has made the calculus reconsider what it can admit. Such repairs enlarge what can be done. Their differences tell where the work falls: the permission to form an expression, the rules under which a proof is available, the practice which gives a rule application, or the states a calculus admits. The Subject argument meets them through its own distinct pressure: the act of understanding an enlarged account still has to be undertaken. No mathematical result is asked to perform that recognition for us.

Nicholas of Cusa approaches the finite intellect’s relation to truth through a mathematical image. Increase the sides of a regular polygon inscribed in a circle: the fit improves, while each finite polygon still has straight edges. The improvement is genuine; the particular approximation has not become identical with its measure. Cusa uses the image within his theology of learned ignorance.[^s01-12] Modern mathematics can define a convergent sequence and its circular limit exactly; his philosophical pressure concerns the finite knower’s possession of infinite truth, not a prohibition on mathematical convergence. The polygon’s approach and the earlier infinite-sphere image perform different operations: approximation within a figure, and the withdrawal of an exclusive centre and enclosing edge. Qualified predication makes the same difference available in ordinary speech. Flasch’s reading of Eckhart holds positive and negative theology within the same philosophical labour: a true determination need not possess the divine exhaustively.[^s01-13] “Insofar as” gives this difference an ordinary expression. To consider a person insofar as they act justly is to attend to a real determination without making justice an inventory of that person. A predicate can be wholly true in the respect it names without becoming the whole truth of its subject. Relinquishing finality differs from retracting the truth which has been articulated. Adam Frank, Marcelo Gleiser and Evan Thompson’s *The Blind Spot* diagnoses the substitution by which abstractions are taken as more fundamental than the experience through which observation, measurement and scientific justification become possible.[^s01-14] Reading an instrument, recognising a discrepancy and judging that a repetition bears on a result belong to inquiry’s activity. A record can make those acts available for examination; understanding and using the record are further acts. The immutable gap is this non-coincidence renewed through determination. Its immutability does not mean that our information cannot improve. I may learn which memory of mine was false, which rule produced an error, which source was excluded or which bodily condition altered my perception. Such discoveries can transform what we know and how we know it. The transformed inquiry still has to be undertaken. An account of that undertaking adds determinate knowledge without substituting for the knowing in which the addition is received. An early aphorism puts both sides of this labour together: “The Mind of Man is just a series of failed attempts to deal with the Unknown, in the process of which, however, we deal with a lot of unknowns.”[^s01-unknown] Its second clause is indispensable. The failure of final capture leaves a world of effective investigations, each capable of giving something real. A God lodged in a currently unexplained event would retreat as the event was explained. The source named in the defining performance has another relation to discovery: the explanation and its apprehension belong within what is. Its presence does not depend on keeping a particular fact unknown. A finite inquirer nevertheless has to proceed. Memory, language, instruments and other people’s testimony are relied upon before any result can certify them all. Inquiry can test and strengthen those dependencies; the testing continues through some actual means. Trust is present in that continuation. It becomes responsible when its particular dependencies can be questioned, corrected or relinquished. How can what forms disclose include a working relation to their own conditions?

Depth around a positioned viewer once made a world newly proportioned. Jean Gebser follows that articulation through Giotto’s emerging depth, Petrarch’s landscape, Alberti’s visual pyramid and Leonardo’s optical craft: the scene becomes intelligible through a determinate relation between eye and world.[^s01-15] Perspective makes something newly explicit while giving its disclosure a sector and a position. That power remains indispensable. Gebser’s *aperspectivity* releases the sector’s claim to exclusive validity. His archaic, magical, mythical and mental structures remain co-present as capacities and distortions within a present life; they are not discarded stages whose force ended when the next arrived. Integral consciousness is a further mutation in which their activity and relations become transparent while the mental-rational achievement is retained. A person may participate in an image and discern how that participation organises perception. The power of the image remains alive in a changed relation to it. Heidegger’s account of the world-picture approaches the other face of this placement. The modern world-picture concerns the world grasped as something represented before a subject, with the representer becoming its normative centre. The world so presented and the standpoint which judges its presentation belong to one event.[^s01-heidegger] A view discloses from somewhere; its produced picture can become the standard by which further disclosure is judged. Gebser makes the positioned sector historically visible; Heidegger exposes the authority its representing subject comes to exercise. The judging position can disappear behind the apparent self-sufficiency of the picture. Accumulating every available viewpoint still leaves the situation from which they are collected and judged; averaging them would erase the differences which make their meeting illuminating. A myth, a perceptual report, a logical inference and a physical measurement accomplish different things. Their conjunction needs the terms of their meeting to be visible, including where a translation fails or a contradiction remains. *Diaphaneity* names this readability of conditions through what appears. Light enters a room through stained glass. Each pane admits and colours it differently; the lead holds the pieces together and marks their limits. An image falls inside, where a viewer encounters its shape and colour. Looking only at the image conceals the aperture which made it possible; looking only at the glass misses the event of light passing through it. Source, pane, seam, room and viewer participate in the appearance without becoming interchangeable parts of one picture. The light carries a capacity for differentiated colour before this window gives it these particular passages. The panes select and transform what can pass; the projected pattern discloses their work. Finishing the picture on the floor does not produce the light which made it visible. The source has priority even though the viewer may encounter the image first and only afterwards look towards the aperture. Understanding proceeds back through a dependence which the original act of seeing already involved. As the light changes, colour crosses a surface which was previously dark; as the viewer moves, the image acquires another relation to the room. A condition of the earlier appearance becomes legible through the difference. A pane can be recognised as a source of colouring, a seam as an obstruction; the new view may change how the window is approached or made. The shared source is disclosed through these transformations, rather than assembled by averaging their colours. Nothing requires pretending the glass has vanished. Mediation becomes intelligible through what it lets through, and its disclosure can change the next act of mediation. An account can acquire this transparency. Its sources, selected contrasts, language and purpose become readable in what it discloses. The reader can follow a claim together with the passage through which it arrived. A mathematical proof retains its inferential obligations; a historical attribution, its textual warrant; an image, the action through which a relation becomes encounterable. Their differences give the joined work its articulacy. Seeing through a situation also requires being with it: attending to its text and subtext and caring what becomes of it.[^s01-p5] Someone who retains the power to decide remains involved through the decision and its consequences. Detachment cannot make that involvement cease. Impartiality has to be worked for within the relation, through attention to what can challenge the judgment and to whose experience its categories may have excluded. The temptation becomes acute when an ultimate standard is invoked but cannot itself be brought into the room. The absent Father is present in the demand for a last word. A local image can occupy his place: the final measure, the ruler, the account to which every reply must conform. Its authority is exercised here while its source is placed beyond answer. The defining performance has exposed the substitution. An account of the whole belongs within the whole; it cannot use the whole’s transcendence of its terms to exempt those terms from examination. Seeing through the surrogate restores the relation to the source which its claimed finality had obscured. The same issue becomes operational in a constructed technical example. An assistant is asked to explain why a request went unanswered. Its accessible archive contains a complaint that no answer arrived, but permissions exclude the folder containing the original delivery failure. It reports that the recipient declined to respond. Adding a note that the answer was produced “in context” changes nothing about this result. The relevant evidence must have an authorised route into the inquiry, its bearing on the inference must be recognised, and the conclusion must become revisable. The context was already doing work before anyone described it: access rules and retained evidence helped determine what could count as an answer. Transparency has a practical test here. Displaying the exclusion may help a person discover the problem; correcting the relevant permission or inference changes what the next inquiry can establish. Description and intervention have distinct offices, and both may be necessary. Co-presence also gives experience a standing which no explanatory picture can grant retrospectively. A dream can be real as an experience without its figures having stood beside the bed. The terror has been lived; the figure’s identity and the cause of its appearance remain questions. Occurrence, report, symbolic articulation and causal interpretation have different evidential tasks. Receiving a report does not authenticate every account of the occurrence. Treating “psychic” as a synonym for unreal would remove it before inquiry began. A person changed by a dream need not deny the change because the dream’s image was not an external object. The contextual turn therefore has two directions. The world becomes articulate to someone; what becomes articulate also discloses the person’s situation. Body, language, history, other people and material means enter what can appear and what can be done. Their reciprocity retains an asymmetry: my account of another person cannot supply their reply. Addressed, they can change the terms under which I approached them. The uncounted person can return as a speaker whose answer bears upon the construction of the question.

The limit has brought the activity of knowing into view. Distinctions still hold; words can refer; arguments can compel; an encounter can correct an error. What has changed is the standing of the achieved form. Its success includes a relation to the conditions through which it succeeds, and those conditions remain able to bear upon its next use. For the condition which cannot be placed among the account’s objects, let zero enter as a promissory sign. The written zero is itself determinate. It marks the difference between acknowledging a place in an account and occupying that place with another representation. The present knowing has not become absent because no final portrait can take its place. What the mark leaves open is already active in the act which reads it. This use marks a limit of representation; it has not yet supplied zero with its mathematical operations, nor claimed that mathematical zero, emptiness, consciousness and subjectivity are one thing under a single proof. There is a positive activity to investigate in every determinate experience: how something is distinguished, held together, remembered and made significant. A learned word, a revised distinction or another person’s answer can change what next becomes possible to think. To inquire into Mind is to enter this formative activity without mistaking its instrument for its Subject. Bohm describes determinate things through movements of enfolding and unfolding. His vortex is a relatively stable form of moving water: a recognisable shape persists through the activity sustaining it. In his proposed implicate order, relatively discrete things emerge within a more primary movement, their relations belonging to their formation.[^s01-16] This gives achieved form a sustaining movement without erasing its determinacy. The physical pattern does not supply its own apprehension; it brings the question of formative activity into focus. The inquiry returns to “I see this” with a different attention. Knower, seeing and seen are distinguishable within one occurrence. Something appears, can be attended to, enters memory, acquires a name. The means are real enough to investigate and to change. How does awareness differentiate itself into the means by which it knows, and the world it knows through them?
