**The tests.**

- *Same beat?* No. Two openings are questions, and they are not the same beat: "What knows?" opens the whole inquiry at its unlocated limit, with no predecessor to receive; "Where does counting begin?" opens a fork inherited from §1's closing ambiguity about the word *binary* (L505) and immediately stages two authoritative answers in conflict. The remaining six are six different events: a call out of street noise, an arithmetic cancellation that matters, a compass instruction, a dated exchange of letters, the book's own scene of writing, a finished thing laid down.
- *Same grammatical machine?* No — eight distinct machines: interrogative fragment; action declarative; locative arithmetic declarative; dialectic interrogative; imperative; absolute date fragment; existential present; present passive.
- *Thesis → examples → prospectus?* No. §0/1 runs question → scene (the Thursday misremembering, L18) → structural claim. §0 runs scene → analysis → Śaiva doctrine (L171). §1 runs arithmetic act → hand demonstration (L303) → history. §2 runs question → fork → premise. §3 runs construction → completed figure → mapping to §2 (L706). §4 runs dated scene → the correspondence → the dream (L927). §5 runs scene → the event that happened to this book (L1121) → the triad. §5→0 runs gesture → what others inherit (L1286) → the tense of the maker's question. Prospectus sentences occur at movement ends (e.g. L730, L1111), not as an opening shape.
- *Different inherited pressures?* Yes — each opening receives its predecessor's own closing question rather than a reusable frame: §0 receives the act that could not be enclosed (L169); §1 receives "In a numeral such as 205…" (L287); §2 receives the exposed ambiguity of *binary* (L505); §3 receives "what a stroke can do… is the question the mathematical substrate now owes an answer" (L692); §4 receives the form "met… by the one whose recognising was never outside the mathematics" (L889); §5 receives "which distinctions can be made inspectable and experimentally consequential" (L1111); §5→0 receives "What happens when such an instrument is released into lives that were never parties to its commission?" (L1273). §0/1 has no predecessor and takes the reader's own question as its inheritance.
- *Does each immediately feel like its section's subject?* Yes, case by case: knowing (the question that cannot be enclosed); differentiation (a name pulling one voice out of noise); zero as working sign (a digit whose absence changes value); two firsts in one field; exact construction as the section's instrument; psyche and order held between two thinkers who refuse each other's annexation; a person whose means have become inspectable artifacts; the made thing facing its consequences.

**Verdict: PASS.** No shared generator template is audible. The eight openings share nothing reusable — no common grammatical machine, no common argumentative shape, no common rhetorical beat — and each lands in its section's subject with its own inherited pressure by the end of the first sentence. The one superficial resemblance (two questions) dissolves on inspection: the two interrogatives open different registers (limit vs fork) with different grammatical bodies (bare fragment vs self-answering fork).

### The removal test, run over the table

Removing the thinker names would leave only generic prose nowhere that I could find. Every tabled paragraph states the doctrine, episode, quotation or calculation in the prose beside the name; the name locates and holds standing, it never carries the claim alone. The three densest name-per-claim sites, checked hardest:

- **L586** (Le Bon, Jung, Desmet, Iakovou in one paragraph): each name carries distinct stated content — the scattered crowd, the quoted sentence with locator, the disputed diagnostic with its bounded standing, the constitutive objection. Strip the names and the claims remain, attributed-less but intact.
- **L833** (Priest, Kapsner, Siderits, Westerhoff): the semantics are built in the paragraph before the names arrive; the four objections are each stated. Names mark the dispute's parties, not its content.
- **L1338** (Heidegger, Plato/Diotima, Aristophanes): the quotations are carried verbatim with locators, the essay's departures are marked as its own ("The connection is the essay's own… not Heidegger's"), and the built-not-awaited conclusion is argued.

No site was found where a name substitutes for an argument, and no failure was solved by proposing added names.
