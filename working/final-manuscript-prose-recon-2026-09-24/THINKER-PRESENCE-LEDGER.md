# Thinker presence ledger

**Purpose.** Recover a thinker as a situated difficulty, not a surname available
for prose enrichment. This is a writing ledger for the assembled candidate
(`working/manuscript-source-enrichment-2026-09-20/assembly/THE-RETURN-OF-ZERO.md`),
not a replacement bibliography and not authority to add quotations. “Frank’s
development” below means the authorial work recorded in the named carriers; it
is never retrospectively assigned to the thinker.

## Ten-point cards

### 1. Descartes — doubt that cannot erase its occurrence
1. **Work / situation:** *Meditations* (1641), where radical doubt seeks an
   indubitable starting point rather than a general theory of subjectivity.
2. **Problem brought:** whether the act of doubting can remove the occurrence of
   doubt.
3. **Exact usable distinction:** the certainty that thinking/doubting occurs is
   not yet the determination that the knower is a “thinking thing.”
4. **Authorial development:** the distinction opens the Subject theorem’s
   difference between an occurring recognition and a portrait of its bearer.
5. **Present explicit home:** §0/1 M02; §0/1 source return, “D. The means can
   become known without becoming their knower.”
6. **Tacit influence elsewhere:** §§0 and 5 whenever a representation or tool
   becomes inspectable without supplying its own knower.
7. **Return it earns:** a definition is compelled to include its own use and
   cannot secure an exterior observer.
8. **Do not reintroduce:** a generic “cogito proves consciousness” sentence,
   Cartesian substance as the essay’s ontology, or a quotation not verified
   against the selected edition.
9. **Prose pressure:** make doubt occur before naming Descartes; preserve the
   directness of the opening rather than adding a history-of-philosophy stop.
10. **Recovery paths:** `.../sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/descartes/descartes-1641-meditations/SOURCE.md`;
    `working/manuscript-source-enrichment-2026-09-20/sections/s01/SOURCE-RETURN.md`.

### 2. Kripke — name, bearer, transmission, circumstance
1. **Work / situation:** Harvard lectures published as *Naming and Necessity*
   (1980/1981); the work contests descriptivist sufficiency for naming.
2. **Problem brought:** how a name continues to concern one bearer when
   descriptions change, overlap, or fail.
3. **Exact usable distinction:** rigidity, causal/transmissive naming practice,
   and necessary identity are distinct from present descriptions.
4. **Authorial development:** Frank continues from name to identity, instance,
   circumstance and the pre-nominal discrimination “this one / another / this
   one again.”
5. **Present explicit home:** §0/1 M02–M03.
6. **Tacit influence elsewhere:** the called name in §0; token, count and
   standing in §§1–2; a source selected for an action in §5.
7. **Return it earns:** an account becomes answerable to another person and
   their correction, not merely to its terms.
8. **Do not reintroduce:** attribute Frank’s pre-nominal count theory to
   Kripke, add an unverified Kripke quotation, or let the passage become a
   classroom summary of possible worlds.
9. **Prose pressure:** let a changed name or shared name create the need before
   theoretical vocabulary arrives.
10. **Recovery paths:** `.../sources/analytic-philosophy/kripke/kripke-1981-naming-and-necessity/{SOURCE,NOTES}.md`;
    `working/manuscript-source-enrichment-2026-09-20/sections/s01/SOURCE-RETURN.md`.

### 3. Nicholas of Cusa and the anonymous *Liber* — approximation and non-enclosure
1. **Work / situation:** Cusa receives and transforms the older “centre
   everywhere, circumference nowhere” sentence; he is not its origin.
2. **Problem brought:** how finite approximation can matter without possessing
   the infinite.
3. **Exact usable distinction:** polygon/circle concerns increasing finite
   approximation; infinite sphere concerns no exclusive centre or enclosing
   circumference.
4. **Authorial development:** these images clarify failed enclosure in the
   Subject/God performance without supplying its proof.
5. **Present explicit home:** §0/1 M04 and the theological direction of M06.
6. **Tacit influence elsewhere:** travelling-jigsaw criterion change; §5→0
   finitude and divine limitation.
7. **Return it earns:** a finite account may be exact and revisable without
   becoming source.
8. **Do not reintroduce:** a Kashmir transmission story, Cusa as author of the
   aphorism, or “infinity” as decorative atmosphere.
9. **Prose pressure:** retain the two images’ unlike operations; do not combine
   them into one elegant sentence.
10. **Recovery paths:** `working/final-manuscript-prose-recon-2026-09-24/sections/s01.md`;
    `.../sections/s01/SOURCE-RETURN.md`; `.../sources/classical-philology/`.

## Use rule

For the final prose pass, a thinker is explicit only when their specific
problem is active in the local movement. Their later presence should normally
be tacit: an inherited distinction changes the prose’s action without
repeating its introduction. The source return, source house and any protected
`NOTES.md` remain read-only evidence routes.

## Descartes

1. **Problem/pressure:** what survives radical doubt, and how the indubitable act differs from a theory of the substance performing it.
2. **Occasion:** the seventeenth-century *Meditations* seeks a foundation for knowledge by methodic doubt.
3. **Vocabulary/distinctions:** doubt, cogito, thinking thing, clear and distinct perception.
4. **Encounter:** `sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/descartes/descartes-1641-meditations/SOURCE.md`; assembly M02.
5. **Frank’s response:** the certainty is that doubting/thinking occurs; “thinking substance” adds a determination. Frank reopens the event as awareness of thinking.
6. **Change in Frank’s argument:** the subject-defining act begins as a performed certainty whose bearer cannot be exhausted by its represented “I.”
7. **Authority:** Descartes warrants the cogito’s text and historical operation.
8. **Comparison/resonance:** the crossed zero and the modern subject use Descartes as a historical hinge, not their derivation.
9. **Departure:** Frank refuses to identify Subject with the determinate res cogitans.
10. **Terminology after attribution:** *cogito*, methodic doubt, thinking thing; later “subject” must carry Frank’s definition, not silently remain Cartesian.

## Kripke

1. **Problem/pressure:** how a name can keep referring to one bearer across counterfactual situations without reducing to a bundle of descriptions.
2. **Occasion:** the 1970 Princeton lectures, published as *Naming and Necessity*, answer descriptivist theories associated especially with Russell.
3. **Vocabulary/distinctions:** rigid designator, bearer, possible/counterfactual situation, necessity, reference.
4. **Encounter:** `sources/analytic-philosophy/kripke/kripke-1981-naming-and-necessity/{SOURCE,NOTES}.md`, especially Frank’s notes on pp. 8–15; assembly M02.
5. **Frank’s response:** beneath naming he finds a counting/instantiation layer, with identity fixed in a circumstance before phonetic naming; “this/I/you” point toward context-sensitive rigidity.
6. **Change:** M02 gains the difference among name, instance and circumstance, preparing objective internality and the personed determinations.
7. **Authority:** Kripke warrants rigid designation and the anti-descriptivist pressure.
8. **Comparison:** links to apoha, Kaplanian demonstratives, Śaiva grammatical persons and world-state spaces are Frank’s argued crossings.
9. **Departure:** Kripke does not supply the psychoid count/name layer, QL context, or `0/1`.
10. **Terminology:** rigid designation and counterfactual situation may remain alive; “instance/circumstance” should be marked as Frank’s extension.

## Infinite-sphere tradition and Nicholas of Cusa

1. **Problem/pressure:** how finite geometry can indicate an infinite whose centre and boundary defeat ordinary location.
2. **Occasion:** Definition II of the anonymous medieval *Book of the Twenty-Four Philosophers* precedes Cusa; Cusa’s fifteenth-century learned ignorance uses polygon/circle and infinite-geometric limits.
3. **Vocabulary:** centre everywhere, circumference nowhere; learned ignorance; coincidence of opposites; polygon and circle.
4. **Encounter:** `sources/religion-theology/book-twenty-four-philosophers/book-twenty-four-philosophers-hudry-1997/SOURCE.md` (resolve if path changes); `sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/cusa/cusa-on-learned-ignorance/SOURCE.md`; assembly M02/M04/M25.
5. **Response:** the sphere becomes an enacted failure of closure; Cusa’s polygon makes approximation’s remainder visible.
6. **Change:** gives the manuscript a whole image for located centres without a final circumference and later differentiates sphere, plane and torus.
7. **Authority:** the anonymous book owns the aphorism’s medieval textual origin; Cusa owns his learned-ignorance geometry.
8. **Comparison:** QL, subjectivity and objective internality refract the image.
9. **Departure:** do not credit Cusa with originating the infinite-sphere definition; topology does not retroactively become his theorem.
10. **Terminology:** learned ignorance and coincidence of opposites can recur; the sphere formula needs first attribution to the anonymous tradition.

## Spinoza

1. **Problem:** what makes a finite determination finite.
2. **Occasion:** Letter 50 to Jarig Jelles (1674) contrasts finite figure with the infinite.
3. **Vocabulary:** determination, negation, figure, finite.
4. **Encounter:** `sources/classical-premodern-philosophy/spinoza/spinoza-1674-letter-50-jelles/SOURCE.md`; assembly M03.
5. **Response:** definition cuts a form from a field and gives both a gift and a danger.
6. **Change:** supplies the Western geometric face of M03’s claim that a mark is made by exclusion.
7. **Authority:** exact letter and historical formula.
8. **Comparison:** paired with Dignāga’s semantic exclusion; neither is made to say the other.
9. **Departure:** Frank adds retained ground, return and ethical/institutional consequences.
10. **Terminology:** *determinatio est negatio* may recur after attribution; “cut,” “gift/danger” are manuscript terms.

## Dignāga, apoha, Pind, and later scholarship

1. **Problem:** how general terms refer without real universals.
2. **Occasion:** Dignāga’s sixth-century *Pramāṇasamuccaya* V; later Dharmakīrti development and modern philology.
3. **Vocabulary:** *anyāpoha*, exclusion of the other, not-non-p, nominally versus verbally bound negation, causal-functional reference.
4. **Encounter:** `sources/indian-philosophy/pind/pind-2009-dignaga-anyapoha-dissertation/SOURCE.md`, q001 PSV V:11d p.85 and qualified q002 p.183; `sources/indian-philosophy/siderits/siderits-tillemans-chakrabarti-2011-apoha/SOURCE.md`; assembly M03/M10/M38.
5. **Response:** exclusion is productive only if its detour remains in the achieved reference; Pind’s positive qualified referent prevents reduction to “mere exclusion.”
6. **Change:** gives M10 an exact semantic practice and later permits a bounded comparison with softmax/argmax.
7. **Authority:** Pind q001 is quotation-ready; q002 is provisionally assigned by Pind; the edited volume remains secondary-collation-limited.
8. **Comparison:** “exclusion that remembers,” mono–poly and machine-learning selection are Frank’s formulations.
9. **Departure:** Frank restores a positive subject/whole relation that Buddhist nominalism does not supply, without declaring apoha defective on its own terms.
10. **Terminology:** *apoha* and exclusion may continue after first teaching; the ML analogy must keep its novelty and boundary visible.

## Cross-ledger safeguards
