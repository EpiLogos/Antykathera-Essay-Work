## 1. Machine cadence — uniform paragraph pulse

**What it looks like:** a proposition; two or three clarifying sentences; a contrast or qualification; a compact maxim-like closer. Then the next paragraph repeats the pulse.

**Example — M01, lines ~16–22:**

> What is it that knows? … Each inquiry can discover something that matters. Yet as its account becomes more complete, the account is being understood. …

> What I know of myself can be mistaken. … Our question concerns their relation.

> The distinction matters wherever an account of intelligence begins to govern … The stakes become personal when the account is an account of us …

> Benjamin Bratton's *Agentworld* brief places … The first two can be investigated while the third remains open.

> Before deciding what another intelligence is, there is work to do …

Each unit is competent. Together they feel like five separately generated answer-blocks rather than one opening that gathers pressure. The thought (knowing / self-knowledge / governance of intelligence / Agentworld / return to the present act) is real; the **delivery** chops it into same-sized moves.

**Where this is not a fault:** M02's theorem performance (lines ~53–71) often sustains longer spans because the self-application genuinely needs duration. Preserve those.

**Obligation for the later writer:** merge neighbouring pulses where one movement of thought is being sold as three completed paragraphs. Do not impose a word-count quota; restore continuity where the break is only model packaging.

**Recurring templates across openings and many movement starts:**

The problem is not that openings are similar; it is that **§2, §3, §4** too often open as conceptual briefings rather than from the pressure their own recovered material can supply.

## 3. Premature paragraph breaks

The fault is not a universal excess of short paragraphs. Most prose paragraphs occupy a narrow medium range. The fault is the blank-line habit: an inferential increment is repeatedly given a fresh paragraph even when it completes the thought immediately before it.

**Example — §2 M19 (lines ~656–688):**  
The Mono–poly / Two Ones / otherness / monopoly / differentiation / trust / knowledge-as-trust sequence is one continuous derivation. It is currently parcelled into roughly eight short-to-medium paragraphs, each ending as if a local conclusion has been reached. The later writer should allow longer spans where premise → complication → consequence belong together — especially around Mono–poly and the first statement of dia/syn.

**Example — §3 M25 (lines ~1022–1052):**  
The eight-determination traversal is already a sequence. Cutting after nearly every determination produces lecture-slide cadence. Where the native field needs the whole chain held, keep more of it in one movement of prose (without turning the section into a dump of the theorem spine).

**Counter-example worth protecting:** Pauli's egg dream (M31, lines ~1356–1373) already sustains duration across narrative, equation and consequence. Do not break it further.

## 4. Announcement instead of occurrence

Soft forms dominate over crude “this section will…”:

- **“makes … available” / “becomes available”** (~24+ cluster): often names a real gain, but frequently substitutes for showing the gain.  
  M02: “The fourth turn makes their relation available as context…” — the contextual turn is real; “makes available” is the soft verb that lets the prose announce disclosure without fully inhabiting the circumstance.

- **“must now” / “now has to”** (~12): §3 and §4 openings; tour-guide tense.

- **“gives X its … / gives the next inquiry”** (~6 “next inquiry” cluster at L160, L1666, L1755, L1994, L2144): latter sections repeatedly hand work forward by naming “the next inquiry” rather than letting the present pressure compel the turn.

**Genuine anticipation to preserve:** a particular unresolved question the reader can carry (e.g. attendance vs standing at the end of M03). Announcing that something will matter later is not that.

## 5. Generic connective enrichment

Connectivity is repeatedly buffed by saying what would be shown.

**Where it shows in the assembly:**

- Residual connective tissue appears as explanatory restatement around already developed cores.
- §2 M19's trust/Mono–poly paragraphs often explain the relation's importance in successive glosses rather than advancing a new necessity.
- §4 M31's Jung/Pauli sequence is strong on disagreement, but some bridging sentences restate that “the common ground” is at stake without adding a new pressure from the correspondence.

**Test for the later writer:** if deleting the paragraph leaves the preceding and following arguments still able to meet, the paragraph was connective furniture.

## 6. Abstract-subject repetition

Frequent grammatical subjects: *the relation, the account, the field, the distinction, the return, the instrument, the inquiry, the development*.

**Necessary uses:** when the Subject theorem, Mono–poly, or O:I genuinely takes a relation as its object.  
**Fault uses:** when an abstract noun + soft verb lets the prose proceed without anyone or anything doing something.

**Examples of actionless progression:**

> The distinction matters wherever an account of intelligence begins to govern…  
> The psychoid relation … now has to be encountered…  
> The return is complete when…

**Repair direction (conceptual, not synonym swap):** restore agents and acts — a person recognising, a scribe marking absence, Hephaestus binding, a patient disagreeing, Brahmagupta stating a rule — wherever the argument's own material supplies them. Keep abstract subjects where the argument *is* about the relation as such.

## 7. Reflexive model antithesis

Classic `not X but Y` is nearly gone. The residual forms:

| Form | Count | Judgment |
|---|---:|---|
| `does not` / `is not` / `cannot` | 211 / 98 / 141 | Many are load-bearing (enclosure failure; fusion/severance; 1/0; apoha). Many are rhythm: deny a near neighbour then affirm. |
| `rather than` | 90 | Often operational; often phantom opposition. |
| `not merely` family | 27 | Usually softens into a preferred term. |

**Genuine boundaries to protect (do not delete mechanically):**

- M02: fusion removes difference; severance removes availability.  
- M02: enclosure fails; disclosure succeeds — distinct respects of one performance.  
- Formal limit / Gödel / saying–showing / empty set generation.  
- Synchrony ≠ diachrony ≠ syn/dia.  
- Physics / psychodynamics / metaphysics separation in plasma thread.

**Phantom / rhythm examples to audit:**

- Repeated “X does not by itself …” chains in M01 (capacity / experience / score reliability): three neighbouring soft denials of the same shape.  
- “Recognition … rather than the production of a more rarefied image” — real distinction, but the `rather than` is the model hinge; the positive claim can often lead.

## 8. Slogan → explanation → slogan

Still present in softened form: compact assertion; explanatory expansion; polished closer.

**Example — M19 close (lines ~688–688):**

> The two logics begin within this necessity. **Dia-ballein…** … **Sym-ballein…** Both are positive operations. Severance denies…; fusion absorbs… A life needs the powers…

The bold definitions are necessary. The surrounding frame (“begin within this necessity” / “A life needs…”) is the slogan envelope. Genuine aphorisms lose force when every movement ends with a portable maxim.

**Protect:** “I am.”; “What is it that knows?”; Mono = continuous/infinite whole / Poly = differentiated/local multiplicity (authorial correction); Compassion as loving sensitivity to origins… — when they arrive as earned turns, not as paragraph closers on a timer.

---

## 9. Tour-guide narration

Less crude than “this chapter will examine,” more common as:

- “The two logics begin within this necessity.”  
- “This gives otherness its force.”  
- “The technological possibility begins here.”  
- “O:I develops this problem through six technological facets.”

These sentences name the essay's next office. Prefer beginning the office.

---

## 10. Artificial symmetry

**Can… can… / A can…, B can…** (~65 `can`…`can` windows): often justified (two people / one person changing names; identification and predication). Elsewhere they create completed parallelism before the thought has moved.

**Three- and four-part sequences:** eight determinations listed as a bullet-like paragraph (M25, M31 close) risk catalogue cadence. The native field *is* sequential; the prose must still make some determinations *happen* rather than only be enumerated.

---

## 11. Imported erudition and random allusion

**Improved since Frank's complaint**, but residual risks:

- **Surname + work supplies/asks** procession in M03–M04 (Spinoza, Dignāga, Russell, Gödel, Wittgenstein early/late, Spencer-Brown, Varela, Whitehead, Eckhart/Flasch, Blind Spot) — each has a real office; the *sequence* still risks feeling like a reading list performed in one movement. The SOURCE-RETURN shows why each is there; the prose must make the *problem* present before the name, and let later returns be tacit.
- **Le Bon / Jung / Desmet / Iakovou** — their sequence is more disciplined than a bare list, but surname stacks remain a failure mode.
- Avoid new “enrichment” that strings suggestive terms (plasma, torus, diaphaneity, psychoid, agentworld) without a living pressure.

### Pattern-specific judgment and protection

| Pattern | Why it is a fault here | Obligation to protect |
|---|---|---|
| Machine cadence | Proof, source encounter, dream, myth and technical case all acquire the same polished paragraph pulse, so heterogeneous thought sounds procedurally generated. | Let paragraph form answer to the local act; do not manufacture variation or flatten exact exposition. |
| Uniform openings | Strong local materials are converted within one or two paragraphs into the same distinction–qualification–prospectus template. | Keep each section's dependency on what precedes it and its actual new burden. |
| Premature breaks | Blank lines repeatedly turn one derivation or narrative movement into a stack of equally weighted cards. | Keep breaks where proof stages, source standing, narrative turns or genuine changes of governing movement require them. |
| Announcement | “Now has to,” “makes available” and similar phrases certify a transition before the material has made it occur. | Preserve explicit warrants where a formal transition is non-obvious; remove the compère, not the dependency. |
| Generic connective enrichment | Transferable connective paragraphs create smoothness without specifying what returns, changes or compels the next step. | Name the exact unresolved pressure; do not solve this through connective synonyms. |
| Abstract-subject repetition | “Relation,” “field,” “account” and “return” often conceal who acts, what mechanism changes, or where authority lies. | Retain these as defined technical objects where they are genuinely the subject. |
| Reflexive antithesis | Necessary boundaries and rhythmic denials share one syntax, so proof-level negation loses audible distinction from a model tic. | Keep formal negation, source-bearing disagreement, operational distinctions, ethical limits and claim-status boundaries. |
| Slogan rhythm | Portable opening and closing maxims make the explanatory middle feel pre-decided and weaken earned aphorisms. | Preserve canonical formulations and compressions that later work actually carries. |
| Tour-guide narration | “Can now,” “the next,” and “this gives” conduct the reader through prepared stations instead of letting the returning problem compel movement. | Keep constructional imperatives where the reader must perform a mathematical act. |
| Artificial symmetry | Balanced syntax can imply equal standing, reciprocal responsibility or achieved synthesis where history and ethics remain asymmetric. | Preserve derived formal symmetries; let Job, Daphne, Pauli/Jung and institutional power disturb them where the material does. |
| Imported erudition | Individually relevant sources accumulate faster than their local necessity can be felt, turning cross-register argument into a validating procession. | Keep sources whose resistance, operation or history changes the argument, together with their limits and warrants. |
