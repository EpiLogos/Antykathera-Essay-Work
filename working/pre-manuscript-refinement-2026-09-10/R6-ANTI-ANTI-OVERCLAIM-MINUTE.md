# R6 authorial correction — anti-anti-overclaim / claim-strength symmetry

**Date:** 2026-09-16  
**Standing:** direct authorial correction governing R6 and the proposed T26 common writing base. It is not manuscript prose and does not itself change a claim's canonical status.

## Authorial problem named

Frank identifies a recurrent model failure in the refinement process: the model can project a generalized fear of “overclaiming” onto the work and then write defensively against imagined objections, unspecified skeptical readers, or generic assumptions about what one is allowed to claim. This can produce hedging language even where the essay is **deliberately arguing the supposedly dangerous proposition**, or where no one has actually made the category mistake the prose keeps disclaiming.

The result is not epistemic rigor. It can become **underclaiming by reflex**: an argument says less than its derivation; source-boundary language leaks into the author's own thesis; caveats become the passage's rhetorical centre; and the work adopts a defensive posture toward objections generated by the model rather than by the material.

R6 must correct this before it proposes a common writing base.

## Governing correction

**The essay is allowed to claim what it argues.**

`Derived` and `Argued` are positive statuses. They do not mean “mention cautiously,” “avoid sounding committed,” or “surround with disclaimers.” If the native theorem derives a relation, state the derived relation. If the philosophical argument argues a metaphysical, ethical, epistemic or technological thesis, state the thesis with the strength the argument earns.

Evidence discipline attaches to the office actually at issue:

- **Attribution:** do not claim that an historical source says what it does not say.
- **Formal consequence:** do not claim that one formalism proves something outside the operation actually derived.
- **Cross-register synthesis:** the author may nevertheless argue a strong relation among separately warranted materials. The fact that the sources do not themselves state Taylor's synthesis is not a reason to weaken Taylor's synthesis.
- **Implementation / observation:** do not describe an unrun experiment or unobserved operational result as achieved.
- **Open question:** keep genuinely unresolved propositions open. Artificial phenomenal localisation is the standing example. Do not use `Open` as a generic tone of philosophical modesty.

These boundaries are instruments for saying the positive thesis more exactly. They are not the thesis.

## Anti-anti-overclaim test

R6 reads the T25 model-authored refinement for both **overclaim and underclaim**.

A qualification, negative, status downgrade or retreat must be able to answer:

1. What exact mistaken stronger proposition would otherwise reasonably be inferred from this passage?
2. What in the actual text, named source, live rival argument, venue brief, or implementation state creates that risk?
3. What necessary information disappears if the qualification is removed?

If no concrete answer exists, the qualification is presumptively defensive debris.

Typical warning forms include repeated or reflexive:

- “does not prove…”
- “does not establish…”
- “cannot by itself…”
- “is not evidence of…”
- “merely…” / “only…”
- “remains Open…”
- “Offered” used as politeness rather than actual proposal status
- a strong positive paragraph immediately followed by a retreat which adds no new distinction
- a boundary against an interpretation which the passage never invited

The audit must not mechanically delete negatives. A clean boundary remains where it preserves a real distinction. The question is whether the negative protects the argument's precision or displaces the argument with the model's anxiety about saying it.

## Phantom-reader rule

No R6 reviewer may invent a skeptical reader, developer preference, disciplinary taboo, or accusation and then force the essay to answer it. If an objection is doing argumentative work, name its actual carrier: a source, a documented rival position, the venue brief, an explicit mathematical convention, an implementation state, or a real ambiguity generated by the prose.

“Someone might think…” is insufficient unless the text itself materially creates that reading.

## Positive-first rewrite rule

Where defensive posture is found, rewrite in this order:

**positive proposition → warrant / derivation → necessary boundary → consequence.**

Do not lead with what the claim is not when the essay's real job is to say what it is.

Where a source boundary is needed, attach it narrowly to the source attribution. Do not let “Jung did not say QL” turn into “therefore the QL/Jung relation should be phrased timidly” when the relation is explicitly Taylor's argued refraction.

Where an implementation boundary is needed, attach it to achieved implementation. Do not let “the experiment is unrun” weaken the philosophical office the experiment was designed to test.

## R5 recheck

R6 specifically rereads every R5 repair that was motivated by the word or idea “overclaim.” The repair survives only if it corrected a **real warrant/category error**.

The intended dispositions are not prejudged. Examples of legitimate correction include a false mathematical attribution or a source being made to prove an authorial metaphysical thesis. Examples of illegitimate correction would include downgrading a native metaphysical thesis merely because an external source does not independently prove it, or surrounding a clearly labelled authorial synthesis with repeated denials of historical identity after that boundary is already secure.

R5 is therefore neither blindly preserved nor reflexively reversed. Its changes are judged against the stronger claim-strength symmetry now authorised.

## Required R6 ledger

Use:

`location · present weakening/defensive posture · canonical positive claim · real boundary (if any) · disposition`

Dispositions:

- **STRENGTHEN** — present prose understates the canonical claim.
- **DELETE DEFENSIVE CAVEAT** — qualification answers no live ambiguity.
- **REWRITE POSITIVELY** — distinction is real but prose is organised around negation.
- **RETAIN BOUNDARY** — qualification performs necessary source/formal/implementation work.
- **RESTORE STATUS** — `Open` / `Offered` / weak modality has leaked into an actually Argued or Derived proposition.
- **NO FAULT** — strong wording and its boundary are already proportionate.

The ledger must include preserved strong claims as well as changed ones so the pass cannot become another one-way caution filter.

## T26 consequence

The proposed common writing base is not ready until this pass is complete. The writing packet must carry one explicit review symmetry:

> A reviewer may flag an overclaim only by naming the exact proposition asserted and the warrant it exceeds. A reviewer may equally flag an **underclaim** when hedging, caveats, status language or defensive negations make the prose say less than the canonical argument.

This correction is part of the writing conditions Frank is being asked to ratify at T26.
