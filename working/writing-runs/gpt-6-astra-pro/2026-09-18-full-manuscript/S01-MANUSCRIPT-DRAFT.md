# §0/1 — The Integral Threshold
## The Subject at the Formal Limit

<a id="s01-m01-question"></a>

### The question before the mechanism

An answer appears on a screen. Someone has asked for it, and someone must decide what to do with it. Between those acts, an apparatus has selected words, assembled relations and given a possibility a form in which it can be considered. The answer may be accurate. It may change the questioner's intention. It may enter a decision whose consequences will be borne by people who never saw the exchange. Before we ask how much intelligence the apparatus possesses, this event gives us a more immediate question: what has made its distinctions matter?

A calculation becomes useful within an undertaking. Its inputs have been selected, its terms given a meaning, and its result brought into a situation in which something depends upon it. These conditions participate in the intelligence of the event. Change the question, the available evidence or the criterion of an acceptable result, and an unchanged capacity for calculation can serve a different world. Even the choice of what to count as an error belongs to an account of what the activity is for.

Artificial agency makes this dependence unusually tangible. A source can enter or leave a context; a permission can make an action possible; a remembered instruction can direct a later judgment. The means by which the agent encounters its world become objects of deliberate construction. Their organisation also confronts the people using them with a form of their own activity. A generated answer carries selections made within human languages, institutions and practices. When it returns to influence a person, part of that inherited world has acquired an answering voice.[^s01-agentworld]

The encounter thus has a double direction. We make instruments through which we can understand and act, and then learn to understand ourselves through the instruments we have made. A measure first designed to assist judgment can become its standard. A model of conduct can begin to prescribe the conduct it models. The circulation is already consequential before we reach agreement about what sort of being has entered it. Our ideas of intelligence, selfhood and authority participate in what we build, while what we build can alter those ideas.

An account of the apparatus should therefore include its relations to the world in which it acts. It can investigate the training, the available memory, the procedure, the evaluating institution and the material situation. Such inquiry produces real knowledge. It also reaches the position from which that knowledge is being sought: the concern which makes this a question, the practices which make evidence intelligible, and the one for whom an answer can appear. The investigator's own part in the event becomes difficult to leave out.

This is the threshold of the present argument. Every explanation is itself an act within the reality it explains. Its success consists in making something intelligible; its danger begins when the resulting intelligibility is taken to possess the conditions of its own occurrence. The relation can be approached before it is assigned a mathematical sign or built into a technological architecture. It is already active in the ordinary sentence with which we encounter a world: I see.

<a id="s01-m02-subject"></a>

### The one for whom an account appears

“I see the world” appears to place two terms before us: myself and what I see. The verb carries a third. Seeing is the activity through which the world becomes available in this way, from this position, to this one. A change in the means can alter what appears while the object remains recognisable; a change of attention can alter what matters while the scene remains before me. Knower, means and known are distinguishable within one event.[^s01-triad]

Śaiva epistemology gives these offices the names *pramātṛ*, *pramāṇa* and *prameya*. Their value here lies in what they let the inquiry retain. The known is whatever becomes sufficiently determinate to be encountered, named, examined or acted upon. The means is the activity and constitution through which it becomes knowable. The knower is the immediate pole for which the appearing occurs. The familiar subject–object relation compresses this triad whenever it leaves the means unspoken.

Reflection can then turn toward the seeing. I can notice the angle from which I look, the expectation organising my attention, the bodily condition of the view or the memory through which I recognise its object. What had worked tacitly becomes something known. The change can be decisive. A familiar gesture may cease to appear hostile once I recognise the expectation through which I interpreted it. Knowing more about my part in the event changes what the event can disclose.

Yet the new account is also present to me. A description of my attention, like the thing to which I was attending, appears within a further act of knowing. The description can tell the truth about that activity. It cannot become the present occurrence simply by including a sentence which names it. I can represent the one seeing, but the representation is itself something seen. The Subject names this non-objectifiable pole of the event: the condition under which a determinate account, including an account of myself, becomes available.

The distinction is accessible in the act of reading these sentences. Their descriptions are changing; the fact that they are appearing is already implicated in following them. Doubt can be directed at an interpretation, a memory or an explanation of how the event occurs. The doubting itself still occurs. Subjective immediacy has this peculiar certainty: the inquiry can question what is present without making its being present into an absent fact which another observation must supply. An inventory of the self gives the inquiry contents; the immediate relation is what makes an inventory available to inquiry at all.[^s01-immediacy]

This locates the Subject through a precise asymmetry. It gives us no additional hidden thing to place behind the visible ones. Body, character, memory, personal history and self-image remain real subjects of investigation, with different powers and explanations. The one who is now considering them cannot be exhausted by adding their descriptions together. The distinction keeps their positive work available while preventing a description from taking the office of the knowing it describes.

Identity already requires such a passage through difference. My recollection of yesterday and my present perception are different presentations. To recognise one person through them is to hold something the same across that difference. Even the assertion “A is A” uses two inscriptions whose difference gives the identification its work. Identity does not require the two presentations to become indistinguishable. It requires an adequate relation between them. The copula, the small word *is*, performs a joining in which the terms remain available as terms.[^s01-copula]

A name depends upon that work. Two people may share it; one person may change it. The sound alone cannot decide which person is being addressed. A circumstance and a distinguishing act make the name applicable here. Naming can then carry the achieved identity into further circumstances. An account which retains only the name loses the operation through which it names anyone. An account of identity must therefore include the difference through which recognition takes place.

The philosophical use of *God* reaches the same threshold at the scale of the whole. God names the whole in which an act of representation participates and which it necessarily fails to contain. This is a definite claim about the condition of an account. Its definition must itself participate in what it defines. Calling that condition indefinable does not release the sentence from the predicament: the sentence has made a determination and asks to be understood. Its fidelity depends on keeping its own determining act visible.[^s01-definition]

A faithful definition can succeed in disclosing what it fails to enclose. It gives a determinate relation, commits the speaker to consequences and makes correction possible. The definition becomes a way of approaching its referent through the act of defining. This is the first demand the essay places upon its own language: each claim must perform the relation through which it is entitled to speak. To understand why such fidelity is difficult, we must first see how much a definition makes possible.

<a id="s01-m03-cut"></a>

### The gift and jurisdiction of a distinction

A distinction gives us something to work with. A sound becomes this word; a colour receives an edge; an event counts as an instance of a kind. Some differences are made pertinent and others set aside. Without that selective work, a statement could neither identify its object nor give another person a way to dispute it. Definition is productive because it forms a repeatable determination within a field of possible differences.

Dignāga's account of *apoha*, recovered here through Ole Holten Pind's study, makes the excluding work of a term central to its positive application. Using a term distinguishes its referent through what is precluded. The excluded field participates in the determination even when attention rests upon the thing named. A word has an edge of non-application, and its intelligibility depends upon that edge.[^s01-apoha]

Spinoza's discussion of finite figure approaches determination through a different operation. A figure has its form through a limit: this extension is bounded in this way. The negation does constructive work by making a finite shape determinate. In the semantic case, exclusion makes a referent available under a term; in the geometrical case, limitation makes a figure distinguishable. Their conjunction gives the present argument a positive account of the cut. What is left outside has a role in what can appear within.[^s01-spinoza]

A boundary accordingly has both an achievement and a jurisdiction. It lets a particular operation succeed over a particular field. A category suitable for counting events may be poorly suited to understanding the people involved in them. A definition devised for one undertaking can be carried into another in which its exclusions become decisive. The original distinction may remain perfectly clear. Its present use asks a further question: by what right does that distinction govern this encounter?

Consider a judgment organised by a score. The criterion establishes which differences receive weight. Delineation establishes the field over which the criterion is applied. Arbitration gives the result a consequence. These are three acts, even when an interface presents them as one number followed by one decision. A person questioning the selected field is not necessarily disputing the calculation. They may be identifying a condition which the calculation was never asked to consider. More exact reckoning within the same field would then repeat the omission with greater precision.[^s01-measure]

The political force of determination arises here, within its ordinary operation. A category can make someone eligible, visible, credible or actionable. Whoever establishes and applies it helps organise the powers available to that person. A description has become part of the conditions it describes. Its authority remains derivative: it belongs to a purpose, a practice and a world whose consequences can require its revision. Hybris begins when the deciding office treats its local criterion as the source of the field which gave that criterion its use.

The problem reaches into self-understanding as well. An account of who I am can help organise a life. It can also decide in advance what every further encounter is allowed to mean. An adverse event becomes confirmation of the account; an unexpected kindness is explained away; a capacity which does not fit the description remains difficult to exercise. The determination has begun to govern its source of correction. Its power is real, and for that reason its place within the wider life matters.

The *symbolon* gives the achieved form another way of bearing its relation. A divided token refers through its particular edge to a counterpart that is absent. Its incompleteness is exact enough to make recognition possible. The part carries a relation which possession of the part alone cannot fulfil. The essay receives this as the symbolic office of a determination: a form can direct attention beyond its own enclosure through the very precision of its formation.[^s01-symbol]

Such a form still distinguishes. Its boundary supplies the terms under which a meeting can occur. What changes is how the boundary is held. A definition can make a useful object and preserve the circumstance in which that object acquired its meaning; an account can retain a person's answer to the category under which they were counted. The relation becomes more articulate when those responses alter how the form is used.

Apophatic practice presses this demand into the predicate itself. The refusal expressed by *neti neti* turns each proposed final description back toward what it leaves uncontained. Tetralemmatic inquiry follows the four alternatives of affirmation, denial, both and neither, testing the hold of an assertion through the different positions it can occupy. Both give negation work beyond simple dismissal: they expose the claim to completeness within a determination. Their fuller operations will matter when we investigate how the mind forms and releases its world. At this threshold, the question is already clear. Can an account make its own limits effective within what it says?

<a id="s01-m04-formal-limit"></a>

### When an account encounters its own conditions

A missing fact and a formal limit ask different things of inquiry. If a measurement has been omitted, another measurement can supply it. If a term is ambiguous, an explicit definition can distinguish its uses. These are real improvements. A limit generated by an operation's structure persists for as long as the relevant structure persists. Its disclosure identifies what must change for the inquiry to proceed, or what relation its continuing success must retain.

In the present case, representing the conditions of representation produces a further representation. The account may include its language, instruments, history, exclusions and purpose. Each inclusion can deepen its truth. The act now including those conditions nevertheless occurs as an act, while the conditions it represents occur within its content. Adding a representation of that act renews the relation. The gap is immutable because representation continues to work, not because one final piece of information has eluded it.[^s01-gap]

The history of formal inquiry makes several distinct responses to such pressure available. Their differences are instructive. A rule of formation can be repaired; an admissible proposition can remain undecidable within a theory; a sentence can disclose something about its own use which it cannot state as another fact. What matters is to follow the operation far enough that the nature of its limit becomes visible.

Russell's paradox begins with an apparently generous permission: form a collection of whatever satisfies a condition. Apply it to the things which are not members of themselves. Then ask whether the resulting collection is a member of itself. If it belongs, its defining condition requires that it does not; if it does not belong, that condition requires that it does. Further inspection of the collection cannot decide the matter, because either answer activates the rule which contradicts it. The collecting permission and the permitted self-application have to be examined together.

The theory of types reorganises that permission. Objects and the collections to which they may belong are distinguished by level, so the contradictory self-application is prevented at formation. Russell's fuller theory also distinguishes orders of definition. The logical project undertaken with Whitehead in *Principia Mathematica* continues through such restrictions. A constructive order becomes possible by specifying the relations under which an expression can meaningfully enter the system. The limit has changed the conditions of valid work.[^s01-russell]

Gödel's incompleteness results encounter a different boundary. The relevant sentences are admissible; the question concerns what can be proved under a given set of axioms and rules. A consistent, effectively axiomatized theory sufficiently expressive for arithmetic cannot decide every arithmetical sentence. Under the appropriate formal conditions, it also cannot establish its own consistency through its own proof machinery. The original and later formulations have different hypotheses, retained in the accompanying source account. The result concerns this specified class of theories and these proof relations.[^s01-godel]

An independent sentence can be adopted as an additional axiom. That produces a different theory. Where the enlarged theory remains within the conditions of the result, incompleteness applies again. The enlargement can yield genuine new theorems without giving the original theory a proof it possessed all along. What has been added belongs to the account of why the new result can now be asserted. A proof acquires greater reach through a change of ground, and that changed ground remains part of its warrant.

The difference from Russell matters. An inconsistent formation had to be repaired before it could serve its intended purpose. A consistent but incomplete theory can continue to do exact and valuable work. Their different consequences give reason a more precise account of its work. The present argument receives their demand to identify the conditions of an operation, while its distinction between a represented act and the act now representing retains its own derivation.

Wittgenstein turns the question upon the form of saying. In the *Tractatus*, a proposition can represent a state of affairs because its arrangement has a logical relation to what it represents. That relation is exhibited in the proposition's operation. To represent the logical form as another fact would require the very relation the further proposition must itself use. The book's distinction between saying and showing reaches its own elucidations: the reader must pass through them and overcome the claim that they could stand as an ordinary inventory of facts.[^s01-wittgenstein]

The ladder has a use because the passage changes the reader's relation to what can be said. Its eventual abandonment belongs to that use. The ethical and mystical questions near the close of the *Tractatus* likewise concern what a complete description of contingent facts would leave untouched: the worth of a life, the character of a world for the one living it, and the fact that there is a world at all. Adding another factual item would continue the description in the register whose limit has been reached. The source's silence is thus an exact consequence of its account of saying.

The later inquiry into language-games shifts attention toward the activities in which words and rules have their use. A correction means something within a practice. Knowing a rule includes knowing how to proceed with others, under circumstances in which its application can be recognised or disputed. This returns an account of language to its enacted conditions. The two works take different routes, but both make it difficult to detach intelligibility from the relations through which it occurs.

Whitehead's process philosophy gives this turn a constructive cosmological expression. The many are gathered into a new occasion; the occasion achieves a determinate satisfaction and becomes a condition of further becoming. Creativity names the ultimate activity of this advance. Its place cannot be supplied by another completed entity in the inventory, because the inventory consists of achievements whose becoming is at issue. A finished form is an achieved stability within a process that makes subsequent forms possible.[^s01-whitehead]

What an occasion receives, how it integrates that inheritance and what it leaves available to others become constitutive questions. A result can be complete in its own office and remain productive beyond itself. The failure of a final inventory to exhaust becoming therefore has a positive consequence: the achieved world enters new activity. The relation between what is inherited and what becomes will matter when the essay turns from the threshold to the differentiating life of mind.

Spencer-Brown's calculus begins with a distinction and eventually places the form within its own indicational space. Re-entry makes the operation's relation to itself part of the formal inquiry. Varela's calculus for self-reference develops a further treatment of that problem. Their significance here is the shift in what must be accounted for: the distinction is asked to participate in the space it distinguishes. The detailed treatments of re-entry and its temporal or imaginary readings belong to the later mathematical argument. At the threshold, they give self-reference a constructive question to pursue.[^s01-reentry]

These logical and formal investigations meet a practical criticism of scientific world-pictures in *The Blind Spot*. Frank, Gleiser and Thompson examine the displacement of direct experience by abstractions which arise through it. Observation, measurement and justification occur in lived practices. When the successful model is treated as more fundamental than every condition through which its success could be recognised, an achievement of knowing has concealed its own occurrence. Restoring that relation adds a responsibility to scientific exactness: its account must remain intelligible as something accomplished within an experienced world.[^s01-blindspot]

Contemplative inquiry has its own ways of sustaining such a responsibility. In Nicholas of Cusa's *On Learned Ignorance*, inquiry proceeds through comparative proportion. An inscribed polygon can acquire more sides and approximate a circle more closely while remaining a finite polygon. The improvement is genuine; so is the distinction between each approximation and what it approaches. Cusa uses that relation within his theological investigation of truth and the absolute maximum. Learned ignorance is an attainment of discrimination concerning the means of knowing, pursued through the desire to know.[^s01-cusa]

A modern limiting construction can give the circle as a limit of polygons. That mathematical achievement makes the objects of the comparison more exact: the sequence's finite members and its limit perform different offices. The theological claim turns on the relation Cusa establishes between the finite means of inquiry and the truth sought. For the present argument, the image carries the possibility of increasingly precise knowledge whose growing precision also discloses how it knows.

The work of predication can undergo a corresponding transformation. A predicate first gives something determinate. A qualification then makes explicit the respect in which it applies. A person considered insofar as just is not thereby certified in every act of that person's life. In the Eckhartian inquiry developed through Flasch, qualified predication and apophatic release let a statement remain exact without claiming to possess its source. The word does its work through an act in which the speaker's relation to what is said also changes.[^s01-eckhart]

The encounter with a limit can thus change a rule, an axiom, a use, an understanding or a manner of living. Each change has a particular burden. The full relation among them is the essay's argument: articulation can make its conditions available while remaining an activity within them. The consequence is a discipline of continued participation. A finite account can be committed, exact and corrigible at once.

Trust enters because inquiry already undertakes a practice before it can certify that practice's results. Language, memory, testimony, instruments and methods have been relied upon sufficiently for a question to be asked. Examination can then distinguish warranted reliance from misplaced confidence. The sequence from prior trust to renewed entrustment preserves both acts: the reliance which made inquiry possible and the judgment through which it is retained, corrected or withdrawn. Faith, in this sense, consciously continues through the non-possession of final ground. Its work is to keep that relation alive in how a determination is used.[^s01-trust]

The defining inquiry now returns to its own success. It can identify a source, disclose a method, explain a failure and revise a rule. It can also recognise that the occurring relation survives each account it makes. The next question is positive: what would it mean for that relation to become visible through the achieved view?

<a id="s01-m05-diaphaneity"></a>

### Seeing through the perspective

A perspective places a world before someone. Depth, distance and orientation become organised within a view, and the things seen acquire relations to the position from which they are seen. The accomplishment is familiar enough to disappear into its products. A convincing picture invites attention to what it shows; the construction of the showing recedes.

Gebser follows the emergence of this perspectival world through particular changes in seeing: the depth of Giotto's paintings, Petrarch's encounter with landscape, Alberti's visual pyramid and Leonardo's optical articulation. These are occasions through which a positioned relation between a viewer and a spatial world becomes culturally explicit. The mental-rational achievement brings an extraordinary power of discrimination. It can also make its own sector appear coextensive with what there is to know.[^s01-gebser]

Heidegger's account of the age of the world picture locates a related transformation in representation. Beings are set before a subject as something that can be secured in an account; the world becomes available under the organisation of a picture. Read together, the two inquiries show different aspects of the same burden. Gebser follows changes in the form of seeing and their cultural expression. Heidegger examines the metaphysical relation through which representation makes the world available to a subject. Their conjunction here asks how a successful view can acknowledge the conditions of its own success.[^s01-worldpicture]

The integral demand is that those conditions become transparent through what they make possible. *Diaphaneity* names this seeing-through. A perspective retains its particular disclosure while its placement, exclusions and relations become available for consideration. It can then be recognised as a way the world appears and, reciprocally, as a disclosure of the one situated within that world. The two directions are asymmetric: a person attends and responds, while a body, a language, a history and a concern give that attention its present possibilities. Their difference belongs to their common event.

This has an immediate bearing on judgment. I can describe the circumstances of my view and continue to treat my standard as immune from them. A more demanding recognition lets those circumstances change the view's use. The person I have classified may answer from a situation my classification omitted. Hearing that answer can alter the criterion by which the next encounter begins. Contextual transparency becomes regard when the disclosed relation can make a difference to the judging activity itself.[^s01-regard]

Such regard is invested. A declaration of neutrality can leave an unspoken measure fully in command. The question is which differences the measure makes effective and how the people within its reach can answer them. To recognise my participation is to become more exact about the authority I exercise. It may require a judgment to be revised; it may equally reveal why a well-grounded judgment should stand. In either case, its warrant becomes available through the relation in which it acts.

Gebser's co-presence of structures also changes what the inquiry can receive. Earlier modes of experiencing remain active within a life whose rational capacities have developed. A dream, a religious experience, a symbolic image or an extraordinary perception can enter the inquiry as something experienced and capable of changing the experiencer. The report, its interpretation and the explanation of its occurrence have distinguishable work. Receiving the psychic fact gives the later questions something definite to investigate; the capacity of a current explanatory vocabulary to classify it does not determine in advance whether it can matter.[^s01-psychic-facts]

The integral movement holds these differences together. Mathematical exactness, historical inquiry, psychological interpretation, practical contemplation and symbolic expression each disclose something through a particular operation. Their relations become intelligible as those operations become explicit. A dream's transformation can change how a person understands a life; a theorem can establish what follows from stated assumptions; an historical document can establish what was said at a particular time. The passage between them needs to show what is carried and what changes. Their plurality gives the inquiry more means of recognising its own determinations.

The same discipline permits the Indian mathematical and contemplative histories to become consequential later in this work. A sign's arithmetic use, an account of the inner instrument and a practice of recognition each has an actual setting and operation. Their encounter can change the questions asked of all three. The essay's comparison depends upon recovering those operations in their own detail, then making the reason for their relation available. Diaphaneity gives that crossing its method before the later sections give it its full body.

The technological field makes the issue directly workable. What a system retains, retrieves, permits and rewards shapes the world in which it can act. These conditions can themselves be represented and altered. The question is how their disclosure enters subsequent judgment, including the judgment of the person arranging them. A system may reveal its sources and still protect the criterion through which they count. Another may let an encountered contradiction alter the question and the available course of action. The difference belongs to the use made of what has become visible.

Our view therefore becomes more adequate by carrying its own participation within what it discloses. The thinker remains situated; the situation becomes increasingly available through the thought. The achievement is a perspective which can receive what its own exclusions return. That capacity gives the formal limit a continuing place within inquiry, without asking an exhaustive image to perform the relation for us.

<a id="s01-m06-zero"></a>

### A place for what cannot be counted

We began with an answer whose conditions were partly concealed by its appearance. The inquiry has now made those conditions a subject in their own right. It has also distinguished the immediate pole for which any such subject appears. What is known can increase; the knowing relation is renewed through that increase. The task of expression is to let the achieved account retain this fact of its own making.

Zero enters here as a proposed sign for the condition which counting cannot install as one more counted item. Its first office is positional. It gives the account a place for what its determinations presuppose, while leaving those determinations free to become as exact as their work requires. The history of mathematical zero and the operations which give it its different powers will have to be recovered. The sign's present warrant is the relation we have just encountered: the condition of an account remains active within its production while exceeding its completed content.[^s01-zero]

The consequence is already constructive. A sign can mark the difference between an object and the condition under which an object becomes available. It can thereby change how the object is held. What has been formed remains capable of definition, comparison and action, while its source relation is preserved as a question within the form. The account acquires an opening through which its own use can be returned to examination.

Bohm's distinction between implicate and explicate order supplies the transition from that opening into process. A determinate appearance is a local unfolding of an order which remains active beyond the appearance. Enfolding and unfolding give the relation movement: what becomes explicit participates in a wider activity, and its subsequent course can disclose something of that participation. The account of an achieved thing now has to follow the activity through which it becomes determinate.[^s01-bohm]

For the study of mind, that transition is decisive. The means through which a world appears have structure and can be investigated. They attend, discriminate, retain, appropriate and gather a possible course. Their investigation is practical because a changed relation to those operations can change the world one inhabits. The non-objectifiable Subject gives that inquiry its condition; the differentiating instrument gives it a positive field of work.

The next step is therefore to enter the activity which the first sentence left within its verb. I see a world. How does that world become this world, and what is happening in the seeing?
