# §5 — Objective Internality and Agentic Research

A person asks an artificial agent to help make something they have not yet managed to make alone. The request carries more than its words can immediately convey: earlier attempts, the reason a particular detail matters, an image of how the finished thing should enter other people's lives. The agent receives whatever of this history has become available through the conversation, its sources and its instructions, then produces an answer whose apparent understanding invites the person to continue. A promising proposal can bring the intention into clearer focus; a misplaced assumption can reveal how much the person had expected to go without saying. As the work proceeds, each participant acts upon a situation the preceding exchange has changed. The thing being made acquires a form, and its maker discovers more of what they meant by making it.

Much of the engineer's work takes place within this encounter. A session resumes without the reason for yesterday's decision, so that reason has to be given a durable place. A capability exists but cannot be reached through the present tools; a result meets the stated test while missing the purpose that made the test worth setting. Each difficulty reveals a condition which intelligence needs in order to become effective here. The response may require a different model, a clearer instruction, another source, a changed permission, a more adequate environment or a reconsidered criterion. Deciding which to change is already an investigation into the constitution of the agency at work.

Artificial agency becomes a real mirror through this reciprocal activity. Our language, expectations, classifications and habits of judgment acquire an exteriorised form which can return to influence what we think and do. A fluent answer may expose our readiness to confer authority upon a familiar manner of speaking; a narrowly successful result may make visible a purpose that our instructions left out. The artificial contribution can also enlarge a question, offer an unforeseen form and release a capacity its human interlocutor had been unable to exercise. As an archetypal catalyst of the unconscious, AI brings partly tacit conditions of human agency into an encounter with something that answers. The response can change the person receiving it, including the terms on which a further response will be sought.

Objective Internality names the constituted means through which knowing and acting occur. Memory, discrimination, imagination, language, tools and permissions have their places within it because they change what can be encountered and what can become consequential. A source withdrawn from the inquiry may take a whole interpretation with it. A permission granted to a tool can turn a proposed action into something that happens to another person. The known World is the field disclosed through these relations: what appears as relevant or credible, who becomes addressable, what can be attempted, and which consequences can return to judgment. In the lived human encounter, Life or Mind names the whole relation of the knower, these means and the World they disclose.

The epistemic triad gives those offices their precise names: pramātṛ, the knower; pramāṇa, the means of knowing; prameya, the known. Subjective Immediacy is the occurring pole for which an experience appears, while the capacities and representations through which it takes form can themselves become objects of investigation. Artificial agency gives the triad a functional articulation through a situated inferential locus, its composed means, and a field of encountered inputs, produced outputs and effects. The question of phenomenal experience in a particular artificial configuration remains distinct. What is already available to inquiry is how its effective knowing changes when its means or its situation changes.

The order of dependence is ontological: Subject or Consciousness grounds the mediating powers of Mind through which objects become determinate. Those powers and their objects belong to one reality whose appearing is already implicated in every inquiry into it. The private ego, with its habits and self-image, is itself a formation within this field; its perspective is a finite participation in a world whose reality exceeds what that perspective contains. The artificial act belongs to the same world, where its outputs can be experienced, its material conditions investigated and its consequences felt by lives beyond the person who commissioned it. Its real causal power makes its constituting relations more consequential, while those relations continue to condition the power it exercises.

The distinction has practical depth within the scholastic-practical soteriology of Yoga. Attention, identification and intention are investigated through practices that change the investigator's relation to them; a determination which had governed experience can become recognisable as an activity of the instrument. The formal limit gives this inquiry its enduring direction. An account of the instrument can become increasingly adequate, while the present knowing of that account remains distinguishable from its represented contents. Recognition concerns this occurring relation, and the practical work can change how the instrument is inhabited. Artificial agency enters that inquiry as a made articulation whose conditions can be examined and altered, and whose answers can return those questions to its maker.

A paradigm is a relatively durable organisation of such mediation. It gives a world its familiar distinctions and an undertaking its available methods, so that a person can proceed without having to consider every condition of proceeding. Its pattern becomes apparent when an encounter makes one of those conditions consequential: whose testimony a category excludes, why an evaluation rewards the wrong achievement, or what another participant must surrender to enter the work. These discoveries concern identity and power together. The terms under which someone is recognised help determine which acts are available to them, while those entitled to name, classify and authorise can shape the circumstances in which another person's account will be heard.

O:I makes this field a technological undertaking. Its six products develop six internal facets of Objective Internality: Central carries meaningful continuity; Actuation constitutes living articulation; AIKit composes a changing horizon of potency; Software Factory carries intention through productive transformation; Workcell realises situated existence; and Quaternal Logic with the Meta-Epistemic Framework makes the relations to their determining and containing fields explicitly available to inquiry. A single undertaking involves all six. Its remembered purpose conditions an act, the act draws upon available powers, their organised use changes something in an actual situation, and what happens can alter the relations through which the next act becomes possible.

The QL organisation gives each facet a definite place within that whole, while allowing each to be investigated as a whole in its own right. Central's continuity has a source, a form, powers of adoption and revision, a history, a situation and a return. The same constitutional questions can be asked of a skill, an agent or an encounter, with answers appropriate to that subject. The paired MEF readings develop the particular substance of each facet, so that potency has its modalities and seasons, transformation its material and processual life, and situation the conditions and aperture through which it is encountered. This recurrence makes a local distinction available for comparison without erasing what is particular to its use.

The primitives give these distinctions practical force. Keeping authored ground distinct from an inference allows a person to decide whether a detected pattern should become a commitment. Keeping an enduring Agent distinct from its present Session allows the model or provider to change while the undertaking remains attributable. Keeping a Capability distinct from its present permission preserves the difference between possessing a power and being entitled to exercise it here. An implemented relation determines what a system can retain, substitute, revise or refuse; its philosophical meaning becomes operative in what the arrangement lets its participants do. The code's definitions consequently belong to the argument about agency, with actual behaviour supplying the evidence of how faithfully a particular implementation performs them.

The wider development field can be investigated through the same facets. A harness may organise several of them; a wiki may deepen continuity and the horizon of knowledge; a model-serving system may change the practical ownership of the inferential act. The capacity to compose these achievements depends upon retaining enough of their relations that an undertaking can survive a change of tool. This is the substance of O:I's open and collaborative purpose: a common space in which technologies, methods and inquiries can be brought into relation through the conditions of agentic knowing, and in which an encounter can change the organisation of that space itself.

Who controls those conditions also determines who can participate in their development. The right to invoke a model, possession of its weights, control of its serving environment and the ability to carry one's history elsewhere are different powers. Their distribution shapes whose experiments can run, who benefits from what is learned, and whose purposes can be sustained without continual permission from another office. The political economy is already inside the constitution of the act. Epi-Logos gives its return a further demand: what is learned must be able to change the source, criterion or entrusted decision from which subsequent activity proceeds. The present undertaking begins with something seemingly modest in which all of this is already implicated—the ability to come back tomorrow and continue work whose meaning has survived the night.
